3. EXACT CONFIGURATION COUNT AND POLYNOMIAL MODULAR MOMENT

For vector labels in F_2^r, a legal ordered star consists of its root
x and d_i physical neighbor labels with sum=xR. Root plus any d_i-1
neighbor labels uniquely determine the omitted label, with coefficient
one in every characteristic. There are a^(d_i) legal stars when the
alphabet has size a=2^r. Candidate R is arbitrary for this count; no
certificate, connectedness, rank condition, or nonemptiness is needed.

For a compatible collection of legal-star counts T_i, the exact expected
count is a product of:
  n_i!/prod_star T_i(star)!;
  prod_(x<y) m_ii(x,y)! prod_x (m_ii(x,x)-1)!! /(n_i d_ii-1)!!;
  prod_(x,y) m_ij(x,y)! /(n_i d_ij)! for each cross factor i<j.
The internal expression is used only for active internal factors, with
the zero-count pairing convention (-1)!!=1. Reverse cross tables,
symmetric internal tables, and even internal diagonal counts are exactly
the compatibility restrictions. A loop contributes two to adjacency and
degree. The vertex multinomial already assigns ALL root labels. There
is no missing or additional root-label multinomial.

The uniform factorial remainders include zero entries. A root contributes
at most one unit of log(Dn+1)+1, a cross factor at most a^2 units, and
an internal factor at most a(a-1)/2+2a+2<=2a^2 units for a>=2. Therefore
with
  a_*=2^K,
  F=K+K(K+3)a_*^2/2,    B=K a_*^D+F,
each type contributes at most exp[n Phi+F(log(Dn+1)+1)] and there are
at most (n+1)^(K a_*^D) types. The source's polynomial remainder is a
valid conservative bound. A one-hot alphabet, including size one, can
be padded with zero-mass symbols to the same bound without changing Phi.

Here
  Phi=sum_i alpha_i H(P_i)-sum_(i<=j) w_ij H(nu_ij),
  w_ii=alpha_i d_ii/2,    w_ij=alpha_i d_ij (i<j).
The factor n in n Phi is correct for unequal class sizes.

Within each old neighbor type, symmetrize physical slots only with slots
of that same type. This preserves legal sums and pooled pair laws and
increases star entropy. Weighted lossless omission gives
  H(P_i)=H(rho_i)+sum_s(1-q_is)H(Z_is|X_i)-sum_t q_it J_it,
  J_it=sum_(s!=t) H(Z_is|X_i)-H((Z_is)_(s!=t)|X_i)>=0.
After replacing conditional entropy by pair entropy minus root entropy,
the root coefficient is alpha_i[1-sum_j d_ij q_ji]=0. Symmetry of q
and reversibility are both used; equality of total degrees is not used.
The resulting symmetrized identity is
  Phi=-sum_(i<=j) w_ij(1-2q_ij) I_ij
      -sum_i alpha_i sum_t q_it J_it <=0.                 (E)
The internal half factor produces precisely the same 1-2q coefficient.
Unsymmetrized Phi is no larger. The calculation is valid also when q=0.

Consequently the unconditional configuration moment is bounded by
  E |{Y in F_2^(n by r): AY=YR}| <=(3D)^F (n+1)^B.
It includes rare inputs with very large kernel dimension. It does not
condition on structural recovery. No bounded O(1) moment is proved here.

The simplicity conditioning is to the EXACT intended factor law.
Internal absent factors contribute no cost, internal degree-one matchings
are simple, and a cross factor with either degree one is already simple.
Otherwise the internal loop/double-edge means sum to (d_ii^2-1)/4;
the cross double-edge mean is (d_ij-1)(d_ji-1)/2. Fixed factorial moments
and finite Bonferroni truncations give the stated positive zero-event
limit, with overlapping distinct bad patterns having positive edge excess.
The total mean is <=K^2 D^2. Labeled-stub realization multiplicities are
constant on each simple factor, and factor conditioning preserves
independence. Hence a fixed bounded cost transfers this UNCONDITIONAL
polynomial moment and the macroscopic first moment to the simple model.

4. POSITIVE-WEIGHT SOURCE: EXACT QUANTITATIVE ONE-HOT GAP

This part is a real one-hot argument using the ORIGINAL INTEGER equation,
not an inference about modular one-hot labelings. Let rho_i be the root
law, V_is=1-||mu_is||^2 for the marginal neighbor unit vector, S_i=sum_s
V_is, and delta=sum_i alpha_i(1-max_a rho_i(a)). This delta is exactly
the normalized minimum row-change distance to an old-type point map.

After symmetrization set L=-Phi, E=sum w I, J_0=sum_i alpha_i sum_t J_it.
With q_*=min_active q>0, c_*=min_active(1-2q)>0, (E) gives
  L>=q_* J_0+c_* E.
For each omission t compare the actual retained law with the reference
having the same root law and independent retained neighbor marginals,
also independent of the root. Its divergence is exactly
  kappa_it=J_it+sum_(s!=t) I(X_i;Z_is).
It is finite even on every boundary face: every actually positive tuple
lies in the positive support of the product of its marginals. Then
  sum_i alpha_i sum_t kappa_it
   <=J_0+2(D-1)E <=C_kappa L,
  C_kappa=max{q_*^(-1), 2(D-1)c_*^(-1)}.
These two coefficients are compared against their respective loss terms;
there is no extra factor from adding them. The directed information sum
has factor two, including for an internal factor.

The test function is
  f_it=||R_(X_i,*)-sum_(s!=t)e_(Z_is)-mu_it||^2.
Its actual expectation is V_it by legality. Its auxiliary expectation
is S_i-V_it+B_i, where
  B_i=E||R_(X_i,*)-sum_s mu_is||^2>=0
is the same for every omitted t. Every OCCUPIED row of R has mass d_i.
The other nonnegative vector in the norm also has mass d_i under BOTH
laws, including auxiliary tuples outside legal support. Hence
  0<=f_it<=2D^2,
  |E_aux f_it-E_actual f_it|<=sqrt(2)D^2 sqrt(kappa_it).
Unused rows of R are irrelevant. If a label occurs in old classes of
different total degrees, legality itself is impossible for that label;
there is no assumption silently equating the degrees.

Summing the exact differences over omissions yields
  (d_i-2)S_i+d_i B_i.
Individual differences may be negative; the proof uses the absolute
Pinsker bounds only after this exact summation, in the correct direction.
Cauchy--Schwarz with sum_i alpha_i d_i<=D gives
  sum_i alpha_i(d_i-2)S_i <=D^2 sqrt(2D C_kappa L).
Reversibility and d_i>=3 give
  sum_i alpha_i(d_i-2)S_i >=sum_i alpha_i S_i
    =sum_j alpha_j d_j(1-||rho_j||^2)>=3 delta.
Thus the source's precise gap is valid:
  Phi<=-gamma_+ delta^2,    gamma_+=9/(2D^5 C_kappa).
There is no candidate-class mass lower bound or division by root masses.
Initial symmetrization transfers the bound to the original laws.

7. MIXED-DEGREE SMALL-SUPPORT COUNT

Let S be a nonempty bad set with no outside vertex having exactly one
edge into S. Write s=|S|, W=N(S) outside S, w=|W|, a internal edges,
b boundary edges, and e=a+b. Loops count once in a and twice in degree.
Then
  2a+b=sum_(v in S)d_v>=d_min s,    b>=2w,
  e<=Ds,    w<=Ds/2,
  e-s-w >=[(d_min-2)/2]s.
This uses the actual varying degree sum, not a common regular degree.
Set beta=(d_min-2)/2>=1/2.

A full witness chooses S,W, all S stubs, selected W stubs, and all pairs.
For s<=rho*n/(4D), every prescribed internal-pair denominator and every
cross-bijection denominator is at least rho*n/2. Independence gives
probability <=(2/(rho*n))^e. The witness number is at most
  binom(n,s)binom(n,w)2^(Dw)(2Ds)^e.
Invalid type prescriptions or stub repetitions can safely be counted
with probability zero. The selected actual stub set has exactly 2e
members; its pairing count is <=(2e)^e. All support compositions among
old types are included in this overcount.

The resulting bound is
  exp(s+w)2^(Dw)(4D/rho)^e
    (s/n)^(e-s-w)(s/w)^w,
with (s/w)^w=1 at w=0 and <=exp(s/euler) otherwise. The displayed source
constant
  C=4(D+1)^2 exp(1+D/2+1/euler) 2^(D^2/2)(4D/rho)^D
absorbs the at most (Ds+1)^2 choices of e,w and all remaining factors.
Therefore E witnesses_s<=C^s(s/n)^ceil(beta*s).

Choosing eta<=min{rho/(4D),1/(4euler*C^2),rho/[2(D+1)]} works uniformly
in d_min. For u_s=C^s(s/n)^(beta*s) and s+1<=eta*n,
  u_(s+1)/u_s<=C(euler*eta)^beta<=1/2.
Summing from s=2 gives O(n^(-(d_min-2))). A singleton cannot be bad in
a simple positive-degree graph, so simplicity conditioning proves the
same bound for all nonempty small sets. No conditional singleton bound
in a looped configuration model is incorrectly asserted.

The complementary graph event simultaneously excludes EVERY nonzero
matrix V with AV=VR and <=eta*n nonzero rows, for every field, width,
and R. An exterior vertex with exactly one edge into the support gives
(AV)_v=V_u!=0 but (VR)_v=0. The coefficient is one, including in F_2.
There is no missing union bound over fields or coefficients.

8. EXACT NEAR-POINT CLOSURE AND UNKNOWN FINEST FRAME

On the macro-good event, an accepted integer one-hot Y differs from a
nearest old-type point map JT in fewer than eta*n rows. Its changed
closed neighborhood contains at most (D+1)eta*n<rho*n vertices. Hence
EVERY old class contains a clean vertex with its entire neighborhood
unchanged. Evaluating the ORIGINAL INTEGER identity AY=YR there gives
QT=TR, row by row. It follows that V=Y-JT is an actual intertwiner with
the same small row support, and the common support event forces V=0.
This proves the claimed coarsening conclusion also for non-surjective
maps and candidate classes of vanishing proportion.

The algorithm enumerates every r<=K and every nonnegative integer R of
row sums <=D, computes the complete binary kernel of
  I_r tensor A-R^T tensor I_n,
and enumerates all basis combinations. It then checks one-hot rows,
nonempty candidate classes, and the ORIGINAL INTEGER equation. Binary
parity solutions alone are never accepted. Every integer equitable
candidate lies in the corresponding binary kernel and is checked.

The true (k,Q,J) is always included. On the structural event each
accepted Y=JT has an onto map T, so r<=k. An accepted true k-class
partition is present, and maximizing r therefore gives k. An onto map
between k-element sets is a permutation. This proves recovery of unknown
k and Q up to class names. It concerns the finest equitable partition
among the supplied bounded class family, not the unrestricted singleton
partition. The algorithm receives neither the certificate nor the true
frame or size proportions.

9. UNCONDITIONAL BIT WORK, MEMORY, AND THE CLOCK CAP

Per candidate, binary Gaussian elimination costs O_K(n^3) bit operations.
A deliberately loose O_(K,D)(n^2) bound per enumerated kernel vector
includes basis combination, one-hot/nonempty tests, class-size counters,
and bounded-degree integer neighbor-count verification. All candidates
form a fixed finite family. The unconditional modular moment therefore
gives base expected work O_(K,D)(n^(B+2)), including rare huge kernels.
This expectation is not multiplied by a graph-success indicator.

A binary basis, one current candidate, the best completed partition, and
workspace use O_K(n^2) bits; a full list of kernel vectors is not stored.
The conservative untruncated total-work bound O(n^(B+3)) is valid. The
proof does not require an O(log n)-bit clock for an uncapped exponential
enumeration; such a clock would be unjustified. If one instruments that
run, the deterministic h_R<=Kn bound permits O(n) counter bits, which
still fits the looser expected-work statement.

The capped implementation instead stops after n^(B+4) BASE operations.
Its binary clock has O(log n) bits; O(log n) clock work per base operation
gives deterministic total bit work O(n^(B+4)log n)<=O(n^(B+5)). Markov
uses the SHARPER expected base bound O(n^(B+2)) and gives O(n^(-2))
interruption probability. It does not apply Markov to a differently
charged total-work quantity. Interruption outputs explicit abstention,
never an unfinished maximum-class certificate. The stated total failure
bound follows:
  O(n^(-(d_min-2))+n^(-2))
    +C(K,D)(n+1)^B exp(-gamma eta^2 n).
All constants and exponents concern fixed K,D. No useful exponent or
practical running time is claimed.

10. UNIFORMITY AND THE TWO EXAMPLES

There are finitely many bounded integer true frames. Each connected
reversible frame fixes its positive rational size proportions. Choosing
one eligible certificate per qualifying frame gives a positive finite
minimum of q_* and c_* in the positive theorem, or of c in the zero-weight
extension. The eventual simplicity thresholds can also be maximized
over this finite family. This supplies uniform eventual bounds without
making any certificate an input. It does not allow growing K,D or a
vanishing strict-upper margin along an infinite frame family.

Positive example: K_5 minus one edge has degrees three and four. Weight
1/3 on edges incident to the two degree-three vertices and 1/6 on edges
inside the other three vertices. Low vertices receive 3*(1/3)=1; high
vertices receive 2*(1/3)+2*(1/6)=1. All active weights are strictly in
(0,1/2). Equal-size matching lifts satisfy every balance condition and
exist for every positive lift size N, with no internal parity condition.
Recovery is asymptotic with high probability, not a claim for every
realization at every N.

Zero-weight example: join two copies of K_(3,3) by one bridge, from a
left vertex of the first to a right vertex of the second. The resulting
twelve-type simple connected base has degrees three and four. Weight
1/3 on core edges and zero on the bridge gives normalized rows and
c=1/3. For ANY symmetric row-normalized certificate, sum the three left
row equations in the first copy and subtract its three right equations.
Internal core weights cancel; the only remaining contribution is the
bridge weight. The demand difference is 3-3=0, forcing that weight zero.
Thus this is genuinely outside the frozen positive-certificate theorem
and inside the addendum. Its independent matching lifts exist for every
N; the bridge in the base does not invalidate the typed sampling model.
