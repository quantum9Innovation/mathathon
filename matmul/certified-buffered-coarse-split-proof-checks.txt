Actual claim
------------
Given an explicit supported Hermitian coarse core K and a positive
threshold t, the algorithm CONSTRUCTS a unitary P,Q basis for H=tK^-1,
with dangerous dimension k bounded by the number of coarse eigenvalues
in [t/3,3t]. It guarantees ||H_QP||<=1e-5, ||H_PP||<4 and no eigenvalue
of H_QQ in [1/2,2]. Expected preparation costs m^(omega+o(1)) in the
stated exact-field arithmetic model, with no input-conditioned logarithm.
The old supplied exact invariant split is therefore unnecessary.

In the raw-factor model S=ZKZ*, ||Z*Z-I||<=1/8, these certificates give
the affine offblocks needed by the shared-series near-real solver.
For actual near-real vertical poles, the constant-coupling argument is
used only within the stated disk. Far poles use the ordinary raw resolver.
The result does not bound k for arbitrary inputs or supply final LRA.

Why the construction is charged and valid
----------------------------------------
R=2tK(K^2+t^2 I)^-1 has norm at most one, and H R has norm at most two.
Both are explicit small-core functions computed by products and inverses,
with their arithmetic charged. This avoids asking an approximate EVD
for accuracy proportional to t/||K|| or 1/(t||K^-1||).

Candidates for an approximate diagonalization of bounded R are accepted
only after exact Frobenius residual and Gram tests at inverse-polynomial
accuracy. The analytical polar factor is a proof device; the algorithm
uses exact thin QR and never computes a matrix polar factor. A largest
computed diagonal gap in [0.7,0.75] is at least 1/[20(m+1)]. The ordered
Sylvester integral gives a dimension-independent subspace bound.

The decisive step filters the selected approximate basis once by R.
Its smallest singular value stays above .69, and the bounded H R
controls WEIGHTED leakage even when H has arbitrarily large norm.
An approximate spectral basis without this filter would not justify
the inverse-weighted assertion. A residual contradiction with a fixed
buffer >1/25 proves that compression to the complement remains safe;
the proof does not replace approximate invariance by exact invariance.

The paid EVD subroutine is Shah (2024), Algorithm 3 and Theorem 4.2:
  https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09880v1
Root and the separate reviewer directly inspected these statements,
including near-unitarity, absolute/relative residual conventions,
random primitive definitions and the failure-parameter range. Algorithm 3
accepts a known norm upper bound, so no eigennorm oracle is introduced.
Constant-success attempts, exact certificates and independent repetition
handle confidence rather than invoking the theorem outside its range.
Dyadic rounding, scalar precision and approximate random primitives are
explicitly charged with polylogarithmic overhead in the source.

This is an analytic algorithm theorem using the cited paid component,
not an implementation or numerical benchmark of Shah's diagonalizer.
No floating-point experiment is presented as proof of the weighted
leakage or preprocessing runtime. Exact core inversions and exact QR
can be ill-conditioned in finite words; their stability is excluded.
