\paragraph{Status and inputs.}
This proof combines a new-to-this-session finite-defect first-moment
calculation with vertex insertion, M\'esz\'aros's established matching
mixing theorem, and established even-size graph-model contiguity. It
does not invoke an unproved shifted version of adjacency invertibility.
Historical novelty is not asserted. All vertex and half-edge labels are
retained in the probability arguments.

\begin{theorem}[Odd block size, $d\ge6$ even]\label{thm:main}
Fix even $d\ge6$ and $0<b<d$. For odd $N$, let $C,D$ be independent
uniform simple $d$-regular graphs on two labelled sets of size $N$.
For any prescribed simple $b$-regular bipartite cross graph with matrix
$B$, put
\[
 A_B=\begin{pmatrix}C&B\\B^T&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad \delta=d-b.
\]
Then, uniformly over all such deterministic $B$,
\[
 \Prb\bigl(\nullity_{\mathbb R}(A_B-\delta I)\ge2\bigr)=o(1).
\]
The planted sign vector is the unique real eigenvector direction at
$\delta$, with probability tending to one. No effective rate or outlier
gap is asserted. This proof does not cover $d=4$, odd $N$.
\end{theorem}

\section{A matching core plus one deterministic root}
Write $N=m+1$, so $m$ is even. The root is the last vertex of each block.
For each fixed integer $r\ge3$, let $R_r$ be the adjacency matrix of
$r$ independent uniform perfect matchings on the $m$ core vertices.
For fixed odd prime $p$, M\'esz\'aros's Theorem~1.6 gives
\[
 e_{m,r,p}:=\sum_{x\in\Fp^m}\sup_z
 \left|\Prb(R_rx=z)-q_{r,x}(z)\right|\longrightarrow0,
\]
where
\[
 q_{r,x}(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 \boldsymbol1_{\{z=ra\one\}},&x=a\one,\\
 p^{-m+1}\boldsymbol1_{\{\one^Tz=r\one^Tx\}},&x\text{ nonconstant}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Over $\Fp$ the symmetric tensor constraint in that theorem is vacuous
because $p$ is odd. The prime need not avoid $r$.

Consider deterministic matrices and vectors $H,J,a,h$ such that
\[
 C=\begin{pmatrix}R_r+H&a\\a^T&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D=\begin{pmatrix}R_{r'}+J&h\\h^T&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad r,r'\ge3,
\]
are $d$-regular. More explicitly, $H,J$ are symmetric integer matrices,
\[
 H\one=(d-r)\one-a,\qquad J\one=(d-r')\one-h,
\]
and $a,h$ are zero-one vectors with exactly $d$ nonzero entries. The
two matching cores are independent. The complement may have parallel
edges; the root itself has a simple star. Set
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}K&u\\v^T&\gamma\end{pmatrix},
\]
where $\gamma\in\{0,1\}$, $u,v$ have $b-\gamma$ ones, and
\[
 K\one=b\one-u,\qquad K^T\one=b\one-v.
\]
We assume $m>d+b$, as holds for all sufficiently large sizes.

\begin{lemma}[Affine core bound]\label{lem:affine}
Fix an odd prime $p\nmid b$. For every $z\in\Fp^m$,
\[
 \sum_xq_{r,x}(\delta x-Hx+z)\le2,
 \qquad
 \sum_x\Prb(R_rx=\delta x-Hx+z)\le2+e_{m,r,p}.
\]
The analogous statements hold for $J,r'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For nonconstant $x$, the scalar reference constraint is
\[
 (b\one-a)^Tx=\one^Tz.
\]
This is a nonzero linear functional: some coordinate outside the
$d$-element support of $a$ has coefficient $b\ne0$ in $\Fp$.
At most $p^{m-1}$ inputs qualify, each with mass $p^{-m+1}$.
For constant $x=c\one$, the exact-vector constraint is
$z=bc\one-ca$. A coordinate outside $\operatorname{supp}a$ fixes
$c$ uniquely, so this contributes at most one. Sum the mixing errors
to obtain the second inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-defect first moment]\label{prop:hybrid}
In the preceding hybrid model,
\[
 \E|\ker_{\Fp}(A_B-\delta I)|
 \le2p+2p^{4-\rank_{\Fp}K}
 +p^2\bigl[(2+e_{m,r',p})e_{m,r,p}+2e_{m,r',p}\bigr].
\]
The bound is uniform over every deterministic admissible $H,J,a,h,B$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write an input as $(x,t,y,s)$, where $x,y\in\Fp^m$ and $t,s$ are root
coordinates. The root equations are
\begin{align*}
 F_1&:=a^Tx+v^Ty+\gamma s-\delta t=0,\\
 F_2&:=u^Tx+h^Ty+\gamma t-\delta s=0.
\end{align*}
The two core equations are
\begin{align*}
 R_rx&=\delta x-Hx-at-Ky-us,\\
 R_{r'}y&=\delta y-Jy-hs-K^Tx-vt.
\end{align*}
Independence expresses the expected kernel size as the sum over all
$(x,t,y,s)$ of the product of these two core probabilities and the
indicators of $F_1=F_2=0$.

Replace the first core probability by $q_{r,x}$. Ignoring root
indicators, for fixed $x,t,s$, Lemma~\ref{lem:affine} bounds the sum
over $y$ of the second probability by $2+e_{m,r',p}$. Summing over
$t,s$ costs $p^2$. The replacement error is at most
$p^2(2+e_{m,r',p})e_{m,r,p}$. Replacing the other probability by its
reference costs at most $2p^2e_{m,r',p}$ in the same way.

Suppose first that both $x,y$ are nonconstant. On the two root
equations, both core reference constraints reduce to the single
global sum constraint
\[
 F_0:=b(\one^Tx+t+\one^Ty+s)=0.
\]
For example, the first core scalar constraint, before using $F_1$, is
\[
 b(\one^Tx+\one^Ty)+dt+(b-\gamma)s-a^Tx-v^Ty=0;
\]
substitution of $F_1$ makes this exactly $F_0=0$.

The three forms $F_0,F_1,F_2$ are independent. The $x$-coefficient
vectors of $F_1,F_2$ are $a,u$. Since $|\operatorname{supp}a|=d>b$
and $|\operatorname{supp}u|\le b$, a coordinate in
$\operatorname{supp}a\setminus\operatorname{supp}u$ first forces the
coefficient of $F_1$ to vanish in any dependence between $F_1,F_2$;
$F_2$ is nonzero because $h$ is nonzero. A coordinate of $x$ outside
$\operatorname{supp}a\cup\operatorname{supp}u$ shows that $F_0$ is
not in their span, since its coefficient there is $b\ne0$.
Thus there are at most $p^{2m-1}$ qualifying quadruples. Each has
reference mass $p^{-2m+2}$, contributing at most $p$.

If $x=c\one$ and $y=e\one$, the first constant reference equation is
\[
 b(c+e)\one=a(c-t)+u(e-s).
\]
A coordinate outside the two supports forces $c+e=0$. A coordinate
in $\operatorname{supp}a\setminus\operatorname{supp}u$ then forces
$t=c$. If $u$ is nonzero, this gives $s=e$; if $u=0$, the second core
reference equation gives $s=e$ using $h\ne0$. These $p$ planted
vectors satisfy all equations and contribute exactly $p$.

Finally let $x=c\one$ and $y$ be nonconstant. For each of the $p^3$
choices of $c,t,s$, the first reference equation prescribes $Ky$.
There are at most $p^{m-\rank K}$ such $y$, and the other reference
mass is at most $p^{-m+1}$. This contributes at most
$p^{4-\rank K}$. The reverse orientation has the same bound. Combining
the cases and the errors proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

The cross matrix satisfies
\[
 \rank_{\Fp}B\ge\frac{N}{b(b-1)+1},\qquad
 \rank_{\Fp}K\ge\rank_{\Fp}B-2.
\]
The first bound follows by greedily selecting rows with disjoint
supports; the second follows by deleting one row and one column.
Consequently Proposition~\ref{prop:hybrid} gives
$\limsup\E|\ker_{\Fp}(A_B-\delta I)|\le2p$ uniformly over the
deterministic complements and cross graph, provided $r,r'\ge3$ range
over a fixed finite set. A real nullity of at least two implies mod-$p$
nullity at least two by vanishing integer minors, so its probability has
limsup at most $2/p$. Taking arbitrarily large fixed odd primes avoiding
$b$ proves uniform $o(1)$ real failure in this hybrid model.

\section{Vertex insertion and the untouched layers}
Put $k=d/2$. On each even set of $m=N-1$ vertices, first take a union
of $d$ independent perfect matchings. Choose $k$ distinct edge
instances uniformly from the $dm/2$ coloured edge instances. Delete
each chosen edge $ij$, add the root $*$, and replace that edge by
$i*$ and $j*$. The old degrees stay $d$, and the root has degree $2k=d$.

With probability $1-O_d(m^{-1})$ the chosen edges have distinct
endpoints. Indeed, in every resulting $d$-regular multigraph the number
of pairs of distinct edge instances sharing an endpoint is at most
$m\binom d2$, whereas the total number of pairs of edge instances is
$\binom{dm/2}{2}$. A union bound over the fixed number $\binom k2$
of sampled pairs gives the assertion. There are no loops initially.
On this good event the new root is a simple star.

The selected colour counts have the multivariate hypergeometric law
with $d$ equal classes of size $m/2$, independently of the random
matchings. One can therefore generate them first, then reveal the
matchings in the hit colours and the uniformly chosen subsets of
edges in those colours. Conditional on this information, all unhit
matchings remain independent and uniform. At most $k$ colours are
hit, so at least $d-k=d/2\ge3$ matchings remain untouched.
The good-endpoint event is measurable with respect to the revealed
information. The residual hit edges and new root star form exactly a
deterministic complement of the hybrid type above, with
$r=d-\#\{\text{hit colours}\}\ge3$.

Perform this independently in the two blocks. Conditional on all
revealed information in both blocks, the hybrid proposition applies
uniformly. Its uniformity permits averaging over the deterministic
complements. The bad-endpoint event has probability $O_d(m^{-1})$.
Thus the failure probability for the pair of inserted matching-model
graphs tends to zero, uniformly over every prescribed cross matrix $B$.

\section{Exact configuration insertion and contiguity}
Let $P_m$ be the uniform pairing model on $dm$ labelled old stubs. Let
$K_m$ be the following insertion kernel: choose $k=d/2$ old pairs
uniformly, delete them, introduce $d$ labelled root stubs, and connect
the $d$ old endpoints bijectively to the root stubs by a uniform
bijection. This is the same graph-level uniform edge-instance insertion
kernel just used for the matching model; the extra stub bijection is
invisible at graph level.

\begin{lemma}[Exact pairing law]\label{lem:pairing}
$P_mK_m$ is the uniform pairing model on $d(m+1)$ stubs conditioned on
the root having no loops.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a final pairing with no root-root pair, remove the root stubs.
The $d$ old partners can be paired among themselves in exactly
$(d-1)!!$ ways. Each is an old pairing with a distinguished selected
$k$-set of old pairs, and the root-to-old bijection is fixed by the
final pairing. Thus every allowed final pairing has exactly the same
number of preimages. Uniformity follows. Other loops and parallel
edges are allowed in this counting argument.
\end{proof}

Let $P_m^0$ be $P_m$ conditioned on having no old loops, and let $Q_m$
be the graph law of the union of $d$ independent uniform perfect
matchings. The established contiguity theorem says $P_m^0$ and $Q_m$
are mutually contiguous for fixed $d\ge3$ and even $m$. This is stated
in Nguyen--Wood, Section~4, proof of Theorem~1.6 (PDF p.~8), crediting
Janson and Molloy--Robalewska--Robinson--Wormald, Theorem~3.

Mutual contiguity is preserved under a common Markov kernel and under
fixed independent products. For the kernel claim, if
$\int f_m\,dQ_m\to0$ with $0\le f_m\le1$, then
$Q_m(f_m>\varepsilon)\to0$ for every $\varepsilon>0$;
contiguity gives $P_m^0(f_m>\varepsilon)\to0$, so
$\int f_m\,dP_m^0\le\varepsilon+o(1)$. Letting $\varepsilon\to0$
proves the claim. The product statement follows by applying the same
bounded-function argument successively to sections.
Therefore the just-proved $o(1)$ failure for $Q_mK_m$ in two blocks
transfers to $(P_m^0K_m)^{\otimes2}$ for every deterministic sequence
of cross matrices.

One final conditioning point is essential: $P_m^0K_m$ need not equal
the unrestricted odd pairing law. We use the following exact comparison.
Let $S$ be the event that the inserted graph is simple, and let $L_0$
be the event that the old pairing was loopless. Whenever the inserted
graph is simple, its root neighbours are distinct. Every inverse pairing
in Lemma~\ref{lem:pairing} then pairs stubs at distinct old vertices,
while all other old edges are unchanged. Hence $S\subseteq L_0$ in
the insertion experiment, and for any graph event $E$,
\[
 (P_mK_m)(E\cap S)
 =P_m(L_0)(P_m^0K_m)(E\cap S).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:pairing}, conditioning the left output on $S$ gives
exactly the uniform simple $d$-regular graph on $N=m+1$ vertices.
For fixed $d$, $(P_mK_m)(S)$ is bounded below by a positive constant:
ordinary configuration simplicity has that property, and conditioning
on no root loops only changes it by a bounded factor. It follows that
\[
 \Prb_{\text{uniform simple odd}}(E)
 \le C_d(P_m^0K_m)(E).
\]
For two independent blocks, the same argument conditions both outputs
on simplicity and costs only another fixed factor. We obtain the
claimed $o(1)$ real-kernel failure for every deterministic sequence $B$.
If uniformity over $B$ failed, selecting a violating sequence would
contradict this conclusion. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\paragraph{Why $d=4$ is not settled here.}
Then $k=2$, and choosing edges from two distinct colours leaves only
two untouched matching layers. This colour pattern has probability
bounded away from zero. M\'esz\'aros's mixing input requires at least
three layers. Conditioning the two selected edges to share a colour
does leave three layers, but that restricted insertion kernel is not
the common uniform edge-instance kernel used above; the required
reverse contiguity comparison would need a new argument.
