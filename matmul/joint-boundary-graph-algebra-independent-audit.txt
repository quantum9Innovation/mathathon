1. NONSYMMETRIC RANK-2p RESTORATION
===================================
Let U have orthonormal columns, at most p in number, and put P=UU^T,
Q=I-P. For any real matrix Dtilde=XY^T, define

  V=Dtilde U=X(Y^T U),
  Z=Q Dtilde^T U=QY(X^T U),
  L=(V,U), R=(U,Z).

Then the exact algebraic identity is

  LR^T=Dtilde P+P Dtilde Q=Dtilde-Q Dtilde Q.             (1)

The transpose in Z is essential for a nonsymmetric Dtilde. The script
uses exactly these factors and explicitly adds a nonsymmetric error to
its auxiliary fractional correction. Their width is at most 2p.

If ||Dtilde-D||<=delta, then (1) differs from D-QDQ by at most 2delta:
apply the triangle inequality to (Dtilde-D)P+P(Dtilde-D)Q. In particular
the script's budget 3delta is valid. Combining these factors with any
approximation to QDQ adds their two error bounds, exactly as tested.
No symmetric or linear approximate solve map is required for this
factor identity after X,Y have been constructed.

2. THE FINITE COMMON-MASS GRAPH INPUTS
======================================
Each test starts with a connected positive-conductance path and a
positive diagonal mass matrix M. Its common kernel in symmetric
mass-normalized coordinates is span(sqrt(M)1). Restricting to its
orthogonal complement is only a dense diagnostic convenience.

The initial path Laplacian is scaled by a positive scalar so that the
reduced A has norm 0.4h. The update vector is the mass-normalized
incidence column of an existing path edge, restricted to the same
kernel complement and normalized to norm one. Increasing that edge's
conductance realizes precisely ww^T. Thus B=A+ww^T has norm at most
1+0.4h<1+h. The vertex masses are identical before and after each
update. The construction is a genuine finite graph pair; scaling A
means scaling its original conductances.

For t>0, the resolvent identity gives

  (A+tI)^(-1)-(B+tI)^(-1)
    =(A+tI)^(-1)w w^T(B+tI)^(-1).

After t=exp(x), the fractional integral has positive weight
(sin(pi alpha)/pi)exp((alpha+1)x)dx. The script uses this correct
weight on its ordinary logarithmic grid, with separate left and right
factors. Its use of a uniform grid does not verify an optimized
Gaussian localization theorem or a sparse shifted-SDD solver.

3. FINITE TERMINAL RANK-ONE COROLLARY
=====================================
Fix 0<alpha<1 and suppose

  A>=0, B=A+ww^T<=(1+h)I, ||w||=1,
  0<epsilon<=1/2, h^alpha<=epsilon/2,
  ||Dtilde-D||<=delta<=epsilon/100,
  D=B^alpha-A^alpha.

Dtilde may be nonsymmetric. The inequalities involving its error and
h^alpha are promises; the construction below does not need to decide
them from an exact norm or fractional-power oracle.

For completeness tr(D)<=1 has an elementary finite-dimensional proof.
For eta>0 let A_eta=A+eta I and g_eta(s)=tr(A_eta+sww^T)^alpha. Then

  g_eta'(s)=alpha w^T(A_eta+sww^T)^(alpha-1)w
           <=alpha(eta+s)^(alpha-1).

Indeed A_eta+sww^T>=eta I+sww^T, and x^(-(1-alpha)) is operator
monotone decreasing on positive definite matrices. Integrating for
0<=s<=1 and then taking eta down to zero gives tr(D)<=1.
Since D>=0, consequently 0<=D<=I.

Set y=Dw and d=w^TDw. From w^TAw<=h, scalar Jensen, and B>=ww^T,

  d=w^TB^alpha w-w^TA^alpha w
    >=1-(w^TAw)^alpha>=1-h^alpha>=3/4.                   (2)

The exact Schur rank-one correction F=yy^T/d satisfies

  0<=D-F, ||D-F||_*<=h^alpha,                            (3)

by the reviewed projected trace bound and its Schur-complement argument.
In addition ||y||<=1 and ||y||^2<=d since 0<=D<=I.

The actual construction is

  yhat=Dtilde w, dhat=w^Tyhat, Fhat=yhat yhat^T/dhat.      (4)

We have ||yhat-y||<=delta, |dhat-d|<=delta, and delta<=1/200.
Thus dhat>=3/4-delta>1/2, so (4) is a well-defined positive
rank-at-most-one matrix. Also ||yhat||<=1+delta. Therefore

  ||Fhat-F||
   <=[(||yhat||+||y||)delta]/dhat
       +||y||^2 |dhat-d|/(d*dhat)
   <=(3+delta)delta/(3/4-delta)
   <5delta<=10delta.                                    (5)

Combining (3) and (5) gives, in particular, the requested budget

  ||D-Fhat||<=h^alpha+10delta
             <=epsilon/2+epsilon/10<epsilon.             (6)

If Dtilde=XY^T is already supplied with q columns, yhat=X(Y^Tw)
uses O(nq) arithmetic and the scalar dhat uses O(n). Returning factors
yhat/dhat and yhat represents Fhat without an additional square root.
These are costs for this final extraction only; they say nothing about
the cost of obtaining the promised auxiliary factors or error bound.
At alpha=1/2, the endpoint promise can also be checked from nonnegative
input h by h<=epsilon^2/4. No analogous exact test at an arbitrary alpha
is presumed by this corollary.
