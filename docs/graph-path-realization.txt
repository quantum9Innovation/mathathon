Sharp fractional-power corrections already occur on weighted paths
================================================================

Working derivation, 2026-11-14. This file proves a graph realization of
the positive-input extremal class in sharp-fractional-holder.tex. The
vertices have arbitrary positive masses. No assertion about external
priority, ordinary unweighted graphs, or degree-normalized graphs
without holding loops is made.

1. Definitions and precise rank-one statement
-------------------------------------------

Let L be the combinatorial Laplacian of a finite undirected graph with
positive edge conductances, and let M=diag(m_v)>0 be its vertex-mass
matrix. Its symmetric mass-normalized operator is

  H=M^{-1/2} L M^{-1/2}.

Masses are fixed when edge conductances change. An increase tau>0 in
the conductance of edge {u,v} changes H by

  E=tau M^{-1/2}(e_u-e_v)(e_u-e_v)^T M^{-1/2}.

Thus E is positive and has rank one. Mass normalization is not assumed
to mean division by the graph's changing degree matrix.

Fix 0<alpha<1 and epsilon>0. Write N_epsilon(T) for the number of
singular values of T strictly greater than epsilon. Define

  P_alpha(epsilon) = sup N_epsilon((A+ww^T)^alpha-A^alpha),

where the supremum is over all finite real symmetric A>=0 and all real
unit vectors w. Define G_alpha(epsilon) by the same supremum restricted
to pairs of mass-normalized Laplacians of CONNECTED WEIGHTED PATHS,
where a single existing edge has its conductance increased and the
normalized update E has operator norm exactly one.

Then, at every epsilon>0,

  G_alpha(epsilon) = P_alpha(epsilon).                         (1)

These are suprema over all finite dimensions, positive masses and
conductances; parameters may depend on epsilon. This statement does
not require a common dimension or a fixed graph as epsilon decreases.

2. Reduction to a positive definite irreducible Jacobi matrix
-----------------------------------------------------------

Take one witness A>=0, ||w||=1. Let K be the cyclic subspace generated
by w under A, namely span{w,Aw,A^2w,...}. Since A is symmetric, K and
K^perp are invariant, and ww^T vanishes on K^perp. The fractional-power
correction therefore vanishes on K^perp. Restrict to K.

For eta>0 replace A by A+eta I. Continuous functional calculus for the
continuous scalar function x^alpha on [0,infinity) implies convergence
of the correction to the original correction in operator norm as
eta decreases to zero. The perturbation remains ww^T, of norm one.
Consequently, every strict singular-value count at a fixed epsilon is
preserved from below for all sufficiently small positive eta. This is
also valid when epsilon happens to equal another singular value: only
the finitely many singular values STRICTLY above epsilon need survive.

It therefore suffices to approximate a positive definite cyclic pair
(A,w). Exact symmetric Lanczos, with the signs of successive basis
vectors chosen appropriately, gives an orthogonal basis Q such that

  J=Q^T A Q is positive definite tridiagonal,
  J_(i,i+1)=J_(i+1,i)<0, and Q^T w=e_1.

Cyclicity ensures that every off-diagonal of this tridiagonal matrix
is nonzero. In dimension one the off-diagonal assertion is vacuous.
Thus the entire correction is unitarily equivalent to

  D_J=(J+e_1e_1^T)^alpha-J^alpha.                              (2)

3. A grounded weighted path with exactly this operator
----------------------------------------------------

The inverse of the positive definite irreducible matrix J with
nonpositive off-diagonals is entrywise strictly positive. For a direct
proof choose a>max_i J_ii sufficiently large and write J=aI-B with
B>=0 and rho(B)<a. Then J^{-1}=a^{-1}sum_{k>=0}(B/a)^k>=0;
the connected tridiagonal pattern makes every entry strictly positive.

Put

  d=J^{-1}e_1>0, D=diag(d), K=D J D.

The off-diagonals of K are negative precisely along a path. Moreover

  K 1 = D J d = d_1 e_1.                                    (3)

For i=1,...,n-1 give the path edge {i,i+1} conductance

  c_i=-J_(i,i+1)d_i d_(i+1)>0.

Attach an additional ground vertex 0 to vertex 1 with conductance
d_1>0. The principal block of this (n+1)-vertex Laplacian on vertices
1,...,n equals K, because its off-diagonals and row sums agree by (3).
Give vertex i>=1 mass d_i^2. The mass-normalized grounded operator is

  D^{-1} K D^{-1}=J.

Increasing only the edge {0,1} by d_1^2 changes that grounded operator
by exactly e_1e_1^T. Both the base and updated grounded objects have
therefore been realized on the SAME weighted path.

4. A finite-mass ground vertex and exact update normalization
-----------------------------------------------------------

Grounding is eliminated by giving vertex 0 the finite mass R^2, R>0.
With the ground coordinate first, the full normalized base operator is

  H_R = [ d_1/R^2    -e_1^T/R ]
        [ -e_1/R          J   ].                              (4)

It is the normalized Laplacian of the connected weighted path just
constructed. For example its kernel vector is (R,d^T)^T, as required.
Increasing conductance {0,1} by d_1^2 gives normalized update

  E_R=v_R v_R^T,  v_R=(-d_1/R,e_1^T)^T,
  ||E_R||=||v_R||^2=1+d_1^2/R^2.                             (5)

Set s_R=(1+d_1^2/R^2)^{-1} and multiply ALL base and updated edge
conductances by s_R, leaving the masses fixed. The resulting normalized
operators are s_R H_R and s_R(H_R+E_R); their difference has norm
exactly one. They still differ only by an increase of edge {0,1}.

As R tends to infinity,

  s_R H_R       -> diag(0,J),
  s_R(H_R+E_R)  -> diag(0,J+e_1e_1^T).

All matrices here are finite dimensional with this witness fixed.
Continuous functional calculus gives

  [s_R(H_R+E_R)]^alpha-[s_R H_R]^alpha -> diag(0,D_J).          (6)

Thus for every strict count at epsilon of the witness (2), a sufficiently
large finite R produces a connected weighted path whose correction has
at least that count. Combining this with the eta-regularization in
Section 2 proves G_alpha(epsilon)>=P_alpha(epsilon). The reverse
inequality holds because each normalized graph pair is a positive
semidefinite pair with a positive rank-one norm-one update. This proves
(1), including its exact normalization and strict-threshold convention.

5. Sharp coefficient, and a connected-path extension to r edges
--------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 2 (positive-input singular-value counts) of
sharp-fractional-holder.tex proves, for every fixed positive integer r,
the generic PSD upper bound and matching asymptotic

  sup_{rank(B-A)<=r, ||B-A||=1}
       N_(exp(-L))(B^alpha-A^alpha)
      = [r/(4*pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha))+o(1)] L^2.                 (7)

Its matching lower bound uses positive rank-one updates and direct
sums. Equation (1) therefore immediately implies for one changed edge
of connected mass-weighted paths

  G_alpha(exp(-L))/L^2 -> 1/(4*pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)).            (8)

The same sharp coefficient multiplied by r holds for a connected
weighted path with r increased edges, each mass held fixed and the
total normalized perturbation of norm one. To prove the lower bound,
take r path witnesses from (1), individually normalized to update norm
one, on disjoint vertex sets. Their direct sum has positive update of
rank r, total update norm one, and adds all singular-value counts.
Order those r paths consecutively and connect consecutive path ENDPOINTS
with arbitrarily small positive conductances. The result is a single
connected weighted path. The added connecting edges are the same before
and after the update, so the normalized perturbation remains the same
block-diagonal rank-r operator, still of norm one. As the new bridge
conductances decrease to zero, the two endpoint operators and their
fractional correction converge to their direct sums. All strict counts
of those witnesses survive for sufficiently small positive bridges.

The generic PSD upper in (7) applies to every such graph pair. For the
lower asymptotic, at each L choose one rank-one path witness arbitrarily
close to its supremal count, take r disjoint copies and apply the bridge
argument. This proves the factor-r coefficient. It does not prove that
every generic rank-r finite-threshold count is realized by a path.

6. Algorithmic and graph-theoretic interpretation
------------------------------------------------

Arbitrary masses do not obstruct shifted Laplacian solvers:

  H+tI = M^{-1/2}(L+tM)M^{-1/2}, t>0.

The middle matrix is symmetric diagonally dominant and positive
definite. Thus the sharp lower examples belong to the same class for
which a fractional-update quadrature can use sparse shifted solves.
No lower bound on the first nonzero graph eigenvalue was assumed.

One may also express these examples as degree-normalized graph
Laplacians WITH holding loops. Scale all conductances in both endpoints
by a common c>0 small enough that the updated non-loop degree at every
vertex is at most its prescribed mass. At each endpoint separately add
a nonnegative self-loop of weight equal to the mass minus that vertex's
non-loop degree. Under the convention that a holding loop contributes
its weight once to the degree and adjacency diagonal, the degree
matrix is exactly M and the loop terms cancel from D-A. The resulting
normalized Laplacian is cH. The off-diagonal conductance change is still
one edge, with compensating decreases of the two endpoint holding
weights. This describes reversible Markov chains with a fixed stationary
mass vector. It does not describe a single-edge update with no other
changes to a degree-normalized loop-free graph. Since both correction
and perturbation scale homogeneously, their intrinsic normalized rank
counts are unchanged by c.

7. Scope and significance
-------------------------

The fractional-power extremal constant (7), the low-rank resolvent
identity, and general rational matrix-function updates are inputs from
previous work, not new claims of this file. The additional statement
proved here is the reduction of every finite positive rank-one PSD
correction, up to arbitrary norm accuracy, to a SINGLE conductance
increase at an end edge of a mass-weighted connected path. Hence
restricting to graphs, and even paths, does not improve the universal
leading compression-rank constant in this model.

This is a structural extremal result; it is not a general quadratic
matrix-multiplication algorithm or a finite-precision complexity claim.
External priority for the realization itself has not yet been audited.
