SHARP POLYNOMIAL AMBIGUITY AND EXACT-RECOVERY ERROR IN THE TWO-CLASS RSBM
Independent proof, November 15, 2026. graph_breakthrough.
Only this file is written for this task. Frozen sources are not edited.

Result
------
Fix integers a>b>0. Put

  D=a+b,       delta=a-b,       t=delta+1,
  s=(D-2)t=(a+b-2)(a-b+1).

Use two planted blocks of size N, independent uniform simple a-regular
internal graphs, and an independent uniform simple b-regular bipartite
cross graph. All limits are through admissible N (aN even); no further
parity restriction is imposed. Let B_N be the event that the graph has
a second equitable (a,b) bisection, modulo global exchange. Then

  P(B_N)=Theta_{a,b}(N^{-s}).                              (1)

The proposed typed motif and proper-intersection argument are valid.
The lower bound needs the conditional-simplicity point explained in
Section 4; it is especially clean here because all motif edges touch
vertices whose stubs are completely saturated by the motif.

For a uniformly hidden planted partition, posterior probabilities are
uniform over the admissible unordered partitions of the observed graph.
Consequently the optimal exact-recovery error is Theta_{a,b}(N^{-s}).
The existing polynomial-moment enumeration algorithm, with a larger
polynomial cutoff, attains an O_{a,b}(N^{-s}) failure probability in
worst-case polynomial bit time. No historical novelty is asserted.


1. Upper bound: start the small-support estimate at its sharp minimum
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Write sigma for the planted sign vector. Every equitable sign vector
tau satisfies A tau=delta tau. It is automatically balanced since
A1=D1 and D-delta=2b>0. Let S be the coordinates where tau differs
from sigma, with k flipped vertices on each planted side. Replace
tau by -tau if necessary so 1<=k<=N/2. Set K=|S|=2k and
w=(sigma-tau)/2. Then

  A w=delta w,
  w=+1 on S_plus, -1 on S_minus, 0 outside S.

At a vertex in S_plus its eigenvector equation says

  delta=#neighbors in S_plus - #neighbors in S_minus.

It has at least delta distinct neighbors in S_plus. Simplicity therefore
forces k-1>=delta. The same conclusion holds on the minus side. Thus

  k>=t=delta+1,                 K>=2t.                    (2)

At any vertex outside S, the equation sum_{u~v}w_u=0 implies that it
cannot have exactly one edge into S. This gives a support certificate
depending only on graph structure; no enumeration of real values is
needed.

Here is a self-contained uniform exposure bound. Work in the colored
configuration model: independent internal pairings of aN stubs in
each block and a cross bijection of bN stubs on either side. Fix a
set S of size K<=epsilon N. Expose all edges incident to S, skipping
already paired stubs. Call a partner choice a collision if its vertex
lies in S or is an exterior vertex touched earlier in this exposure.
There are at most DK exposures. At every step the number of collision
candidate stubs is at most D K+D^2 K. All relevant unexposed pools
have size at least c_{a,b}N if epsilon is sufficiently small. Hence
each conditional collision probability is at most C_{a,b}K/N.

Let e be the number of edges with both ends in S, B the number with
one end in S, and T the exterior neighbor set. There are e+B actual
exposures, and precisely |T| first exterior touches. If no exterior
vertex has exactly one neighbor-edge in S, then |T|<=B/2. Therefore
the collision count is at least

  e+B-|T|>=e+B/2=DK/2.

This remains true in the configuration model with loops and multiple
edges, using stub multiplicities. Pad the sequence with noncollisions
to length DK. A union bound over collision indices gives

  P_config[S has this certificate]
    <=2^(DK)(C_{a,b}K/N)^(DK/2).

There are at most (2eN/K)^K choices of S. Thus, for a constant A=A(a,b)
and eta=(D-2)/2>0,

  P_config[there is such a support of size K]
    <=[A(K/N)^eta]^K.                                    (3)

The consecutive ratio on the right is at most
A exp(eta)((K+1)/N)^eta. Shrink fixed epsilon so this is <=1/2 for
2t<=K<=epsilon N and all large N. Summing (3) from the minimum (2)
gives O(N^{-2t eta})=O(N^{-s}). The configurations conditioned simple
have the exact uniform-simple factor law. Their joint simplicity
probability stays bounded below, so the same upper bound holds there.
One does NOT assert that (2) holds in the nonsimple configuration
model; it is used only to restrict the event being bounded after
conditioning on simplicity.

For the remaining K>=epsilon N, the common flip fraction r=k/N lies
in [epsilon/2,1/2]. The exact Boolean star-type enumeration has entropy
exponent

  Phi=H(star_plus)+H(star_minus)
        -(a/2)[H(edge_internal_plus)+H(edge_internal_minus)]
        -b H(edge_cross).

Symmetrize internal and cross stub slots. Deleting one internal slot
using the INTEGER eigenvector constraint gives the exact identity

  L:=-Phi=((a-2)/2)(I_int_plus+I_int_minus)
             +b I_cross+J_plus+J_minus,

where J is conditional total correlation of the retained neighbors.
Retain the actual internal one-slot conditional laws given the root,
but replace each cross slot by an independent sample from the opposite
root marginal. If P is the actual truncated-star law and R this law,

  KL(P||R)=J_plus+b I_cross<=L.

For the deleted internal sign W and its original conditional mean m(X),
exchangeability gives E_P(W-m(X))^2=E Var(internal_slot|X).
Under R conditional independence implies that this expectation increases
by at least b Var(opposite_root), since a>=2. The squared function
lies in [0,4a^2] under both laws. Pinsker consequently yields

  Phi<=-(b^2/(8a^4)) Var(opposite_root)^2
       =-(2b^2/a^4)r^2(1-r)^2.                            (4)

This argument includes a=2,b=1; the coefficient a-2 may vanish without
invalidating the positive cross-variance term. There are polynomially
many fixed-degree Boolean types, and uniform factorial errors cost
only a polynomial prefactor. Thus the macroscopic probability is
O(N^C exp(-cN))=o(N^{-s}). Conditioning simplicity costs a constant.
Together with (3), this proves P(B_N)=O(N^{-s}) at all admissible N.

2. A typed fixed motif producing a second bisection
---------------------------------------------------
Define a simple colored graph H with a fixed planted-plus/minus type
on each vertex. It contains

  S_plus={s_plus_i:1<=i<=t},
  S_minus={s_minus_i:1<=i<=t},
  W_plus={w_plus_ij:1<=i<=t,1<=j<=b},
  W_minus={w_minus_ij:1<=i<=t,1<=j<=b}.

All listed vertices are distinct. Put a clique K_t on S_plus and a
clique K_t on S_minus. For each i,j add the four edges

  s_plus_i--w_plus_ij       (internal plus),
  s_minus_i--w_minus_ij     (internal minus),
  s_minus_i--w_plus_ij      (cross),
  s_plus_i--w_minus_ij      (cross).

Each S vertex has delta clique neighbors, b internal W neighbors,
and b cross W neighbors. Thus its prescribed internal degree is
delta+b=a and its cross degree is b: ALL its stubs are saturated.
Every W vertex has one prescribed internal edge and one prescribed
cross edge, and no further prescribed edges. Its prescribed total
degree is two. The graph is simple. It is connected: the two nonempty
cliques are linked through every W pair of paths. Since delta>=1,
t>=2, and the minimum degree of H is two.

It has

  v=2t(1+b),
  e=t delta+4bt,
  e-v=t(delta+2b-2)=t(D-2)=s.                              (5)

When H occurs in a graph of the model, flip precisely S_plus and
S_minus. At an S vertex the b internal W edges become cross and the
b cross W edges become internal, preserving both required degrees.
At each W vertex one prescribed internal and one prescribed cross
edge exchange roles. Any other neighbor remains unflipped. Vertices
outside H have no S neighbors, because every S stub is already used
inside H. They are unaffected. The new assignment is balanced since
exactly t vertices flip on each side. For N>t it differs from both
orientations of the planted partition. Thus

  {H occurs as a typed subgraph} subset B_N.               (6)

For a=2,b=1 this is the earlier eight-vertex path motif, possibly with
extra edges among its zero-valued endpoints. The general construction
needs no exact induced-subgraph condition.


3. Proper intersections have strictly smaller excess
-----------------------------------------------------
LEMMA. If a finite connected graph H has minimum degree at least two,
then every proper subgraph F with a nonempty vertex set satisfies

  e(F)-v(F)<=e(H)-v(H)-1.                                 (7)

Here F may contain isolated vertices. If it spans H but omits an edge,
(7) is immediate. Otherwise let R=V(H)\V(F), r=|R|>0. Since V(F)
is nonempty and H connected, the number B of edges from R to its
complement is positive. Let E_R count edges induced by R. Minimum
degree gives 2E_R+B>=2r. Removing R deletes E_R+B edges, so

  E_R+B>=r+B/2>r.

The integer number of deleted edges is at least r+1. Omitting any
further edges can only lower excess. This proves (7). It is important
that H is connected as well as minimum-degree-two; either hypothesis
alone would not give the required strict inequality.

The lemma applies to the intersection of two embedded copies of H:
use every common vertex, including those not incident to a common
edge. If the intersection is not the whole copy and has vertices,
its excess is at most s-1. If the full vertex-and-edge images agree,
there are only finitely many ways to identify the abstract labeled
motifs (at most a fixed number of typed automorphisms). These are
the diagonal terms, not proper intersections.


4. Typed configuration counts and conditional simplicity
----------------------------------------------------------
Let Y count injective typed labeled embeddings of H. The fixed typed
parts each have t(1+b) vertices. There are Theta(N^v) embeddings.
For a fixed embedding, assign distinct internal and cross stubs to
its prescribed incident edges. The number of assignments is

  c_H=product_{u in V(H)}(a)_{h_int(u)}(b)_{h_cross(u)}>0,

a fixed constant depending on a,b. Its internal edge count per block
is e_int=t delta/2+bt, an integer because t delta=delta(delta+1)
is even. Its cross edge count is e_cross=2bt. For any prescribed
stub assignment the probability is exactly

  product_{j=0}^{e_int-1}(aN-2j-1)^(-2)
       *1/(bN)_{e_cross}=Theta(N^{-e}).                   (8)

These assignments are disjoint events for this particular H: every
prescribed edge touches an S vertex, and all S stubs of each color
are saturated. A configuration containing the simple H cannot have
an extra parallel copy of one of its required edges without losing
another required S edge. Its physical stubs on every required edge
are uniquely determined. Even without disjointness, an upper bound
by the finite assignment sum and a lower bound from one assignment
would already give the needed Theta estimate.

Conditional on a prescribed stub assignment, the remaining pairings
and cross bijection are uniform. Every S residual degree is zero.
At each W vertex the residual degrees are a-1 internally and b-1
across; all other vertices retain degrees a and b. No prescribed
edge has two residual-positive endpoints, since all H edges touch S.
Therefore a residual pairing cannot duplicate a prescribed edge.
Simplicity of the whole graph is exactly simplicity of these residual
bounded-degree configurations.

Their degree sequences differ from the regular sequences at only
O_{a,b}(1) vertices and their total stub counts are Theta(N). Their
simplicity probabilities stay bounded below, uniformly in embeddings
and stub assignments. One direct proof uses factorial moments of
loops and double-edge pairs: disjoint small defect patterns give
the usual finite Poisson means, O(1) modified degrees affect those
means by o(1), and overlapping patterns lose a power of N. The
limits equal those of the original regular factors. The internal
limits are exp(-(a^2-1)/4) for each block, and the cross limit is
exp(-(b-1)^2/2), with b=1 giving automatic residual cross simplicity.
Only positivity is needed.

For an alternative primary reference, Janson, The probability that
a random multigraph is simple, II, Theorems 1.1--1.2 and the bipartite
discussion in Section 5, establishes the required bounded-degree
simplicity principle:
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.6344
The theorem statement and configuration-model definitions were checked.
The finite-modification argument above explains its conditional use
here rather than incorrectly assuming that unconditional simplicity
alone guarantees conditional simplicity after an arbitrary motif.

The full unconditioned simplicity probability also stays bounded
below. Dividing the probability of motif AND simplicity by that of
simplicity therefore proves, in the requested uniform-simple model,

  P[fixed H embedding occurs]=Theta(N^{-e}),
  E Y=Theta(N^{v-e})=Theta(N^{-s}).                        (9)

All prescribed remaining stub counts are admissible: internal totals
are aN-tD, and tD=(delta+1)(delta+2b) is even. Cross residual totals
on the two sides agree. No new parity restriction on N is introduced.


5. Second moment and the lower bound
------------------------------------
For any fixed simple typed graph J whose vertex degrees do not
exceed the available degrees, the same finite stub-assignment upper
count gives

  P_simple[J occurs at specified vertices]<=C_J N^{-e(J)}.

If degree compatibility fails the probability is zero. Conditioning
by the positive full simplicity probability suffices for this upper
bound; no conditional lower estimate is needed for unions of motifs.

For a pair of embedded H copies let F be their intersection, counting
common vertices and common edges. There are finitely many overlap
identification types, depending only on H. For any such type their
union has

  v_union=2v-v(F),          e_union=2e-e(F),
  e_union-v_union=2s-[e(F)-v(F)].

The contribution of this type to E Y^2 is therefore at most
C N^{-2s+e(F)-v(F)}. There are three cases.

  * Disjoint vertex sets: O(N^{-2s}).
  * Proper nonempty intersections: O(N^{-s-1}) by (7).
  * Identical vertex-and-edge images: O(N^{-s}), with a finite
    multiplicity of abstract embeddings.

Consequently

  E Y^2=O(N^{-s}).                                        (10)

Since E Y=Theta(N^{-s}), Cauchy--Schwarz yields

  P(Y>0)>=(E Y)^2/E Y^2=Omega(N^{-s}).                     (11)

Together with (6) and the upper bound in Section 1, this proves (1).
The proof does not require disjoint occurrences to be independent.
Potentially incompatible overlap types simply have zero probability.


6. Uniform posterior and the information-theoretic sharp rate
--------------------------------------------------------------
For the recovery statement, hide the balanced planted partition
uniformly, and observe only the labeled adjacency matrix and a,b.
This qualification matters: with the planted sets deterministically
declared to be the first and last N vertex labels, an estimator could
output those sets without inspecting the graph.

Let G_a(N) be the number of simple a-regular graphs on N labeled
vertices and B_b(N) the number of simple b-regular bipartite graphs
on the fixed labeled parts. For any fixed balanced ordered sign
assignment sigma and any observed graph G,

  P(G|sigma)=1/[G_a(N)^2 B_b(N)]

if sigma is equitable (a,b), and zero otherwise. The decomposition
of G into its two internal graphs and cross graph is unique once
sigma is specified. Thus the posterior is uniform over valid ordered
sign vectors, or equivalently over valid unordered partitions after
identifying sigma and -sigma.

Let K(G) be the number of valid unordered partitions. The minimum
conditional error of any estimator, including randomized ones, is

  1-1/K(G).

Hence the optimal Bayes error R_N satisfies

  (1/2)P[K(G)>=2] <= R_N <= P[K(G)>=2].                    (12)

The ambiguity event is invariant under relabeling. Its probability
in the hidden model equals P(B_N) computed with fixed planted sets.
Combining (1),(12) gives R_N=Theta(N^{-s}). In particular this is an
information-theoretic obstruction, not just a limitation of a chosen
spectral algorithm. The motif also supplies an extra real eigenvector,
so P[nullity_R(A-delta I)>=2]>=c N^{-s}; no matching real-nullity
upper bound is inferred from Boolean rigidity.


7. A worst-case polynomial algorithm attaining the rate
-------------------------------------------------------
Use the established fixed-prime polynomial kernel moment in the SAME
random-cross uniform-simple model. At p=3 it gives

  E |ker_F3(A-delta I)| <= C_{a,b}(N+1)^kappa,
  kappa=4*3^(D+1)+64,                                    (13)

a deliberately conservative fixed exponent. The actual consolidated
statement was checked in regular-community-recovery/sections/01-results.tex,
SHA256 5a3ce3cef6d239b936484c0615538c331bf3f942c77ee20855e363cb91732eb6.
This moment has an independent full proof audit and is a separate
input; no sharp saddle or real-corank failure rate is assumed here.

Compute a basis of this field kernel. If its size exceeds

  T_N=N^(kappa+s+1),

report failure. Otherwise enumerate it, discard vectors with a zero
coordinate, map field values 1,2 to signs +1,-1, and check every
candidate using the integer neighbor counts. Every valid sign
assignment appears, since p=3 distinguishes the two signs. Identify
global exchanges; output the verified partition if unique, and report
ambiguity otherwise. On the no-cutoff, unique-partition event this
returns the planted partition.

Markov and (13) give cutoff probability O(N^{-s-1}). The ambiguity
probability is O(N^{-s}). Thus the algorithm's total failure is
O(N^{-s}), attaining the exponent in the optimal Bayes lower bound.
The cutoff gives polynomial worst-case runtime at fixed a,b; a direct
implementation uses O(N^(kappa+s+2)+N^3) field/verification operations,
with polynomial bit overhead. Without truncation it lists all valid
partitions in expected polynomial time.

Reporting ambiguity counts as failure. Alternatively choosing any
verified partition on ambiguity has the same O(N^{-s}) upper bound
and is a valid exact-recovery estimator in the usual error convention.
No practical runtime, near-linear complexity, noisy-data robustness,
or positive analytic eigengap is claimed.


8. Scope and assessment
-----------------------
The candidate survives the requested adversarial checks. The important
qualifications are fixed degrees, random independent cross graph,
uniform simple internal factors, a>b>0, and every admissible N.
The lower motif event does not apply with the same probability to
an arbitrary prescribed/adversarial cross matrix. The statistical
lower bound uses the uniformly hidden planted partition. The claimed
sharp exponent concerns nonunique Boolean/equitable bisections and
optimal exact recovery, not all possible real eigenspace collisions.

The motif, the connected minimum-degree-two intersection lemma,
the conditional-simplicity justification, and the posterior calculation
are all supplied here. The general Boolean entropy and polynomial
moment inputs come from the existing research-session sources and
their derivations described above. Historical priority has not been
established by this proof note.
