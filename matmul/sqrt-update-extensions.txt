SQUARE-ROOT UPDATES: DOWNDATE AND SIGNED EXTENSIONS
===================================================
2026-11-15. Owner: sqrt_update_sota. Internal proof/priority record.
This file leaves nongalactic-wildcard-search.txt unchanged.

RESULT AND LIMIT OF THE NOVELTY CLAIM
------------------------------------
The positive-update construction extends to promised PSD downdates,
including singular and exactly critical endpoints, with the SAME
conventional arithmetic count and logarithm-squared output width.
The direct proof below needs eta=O(rho^4), just as for positive updates.
The apparent near-critical denominator has a bound in terms of the
update energy and the positive quadrature shift alone.

General signed low-rank changes between PSD endpoints follow at the
same cost, either with an indefinite small core or with two semidefinite
calls. Section11 additionally normalizes the signed factors, giving
error eps||B-A||^(1/2), even if the input factors have arbitrarily severe
cancellation. The latter two-call construction is particularly simple.
There is also an
exact covariance-factor refactorization that reduces any promised
downdate to the original positive-update theorem at the same raw cost.

These are real mathematical extensions, not evidence for an independent
seminal mechanism. BCKS2021 already handles general low-rank changes;
SDA2023/2024 already explicitly handles square-root downdates. The
potential contribution remains the complete raw-factor, update-scale,
condition-independent arithmetic theorem. Once the positive theorem is
available, the refactorization below makes its extension to downdates
and signed changes a short constructive consequence.

All square-root statements use exact arithmetic, comparisons, and
positive scalar square roots. No finite-word stability is asserted.
The PSD endpoint assumptions are promises; verifying those promises is
not required by the theorem. Any failed small-factorization test returns
zero and is included in the probability-of-failure event.

1. PROMISED DOWNDATE THEOREM
----------------------------
Given X in R^(N x m), U in R^(N x r), 1<=r<=m<=N, and eps,delta in
(0,1), set

  A=XX^T, E=UU^T, B=A-E,

and assume B>=0. There is an explicit randomized construction of Z with

  width K=O(r log^2(2r/eps)),
  ||ZZ^T-[A^(1/2)-B^(1/2)]||_2 <= eps ||U||_2,

with probability at least 1-delta, in conventional arithmetic

  ~O(Nmr+m^3).

Only dimension, accuracy, and failure logarithms are suppressed. No
lambda_min(B), distance to a critical downdate, kappa(A), kappa(B),
base-to-update norm ratio, supplied root, or supplied inverse is needed.
Both A and B may be singular. Dense output adds O(N^2 K).

The promise implies span(U) is contained in span(X). Thus every exact
correction and every output factor lies in span(X), of dimension <=m.
The displayed width can, if useful, be capped by m via thin QR; no
claim of relative error in the correction norm is made.

2. EXACT SMALL-CORE IDENTITY AND ITS UNIFORM BOUND
-------------------------------------------------
For t>0 write

  R=(A+tI)^(-1), R_B=(B+tI)^(-1),
  V=RU, K=U^T R U, S=I-K.

Since A+tI-E=B+tI>0, the matrix I-R^(1/2)UU^T R^(1/2) is SPD.
The nonzero eigenvalues of R^(1/2)UU^T R^(1/2) and U^T R U agree,
so S is SPD even when B has a nullspace. Woodbury gives

  R_B-R = V S^(-1)V^T =: D_t >=0,
  S^(-1) = I+U^T R_B U.                              (D1)

The second identity is exact, not an approximation or an assumption on
the downdate margin. Set a=||U||_F^2. Then

  ||S^(-1)|| <= M_t:=1+a/t,
  lambda_min(S) >= 1/M_t=t/(t+a),
  0 <= D_t <= R_B <= I/t.                             (D2)

The inequality ||S^(-1)||<=1+a/t is the crucial observation: all
amplification is a polynomial in a/t, with no base-spectrum parameter.
The identities themselves are standard Woodbury/Schur-complement algebra.

3. RAW INEXACT RESOLVENT AND CORE POSITIVITY
-------------------------------------------
Use the same single constant-distortion OSE of X and the same even
Neumann polynomial as in the positive construction. On its event of
probability at least 1-delta, for every t used by the algorithm,

  Rhat=R+E_R, 0<=E_R<=eta I/t,
  ||Rhat|| <= (1+eta)/t, eta=eta0^ell, eta0=1/4,

where ell is even. All actions use X, X^T, and an m-dimensional
Cholesky preconditioner; no N-dimensional inverse is formed.

Set

  Vhat=Rhat U,
  F=U^T E_R U >=0,
  Shat=I-U^T Vhat=S-F.

If

  gamma_t:=eta*(a/t)*(1+a/t) <= 1/2,                 (D3)

then

  C=S^(-1/2) F S^(-1/2) >=0, ||C||<=gamma_t,
  Shat=S^(1/2)(I-C)S^(1/2)>0,
  ||Shat^(-1)|| <= 2M_t.

The square roots in these analytical expressions are not computed by
the algorithm. It computes only an ordinary Cholesky factor of Shat.
The same common polynomial is applied to every RHS, so Shat is exactly
symmetric in the arithmetic model. A generic RHS-dependent iterative
solve would not automatically give this property.

For the dyadic logarithmic quadrature grid write s=t/a and let
s_min be its smallest value. It is enough to choose

  eta <= s_min^2/[4(1+s_min)].                        (D4)

Indeed (1+s)/s^2 decreases with s>0, so (D3) holds simultaneously
at every node. For the square-root grid s_min=Theta(rho^2), (D4)
is achieved by eta=c rho^4 for a sufficiently small universal c.
Thus ell=O(log(2/rho)), independently of the downdate margin.

4. SHARP ENOUGH NODE ERROR: eta=O(rho^4) STILL SUFFICES
------------------------------------------------------
Return the approximate PSD node

  Dhat_t=Vhat Shat^(-1)Vhat^T.

The following estimate holds under eta<=1 and (D3):

  ||Dhat_t-D_t|| <=8 eta [a/t^2+a^2/t^3].             (D5)

Proof. Define purely for analysis

  Y=V S^(-1/2), T=(Vhat-V)S^(-1/2).

By (D2) and ||V||<=sqrt(a)/t,

  ||Y||^2=||D_t||<=1/t,
  ||Y||<=sqrt(a M_t)/t,
  ||T||<=eta sqrt(a M_t)/t.

Also Dhat_t=(Y+T)(I-C)^(-1)(Y+T)^T. Since
||(I-C)^(-1)||<=2 and ||(I-C)^(-1)-I||<=2gamma_t,
expansion yields

  ||Dhat_t-D_t||
    <=2gamma_t||Y||^2+4||Y||||T||+2||T||^2
    <=(2+4+2eta) eta a M_t/t^2
    <=8eta [a/t^2+a^2/t^3].

The exact bound ||Y||^2<=1/t is essential in the first term. Bounding
all appearances of Y by sqrt(a M_t)/t instead would introduce avoidable
higher powers of a/t and suggest the unnecessarily small eta=rho^8.

5. QUADRATURE, FACTOR CONSTRUCTION, AND FULL COST
-------------------------------------------------
The target has the positive integral

  A^(1/2)-B^(1/2)
    =pi^(-1) integral_0^infinity t^(1/2) D_t dt.

The exact-node envelope is

  ||D_t|| <= min(1/t, a/t^2).

The same logarithmic trapezoid argument as for positive updates gives
q=O(log^2(1/rho)) positive nodes t_j=a s_j and positive weights

  w_j=(h/pi)t_j^(3/2),

with quadrature error O(rho sqrt(a)), s_min=Theta(rho^2), and
s_max=Theta(rho^(-2)). For literal tail cutoffs, use the LEAST integer
K with 2^(-K/2)<=rho/C0, and dyadic log-grid t_j=a*2^(j/L0),
-L0*K<=j<=L0*K, where L0 is a power of two comparable to log(2/rho).
The least-cutoff choice is what gives the stated Theta estimates.
The construction uses dyadic roots for the
geometric grid and scalar square roots for the weights. Constants such
as log(2)/pi are handled exactly as in the positive theorem.

From (D5), the total weighted node error is bounded by

  C eta sqrt(a) h sum_j [s_j^(-1/2)+s_j^(-3/2)]
    <=C' eta sqrt(a)[rho^(-1)+rho^(-3)].

The last step is an ordinary geometric-series bound; its constant is
uniform as h decreases. Taking eta=c rho^4 gives O(rho sqrt(a)).
Choose rho=eps/(C0 sqrt(r)) and constants so that quadrature, node, and
weight-approximation errors sum to eps||U||, since sqrt(a)<=sqrt(r)||U||.
Relative O(rho) weight perturbations are harmless: the sum of weighted
exact-node norms is O(sqrt(a)), and (D5) adds only O(rho sqrt(a)).

At each node, Cholesky-factor Shat=C_j C_j^T and set

  Z_j=sqrt(w_j) Vhat_j C_j^(-T), Z=[Z_1 ... Z_q].

Then ZZ^T is a PSD matrix exactly in the model. The algorithm can test
Cholesky pivots and return zero if a test fails; no test fails on the
single OSE success event, which controls all poles simultaneously.

Input/OSE/sketched-Gram preparation: ~O(Nm+m^3).
Reusable X^T U: O(Nmr).
Per node: O(m^3) for the m-core Cholesky; O(ell Nmr) for raw polynomial
actions; O(ell m^2r) for triangular solves; O(Nr^2+r^3) for the small
core and factor normalization. Because r<=m<=N, these sum to

  O(~Nm+~m^3+q[m^3+ell Nmr])=~O(Nmr+m^3).

The width, storage and dense-output accounting are the same as in the
positive theorem. No inverse of B, or even a factor of B, is an input.

6. GENERAL SIGNED UPDATE: DIRECT SMALL-CORE CONSTRUCTION
-------------------------------------------------------
Suppose now J is an explicit symmetric involution of order r, J^2=I,
and A=XX^T, B=A+UJU^T are both PSD. The norm bound ||J||=1 gives
||UJU^T||<=||U||^2<=a. Define

  R=(A+tI)^(-1), R_B=(B+tI)^(-1), V=RU,
  S=J+U^T R U.

S is invertible and

  S^(-1)=J-J U^T R_B U J,
  ||S^(-1)||<=M_t=1+a/t,
  D_t=R-R_B=V S^(-1)V^T.                             (S1)

One can establish invertibility from the determinant lemma using
det(B+tI)>0; (S1) then follows by direct multiplication/Woodbury.
The inertia of S equals that of J. For example, apply the Schur-
complement inertia formula to the symmetric block matrix

  [ A+tI   U ]
  [ U^T   -J ].

Its two Schur complements involve B+tI and -S, respectively; the two
positive N-dimensional blocks cancel in the inertia count.

Use the same Rhat=R+E_R, Vhat=Rhat U, and now set
Shat=J+U^T Rhat U. If eta*(a/t)*M_t<=1/2, the perturbation of S
has norm <=1/(2M_t), so Shat remains invertible with the same inertia
and ||Shat^(-1)||<=2M_t. Consequently

  ||Vhat Shat^(-1)Vhat^T - V S^(-1)V^T||
    <=6 eta a M_t/t^2 +2 eta a^2 M_t^2/t^3.           (S2)

To verify (S2), use ||Vhat-V||<=eta sqrt(a)/t,
||Vhat||<=2sqrt(a)/t, and the inverse-difference bound
||Shat^(-1)-S^(-1)||<=2eta a M_t^2/t. Expanding in the two V factors
gives the coefficients 4+2 for the first term and 2 for the last.

For square-root weights the scaled powers in (S2) range from s^(-1/2)
through s^(-7/2). Summing the geometric series gives total node error
O(eta sqrt(a)rho^(-7)). Taking eta=c rho^8 yields O(rho sqrt(a)) and
also ensures the inertia condition. The iteration count is still
O(log(2/rho)); only its constant changes.

An actual signed factor requires no small eigenvalue or matrix-root
oracle. Symmetric elimination with congruence gives a diagonal D and
invertible T satisfying T^T Shat T=D. FIRST search every remaining
diagonal entry and permute a nonzero one into the pivot. Only if ALL
remaining diagonals vanish, select a nonzero off-diagonal entry a_ij
and replace e_i by e_i+e_j; its diagonal is then 2a_ij!=0. If all
remaining entries vanish, the core is singular and the algorithm
returns zero. On the success event this never happens. The whole
congruence calculation is O(r^3).

Write Sigma=diag(sign(D_ii)) and W=T|D|^(-1/2). Then

  Shat^(-1)=W Sigma W^T,
  Z_j=sqrt(w_j) Vhat_j W,
  Dhat_j=w_j^(-1) Z_j Sigma Z_j^T.

Concatenate the node factors and signatures. This returns

  F=Z Sigma_all Z^T=Z_+Z_+^T-Z_-Z_-^T,
  ||F-[B^(1/2)-A^(1/2)]||<=eps||U||,
  total width O(r log^2(2r/eps)),
  arithmetic ~O(Nmr+m^3), probability >=1-delta.       (S3)

The exact resolvent difference obeys ||D_t||<=min(1/t,a/t^2) on the
positive real axis, because both resolvents lie between 0 and I/t.
On complex quadrature-strip boundaries a fixed larger constant is
allowed. Thus the same uniform quadrature envelope applies to (S3).

For signed input, eps||U|| in this direct construction is a
REPRESENTATION-SCALE tolerance. It is not automatically
eps||UJU^T||^(1/2): signed factors can have severe cancellation.
Section11 supplies an exact small-core normalization that upgrades the
final theorem to the intrinsic update norm without a conditioning cost.

7. SIMPLER SIGNED REDUCTION USING ONLY PSD CORES
-----------------------------------------------
Let P_+=(I+J)/2 and P_-=(I-J)/2. These are orthogonal projections, and
U_+=UP_+, U_-=UP_- satisfy

  UJU^T=U_+U_+^T-U_-U_-^T,
  ||U_+||,||U_-||<=||U||.

Let C=A+U_+U_+^T. Then C>=A,B and a raw factor of C is simply
[X,U_+]. Apply the positive-update theorem to C^(1/2)-A^(1/2), and
the downdate theorem to C^(1/2)-B^(1/2). With eps/2 and delta/2
in each call, subtraction returns (S3), with the same order of width
and arithmetic and only PSD small-core Cholesky factorizations.

The intermediate factor has <=m+r<=2m columns. The raw-solve proof
works with this constant enlargement. If one insists on the original
convention that its width cannot exceed N, the exceptional case
m+r>N has N<2m; QR of [X,U_+]^T produces an N-column covariance
factor in O((m+r)N^2)=O(m^3), already inside the budget. For diagonal
J, simply use the positive and negative column groups instead.

This version needs eta=O(rho^4) in each call. It establishes that the
signed extension is a direct closure of the two semidefinite theorems,
not a separate fundamentally new indefinite solver.

8. EXACT RAW COVARIANCE REFACTORIZATION FOR ANY PROMISED DOWNDATE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
There is an alternative that makes the closure even more explicit.
Under UU^T<=XX^T, construct an EXACT Y in R^(N x m) with

  YY^T=XX^T-UU^T

in ~O(Nmr+m^3) conventional arithmetic with probability >=1-delta.
The factor Y is an arbitrary covariance factor, not a principal root.
The construction below uses only arithmetic, comparisons, and square
roots, including at exactly singular downdates.

Step 1: recover the minimum-norm coefficient. The promise implies
U=XC for some C. Draw an OSE S0 that is injective on span(X); it is
enough to use the same constant-distortion embedding as before. Form
W=S0X and V0=S0U. The solution sets of WC=V0 and XC=U are identical:
their residual is in span(X), where S0 is injective. Also ker(W)=ker(X).
Compute the minimum-Euclidean-norm solution C of WC=V0 using an exact
rank-revealing QR of W and small SPD solves. For example, if
W=Q0 R0 Pi^T with R0 full row rank, use

  C=Pi R0^T(R0 R0^T)^(-1) Q0^T V0.

This is a formula for an implemented QR/Cholesky calculation, not a
pseudoinverse oracle. Its cost is ~O(Nm+m^3+Nmr). The minimum-norm
choice is essential: it has range(C) contained in range(X^T).

Step 2: prove the contraction. On the nonzero singular subspace of X,
the inequality XCC^TX^T<=XX^T implies CC^T<=I. Outside range(X^T)
the minimum-norm C vanishes. Equivalently this is the elementary
finite-dimensional contraction-factorization statement.

Step 3: small factor and low-rank transform. Compute thin rank-revealing
QR C=Q R, with k<=r columns in Q. Then RR^T<=I_k. Use a PSD Cholesky
factor L of I_k-RR^T (possibly singular), and set

  T=I_m+Q(L-I_k)Q^T,
  Y=XT=X+(XQ)(L-I_k)Q^T.                            (R1)

Because Q^TQ=I and the orthogonal-complement cross terms vanish,

  TT^T=I_m-QQ^T+QLL^TQ^T=I_m-CC^T.

Thus YY^T=XX^T-UU^T exactly. The products in (R1) cost O(Nmr),
not O(Nm^2). The small QR/Cholesky work is O(mr^2+r^3), within m^3.
Zero PSD Cholesky pivots are allowed; for a PSD residual matrix, a
zero diagonal forces its corresponding row/column to vanish, so a
square-root-only Cholesky algorithm can skip that pivot safely.

Applying the original positive-update theorem to the raw base Y and U
now approximates

  (YY^T+UU^T)^(1/2)-(YY^T)^(1/2)=A^(1/2)-B^(1/2).

The refactorization and update calls can each receive delta/2. Their
cost and output width retain the theorem's order. This alternative is
especially useful for exposing the novelty limitation: after the
positive-update theorem, the downdate theorem is already obtainable
through standard small-factor manipulations and an exact sketched solve.
No first-discovery claim for the identity (R1) is made.

9. FIXED FRACTIONAL POWERS: PROOF AND SCALAR-MODEL CAVEAT
--------------------------------------------------------
For fixed alpha in (0,1), beta=1-alpha, the analytic argument extends
to A^alpha-B^alpha. The exact integral is

  B^alpha-A^alpha
    =c_alpha integral_0^infinity t^alpha(R_A-R_B)dt,
  c_alpha=sin(pi alpha)/pi>0.

With a=||U||_F^2 and s=t/a, the logarithmic integrand has norm

  <=C_alpha a^alpha min(s^alpha,s^(-beta)).

It is analytic in every fixed strip |Im(log s)|<=d<pi, with an
integrable uniform boundary envelope: sector resolvent bounds and
the resolvent identity give this directly. The trapezoid discretization
error is C_alpha a^alpha exp(-2pi d/h). Choose

  rho=eps/(C_alpha r^alpha),
  h=Theta_alpha(1/log(2/rho)),
  s_min=Theta_alpha(rho^(1/alpha)),
  s_max=Theta_alpha(rho^(-1/beta)).

The truncated rule then has error O_alpha(rho a^alpha), uses
q=O_alpha(log^2(2r/eps)) nodes, and has positive weights
w_j=c_alpha h t_j^(alpha+1). Dyadic logarithmic grid endpoints can
be chosen by integer comparisons so that no input norm/log oracle is
needed to choose the node count.

For the downdate estimate (D5), weighted node errors are bounded by

  C_alpha eta a^alpha h sum_j [s_j^(alpha-1)+s_j^(alpha-2)]
    <=C'_alpha eta a^alpha s_min^(-(2-alpha)).

Hence eta=c_alpha' rho^(2/alpha) suffices both for core positivity
and for O_alpha(rho a^alpha) total node error. For the direct signed
estimate (S2), the largest negative power is s^(alpha-4), so taking
eta=c_alpha'' rho^(4/alpha) suffices. Both choices give
ell=O_alpha(log(2r/eps)). The two-PSD-call signed reduction can instead
use the downdate exponent 2/alpha in both calls.

Since a^alpha<=r^alpha||U||^(2alpha), the resulting error is

  eps||U||^(2alpha),

with width O_alpha(r log^2(2r/eps)) and raw arithmetic
~O_alpha(Nmr+m^3), PROVIDED the scalar coefficients are available in
the stated arithmetic model.

That final proviso matters. For a fixed DYADIC alpha, repeated positive
square roots and products compute a^alpha and t_j^(alpha+1), so this
is genuinely the original arithmetic+sqrt model. For arbitrary fixed
alpha, a^alpha is an input-dependent scalar power and is not silently
a unit-cost arithmetic/square-root operation. A full generic-alpha
operation theorem must either add a fixed scalar-power primitive or
charge its evaluation cost. Already X=0,r=1 reduces the task to
uniform relative approximation of a^alpha for arbitrary positive a.
Thus the general-alpha rank/analytic extension is immediate, while the
restricted scalar-operation extension must be qualified explicitly.

11. INTRINSIC UPDATE-NORM ACCURACY DESPITE SIGNED CANCELLATION
-------------------------------------------------------------
The signed theorem can in fact achieve the stronger target

  ||F-[B^(1/2)-A^(1/2)]|| <= eps||B-A||^(1/2)         (I1)

in the SAME ~O(Nmr+m^3) arithmetic and O(r log^2(2r/eps)) width,
without dependence on the conditioning or cancellation of the supplied
representation E=UJU^T. The following exact normalization supplies it.

Compute a thin rank-revealing QR U=Q R, Q^TQ=I_k, k<=r. Form the
symmetric core C=RJR^T, so E=Q C Q^T. Set

  c=||C||_F.

If c=0, then E=0 and return zero. Otherwise

  ||E||_2=||C||_2 <=c<=sqrt(k)||E||_2.

All these quantities are computable with ordinary arithmetic and one
scalar square root, without an eigenvalue or norm oracle. Take an
arbitrary PSD Cholesky factor L satisfying

  LL^T=cI_k+C >=0,

and define

  Uc=Q [ L, sqrt(c) I_k ], Jc=diag(I_k,-I_k).

Then

  Uc Jc Uc^T=Q[(cI+C)-cI]Q^T=E,
  Uc Uc^T=Q(2cI+C)Q^T,
  ||Uc||_2^2 <=3c<=3sqrt(r)||E||_2.                  (I2)

The factor width is at most2r. Critical singularity of cI+C causes no
problem: the same PSD Cholesky convention as in Section8 handles zero
pivots exactly. Forming C and L costs O(r^3), and QR and multiplication
by Q cost O(Nr^2); both are inside the original budget because r<=m.

Apply either signed construction to (X,Uc,Jc) with

  eps'=eps/[sqrt(3) r^(1/4)].

Its error eps'||Uc|| is at most the right-hand side of (I1). Since
log(2r/eps')=O(log(2r/eps)), the output rank and operation count retain
their claimed orders. A width2r update rather than r only changes
constants: Nr^2 and r^3 are still absorbed by Nmr and m^3. Padding the
raw base with zero columns to width max(m,2r)<=2m is another way to
invoke exactly the same shifted-backend proof; its cost bounds remain
valid with this constant factor enlargement. If desired, the two-call
PSD reduction avoids indefinite factorization throughout.

This method also makes precise why huge cancelling input factors need
not force a condition parameter in exact arithmetic: the input is
compressed to its small symmetric core and then shifted by its own
computable Frobenius norm, before constructing the correction. In
finite precision, cancellation in forming C would require a separate
analysis; none is asserted by the arithmetic theorem.

For a fixed fractional power alpha, (I2) gives

  ||Uc||^(2alpha) <=3^alpha r^(alpha/2)||E||^alpha.

Using eps'=eps/[3^alpha r^(alpha/2)] proves the intrinsic guarantee
eps||E||^alpha with exactly the same rank/cost orders. The scalar-power
qualification from Section9 remains: dyadic alpha uses only arithmetic
and square roots; arbitrary fixed alpha needs its scalar evaluation
primitive/cost stated explicitly.

The shift decomposition C=(cI+C)-cI is elementary and should not itself
be claimed as new. Its role here is to close the exact input-normalized
algorithm theorem. Prior general low-rank-update methods and classical
rank bounds remain mandatory comparisons.
