1. Universal denominator and its numerator degree
--------------------------------------------------
For each connected pattern, let e be total edges, v vertices, and c the
number of used colors. Before clearing denominators, the numerator degree
is (e-v+1)+(v-1)=e. The denominator degree is
sum_(used colors)(e_color-1)=e-c. Its (1-jx) multiplicity is at most
floor(q/(j+1)), since total edges are <=q. Hence it divides the universal
g_q independent of the generator count, and the cleared numerator has
degree at most G_q+e-(e-c)=G_q+c<=G_q+q.
The subtraction of the invariant vector is -x*g_q. It has degree G_q+1,
including q=1 where g_q=1. The constant word contributes 1-x exactly.
The harmonic inequality G_q<=q log q is valid for all q>=1.
Thus the degree B_q=q(1+log q) is correct independently of r and D.
No coefficientwise bound or count of patterns is needed after this step.

2. Reciprocal mesh, Markov constants, and all-N extension
----------------------------------------------------------
Set B=B_q>=1, p=ceil(B)<=2B, M=ceil(32B^2). Then M>=8p^2 and M>=q.
The reciprocal mesh {1/L:L>=M}, together with its limit 0, has maximum
gap <=M^-2. On [0,1/M], all jx<=1/2 and
-log g_q(x)<=2x sum_j j*b_j<=2qB/M<=1.
Consequently e^-1<=g_q<=1. The normalized expected trace at every mesh
point is bounded by H, despite having actual dimension D(L-1), because
the normalization is DL and all eigenvalues lie in [-K,K]. Thus |f_h|
is <=H at the mesh and at zero by continuity.

The interval-scaled Markov derivative bound is
||f_h'||<=2p^2 M ||f_h||. Comparing a maximum point with a nearest mesh
point yields ||f_h||<=H+(2p^2/M)||f_h||<=H+||f_h||/4, hence certainly
<=2H. Repeating Markov gives first/second numerator derivative bounds
O(B^4 H), O(B^8 H). These inequalities also hold for complex polynomials:
apply the real inequality after rotating the derivative value's phase.

The logarithmic denominator derivatives are bounded by 2qB and 4q^2B;
with g_q>=e^-1, product differentiation gives the claimed rational
first/second derivative bounds. All constants are numerical and have no
hidden dependence on the generator count, coefficient size, or dimension.

For N>=M, ordinary Taylor's theorem gives O(B^8 H/N^2). For N<M the
actual normalized trace remains bounded by H, nu_0 by H, and nu_1 by
O(B^4 H). Multiplying the trivial error bound by N^2 gives
2M^2 H+O(B^4 M H)=O(B^8 H), because M<=33B^2 and B>=1.
This patch does not evaluate a rational formula at N<q. It is valid even
for N=1 (the new space is then zero dimensional).

3. Smooth extension, changing r,D, and exact support
----------------------------------------------------
At Chebyshev index j the coefficient error is
O(j^8(1+log j)^8/N^2). Cauchy--Schwarz with
sum_(j>=1)(1+log j)^16/j^2<infinity bounds its sum by a universal
multiple of the L2 norm of nine angular derivatives. Parseval supplies
the j^18 weighted coefficient square sum. Smooth angular functions have
superpolynomial coefficient decay; the corresponding Chebyshev sums
converge with every finite derivative on [-K,K], since derivatives of
T_j have only polynomial growth in j. They therefore pass through the
published compactly supported distributions.

The constant term has EXACT zero second-order remainder:
nu_0(1)=1, nu_1(1)=-1, and normalized trace of identity=1-1/N.
The published local smooth-extension paragraph writes nu_i(1)=0 for i>=1,
which is incompatible with this normalization; the new derivation correctly
handles the constant separately and does not rely on that sentence.

The coefficients nu_0 and nu_1 coincide with the published functionals:
their rational functions agree at infinitely many reciprocal integers,
so every Taylor coefficient is the same. Corollary saf supp gives the
EXACT support inclusion for nu_1, while nu_0 is the free spectral measure.
The support proof has dimension-dependent intermediate constants, but
no estimate using those constants is imported. For every fixed finite
coefficient system, the exact inclusion suffices to annihilate the cutoff.
When r,D and coefficients change with the lift degree, apply the argument
separately to each finite system. Its hypothetical reciprocal interpolation
uses that same coefficient system at every mesh size; it does not require
the family itself to be constant with lift degree. Thus there is no
uniformity gap from varying coefficient dimension or generator number.

4. Cutoff and graph consequence
--------------------------------
A fixed C-infinity transition of width epsilon/2 on both sides of the
free spectral interval has angular ninth-derivative L2 norm
O((K/epsilon)^9), including lower chain-rule terms since epsilon<K.
It vanishes on a neighborhood of [-rho,rho], so both support distributions
annihilate it. Multiplying the normalized O(N^-2) error by DN gives
expected unnormalized cutoff trace O(D/N*(K/epsilon)^9). Its trace is
nonnegative and at least one if any eigenvalue reaches the exterior
threshold, proving the probability estimate by Markov's inequality.
Boundary thresholds at K follow from increasing events as epsilon tends
upward from below; thresholds strictly above K are impossible.

For a loopless base multigraph, one generator per undirected edge gives
exactly adjacency on the fibrewise new space. The full unitary norm is
bounded by the scalar base adjacency norm using block-vector norms; setting
all labels to identities gives equality. The free-labelled graph is a
forest: any reduced closed walk would produce a nonempty freely reduced
word in distinct edge letters. Every component covers the connected base,
so it is its universal covering tree. Parallel base edges do not alter
this argument because they have distinct generators. Thus the free norm
is exactly the universal-cover norm, and K<=Delta. The displayed graph
probability bound follows, including cases whose threshold is already >K.
For fixed bounded Delta and D_N=o(N), choosing vanishing epsilon slowly
enough gives the stated upper spectral convergence in probability.

5. Diamond probability and tangle constants
-------------------------------------------
The necklace base G_m is simple, connected and cubic for m>=3, on 4m
vertices. Gauge-fixing three internal spanning-tree permutations of each
diamond leaves two independent uniform chord permutations. Its one-point-
per-fibre diamond copies are common fixed points, with factorial moments
E(X)_k=1/(n)_k. Exact inclusion-exclusion gives the displayed p_n;
the first two Bonferroni terms give 1/n-1/[2n(n-1)]<=p_n<=1/n.
The internal edge sets are disjoint across blocks, so their events are
independent regardless of connector permutations. At least one diamond
therefore has exact probability 1-(1-p_n)^m.

The cubic alpha^3-alpha-2 has a unique root alpha>1, lying between sqrt(2)
and 2. Its t=1/alpha gives q=2t^2<1. Substitution verifies all three
core/branch eigenfunction equations, including Lb=2+tb. There are 2^k
vertices at branch level k>=1. Thus the truncation contains
4+sum_(k=1)^R 2^k=2^(R+1)+2 vertices and squared norm M_R as written.
Its lost boundary energy is exactly
2^(R+1)*b^2*t^(2R+1)=2b^2*t*q^R (also valid at R=0).
This proves its Rayleigh quotient L_R exactly; no factor two is missing.
The positive square-summable eigenfunction and the weighted quadratic-form
inequality bound both ends of adjacency spectrum by L (apply the positive
bound to absolute values if needed), so the infinite tangle norm is L.

6. Covering pushforward and projected new-space witness
-------------------------------------------------------
The core embedding extends recursively to a local bijective covering of
its finite cubic component by the infinite cubic core completion. A finite
truncation Perron vector, extended by zero, satisfies A h>=mu_R h
pointwise, with mu_R>=L_R. This pointwise statement is valid because it
uses the Perron vector of the truncation, not the original cut-off trial
vector. The covering pushforward is a finite sum; adjacency commutes with
it by local bijectivity, including when branches collide in the image.
Hence g>=0 obeys A_H g>=mu_R g and has support at most S_R.

Projection onto old fibre constants has squared norm
n^-1 sum_v |sum_i g(v,i)|^2 <=(S_R/n)||g||^2.
With delta<=S_R/n<=1/2, the new projection is nonzero. Old and new spaces
reduce adjacency; its old-space norm is at most three. The new Rayleigh
quotient is therefore at least (L_R-3delta)/(1-delta).
Since 0<L_R<=3, this is >=L_R-6S_R/n. This proves the deterministic
spectral implication for every embedded diamond, without an assumption
that its outward finite neighborhood is a tree in the random lift.
That detail is essential and correctly handled by pushforward.

7. Sharp worst-case scope and limits
-------------------------------------
For m_n/n->c finite, p_n~1/n gives diamond-event probability ->1-exp(-c).
For m_n/n->infinity it tends to one. Fixing a sufficiently large but
finite truncation radius and then sending n->infinity gives the claimed
new-eigenvalue lower bound at every fixed epsilon. Thus simple cubic
bases of size proportional to lift degree have a fixed positive chance
of a fixed outlier above 2sqrt(2), while every deterministic bounded-degree
base sequence of size o(lift degree) satisfies the uniform upper theorem.

This proves the claimed sharp worst-case scale in base size alone. It
is not a necessity statement for each individual growing base family,
a simultaneous event over bases chosen after sampling, a result for
uniform simple random regular graphs, or a full critical-window law.
These limitations are accurately stated. The proof is a refinement and
combination of published mechanisms; historical novelty has not been
certified by this audit.
