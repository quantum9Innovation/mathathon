\paragraph{Status.}
We prove an explicit obstruction on an unweighted four-vertex host,
valid for every exponent in $(0,1)$. It excludes implementing a certain
PSD response by only inserting nonnegative graph edges. It is not a
lower bound against general PSD factors, graph sparsification of the full
updated form, or dynamic partitioning algorithms. Historical novelty is
not certified. The matrix-power integral and Schur-complement machinery
are classical~\cite{KA,DB}.

For a connected graph with Laplacian $L$ and positive degree matrix $D$,
write
\[
 H_\alpha(L,D)=D^{1/2}(D^{-1/2}LD^{-1/2})^\alpha D^{1/2},
 \qquad 0<\alpha<1.
\]
This matrix has kernel $\R\mathbf1$. Set
$c_\alpha=\sin(\pi\alpha)/\pi>0$ and, for $t>0$,
\[
 K_t=L+tD,\qquad F_t=D-tD K_t^{-1}D.
\]
The scalar positive-power integral gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:int}
 H_\alpha=c_\alpha\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}F_t\,dt.
\end{equation}
Both endpoints converge in matrix norm, since
$0\preceq F_t\preceq D$ and $0\preceq F_t\preceq L/t$.
The variational formula
\[
 x^TF_tx=\min_{y\in\R^n}
 \left[(x-y)^TD(x-y)+t^{-1}y^TLy\right]
\]
shows joint monotonicity in $L,D$. Consequently a positive edge edit,
which increases both matrices in Loewner order, gives a PSD response
$\Delta H=H'_\alpha-H_\alpha\succeq0$.
Each $H_\alpha$ is itself a graph Laplacian: $K_t^{-1}$ is entrywise
nonnegative by the maximum principle, so $F_t$ has nonpositive
offdiagonal entries, and $F_t\mathbf1=0$. These properties survive
the positive integral.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:main}
Let $L,D$ describe the unweighted path $1$--$2$--$3$--$4$, and let
$L',D'$ result from doubling edge $\{1,2\}$. For every $0<\alpha<1$,
\[
 \Delta H\succeq0,\qquad \Delta H\mathbf1=0,
 \qquad (\Delta H)_{34}>0.
\]
Thus $\Delta H$ is not the Laplacian of a graph with nonnegative weights.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Give the first edge weight $w>0$ and leave the other two weights one.
Write $a=1+t>1$. Then $K_t(w)$ has diagonal
$(wa,(w+1)a,2a,a)$ and offdiagonal $(-w,-1,-1)$.
Eliminating vertex $1$ gives the effective diagonal at vertex $2$
\[
 b_2(w)=a+w(a-a^{-1}),
\]
strictly increasing in $w$. Eliminating vertex $2$ gives the effective
diagonal at vertex $3$
\[
 b_3(w)=2a-b_2(w)^{-1},
\]
also strictly increasing in $w$. The restriction of the inverse to
vertices $3,4$ is the inverse of the remaining Schur complement, so
\[
 [K_t(w)^{-1}]_{34}=\frac1{a b_3(w)-1}
\]
strictly decreases in $w$. Degrees $d_3=2,d_4=1$ are unchanged. Hence
\[
 [F_t(w)]_{34}=-\frac{2t}{a b_3(w)-1}
\]
strictly increases in $w$. The positive convergent integral
\eqref{eq:int}, applied to $w=1$ and $w=2$, proves the strict inequality.
The PSD and kernel assertions were established above.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{An explicit positive certificate.}
Putting $z=t(t+2)$ and simplifying the same elimination gives
\[
 [F_t(w)]_{34}=
 -\frac{2[1+(w+1)z]}{(t+2)[w+2+2(w+1)z]}.
\]
Consequently the proposition's positive entry has the exact formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cert}
 (\Delta H)_{34}
 =c_\alpha\int_0^\infty
 \frac{2t^{\alpha-1}(1+t)^2}
 {(t+2)[3+4t(t+2)][4+6t(t+2)]}\,dt>0.
\end{equation}
The integral converges at zero and infinity for every exponent in the
stated range. It also gives a stable independent scalar check against a
dense fractional matrix power.

\paragraph{Algorithmic interpretation.}
An insertion of a nonnegative graph edge adds
$s(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^T$, $s\ge0$, whose offdiagonal entries are
nonpositive. Every sum of such updates has the same sign restriction.
Proposition~\ref{prop:main} therefore rules out realizing this exact
response by only such insertions, and the set of nonnegative Laplacians
is closed, so even arbitrarily accurate absolute operator-norm
approximation by only positive edge insertions is impossible.
A graph representation of the \emph{full updated form} can still exist:
it may reduce some old edge weights while increasing others. A general
low-rank PSD factor has no offdiagonal sign restriction. Thus an
end-to-end normalized fractional update algorithm must retain this
distinction when connecting its factor to graph partitioning.
