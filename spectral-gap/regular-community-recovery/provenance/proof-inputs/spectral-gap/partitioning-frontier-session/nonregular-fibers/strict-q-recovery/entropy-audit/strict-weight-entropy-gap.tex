\paragraph{Scope and outcome.}
For a nonregular reversible degree frame admitting strict symmetric
omission weights, this note proves an explicit quadratic entropy gap.
It is stronger than the compactness gap needed by the recovery
composition. It has no lower bound on candidate-class masses, allows
unused labels and repeated or singular candidate rows, and does not
use an internal-degree lower bound. It also proves the exact-type
macroscopic exclusion and the unconditional modular first-moment bound.
The separate support exclusion and enumeration compose with these
inputs; this note identifies their hypothesis gates without replacing
their independent audit. All logarithms are natural.

\paragraph{True frame and strict hypothesis.}
Let $Q=(d_{ij})_{i,j\in[k]}$ be a nonnegative integer matrix. Let
$\alpha_i>0$ sum to one, with
\[
 \alpha_i d_{ij}=\alpha_jd_{ji},\qquad
 3\leq d_i:=\sum_jd_{ij}\leq D.                    \tag{1}
\]
Assume there are symmetric weights on every active factor such that
\[
 q_{ij}=q_{ji},\qquad 0<q_{ij}<\tfrac12,
 \qquad \sum_jd_{ij}q_{ij}=1\quad(i\in[k]).         \tag{2}
\]
For an internal factor, its physical-slot weight is $q_{ii}$ and
its edge coefficient below is $1-2q_{ii}$. An absent factor has no
weight or term. Set
\[
 a=\min_{d_{ij}>0}q_{ij}>0,\qquad
 b=\min_{d_{ij}>0}(1-2q_{ij})>0,
 \qquad C_q=\max\left\{\frac1a,\frac{2(D-1)}b\right\}.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
The letter $b$ here denotes a strictness margin, not a graph degree.
Connectedness is unnecessary for the local entropy statements.
It will be imposed for uniformity over bounded frames and for the
intended recovery model. In fact (2) already forces $d_i>2$;
the integer minimum degree in (1) is stated explicitly to expose its
role in the variance estimate.

\paragraph{Candidate laws, including all boundary faces.}
Fix $r\geq1$ and a nonnegative integer matrix $R\in\mathbb Z^{r\times r}$
with every row sum at most $D$. No balance, connectedness, invertibility,
or distinct-row assumption on $R$ is made. At old type $i$ let $P_i$
be a probability law on the finite set of ordered stars
\[
 (X_i,Y_{i1},\ldots,Y_{id_i})\in[r]^{d_i+1},
 \qquad \sum_{s=1}^{d_i}e_{Y_{is}}=R_{X_i,*}.       \tag{4}
\]
Here $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ are distinct real standard basis vectors.
Each physical slot has its prescribed old neighbor type $j(s)$,
with exactly $d_{ij}$ slots of type $j$.
Write $\rho_i$ for the root law. On every active factor let
$\nu_{ij}$ be the root--neighbor law pooled uniformly over its slots.
Require
\[
 \nu_{ii}(x,y)=\nu_{ii}(y,x),\qquad
 \nu_{ij}(x,y)=\nu_{ji}(y,x).                       \tag{5}
\]
Thus the two marginals are $\rho_i,\rho_j$.
Define
\[
 w_{ii}=\frac{\alpha_id_{ii}}2,\qquad
 w_{ij}=\alpha_id_{ij}\quad(i<j),                  \tag{6}
\]
and the normalized entropy and defect
\begin{align}
 \Phi(P)&=\sum_i\alpha_i\HH(P_i)
                     -\sum_{i\leq j}w_{ij}\HH(\nu_{ij}),\nonumber\\
 \delta(P)&=\sum_i\alpha_i(1-\max_x\rho_i(x)).       \tag{7}
\end{align}
The maximum is over all $r$ labels, including labels of zero mass.
If laws of this kind do not exist, assertions about all such laws
are vacuous; in particular an incompatible candidate $R$ need not
admit any law. We use the convention $0\log0=0$.

For empirical root laws from a one-hot labeling $Y$ of a graph whose
planted indicator is $J$, the exact interpretation of $\delta$ is
\[
 \delta(P)=\frac1n\min_T|\{v:Y_v\ne(JT)_v\}|,       \tag{8}
\]
where $T$ ranges over all $k$-by-$r$ one-hot matrices. A map $T$ need
not be onto. Indeed each old block chooses its maximizing root label
independently, producing (8). No mass condition on candidate classes
is present in this identity.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit strict-weight entropy gap]\label{thm:gap}
Every compatible collection of legal laws just specified satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \Phi(P)\leq-c_q\delta(P)^2,
 \qquad c_q=\frac{9}{2D^5C_q}>0.}                 \tag{9}
\]
In particular $\Phi(P)=0$ if and only if the laws are induced by an
old-type point map $T$ with $QT=TR$: all root laws are the point masses
$\rho_i=\delta_{T(i)}$, and every neighbor slot of old type $j$ has
label $T(j)$. The inequality is uniform over $r$, $R$, the candidate
class masses, and the positive old proportions, with $D,q$ as in
(1)--(3). Neither connectedness nor $\alpha_{\min}$ enters its constant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First symmetrize each star law over permutations of slots with the
same old neighbor type, denoting the law by $\overline P_i$.
This preserves the sum equation, root laws, and pooled pair laws.
Concavity raises star entropy, so
\[
 \Phi(P)\leq\Phi(\overline P),\qquad
 \delta(P)=\delta(\overline P).                    \tag{10}
\]
These averaged laws need not be empirical integer types. No permutation
between different old neighbor types is used.

Work henceforth with the symmetrized laws, so that every slot toward
$j$ has pair law $\nu_{ij}$. Write $I_{ij}=\II(\nu_{ij})$.
For omission of physical slot $t$, let
\[
 J_{it}=\sum_{s\ne t}\HH(Y_{is}\mid X_i)
           -\HH((Y_{is})_{s\ne t}\mid X_i)\geq0.
\]
Equation (4) determines an omitted label uniquely from the root and
the retained labels. Consequently deletion is lossless and
\[
 \HH(\overline P_i)=\HH(\rho_i)
                 +\HH((Y_{is})_{s\ne t}\mid X_i).
\]
Average over omissions with physical-slot weights $q_{it}=q_{i,j(t)}$.
Their sum is one by (2), giving
\begin{align}
 \HH(\overline P_i)
 &=\HH(\rho_i)+\sum_s(1-q_{is})\HH(Y_{is}\mid X_i)
                            -\sum_tq_{it}J_{it}\nonumber\\
 &=(2-d_i)\HH(\rho_i)
       +\sum_jd_{ij}(1-q_{ij})\HH(\nu_{ij})
                            -\sum_tq_{it}J_{it}.   \tag{11}
\end{align}
Inserting (11) into (7), reversibility and symmetry give coefficient
$w_{ij}(1-2q_{ij})$ on each undirected pair entropy. Use
\[
 \HH(\nu_{ij})=\HH(\rho_i)+\HH(\rho_j)-I_{ij},
\]
with two root-entropy terms for an internal factor. The root coefficient
at type $i$ is
\[
 \alpha_i\left[(2-d_i)+\sum_jd_{ij}(1-2q_{ij})\right]=0.
\]
Thus the exact nonnegative loss is
\[
 L:=-\Phi(\overline P)
   =\underbrace{\sum_{i\leq j}w_{ij}(1-2q_{ij})I_{ij}}_{L_I}
    +\underbrace{\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_tq_{it}J_{it}}_{L_J}\geq0.
                                                               \tag{12}
\]
The internal weight is $\alpha_id_{ii}/2$ throughout this calculation.
Internal degrees zero and one need no separate entropy argument.

Let $P_i^{-t}$ be the joint law of the root and retained labels.
Let $\mathsf Q_{it}$ keep root law $\rho_i$ and sample the retained
labels independently from $\rho_{j(s)}$, independently also of the root.
The divergence is finite even at zero marginals: any positive actual
tuple has every coordinate in a positive marginal support. Direct
expansion gives
\[
 \kappa_{it}:=\KL(P_i^{-t}\Vert\mathsf Q_{it})
       =J_{it}+\sum_{s\ne t}I_{i,j(s)}.             \tag{13}
\]
Set $E=\sum_{i\leq j}w_{ij}I_{ij}$ and
$J_0=\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_tJ_{it}$. Then
\begin{align}
 \sum_i\alpha_i\sum_t\kappa_{it}
 &=J_0+\sum_i\alpha_i(d_i-1)\sum_jd_{ij}I_{ij}\nonumber\\
 &\leq J_0+2(D-1)E
 \leq \frac{L_J}{a}+\frac{2(D-1)L_I}{b}
 \leq C_q L.                                    \tag{14}
\end{align}
Here $\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_jd_{ij}I_{ij}=2E$, including internal
factors. The last comparison uses a maximum of the two coefficients,
rather than summing two separate bounds on the same total loss.

For each old type $j$ put
\[
 V_j=1-\|\rho_j\|_2^2.
\]
For slots at a fixed type $i$, write $V_{is}=V_{j(s)}$ and
$\mu_{is}=\E e_{Y_{is}}$. Define on retained data
\[
 f_{it}=\left\|R_{X_i,*}-\sum_{s\ne t}e_{Y_{is}}-\mu_{it}\right\|_2^2.
\]
Under the actual law, the sum equation makes
$\E_{P_i^{-t}}f_{it}=V_{it}$. Under the auxiliary product law,
independence gives
\[
 \E_{\mathsf Q_{it}}f_{it}
       =\sum_{s\ne t}V_{is}+B_i,
 \qquad B_i=\E\left\|R_{X_i,*}-\sum_s\mu_{is}\right\|_2^2\geq0.
                                                               \tag{15}
\]
The same $B_i$ occurs for every omission. Neither injectivity nor any
spectral property of $R$ is used. Summing the differences of (15)
and the actual expectations yields
\[
 \sum_t\bigl(\E_{\mathsf Q_{it}}f_{it}-\E_{P_i^{-t}}f_{it}\bigr)
        =(d_i-2)\sum_sV_{is}+d_iB_i.               \tag{16}
\]
Individual differences can have either sign.

For every root of positive $\rho_i$-mass, legality forces the row
$R_{X_i,*}$ to have mass exactly $d_i$. This also holds under the
auxiliary law, which keeps that root marginal. A zero-mass root label
never contributes. The vector $\sum_{s\ne t}e_{Y_{is}}+\mu_{it}$ is
nonnegative and has mass $d_i$, including on auxiliary tuples outside
the legal-star set. Therefore
\[
 0\leq f_{it}\leq2d_i^2\leq2D^2
\]
under both laws. The range form of total variation and Pinsker's
inequality (with natural logarithms) imply
\[
 \left|\E_{\mathsf Q_{it}}f_{it}-\E_{P_i^{-t}}f_{it}\right|
       \leq2D^2\TV(P_i^{-t},\mathsf Q_{it})
       \leq\sqrt2D^2\sqrt{\kappa_{it}}.            \tag{17}
\]
Weight (16) by $\alpha_i$, discard the nonnegative $B_i$, and apply
(17), weighted Cauchy--Schwarz, (14), and $\sum_i\alpha_id_i\leq D$:
\begin{align}
 \sum_i\alpha_i(d_i-2)\sum_sV_{is}
 &\leq\sqrt2D^2\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_t\sqrt{\kappa_{it}}\nonumber\\
 &\leq D^2\sqrt{2\left(\sum_i\alpha_id_i\right)
                         \left(\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_t\kappa_{it}\right)}
 \leq D^2\sqrt{2D C_qL}.                          \tag{18}
\end{align}
On the other hand, $d_i-2\geq1$ and reversibility imply
\begin{align}
 \sum_i\alpha_i(d_i-2)\sum_sV_{is}
 &\geq\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_jd_{ij}V_j
   =\sum_j\alpha_jd_jV_j\nonumber\\
 &\geq3\sum_j\alpha_jV_j\geq3\delta(P).             \tag{19}
\end{align}
The final inequality uses $\sum_x\rho_j(x)^2\leq\max_x\rho_j(x)$.
Combining (18)--(19) and squaring proves
$L\geq9\delta(P)^2/(2D^5C_q)$. Equation (10) returns the bound to
the original unsymmetrized laws.

If $\Phi(P)=0$, (9) forces $\delta=0$, so every root law is a point
mass $T(i)$. A pooled type-$j$ slot has point-mass marginal $T(j)$;
averaging can give a point mass only if every physical slot already
has that point mass. The stars are deterministic, even before
symmetrization. Their legal equations are precisely $QT=TR$.
Conversely every such point map has zero star and pair entropies and
thus $\Phi=0$. This proves the equality characterization.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Unused labels and different degrees.}
For a label $x$ of zero total mass $\sum_i\alpha_i\rho_i(x)$, its
row of $R$ is irrelevant to all expectations and can be arbitrary
within the candidate enumeration. If $x$ occurs in old type $i$,
(4) forces its row sum to equal $d_i$. It therefore cannot occur in
two old types of different total degrees. This restriction follows
from legality; it is not an added assumption and causes no singular
division. Labels with mass tending to zero are covered by the same
constants in (9), (13), and (17).

\begin{corollary}[Compactness formulation with no missing faces]
For fixed $Q,\alpha,q,r,R$ and every $\eta>0$, the set of compatible
legal laws with $\delta\geq\eta$ is compact. If nonempty, its entropy
maximum is at most $-c_q\eta^2<0$. Empty candidate classes and vanishing
candidate proportions do not have to be removed from this set.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each local law lies in a finite-dimensional probability simplex on
its legal-star set. Compatibility (5) is closed and linear. The root
marginals depend continuously on the local laws, and $\delta$ is
continuous, being a finite sum of maxima of finitely many coordinates.
Thus $\delta\geq\eta$ is closed in a compact product of simplexes.
Entropy is continuous with $0\log0=0$. Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} gives
the stated explicit maximum bound. Alternatively, its zero-phase
characterization and compactness alone give a strict negative maximum.
Neither proof deletes boundary faces or assumes a positive candidate
mass lower bound.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Configuration count.}
Now let $n_i=\alpha_i n$ be positive integers satisfying
$n_id_{ij}=n_jd_{ji}$ and $n_id_{ii}$ even. Independently pair the
labeled internal stub pools and match the active cross stub pools.
A loop contributes two to adjacency and degree. For a finite alphabet
of size $m$, ordered legal stars determine each omitted slot uniquely;
their number at old type $i$ is at most $m^{d_i}$. Let $T_i(s)$ be
star counts and let $m_{ij}(x,y)$ be the pooled directed stub counts.
Compatibility requires reverse cross tables, symmetric internal tables,
and even internal diagonal entries. Put $M_{ij}=n_id_{ij}$ and let
$P(u)=(u-1)!!$ for even $u\geq0$, with $P(0)=1$. The exact
contribution of a compatible collection of star counts is
\begin{align}
 W(T)={}&\prod_i\frac{n_i!}{\prod_sT_i(s)!}
  \prod_{i:d_{ii}>0}
    \frac{\prod_{x<y}m_{ii}(x,y)!\prod_xP(m_{ii}(x,x))}{P(M_{ii})}
 \nonumber\\
 &\hspace{20mm}\cdot
   \prod_{i<j:d_{ij}>0}\frac{\prod_{x,y}m_{ij}(x,y)!}{M_{ij}!}.
                                                               \tag{20}
\end{align}
The first multinomial assigns all roots and all ordered stars to
labeled vertices. There is no additional root-label multinomial.
Internal opposite categories are paired by bijections and diagonal
categories by perfect pairings; cross categories are matched by
bijections. Independence gives (20). Conversely a configuration and
its labeling determine the ordered stars, so the count is exact.

For $m_0\geq\max\{2,m\}$, $k\leq K$, define
\[
 F(m_0)=K+\frac{K(K+3)}2m_0^2,
 \qquad B(m_0)=Km_0^D+F(m_0).                     \tag{21}
\]
Uniform factorial estimates, including zero counts, give
\[
 W(T)\leq\exp\{n\Phi(P)+F(m_0)[\log(Dn+1)+1]\}.   \tag{22}
\]
For clarity, if $f(u)=u\log u-u$, $f(0)=0$, then
$0\leq\log u!-f(u)\leq\log(u+1)+1$.
The identity $P(u)=u!/[2^{u/2}(u/2)!]$ gives
$|\log P(u)-f(u)/2|\leq2[\log(u+1)+1]$.
A root multinomial contributes upper error at most one such unit,
a cross factor at most $m^2$, and an internal factor at most
$m(m-1)/2+2m+2\leq2m_0^2$. All counts are at most $Dn$.
At most $K$ roots, $K$ internals and $K(K-1)/2$ cross factors yield
(21)--(22). There are at most $(n+1)^{Km_0^D}$ collections of types.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform macroscopic first moment]\label{thm:macro}
Let $k,r\leq K$, set $m_0=\max\{2,K\}$ and let $R$ range over all
nonnegative integer $r$-by-$r$ matrices of row sums at most $D$.
For each fixed $R$, let $N_{R,\eta}$ count one-hot labelings satisfying
the exact integer equation $AY=YR$ and $\delta(Y)\geq\eta$.
Labels are allowed to be unused. Then
\[
 \E_{\rm config}N_{R,\eta}
 \leq(3D)^{F(m_0)}(n+1)^{B(m_0)}e^{-c_q\eta^2n}.   \tag{23}
\]
The bound also holds for the more restricted family requiring every
candidate class nonempty. Summing (23) over the finite candidate family
costs at most
\[
 M(K,D)=\sum_{r=1}^K(D+1)^{r^2}.                   \tag{24}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use alphabet $[r]$ with the integer one-hot legal equation (4).
The exact count (20) applies. Its normalized entropy is exactly (7),
so (9) bounds it by $-c_q\eta^2$. Sum (22) over the indicated number
of types and use $Dn+1\leq D(n+1)$ and $e<3$. This proves (23),
uniformly in the candidate $R$. The true row-sum constraint only
reduces the crude entrywise count (24).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Unconditional modular first moment]\label{thm:modular}
For every prime $p$, $r\leq K$, and arbitrary
$R\in\F_p^{r\times r}$, put
\[
 Z_R(A)=|\{Y\in\F_p^{n\times r}:AY=YR\}|,
 \qquad m_*=p^K.
\]
Under (1)--(2),
\[
 \E_{\rm config}Z_R(A)
 \leq(3D)^{F(m_*)}(n+1)^{B(m_*)}.                 \tag{25}
\]
This expectation is unconditional: it includes rare graphs of large
kernel dimension. The proof only needs non-strict feasible omission
weights; strictness is required for (9), not (25).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the vector alphabet $\F_p^r$, with $m=p^r\leq m_*$, and legal
sum $\sum_sY_{is}=X_iR$. Any omitted slot is determined with
coefficient one, so there are exactly $m^{d_i}$ legal stars.
The weighted entropy cancellation (11)--(12) is valid for finite-field
labels: it uses unique deletion and real entropies, not real variance.
It gives $\Phi\leq0$ without dividing by $d_i$ in the field.
Apply (20)--(22) and sum over types as before. The real one-hot
variance proof is not used for modular labels.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Simplicity conditioning and admissible sizes.}
For a fixed frame and positive old proportions, the independent
factor simplicity probability tends to $e^{-\Lambda(Q)}>0$, where
\[
 \Lambda(Q)=\sum_{i:d_{ii}\geq1}\frac{d_{ii}^2-1}{4}
       +\sum_{i<j:d_{ij}>0}\frac{(d_{ij}-1)(d_{ji}-1)}2
       \leq K^2D^2.                              \tag{26}
\]
An internal degree-zero factor is absent and contributes zero to
$\Lambda$; degree one contributes zero. A cross matching likewise
contributes zero. One direct proof counts loops and unordered pairs
of parallel edges. Their respective internal means are
$(d_{ii}-1)/2$ and $(d_{ii}-1)^2/4$, and the cross mean is the second
summand in (26). For fixed factorial-moment order, disjoint patterns
give product limits; a consistent overlapping union of distinct bad
patterns has more prescribed pairs than free vertices and contributes
$O(1/\min_i n_i)$. Bonferroni truncation gives the Poisson zero-event
limit. This argument is local to each factor and does not require
equal total row degrees.

Each simple internal graph has $(d_{ii}!)^{n_i}$ labeled-stub
realizations, and each simple cross factor has
$(d_{ij}!)^{n_i}(d_{ji}!)^{n_j}$. Thus conditioning yields precisely
the product of uniform simple regular/biregular factors.
Eventually its cost in a nonnegative expectation is at most
$2e^{K^2D^2}$. This applies to both (23) and (25).
Only actual balance and parity requirements are imposed:
$n_id_{ij}=n_jd_{ji}$ and $n_id_{ii}$ even. No even-size condition is
introduced for absent internal factors or cross matchings.

\begin{proposition}[Uniformity over the finite true-frame family]
Fix $K,D$. Restrict to connected reversible nonnegative integer
frames with $k\leq K$, all row totals at most $D$, and strict
feasibility (2). There are choices of $q$ and constants
$c_*(K,D)>0$, $n_0(K,D)$ such that (23), after simplicity conditioning,
holds uniformly for every such true frame, every admissible
$n\geq n_0$, and every candidate $r,R$, with $c_q$ replaced by $c_*$.
The modular moment and its simplicity factor are likewise uniform.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
There are finitely many matrices: every entry is an integer between
zero and $D$, and $k\leq K$ is fixed. Positive reversibility makes
the off-diagonal support undirected. Along a frame path,
$\alpha_j/\alpha_i=d_{ij}/d_{ji}$ is a ratio of positive integers
between one and $D$. Connectedness fixes all proportions uniquely,
including the trivial one-type case, and gives
\[
 \alpha_{\min}\geq\rho_*:=\frac1{KD^{K-1}}.        \tag{27}
\]
Hence every active pool grows at least linearly with $n$ uniformly.
The factor simplicity estimates above have an eventual threshold for
each frame; their finite maximum gives a uniform $n_0$.

For each eligible frame maximize $\theta$ in the rational finite LP
\[
 \sum_jd_{ij}q_{ij}=1,\qquad q_{ij}=q_{ji},\qquad
 \theta\leq q_{ij}\leq(1-\theta)/2,
 \qquad\theta\geq0.                              \tag{28}
\]
The feasible region is compact, and strict feasibility is equivalent
to a positive optimal value. A rational optimal point can be chosen.
Choose one per frame. The minimum $\theta_*(K,D)$ of their positive
optimal values is positive because the family is finite. Then
$a,b\geq\theta_*$, so
\[
 C_q\leq\frac{2(D-1)}{\theta_*},\qquad
 c_q\geq c_*:=\frac{9\theta_*}{4D^5(D-1)}>0.        \tag{29}
\]
No actual recovery algorithm needs to know these analysis weights or
the hidden true frame. They certify uniform estimates for the finite
model family. The family is nonempty, for example the one-type
degree-three frame, so the minimum can be taken literally.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The exact entropy inputs available for composition.}
For any fixed $\eta>0$, a finite union and Markov applied to (23)
exclude all integer candidates with $\delta\geq\eta$ with probability
at most a polynomial times $e^{-c_*\eta^2n}$. This includes candidates
with tiny nonempty classes and arbitrary zero rows for unused labels.
For $p=2$, (25) says the full binary solution count $2^{h_R}$ has
polynomial expectation, uniformly in every bounded candidate quotient.
It therefore supports expected polynomial exhaustive kernel enumeration
without conditioning that expectation on structural success.

The frozen support argument was read in full. Its small-support
count uses $2a+b=ds$ in the regular case. For a nonregular frame the
same relation is $2a+b=\sum_{v\in S}\deg(v)\geq3s$; the condition
$b\geq2|W|$ still yields $e-s-|W|\geq s/2$. All other witness bounds
use maximum degree $D$ and the pool lower bound (27). Thus the
entropy hypotheses do not introduce a conflict with that extension.
The near-point clean-vertex step uses only bounded degree, (27),
and the integer identities $AJ=JQ$, $AY=YR$: every unchanged clean
old type gives $QT=TR$. Row regularity is unnecessary there.
The full support proof and complete algorithmic composition remain
separately auditable components of the assembled recovery theorem.

\paragraph{Strictness and scope barriers.}
If some $q_{ij}=0$ or $1-2q_{ij}=0$, (12) need not control all terms
in (13), and $C_q$ becomes unavailable. This proof makes no
boundary-feasible rigidity claim. Minimum degree alone is insufficient:
the separate $K_{3,12}$ information obstruction fails the omission
LP. Strictly feasible nonregular bases do exist: in $K_5$ minus an
edge, put weight $1/3$ on edges incident to either deleted-edge
endpoint and $1/6$ on edges among the other three vertices.
Every vertex has weight sum one and all edge weights lie strictly
between zero and one-half. This proof makes no historical priority,
spectral-simplicity, noisy-recovery, or growing-$K$ claim.
