# Executive map

## The shortest honest summary

**Paper:** For fixed degrees $d_1\geq d_2\geq 3$, a uniformly random simple bipartite biregular graph is asymptotically almost Ramanujan. The upper edge of its adjacency spectrum is, asymptotically almost surely, no larger than the Alon-Boppana limit plus a vanishing error:

$$\eta\leq \sqrt{d_1-1}+\sqrt{d_2-1}+\epsilon_n',\qquad \epsilon_n'\to0.$$

This is Theorem 4(i), pages 8 to 9. It matches the lower spectral constraint in Equation 2 up to vanishing errors.

**Interpretation:** A typical sparse biregular graph has almost the best possible second-eigenvalue behavior. The proof also reveals the lower positive spectral edge and, in the genuinely rectangular case, full row rank of the biadjacency matrix.

## The strongest packaged conclusion

Let $X\in\{0,1\}^{n\times m}$ have row sums $d_1$ and column sums $d_2$, with $nd_1=md_2$, $n\leq m$, and fixed $d_1>d_2$. For a uniform simple graph with this biadjacency matrix, Theorem 4 states that, asymptotically almost surely:

$$\lambda_2(A)\leq \sqrt{d_1-1}+\sqrt{d_2-1}+o(1),$$

$$\eta_{\min}^{+}\geq \sqrt{d_1-1}-\sqrt{d_2-1}-o(1),$$

$$\operatorname{rank}(X)=n.$$

The rank conclusion has no square analogue in the paper. It comes from imaginary non-backtracking eigenvalues generated by nullvectors, not from a separate rank argument.

The non-backtracking version proved first is Theorem 3:

$$|\lambda_2(B)|\leq ((d_1-1)(d_2-1))^{1/4}+\epsilon_n,$$

where $\lambda_2(B)$ means second largest in absolute value after the dominant pair.

## Main proof mechanism

Set $a=d_1-1$ and $b=d_2-1$, with $a\geq b$.

1. **Configuration model.** A uniform random matching of left and right half-edges produces the correct uniform simple-graph law after conditioning on simplicity. Fixed-degree simplicity has probability bounded away from zero, so vanishing-probability estimates transfer.
2. **Ihara-Bass transfer.** Each nonzero singular value $\xi$ of $X$ is related to non-backtracking eigenvalues $\lambda$ by Equation 7:

   $$\lambda^4-(\xi^2-d_1-d_2+2)\lambda^2+ab=0.$$

3. **Bulk geometry.** Put $x=\lambda^2$ and $y=\xi^2$. Then

   $$y=x+\frac{ab}{x}+a+b.$$

   The adjacency bulk corresponds to $|x|=\sqrt{ab}$, hence $|\lambda|=(ab)^{1/4}$. An adjacency outlier beyond the upper bulk edge forces a larger non-backtracking eigenvalue.
4. **Centered non-backtracking moments.** The authors subtract a rank-two normalization and bound the remaining operator by a high trace moment.
5. **Local tree-likeness.** Radius-$O(\log n)$ neighborhoods contain at most one cycle with high probability. Only relatively simple path pieces need counting.
6. **Circuit bookkeeping.** Circuit multiplicity, edge appearance probabilities, half-edge labelings, cycle excess, and left-right imbalance supply the moment estimate.
7. **Return through Ihara-Bass.** The non-backtracking bound excludes both upper and lower adjacency outliers and excludes the dangerous imaginary eigenvalues associated with rank loss.

## The three applications

| Application | What the spectral gap buys | Location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Community detection | Frame eigenvectors can dominate stochastic block eigenvectors after scaling all block degrees. Leading-eigenvector clustering then recovers classes. | Section 6, Theorem 19 |
| Error-correcting codes | A smaller $\eta$ improves the Janwa-Lal minimum-distance bound for a Tanner code built on the graph. | Section 7, Theorem 21 and Corollary 22 |
| Matrix completion | Expander mixing controls full-entry squared error by sampled-entry behavior, with factor $\eta/\sqrt{d_1d_2}$. | Section 8, Theorems 24 to 26 |

## What mastery should enable

After using this dossier, the reader should be able to:

1. State the random model precisely, including $nd_1=md_2$, fixed degrees, simplicity, uniform conditioning, and the limit $n\to\infty$.
2. Explain why adjacency eigenvalues occur in signed pairs and why zero eigenvalues are inevitable when $n<m$.
3. Derive Equation 7 and classify its real, complex, and purely imaginary solutions.
4. Prove the full-row-rank conclusion from a rank-deficient nullvector and the non-backtracking bound.
5. Explain why $B^\ell$ is replaced by a tangle-free, almost-centered moment rather than studied by ordinary adjacency walks.
6. Reproduce the moment scales $\alpha^\ell\exp((\log n)^{3/4})$ and $(d-1)^\ell n^{-1}\exp((\log n)^{3/4})$, and explain the later $\ell$th root.
7. Read Figure 3 as a visualization of the spectral transfer rather than merely two attractive plots.
8. Explain the role of every figure and identify Figure 5's indexing inconsistency.
9. State which conclusions are asymptotic and why the numerical coding example does not by itself give a finite-size guarantee.
10. Distinguish the paper's assertions, the reconstructed derivations in this dossier, and the unresolved print-level proof issues.

## Limits in one paragraph

The theorem is for fixed degrees and gives no explicit finite-$n$ error probability or optimal vanishing rate. Its technical trace calculations rely on an imported matching-moment estimate and delicate combinatorics. This dossier independently derives the spectral bridge and checks the moment argument's logical architecture, but it does not certify every imported theorem or repair every local formula. Several published-statement issues are documented rather than hidden.
