1. This is an ordinary unit-mass weighted graph, without hidden signs
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix b=e_p-e_q. An isometry U:R^r->1-perp satisfying
U^T b=sqrt(2/r)*1 exists by an orthogonal basis change, since both
specified vectors have squared norm two. Thus H=U diag(j/r)U^T is PSD,
annihilates constants, and has norm one. For i!=j, |H_ij|<=1, so
L_ij=-1/n+delta H_ij<=-1/(2n)<0 at delta=1/(2n).
Zero row sums make its diagonal exactly the sum of the positive incident
conductances. Hence L is precisely a positively weighted complete-graph
combinatorial Laplacian, with ordinary Euclidean unit vertex masses.
There is no signed-graph or generalized-mass substitution in this step.

Its positive eigenvalues are lambda_j=1+delta*j/r. The positive edit
is an increase of the one existing edge pq by d=gamma*delta/2.
Its support-basis matrix is exactly
 I+(delta/r)(diag(j)+gamma*11^T).
The leverage is gamma*delta times the mean of lambda_j^-1, proving both
stated bounds. In particular tau~gamma/(2n)->0 for fixed gamma.
Reference positive eigenvalues lie near one, not near a vanishing gap at
zero. The common kernel is exactly constants before and after the edit.

2. Bulk secular roots and normalization constants
---------------------------------------------------
Positive rank-one strict interlacing is valid because all coordinates
of the update vector in the simple old eigenbasis are nonzero. For j<r,
there is exactly one root nu_j=j+theta_j with 0<theta_j<1. The secular
sign is correct: 1+gamma sum_i(i-nu)^-1=0, and the secular function is
strictly increasing between its poles.

For r/4<=j<=3r/4 and theta<=1/4, paired +/-h terms sum to
2theta/(h^2-theta^2). Their infinite sum is <1: use
2theta/(1-theta^2) times sum h^-2, maximized at theta=1/4.
The unpaired harmonic tail is uniformly bounded on this bulk; the claimed
log4+1 bound is conservative for r>=64. In particular |S_j|<=8 is valid.
At theta=gamma/2 the secular value is <=-1+8gamma<0; at theta=2gamma
it is >=1/2-8gamma>0 for 0<gamma<=1/32. Thus the displayed bracket holds.

Since theta<=1/16, the off-pole squared sum is <=
2(16/15)^2*zeta(2)<4, hence certainly <=8. Also theta^-2>=256.
Writing N_j=theta/sqrt(1+theta^2 S_2) shows
 N_j>=theta/sqrt(2)>=gamma/sqrt(8), N_j<=theta<=2gamma.
For i<j, |i-j-theta|=j-i+theta<=j-i+1, giving exactly
 |V_ij|^2>=gamma^2/[8(j-i+1)^2].
No perturbation expansion in gamma*r or an unproved uniform asymptotic
is used; gamma*r may grow arbitrarily.

3. Equal-rank cutoff windows and Hilbert--Schmidt lower bound
--------------------------------------------------------------
For every stated central k, nu_k<=k+2gamma while nu_(k+1)>k+1.
The displayed open interval for t is therefore above both kth eigenvalues
and below both (k+1)st eigenvalues. Both projectors have exactly rank k;
the common zero mode is excluded from both by the (0,t) convention.
All columns j=k+1,...,k+q, q=floor(r/16), remain in the bulk, and rows
k-a for 0<=a<q exist. The overlap identity is
 Tr(P(I-Q))=sum_(i<=k,j>k)|V_ij|^2.

In the retained q-by-q rectangle, for every 1<=h<=q there are exactly
h pairs a+b=h. Since h/(h+1)^2>=1/(4h), the leakage is at least
 gamma^2/32 sum_(h=1)^q 1/h >=gamma^2/32 log(q+1).
Equal ranks give ||P-Q||_F^2=2Tr(P(I-Q)), proving the claimed
coefficient gamma^2/16. The central index and rounding assumptions cover
all retained rows/columns, including the smallest permitted r.

4. Many fixed-size singular values: compression reconstructed explicitly
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
The geometric blocks have R_l=Q^l, Q=2^16, and 2R_l<=r/16.
They are disjoint and remain safely in the stated bulk at every central
cutoff. After one harmless global sign, their overlap entries are
N_(k+b)/(a+b+theta_(k+b)).

The column unit vector on block h is proportional to 1/N_(k+b).
If A_h=sum_(b in block h) N_(k+b)^-2, its normalization produces
 c_h=sqrt(R_h)/sqrt(A_h).
This lies between the minimum and maximum N_j on that block, exactly
as asserted. Thus the compressed matrix really is K diag(c_h), with
the displayed K, rather than a missing extra factor of R_h.

K need not be symmetric, but the proof does not use symmetry. Each
diagonal is >=1/5 (even 1/4 is available). Each off-diagonal entry is
<=sqrt(min(R_l,R_h)/max(R_l,R_h)), so both maximum row and column sums
of the off-diagonal part are <=2/(sqrt(Q)-1)=2/255<1/30.
Its operator norm has the same bound by Schur's inequality. Hence
 sigma_min(K)>1/5-1/30=1/6,
 sigma_min(K diag(c))>gamma/(6sqrt(8))>gamma/20.

Orthogonal row and column compression cannot increase the relevant
singular values, so the full cross-overlap has at least s such values.
For equal-rank orthogonal projections these are principal-angle sines,
each appearing twice among nonzero singular values of P-Q. Thus at least
2s singular values meet the claimed threshold. Since
s=1+floor(log_Q(r/32)) for sufficiently large r, this is Omega(log n).
The conclusion is uniform over all the stated cutoff intervals.

5. The integral includes a genuine logarithmic measure factor
--------------------------------------------------------------
The central k range gives at least r/4 disjoint intervals. Each has
length (1-2gamma)*delta/r, and all lie below 1+delta. Consequently their
total dt/t measure is at least
 delta(1-2gamma)/[4(1+delta)] >=delta/8,
using gamma<=1/32<1/8 and delta<=1/2. Multiplying by the HS lower bound
proves the stated delta*gamma^2*log(q+1)/128. A sharper unused coefficient
is 5/512, confirming the conservative 1/128 allocation.

Since log(1+tau)<=tau<=gamma*delta, the ratio to log(1+tau) is at least
gamma log(q+1)/128 and diverges for fixed gamma. The integral itself may
tend to zero; the theorem correctly asserts divergence of its RATIO.
Outside the displayed windows its integrand is nonnegative. At very low
and sufficiently high t both projectors agree, so no integrability issue
at t=0 or infinity is being used implicitly.

6. Added theorem at EXACTLY prescribed leverage
------------------------------------------------
For any fixed tau_*>0 the source now sets
 a_r=mean_j (1+delta*j/r)^-1, d=tau_*/(2a_r),
 gamma=2d/delta=tau_*/(delta*a_r).
This preserves the same ordinary graph and gives leverage exactly tau_*.
For sufficiently large r, gamma>=1/32, without an upper restriction.
The earlier small-gamma normalization estimates are not reused incorrectly.

Let c=1/64 and g_j(theta)=sum_i(i-j-theta)^-1. Independently substituting
i->r+1-i verifies the reflection identity
 g_j(1-c)=-g_(r-j)(c).
The mirror index is r-j and is still in the bulk. Since g_j(c)<=-56,
the secular function is negative at c for gamma>=1/32 and positive at
1-c. Thus c<=theta_j<=1-c uniformly for every such gamma.
The two nearest inverse-square denominators contribute at most 8192,
and all remaining terms total <4, so N_j>1/sqrt(8196)>1/100.
Also N_j<=1 is safe. The same compression has sigma_min(K)>1/6,
now c_h>=1/100, so it has s singular values >1/600. Equal-rank projector
difference therefore has at least 2s of them.

At k=r/2, the new open cutoff interval is genuinely above the kth old
and new eigenvalues and below both next ones. Choosing finite r sufficiently
large makes 2s exceed any prescribed integer h. Thus at every fixed positive
leverage and every tolerance below 1/600 there is NO finite dimension-
independent projector singular-count bound, even over these weighted graphs.
This strengthened quantifier resolves the limitation of the original
varying-tau family. It does not claim a divergent integrated ratio at fixed
tau; the original small-gamma family supplies the integral obstruction.

7. Scope and interpretation
-----------------------------
The first edit has size d=gamma/(4n), whereas every reference edge weight
lies between 1/(2n) and 3/(2n). It is a small positive increase of one
existing edge, not necessarily doubling and not an unweighted insertion.
In the fixed-tau extension d approaches tau_*/2 while original edge
weights remain of order 1/n; arbitrary positive edit size is explicitly
allowed. Both constructions have unit vertex masses and growing complete-
graph degree. Neither establishes a sparse or bounded-degree obstruction.

The common cutoff gap shrinks like delta/r=Theta(n^-2). Thus the examples
avoid a small baseline lambda_2 but do not contradict gap-dependent
projector perturbation bounds. The fractional relative response tends to
zero only for the original small-gamma, tau->0 family; at fixed tau_* its
stated general norm bound is a fixed constant. The paragraph saying
'for the constructed sequence' should be understood as the original
family, rather than the fixed-tau extension. Specifying that family by
theorem number would improve wording but is not a mathematical defect.

These results refute the specified projector-budget/count targets. They
are not an algorithm for balanced partitioning, a cut-recourse lower bound,
a finite-precision theorem, or a novelty certificate for the classical
orthogonality-catastrophe mechanism. The proof uses explicit finite matrices
and no unverified infinite-volume or scattering transfer.
