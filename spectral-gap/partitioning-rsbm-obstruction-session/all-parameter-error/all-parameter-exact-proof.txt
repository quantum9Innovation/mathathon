EXACT BOOLEAN AMBIGUITY AND BAYES ERROR FOR ALL TWO-CLASS PARAMETERS
================================================================
Derived proof, 2026-11-15. This is a new file; older frozen proofs and

THEOREM AND MODEL
-----------------
Fix integers a>=0, b>=1, D=a+b>=3. In two planted labeled classes
V_+,V_- of size N, choose independent uniform simple a-regular internal
graphs and an independent uniform simple b-regular bipartite cross graph.
Take limits through every admissible N (aN even, with sufficiently large
N for the factors to exist). The degree-zero factors are absent.

Let K(G) count equitable (a,b) unordered bisections of the observed graph.
Set delta=a-b and define m, nu and C as follows:

 If delta>=0, put m=t=delta+1 and
   nu=(D-2)t,
   C=((bt)!)^2(a!)^(2t)/[(t!)^2(b!)^(2t)a^(t*delta)].

 If delta<0, put m=s=b-a and
   nu=(D-2)s,
   C=((as)!)^2(b!)^(2s)/[(s!)^2(a!)^(2s)b^(s^2)].

Then

 Pr[K>=2] ~ C N^(-nu),
 Pr[K>=3] = O_(a,b)(N^(-nu-1)).                         (T1)

If the balanced unordered planted partition is hidden uniformly, then
the optimal exact-recovery Bayes error is

 R_N ~ (C/2) N^(-nu).                                  (T2)

A deterministic polynomial worst-case estimator attains the leading
coefficient in (T2). Its exponent may depend on the fixed degrees.
This is a Boolean partition statement, not a matching error asymptotic
for arbitrary extra real eigenvectors.

Special cases are
 (a,b)=(1,2): nu=1, C=2;
 (1,3): nu=4, C=16;
 (a,a), a>=2: nu=2a-2, C=(a!)^2;
 (0,b), b>=3: nu=b(b-2), C=(b!)^(2b-2)/b^(b^2).
All constants use block size N, rather than total size 2N.

1. MINIMUM BALANCED FLIPS
-------------------------
Let sigma be +1 on V_+ and -1 on V_-. An equitable sign tau satisfies
A tau=delta tau. Since A1=D1 and D-delta=2b>0, every such tau is
automatically balanced. Relative to sigma it flips equally many k
vertices in each old class. Identifying global sign permits k<=N/2.
Write S_+,S_- for the flips and w=(sigma-tau)/2. Then

 w=1_(S_+)-1_(S_-),  Aw=delta w.

At a positive support vertex the equation is

 delta=neighbors in S_+ minus neighbors in S_-.

If delta>=0, simplicity gives delta<=k-1, hence k>=delta+1=t.
At k=t, it forces two K_t internal cliques and NO support-cross edge.
Each support vertex then has b internal and b cross boundary edges.
This includes delta=0: t=1, both cliques are singletons.

If delta=-s<0, the equation gives -s>=-k, hence k>=s.
At k=s, it forces NO internal support edge and ALL support-cross edges:
the support core is K_(s,s). Each support vertex then has a internal
and a cross boundary edges, since b-s=a.

These minimum assertions use the simple model. They are not imposed on
an unconditioned configuration with loops or multiple edges. At large
N>2m, every minimum alternative unordered partition has a unique k=m
orientation; its opposite orientation has N-m>m flips on each old side.

2. ALL LARGER FLIP SETS ARE LOWER ORDER
--------------------------------------
The following support exposure applies for every delta, including zero.
In the independent internal/cross colored configuration model, a fixed
h-vertex support S can support a vector with nonzero values there only
if no outside vertex has exactly ONE incident edge into S. This follows
from Aw=delta w at a zero coordinate. Expose all incident edges, skipping
already matched stubs. If h<=epsilon N for a sufficiently small fixed
epsilon, every active typed pool has Theta(N) remaining stubs. A target
in S or an already touched exterior vertex has conditional probability
at most C_(a,b)h/N. Zero internal pools are omitted, so no division by
a occurs at a=0; degree-one internal factors cause no change.

If e edges lie inside S and B leave it, there are e+B actual exposures.
The number of distinct exterior neighbors is at most B/2. Thus at least
e+B/2=Dh/2 exposures are collisions. After padding by dummy noncollisions
to Dh steps, a conditional index-set union bound gives

 Pr[S has the necessary property]
  <=2^(Dh)(C h/N)^(Dh/2).

Union bounding over supports yields

 Pr[there is such a support of size h]
  <=[A(h/N)^eta]^h,  eta=(D-2)/2>0.                    (U1)

The consecutive ratio is at most A exp(eta)((h+1)/N)^eta, so the sum
from h=2m+2 to epsilon N is

 O(N^(-(D-2)(m+1)))=O(N^(-nu-(D-2))).                 (U2)

Conditioning all actual configuration factors simple costs a fixed
positive constant, justified in Section 5 below. Apply the minimum flip
threshold only after this conditioning.

For macroscopic flips one must NOT use the old a>=2 omitted-internal
argument at a=0 or a=1. The actual all-slot source is

with its exact type-count companion

Both complete files were read. Here is the relevant derivation. Encode
candidate signs as two distinct real one-hot labels, with candidate frame
R=[[a,b],[b,a]]. First symmetrize each star law under slot permutations
WITHIN each old neighbor type. This preserves its pooled edge laws and
root law, while weakly increasing star entropy. Thus the original type
exponent is at most the symmetrized exponent Phi used below; no empirical
slot-exchangeability assumption is imposed. For a physical D-neighbor
star, every omitted slot is determined by the root and retained labels.
Average the entropy deletion identity over all D slots. Matching reversal
and regularity then give

 L=-Phi=(D-2)/D sum_(i<=j) omega_ij I_ij
       +(1/D)sum_i alpha_i sum_t J_it >=0,             (U3)

where alpha_+=alpha_-=1/2, omega_ii=alpha_i a/2,
omega_+-=alpha_+ b, and J_it is conditional total correlation of the
retained slots. Absent internal factors have zero weight.

For each omission, compare to the same root law with root-independent,
independent neighboring marginals. Its KL divergence is
J_it+sum_(u!=t)I(root;neighbor_u). Summing omissions at a fixed old type
and using (U3) bounds this sum by
2D(D-1)L/[alpha_i(D-2)]. The inferred omitted one-hot vector has actual
squared deviation V_t from its marginal mean. Under the product law its
expectation is sum_(u!=t)V_u+B_i, with B_i>=0 independent of t.
Summing the differences gives (D-2)sum_u V_u+D B_i. Each squared function
is in [0,2D^2]. Pinsker and Cauchy--Schwarz therefore yield

 L >= alpha_i(D-2)^3/[4D^6(D-1)] (sum_u V_u)^2.

This forces a quantitative gap in the defect from an old-block point
map. A balanced oriented flip fraction r=k/N<=1/2 has defect r, hence
its UNNORMALIZED exponent is at most

 -c_D N r^2,  c_D=(D-2)^3/[4D^6(D-1)]>0.              (U4)

There is no internal-slot assumption, no sign restriction on delta, and
no invertibility assumption on R. In particular delta=0 is covered.
The exact fixed-alphabet configuration count has polynomially many types
and polynomial zero-safe factorial remainders. For r>=epsilon/2, (U4)
therefore makes the entire macroscopic range exponentially small.

Combining (U2)--(U4), an alternative whose least-oriented flip count is
k>=m+1 occurs with probability

 Pr(E_large)=O(N^(-nu-1)),                            (U5)

because D>=3. This covers all admissible N and all the boundary cases.

3. ALL MINIMUM BOUNDARY PATTERNS AND THEIR COLLISIONS
----------------------------------------------------
Use c=b in the delta>=0 case, and c=a in the delta<0 case. Every support
vertex then has c internal and c cross edges to exterior vertices.
At an exterior old-plus vertex, the zero equation for w requires equally
many neighbors in S_+ internally and S_- across; call that number m_v.
The minus statement is identical. The sum on EACH side is cm.

Thus each exterior touched set W_+,W_- has at most cm vertices. A minimum
flip is LEADING when both have cm vertices, so every positive m_v=1.
Its complete incident motif has

 v_0=2m+2cm,
 e_0=2e_int+e_cross,

where
 delta>=0: e_int=t delta/2+bt per internal factor,
            e_cross=2bt;
 delta<0:  e_int=as per internal factor,
            e_cross=s^2+2as=sD.

In both cases e_0-v_0=(D-2)m=nu. A nonleading minimum flip identifies
at least one exterior vertex while keeping all e_0 support-incident
edges distinct and unchanged in number. There are finitely many simple
typed incidence patterns, so placement and prescribed-stub upper bounds
give

 Pr(E_collision)=O(N^(-nu-1)).                         (U6)

When c=0 no exterior vertex exists and this event is empty. It is also
empty at delta=0 (m=1), and in the disassortative case if a<=1 or s=1,
since m_v<=min(a,s). Emptiness is not required for the upper bound.

For fixed support sets of size m and exterior sets of size cm, specify
the four maps

 W_+ -> S_+ internally,  W_+ -> S_- across,
 W_- -> S_- internally,  W_- -> S_+ across.

Each map has exactly c preimages per support vertex and has
(cm)!/(c!)^m choices. Empty maps count one when c=0. The number of
leading data choices is EXACTLY

 A_N=binom(N,m)^2 binom(N-m,cm)^2 [(cm)!/(c!)^m]^4,

and

 A_N ~ N^(v_0) ((cm)!)^2/[(m!)^2(c!)^(4m)].            (L1)

These are functions on fixed labeled SETS. No additional automorphism,
m!, (cm)!, or global-orientation factor is allowed. A realized leading
flip uniquely determines its exterior sets and all four maps. Conversely
the motif saturates every support vertex and balances each exterior
vertex's support contributions, so any occurrence creates that flip.
Edges among exterior vertices or elsewhere do not matter.

The leading motif is connected and has minimum degree at least two.
For delta>0 its two clique cores are joined by the exterior two-edge
paths. For delta=0, a=b>=2, its core centers are singletons and the whole
motif is K_(2,2a). For delta<0 the support core K_(s,s) is connected;
all exterior vertices attach to it. If a=0 there is no exterior set and
the whole motif is an isolated K_(b,b). Every support vertex has motif
degree D>2; every exterior vertex has motif degree two.

4. EXACT LEADING PATTERN PROBABILITY
-----------------------------------
For c>0, physical stub assignments number

 c_H=(a!b!)^(2m)(ab)^(2cm).

For a=0, c=0, omit the exterior stub factor and internal factors:
c_H=(b!)^(2b). This convention avoids every 0^0 expression.

The support vertices use all their a internal and b cross stubs on
distinct specified neighbors. Thus physical assignments for a fixed
pattern are disjoint: a configuration realizing the distinct prescribed
neighbors determines exactly which physical stubs realize each edge.
No extra parallel copy of a support edge can coexist with all required
neighbors because support stubs are saturated.

For one physical assignment the exact configuration probability is

 P_N^*=prod_(j=0,...,e_int-1)(aN-2j-1)^(-2)
           *[(bN)_(e_cross)]^(-1),                    (L2)

with the internal product omitted at a=0. The two factors are respectively
ordinary internal pairings and a cross-stub bijection; there is no edge
orientation factor. The unconditioned event probability is c_H P_N^*.

5. COMPLETION, PARITY, AND A SIMPLICITY RATIO TENDING TO ONE
-----------------------------------------------------------
After prescribing one assignment, all residual pools remain independent
and uniform. Support vertices have residual degrees zero. Exterior W
vertices, if present, have residual internal degree a-1 and cross degree
b-1; all other vertices retain a,b. Every prescribed edge has a support
endpoint with residual degree zero. A residual edge can therefore NEVER
duplicate a prescribed edge. Full simplicity is EXACTLY residual-factor
simplicity; no additional forbidden-pair correction is present.

For every boundary map and every stub assignment, the residual degree
lists are the same up to labels. Their simplicity probability s_N^*
is therefore exactly the same finite-N quantity for all the leading data.
They differ at only finitely many vertices from regular bulk sequences.

If delta>=0, the residual totals are
 L_a=aN-tD,   L_b=bN-2bt.
Their normalized second factorial moments are
 gamma_a^*=(a-1)(1-bt/L_a) -> a-1,
 gamma_b^*=(b-1)(1-bt/L_b) -> b-1.
The internal total is even since tD=delta(delta+1)+2b(delta+1) is even.

If delta<0 and a>=1, the totals and moments are
 L_a=aN-2as,
 gamma_a^*=(a-1)(1-as/L_a) -> a-1;
 L_b=bN-sD,
 gamma_b^*=(b-1)(1-as/L_b) -> b-1.
The internal total is even whenever aN is even; the two cross totals
agree and need not themselves be even. At a=1 the residual internal
graph is a perfect matching on N-2s vertices and is always simple.

If a=0, internal factors are absent, W is empty, and the residual cross
graph is b-regular on N-b vertices per side. Its second factorial-moment
parameter is exactly b-1. No absent internal parameter is divided by.

The bounded-degree configuration defect calculation gives independent
loop and double-edge limits with means gamma/2 and gamma^2/4 in an
internal factor, and double-edge mean gamma_left gamma_right/2 in a
cross factor. Thus the original and residual product simplicity
probabilities have the SAME positive limit

 s_infty=exp[-1_{a>=1}(a^2-1)/2-(b-1)^2/2].

At a=0 the internal factor is ONE, not exp(1/2). At a=1 it is also one.
The standard bounded-degree factorial-moment proof applies to the finite
degree modifications: disjoint defects give the same limiting moments,
and overlapping prescriptions lose at least one power of N. Hence

 s_N^*/s_N=1+o(1), uniformly over every leading data choice.           (L3)

This is a ratio, not merely a positive lower bound. It also proves
eventual simple completability at every sufficiently large admissible N.
Conditioning yields exactly the independent uniform simple factors,
since each simple internal/cross factor has a constant number of labeled
stub realizations. The degree-zero and degree-one cases obey this too.

6. FIRST MOMENT AND THE TWO EXPLICIT CONSTANTS
---------------------------------------------
For delta>=0, simplify c_H P_N^* using (L2)--(L3). The exterior stub
factor cancels both boundary-degree powers in the denominators, leaving

 Pr_simple(fixed leading data)
  =(1+o(1))(a!b!)^(2t)a^(-t delta) N^(-e_0).

Multiplication by (L1) gives

 EX ~ N^(-nu)
   ((bt)!)^2(a!)^(2t)/[(t!)^2(b!)^(2t)a^(t delta)].      (L4+)

This formula is well-defined at delta=0 because a=b>=2; it reduces to
C=(a!)^2 and nu=2a-2.

For delta<0 and a>=1, internal denominator powers are a^(2as), cross
powers are b^(s^2+2as), and the exterior factor is (ab)^(2as). Thus

 Pr_simple(fixed leading data)
  =(1+o(1))(a!b!)^(2s)b^(-s^2) N^(-e_0).

The same expression with a!=1 holds at a=0 by DIRECT omission of the
empty factors; it is not obtained by manipulating 0^0. With (L1),

 EX ~ N^(-nu)
   ((as)!)^2(b!)^(2s)/[(s!)^2(a!)^(2s)b^(s^2)].        (L4-)

At a=0,s=b this is (b!)^(2b-2)/b^(b^2). A leading occurrence is exactly
an isolated complete b-by-b component; its simplicity completion has
already been included in (L3).

7. FACTORIAL SECOND MOMENT AND ALL MULTIPLE-STATE EVENTS
--------------------------------------------------------
Let X count LEADING FLIP SETS, rather than labeled drawings. Every
leading motif has the same vertex/edge counts for the fixed parameter
case, is connected, and has minimum degree at least two. For any
proper subgraph F with nonempty vertex set of such a motif H,

 e(F)-v(F)<=e(H)-v(H)-1.

Indeed if F spans, an edge was deleted. Otherwise delete r>0 vertices;
connectedness gives B>0 edges to the retained nonempty set and minimum
degree gives 2E_R+B>=2r. At least E_R+B>=r+B/2>r edges are removed,
hence at least r+1. Further deletions only decrease excess.

Support vertices are exactly the motif-degree-D vertices and exterior
vertices have degree two. For a=0 every motif vertex is support. Thus
identical vertex-and-edge images give identical support sets. A realized
leading support set determines one unique boundary data choice. Pairs
with the same flip set are absent from X(X-1), and distinct data for that
same support cannot co-occur.

For a proper nonempty intersection F, include ALL common vertices,
including isolated ones. Its typed union J has

 e(J)-v(J)=2nu-[e(F)-v(F)]>=nu+1.

There are only finitely many overlap types. At specified vertices a
compatible union has probability O(N^(-e(J))) by its finite physical
stub bound and positive simplicity conditioning, while placements number
O(N^(v(J))). Incompatible degree or type identifications contribute zero.
Disjoint unions contribute O(N^(-2nu)), also O(N^(-nu-1)) since nu>=1.
The equality case nu=1 occurs at (a,b)=(1,2) and causes no problem.
At a=0, distinct minimal isolated K_(b,b) components cannot partly overlap,
so only the even smaller disjoint contribution is possible.

Consequently

 E[X(X-1)]=O(N^(-nu-1)),
 Pr(X>=1)=EX+O(N^(-nu-1)),
 Pr(X>=2)=O(N^(-nu-1)).                                (S1)

Outside E_large union E_collision, every alternative unordered partition
is uniquely one leading minimum flip, so K=1+X. Equations (U5), (U6),
(L4+)/(L4-), and (S1) prove (T1). Conditional on ambiguity, K=2 with
probability 1-O(1/N). No independence of disjoint motif occurrences was
assumed, and no statement about real kernel dimension follows from this.

8. UNIFORM HIDDEN PRIOR AND THE BAYES HALF
-----------------------------------------
Given a balanced fixed plant, every compatible labeled graph has the
same likelihood 1/[G_a(N)^2 B_b(N)], and all incompatible graphs have
likelihood zero. The internal/cross decomposition is determined by the
plant and G. These factor counts depend only on N,a,b. In particular
G_0(N)=1 and G_1(N) is the perfect-matching count on admissible N.

Thus a uniform hidden balanced unordered plant gives an EXACTLY uniform
posterior over the K(G) valid unordered partitions. Its optimal conditional
success is 1/K(G), attained by selecting any verified valid partition.
The hidden-model ambiguity probability equals its fixed-plant probability
by vertex-relabeling invariance. There is no additional size-bias factor
two to insert into that probability.

Therefore

 R_N=E[1-1/K(G)],
 0<=R_N-(1/2)Pr(K>=2)<=(1/2)Pr(K>=3)=O(N^(-nu-1)).

Together with (T1) this proves (T2). The known deterministic-plant problem
would admit a constant zero-error estimator, so the hidden-prior condition
is essential. For a=0, connected bipartite components have equal sides
and may be independently reoriented; a single leading small component
and the rest give exactly the same two-state posterior mechanism.

therefore has deterministic polynomial worst-case running time.

On completion, select any verified valid unordered partition, including

probability, which is o(N^(-nu)). Hence it attains (T2)'s leading C/2.

SCOPE
-----
All degrees and the prior are fixed as stated. Random uniform cross
factors are used in the motif and configuration counts; the same exact
constant is not asserted for an adversarial prescribed cross graph.
Nothing here gives the sharp probability of arbitrary extra real
eigenvectors, an analytic eigengap, a growing-degree theorem, or a
historical-priority claim. The degree threshold D>=3 is essential to
the positive all-slot gap and the support-excess bounds.
