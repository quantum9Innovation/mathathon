1. GENERAL-d IMPEDANCE AND TARGET CONSTANT
-----------------------------------------
For q=d-1, Q=q^h and a complete depth-h q-ary tree with equally split
total root current J, the exact access energy is

 J^2 sum_(k=1)^h q^(-k)
       =J^2(1-1/Q)/(q-1)=J^2(1-1/Q)/(d-2).

There are two such top trees. Adding bridge resistance one or one-half
gives the limiting common impedance coefficients

 R_d=1+2/(d-2)=d/(d-2),
 R'_d=1/2+2/(d-2)=(d+2)/(2(d-2)),
 R_d/R'_d=2d/(d+2).

The bottom labeled tree remains binary. Its root, after attachment to
a top-tree leaf, has one parent and two children, of degree three<=d.
All top internal vertices have degree q+1=d; each edited root has q
children plus the bridge, also degree d. Ports still have degree three;
necks and bottom labeled leaves have degree two. Thus the generalization
does not inadvertently exceed the host degree cap.

With c=1/R_d,c'=1/R'_d, f(g)=(c'+g)/(c+g), and
r_i=(2d/(d+2))exp(-is/m), the given formula for g_i is exactly the
inverse of f. For 0<s<log(2d/(d+2)), all r_i>1, g_0=0, the g_i
strictly increase and remain finite, and q_i=g_i-g_(i-1)>0.

The inverse-star matrix K=diag(q)-q q^T/(c+g_m), and the corresponding
K', have the stated trailing determinant ratio
f(g_(i-1))/f(g_m). Successive ratios have logarithm exactly s/m.
Consequently the separated-mass construction allocates m equal limiting
relative correction eigenvalues exp(alpha s/m)-1. The formula reduces
to 3 versus 5/2 and log(6/5) at d=3, as required.

largest k diagonal grades uniquely dominates. If P_k is the product of

5. BIPARTITE CUBIC RESERVOIRS AND THEIR GAP
-----------------------------------------
The proposed bipartite source is justified by the same external theorem.
Take a simple cubic graph on sufficiently large even N with all adjacency
eigenvalues other than 3 in [-3+gamma_0,3-gamma_0]. Such a graph is
connected and nonbipartite. Its bipartite double cover has adjacency
spectrum plus/minus that original spectrum. The cover is simple cubic,
connected, and its positive Laplacian gap is at least gamma_0; the new
adjacency eigenvalue -3 only gives Laplacian eigenvalue six and creates
no additional zero. Its size is 2N, a multiple of four.

Replace Q distinct cover edges by length-three paths. Every selected
edge adds two vertices, which have opposite colors. Choosing one of
them for the external attachment produces a degree-two internal port;
the other subdivision vertex is allowed to retain degree two.

The source's uniform bound gamma_bip=(2+21/gamma_0)^-1 is correct.
For a selected old edge x--y, its old squared difference is at most
three times the three-edge path energy. Thus E_old<=3E and
V_old<=3E/gamma_0. Assign each of the two new vertices to its adjacent
old endpoint. At most three assignments reach any old vertex, so their
variance about the old mean is bounded by 2E+6V_old. The total variance
is at most 2E+7V_old<=(2+21/gamma_0)E. This remains uniform when selected
edges share old endpoints and when Q grows, provided enough distinct
edges are available. All sufficiently large volumes provide them.

The available reservoir sizes are 4k+2Q. For fixed Q and prescribed
positive M_i, these arithmetic progressions approximate n_i(a)/a->M_i;
the parity constraint does not restrict the mass ratios.

6. GLOBAL BIPARTITE COLOR CONSISTENCY
------------------------------------
Both top trees are level-regular, so all anchor attachments have the
same depth. For a fixed bottom label i, every repeated copy has the
same depth to that leaf and uses the same neck length ell_i. Hence
ALL Q attachments of a single target reservoir require the same global
port color. On each chosen cover edge, the two subdivision vertices
offer exactly the two choices of color, so the required one can always
be selected. Choosing colors consistently at the edited roots makes
the bridge legal as well. This gives a global bipartition despite the
many cycles through reservoirs. New ports are distinct, each has one
external edge, and no multiedge or loop is introduced.

Alternatively adjusting ell_i to a specified parity changes it by at
most one and ell_i/Q by O(1/Q); this also preserves the impedance limit.
Thus no hidden parity or single-port obstruction remains.
