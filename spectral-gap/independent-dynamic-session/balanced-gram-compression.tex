\paragraph{Scope and result.}
This is a complete compression theorem conditional on a supplied raw
factor $Z$.  Its Gram computation uses only shifted Laplacian solves,
not fractional-power vector actions or fractional Gaussian samples.
It charges all matrix products required to construct the final factor.
The two dimensionless moment bounds stated below are the essential
input from the raw construction; a small factor width alone does not
give them.  The final accuracy is two-sided unless the raw factor
already satisfies a one-sided inequality.

All matrix functions of a connected Laplacian $L$ act on
$\mathcal H=\mathbf1^\perp$; inverse powers are zero on the kernel.
Assume the columns of $Z\in\R^{n\times q}$ belong to $\mathcal H$.
Set
\[
 a_0=\|Z\|_F^2,\qquad
 a_1=\tr(Z^TL^\dagger Z),\qquad
 H=Z^T(L^\dagger)^\alpha Z,\qquad
 c_\alpha=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}\pi,
 \quad\beta=1-\alpha.
\]
For $Z=0$ the conclusions are immediate, so assume $a_0,a_1>0$.

\begin{theorem}[Gap-independent construction of the small Gram matrix]
Fix $0<\alpha<1$ and $0<\gamma<1$.  Suppose a scale $d>0$ and an
explicit upper bound $P\ge1$ are available such that
\[
 a_0d^{-\alpha}\le P,\qquad a_1d^{1-\alpha}\le P.
\]
There is a logarithmic quadrature with
\[
 N=O_\alpha\bigl(\log^2(2P/\gamma)\bigr)
\]
positive nodes and weights that constructs a symmetric
$\widehat H$ satisfying
$\|\widehat H-H\|\le\gamma$.
It uses $qN$ solves in $L+sI$, each to relative energy error
\[
 \eta=\min\left\{1,\frac{\gamma\alpha\beta}{8c_\alpha P}\right\}.
\]
The non-solver cost for the Gram matrix is $O(Nnq^2)$ arithmetic,
with $O(nq+q^2)$ storage when nodes are processed sequentially.
If the solves all succeed with probability at least $1-\delta$,
the Gram guarantee has the same probability.
No node count or solve tolerance depends on a graph spectral endpoint.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The scalar Stieltjes formula and spectral calculus give
\[
 H=c_\alpha\int_0^\infty
       s^{-\alpha}Z^T(L+sI)^{-1}Z\,ds.
\]
The kernel causes no term because $Z^T\mathbf1=0$.
The trace inequalities
\[
 0\preceq Z^T(L+sI)^{-1}Z,
 \qquad
 \tr Z^T(L+sI)^{-1}Z\le\min(a_1,a_0/s)
\]
imply the tail bounds
\[
 \left\|c_\alpha\int_0^{T_-}\cdots ds\right\|
       \le\frac{c_\alpha a_1}{\beta}T_-^\beta,
 \qquad
 \left\|c_\alpha\int_{T_+}^{\infty}\cdots ds\right\|
       \le\frac{c_\alpha a_0}{\alpha}T_+^{-\alpha}.
\]
In particular, the following choices control each tail by $\gamma/4$:
\[
 T_-=d\min\left\{1,
       \left(\frac{\gamma\beta}{4c_\alpha P}\right)^{1/\beta}\right\},
 \qquad
 T_+=d\max\left\{1,
       \left(\frac{4c_\alpha P}{\alpha\gamma}\right)^{1/\alpha}\right\}.
\]

For completeness we control quadrature as an operator, rather than
bounding its entries separately.  With $s=de^y$, define
\[
 K(y)=c_\alpha (de^y)^\beta
                Z^T(L+de^yI)^{-1}Z.
\]
This is analytic for $|\operatorname{Im}y|<\pi$.
For $|\theta|\le w<\pi$ and $t>0$,
\[
 |\lambda+te^{i\theta}|\ge\cos(w/2)(\lambda+t),
 \qquad \lambda\ge0.
\]
Expanding the resolvent in a real orthonormal eigenbasis therefore gives
\[
 \|K(x+i\theta)\|
 \le c_\alpha\sec(w/2)
       \min\{a_1(de^x)^\beta,a_0(de^x)^{-\alpha}\}.
\]
Put $A_*=a_0^\beta a_1^\alpha$.  Integrating the envelope, split at
$de^x=a_0/a_1$, yields
\[
 \int_{\R}\|K(x\pm iw)\|\,dx
 \le M:=\frac{c_\alpha\sec(w/2)}{\alpha\beta}A_*
 \le \overline M:=\frac{c_\alpha\sec(w/2)}{\alpha\beta}P.
\]
The last inequality follows from
$A_*=(a_0d^{-\alpha})^\beta(a_1d^\beta)^\alpha\le P$.

Shifting the Fourier integral to a strip boundary gives
$\|\widehat K(\xi)\|\le M e^{-w|\xi|}$.
Poisson summation, justified by these exponential envelopes, then gives
\[
 \left\|\int_\R K(x)\,dx-h\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}K(jh)\right\|
 \le\frac{2M}{e^{2\pi w/h}-1}.
\]
Take $w=\pi/2$ and
\[
 h=\min\left\{1,
       \frac{2\pi w}{\log(1+8\overline M/\gamma)}\right\}.
\]
The infinite trapezoid error is at most $\gamma/4$.
Retain indices
\[
 j_- =\left\lfloor\frac{\log(T_-/d)}h\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 j_+ =\left\lceil\frac{\log(T_+/d)}h\right\rceil,
\]
with nodes $s_j=de^{jh}$ and weights
$\omega_j=c_\alpha h s_j^\beta$.
The omitted discrete tails have the same bounds as above because
\[
 h\sum_{j<j_-}s_j^\beta
   =\frac{h s_{j_-}^\beta}{e^{\beta h}-1}
   \le\frac{T_-^\beta}{\beta},
 \qquad
 h\sum_{j>j_+}s_j^{-\alpha}
   =\frac{h s_{j_+}^{-\alpha}}{e^{\alpha h}-1}
   \le\frac{T_+^{-\alpha}}\alpha.
\]
These contribute at most $\gamma/2$ together.  The number of retained
indices is $N=O_\alpha(\log^2(2P/\gamma))$; the absolute scale $d$
cancels from both cutoff ratios.

Now solve $A_jX_j=Z$ with $A_j=L+s_jI$.
If every column of $\widehat X_j$ has relative $A_j$-energy error
at most $\eta$, put $E_j=\widehat X_j-X_j$ and
$t_j=\tr Z^TA_j^{-1}Z$.  Summing the squared column guarantees gives
\[
 \|A_j^{1/2}E_j\|_F\le\eta\sqrt{t_j},
 \qquad
 \|Z^TE_j\|\le
      \|A_j^{-1/2}Z\|_F\|A_j^{1/2}E_j\|_F
      \le\eta t_j.
\]
Thus symmetrizing each computed $Z^T\widehat X_j$ does not increase
its error to the exact symmetric block.

The total weighted trace is bounded without a factor $N$.
Splitting the infinite logarithmic sum at $s_*=a_0/a_1$, and using
$h\le1$, gives
\[
 \sum_j\omega_j t_j
 \le c_\alpha A_*\left(
       \frac{h}{1-e^{-\beta h}}+
       \frac{h}{1-e^{-\alpha h}}\right)
 \le\frac{2c_\alpha A_*}{\alpha\beta}
 \le\frac{2c_\alpha P}{\alpha\beta}.
\]
Here $x/(1-e^{-x})\le1+x\le2$ for $0<x\le1$.
The accumulated solve error is consequently at most $\gamma/4$ for
the stated $\eta$.  Together with the three quadrature errors this
gives $\|\widehat H-H\|\le\gamma$.

There are $q$ right-hand sides per node.  Forming each small Gram
$Z^T\widehat X_j$ costs $O(nq^2)$, which proves the charged cost.
For randomized solvers, give each column solve failure probability
$\delta/(qN)$ and use a union bound, with the standard logarithmic
confidence overhead.  No assumption about linearity of the approximate
solve map was used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rank compression with an explicit output factor]
Let $L'=L+uu^T$ with $u\in\mathcal H$, and write
\[
 \Delta=L'^\alpha-L^\alpha,\quad
 C=(L^\dagger)^{\alpha/2}\Delta(L^\dagger)^{\alpha/2},\quad
 B=\alpha\log(1+u^TL^\dagger u).
\]
Suppose the raw factor satisfies
\[
 \left\|(L^\dagger)^{\alpha/2}ZZ^T(L^\dagger)^{\alpha/2}-C\right\|
       \le\varepsilon/8,
 \qquad 0<\varepsilon<1.
\]
Construct $\widehat H$ as above with $\gamma=\varepsilon/8$.
Let $V$ have orthonormal columns spanning the eigenspaces of
$\widehat H$ with eigenvalues strictly greater than $\varepsilon/2$.
The explicit returned factor is $X=ZV$, and $F=XX^T$.  Then
\[
 -\frac\varepsilon8 L^\alpha
       \preceq\Delta-F\preceq\frac{3\varepsilon}4L^\alpha
       \quad\hbox{on }\mathcal H,
\]
and its column count $r$ satisfies the strict log-determinant bound
\[
 r<\frac{B}{\log(1+\varepsilon/4)}
 \quad\text{if }r>0,
 \qquad
 r\le\max\left\{0,
       \left\lceil\frac{B}{\log(1+\varepsilon/4)}\right\rceil-1\right\}.
\]
The additional dense arithmetic is $O(q^3+nqr)$ in the usual small
symmetric-eigendecomposition model; thus the complete compression
cost, including the Gram and returned factors, is
\[
 qN\,T_{\rm SDD}(m+n,\eta,\delta/(qN))
       +O(Nnq^2+q^3+nqr).
\]
If the raw inequality additionally satisfies $ZZ^T\preceq\Delta$,
the lower residual bound improves to $0\preceq\Delta-F$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $G=(L^\dagger)^{\alpha/2}Z$, so $H=G^TG$, and let $P_V=VV^T$.
The discarded subspace of $\widehat H$ has quadratic form at most
$\varepsilon/2$.  Hence
\[
 \|(I-P_V)H(I-P_V)\|
       \le\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/8=5\varepsilon/8.
\]
The nonzero spectra of $G(I-P_V)G^T$ and
$(I-P_V)H(I-P_V)$ agree, so
\[
 0\preceq GG^T-GP_VG^T\preceq(5\varepsilon/8)I.
\]
Combining this with $-(\varepsilon/8)I\preceq C-GG^T
\preceq(\varepsilon/8)I$ proves the stated two-sided residual after
congruence by $L^{\alpha/2}$.

On the $r$-dimensional selected subspace,
$V^THV\succ(3\varepsilon/8)I$.
By the min--max principle, $H$ and therefore $GG^T$ have at least
$r$ eigenvalues strictly above $3\varepsilon/8$.
Weyl's perturbation bound transfers these to $r$ eigenvalues of $C$
strictly above $\varepsilon/4$.  Finally
\[
 B=\log\det(I+C)>r\log(1+\varepsilon/4)
\]
when $r>0$.  This proves the strict integer count, including the
case where the displayed ratio is an integer.
The factor multiplication costs $O(nqr)$ and is included explicitly.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Precision and interpretation.}
The guarantees do not require a gap near the cutoff.  A backward-stable
small-matrix eigensolve may be used by budgeting its symmetric backward
error inside $\gamma$ (for example, use Gram quadrature error
$\varepsilon/16$ and small-eigensolve backward error
$\varepsilon/16$).  Its tolerance depends on $q$, $P$, and
$\varepsilon$, rather than a graph spectral gap.  If numerical dense
spectral arithmetic is charged beyond the standard $O(q^3)$ model,
its usual accuracy logarithms must also be included.

For $P=\operatorname{poly}(n,1+\tau,1/\varepsilon)$ and a raw width
$q=\operatorname{polylog}(n,1+\tau,1/\varepsilon)$, all displayed
non-solver work is $(m+n)$ times accuracy and dimension logarithms,
and the SDD tolerances have only such logarithms.  The assertion about
$q$ is an input from the graph raw-factor construction, not a consequence
of the general matrix log-determinant budget.  Nearly-linear total
construction still requires adding the raw-factor construction cost.
This theorem closes the Gram/compression step under those charged
assumptions; it makes no independent claim that they hold for a proposed
raw construction.
