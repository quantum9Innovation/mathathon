1. EXACT TARGET PIVOTS
---------------------
For fixed m>=2 and 0<s<log(6/5), put c=1/3,c'=2/5,
f(g)=(c'+g)/(c+g), r_i=(6/5) exp(-i s/m),
g_i=(c'-r_i c)/(r_i-1), and q_i=g_i-g_(i-1).
The function f strictly decreases from 6/5 to one. Thus g_0=0,
0<g_1<...<g_m<infinity, and every q_i>0.

With T=diag(1/q_i), K=(T+3J)^-1 and K'=(T+(5/2)J)^-1 are SPD,
and K'-K is positive rank one. Writing g_m=sum q_i, Woodbury gives

 K=diag(q)-q q^T/(c+g_m),
 det K_(i:m,i:m)=(prod_(j>=i)q_j)(c+g_(i-1))/(c+g_m).

Consequently the ratio of the two trailing determinants is
f(g_(i-1))/f(g_m), and the ith reverse Schur pivot ratio has logarithm
log f(g_(i-1))-log f(g_i)=s/m. This checks exactly, with no reversal
of the pivot order. The separated-mass fractional limit therefore
produces m relative correction eigenvalues tending to exp(alpha s/m)-1.

2. DEGREE AND IDEAL IMPEDANCE
----------------------------
Let Q=2^h. A complete binary tree of depth h feeds Q identical rooted
full binary trees B with m labeled leaves each. Identify each B root
with its feeding tree's leaf. A labeled leaf i reaches reservoir i
through a neck of ell_i unit edges; every copy reaches a DISTINCT
port in the SAME reservoir i. The anchor side has its own depth-h
binary tree, with its Q leaves identified with Q distinct anchor ports.
The top roots are joined by the unit edited bridge.

All degrees are at most three: the two bridge roots have two children;
each B root has one parent and two children; each labeled leaf has one
parent and one neck; each reservoir port has internal degree two and
one attachment. Identifying left leaves with ports adds no extra edge.
The bridge is indeed a bridge, since removing it separates the anchor
side from every target side. Multiple ports in one reservoir create
cycles on that side but no alternative crossing of the edited bridge.

For arbitrary SIGNED terminal currents j_i, write J=sum j_i. With
ideal zero-resistance reservoirs, copy symmetry forces each B copy
to carry j_i/Q at label i. Each top tree contributes (1-1/Q)J^2.
The B-copy contribution is j^T R_B j/Q, and the neck contribution is
sum ell_i j_i^2/Q. Adding bridge resistance one or one-half gives

 Z_Q=diag(ell_i/Q)+(3-2/Q)J_matrix+R_B/Q,
 Z'_Q=diag(ell_i/Q)+(5/2-2/Q)J_matrix+R_B/Q.

Here J_matrix=11^T, distinct from scalar current J. Taking positive
integer ell_i with ell_i/Q->1/q_i gives precisely the target limits.
This is a quadratic-form identity for every signed j, hence a matrix
identity; checking only positive unit demands would have been weaker.

3. UNIFORM RESERVOIR GAP AND CONTACT ERROR
----------------------------------------
Subdividing Q distinct old edges once adds Q degree-two ports. If the
old cubic reservoir has gap gamma_0, its old energy is at most twice
the subdivided energy E. Let mu be the old-vertex mean and V_old its
variance. Then V_old<=2E/gamma_0. Assign each port to one endpoint
of its old edge. At most three ports are assigned to any old vertex,
so the source's estimate

 sum_ports(x_p-mu)^2<=2E+6V_old

is correct even when subdivided edges share old endpoints. Adding
V_old and minimizing over the new mean gives gap at least
gamma=(2+14/gamma_0)^-1. This bound is independent of Q and volume.
For fixed Q, old even reservoir sizes plus Q approximate arbitrary
positive mass ratios. Choosing Q distinct edges is possible at all
sufficiently large old sizes.

For ports represented by coordinate columns P, H=P^T L_res^dagger P
satisfies 0<=H<=gamma^-1 I. A port current vector a, balanced by
uniform mean current -sum(a)/n on the reservoir, has minimum internal
energy exactly a^T H a. This is Thomson's principle applied to
P a-(sum a)/n*1; L_res^dagger kills the constant term.

Dropping all internal contact energies gives the ideal impedance as a
lower bound on the true impedance. For an upper bound, use the ideal
core flow and equal port currents j_i/Q and -J/Q, then choose minimum
internal reservoir flows for those demands. Its extra energy is at most

 (R/Q)(sum j_i^2+J^2), R=gamma^-1.

Thus the claimed Loewner bounds hold uniformly over every contact tuple
0<=H_i<=R I. The circuit and contacts are fixed-dimensional after Q
is fixed. Their terminal matrices form a compact family close to K,K'.
Since K,K' are SPD, all relevant grounded pivot determinants remain
positive, and continuity gives the uniform pivot lower bounds in the
source before any masses are selected.

An additional useful identity is EXACT for true as well as ideal
contacts:

        Z'_true(H)=Z_true(H)-(1/2)11^T.

Every feasible terminal flow carries exactly total current J through
the bridge. Changing only its resistance subtracts J^2/2 from every
feasible flow's objective; all other minimization terms are unchanged.
This confirms positivity and rank one of the resulting stiffness
update without needing a separate delicate elimination argument.

4. DIRECT RIGOROUS RESERVOIR-VOLUME LIMIT
---------------------------------------
Fix m,Q,the entire finite core, the separated terminal masses, and
the finite anchor mass in that order. Let reservoir volumes n_i(u)
satisfy n_i(u)/u->M_i>0. Let P_u be the isometry of normalized
reservoir indicators, zero on the core. In P_u and its orthogonal
complement the host Laplacian has blocks

             L_u = [ A_u  B_u ; B_u^T  F_u ].

There are fixed-dimensional A_u=O(1/u), B_u=O(u^-1/2), while
F_u>=eta_0 I with eta_0>0 independent of u. The latter bound is
valid: internal reservoir gaps bound every mean-zero reservoir norm;
the fixed core's Dirichlet energy with reservoir pins zero bounds
its norm, after bounding the finitely many pin deviations by those
mean-zero norms. Host and updated endpoint operator norms are uniformly
bounded by six and eight. The static Schur matrix

       S_u=A_u-B_u F_u^-1 B_u^T

is exactly the true terminal mean-coordinate stiffness normalized
by diag(n_i(u)^-1/2). This is also obtained by dualizing the uniform
terminal-demand/contact-energy calculation in Section3.

Extract any contact-convergent subsequence H_i(u)->H_i*. Compactness
is available since Q is now fixed. Along it,

           u S_u -> H_eff=M^-1/2 K_full(H*)M^-1/2,

and similarly for the updated pair. K_full is the full mean stiffness
before grounding the anchor. It is PSD of rank m with kernel constants;
grounding its anchor gives the SPD K_Q(H) used previously.

For every fixed t>0, an exact Schur inverse gives

 P_u^T(uL_u+tI)^-1P_u
  =[uA_u+tI-u B_u(F_u+t/u I)^-1 B_u^T]^-1.

Its denominator differs from u S_u+tI by

       u B_u[F_u^-1-(F_u+t/u I)^-1]B_u^T,

whose norm is O(t/u), because ||B_u||^2=O(1/u) and the bracket is
O(t/u) with a uniform inverse bound eta_0^-1. Therefore the compressed
resolvent tends to (H_eff+tI)^-1.

Moreover ||P_u^T(uL_u)P_u||=||u A_u|| is uniformly bounded. The
compression inverse inequality yields the common integrable dominator

 0<=P_u^T(uL_u)(uL_u+tI)^-1P_u<=min(1,M/t)I.

Integrating against (sin(pi alpha)/pi)t^(alpha-1) dt, valid for
0<alpha<1, proves directly

          P_u^T(uL_u)^alpha P_u -> H_eff^alpha,

and likewise for the updated graph. This is the compressed-power lemma
already independently proved in unweighted-fractional-response.tex;
the displayed proof shows why its hypotheses hold here, even though
the contact matrices may not converge along the original full sequence.

Choose V_u with range perpendicular to (sqrt(n_i(u))) and converging,
in macro coordinates, to V with range perpendicular to (sqrt(M_i)).
Then every P_u V_u vector is orthogonal to the PHYSICAL graph constant
vector: it is zero on the core and its reservoir sums cancel exactly.
On this m-dimensional subspace, the compressed fractional pair tends
to the positive-definite effective fractional pair on its common
positive support. Its m generalized Ritz values therefore converge
to the mass-pair relative values. Generalized min--max bounds them
above by the full graph's first m relative eigenvalues. Thus all m
strict lower witnesses survive in the actual finite host.

This proof needs no assertion that the high response disappears.
Indeed it should NOT disappear: the local high response consumes the
complementary logarithmic budget. The volume argument transfers only
the desired low relative eigenvalues by a rigorous Ritz lower bound.

5. LIMIT ORDER AND COEFFICIENT
-----------------------------
For fixed m and s<log(6/5), first choose Q to control ALL contact
tuples; next choose separated-mass parameter delta uniformly on that
compact family; next choose a sufficiently large finite anchor mass;
last choose reservoir volumes u. The mass pair has m eigenvalues as
close from below as desired to exp(alpha s/m)-1, allowing the small
source eta allocation. Every approximation parameter is finite.

Uniformity in the last step follows by contradiction: any bad volume
sequence has a contact-convergent subsequence, to which the preceding
compressed-resolvent proof applies. All the prescribed mass-pair lower
inequalities hold uniformly over that compact contact family by the
earlier choices. This rules out circular tuning of contacts and masses.

For each small epsilon, choose an integer m strictly below
alpha(s-eta)/log(1+epsilon), with m log(1+epsilon)->alpha(s-eta).
The desired threshold margin is strictly positive, so perform every
finite approximation inside it. Hence liminf epsilon W>=alpha(s-eta).
Let eta->0 and s->log(6/5) to obtain alpha log(6/5).

Graph size, neck lengths and mass ratios may grow arbitrarily fast;
the lower bound is existential. It does not claim an efficient
extremal-example generator, finite-precision robustness, or a uniform
finite-size convergence rate.

6. ADDITIONAL SCOPE NOTE
-----------------------
As written, subdividing one edge once need not preserve bipartiteness
of a bipartite reservoir. The sharpness source claims no bipartite
restriction, so this is not an error. If bipartite hosts are desired,
use bipartite cubic expanders and subdivide each chosen edge by TWO
vertices. The two new degree-two vertices offer opposite port colors;
choose one for the single external attachment. The unused subdivision
vertex may retain degree two, which is permitted. The standard
variance/gap proof changes only constants, and integer neck lengths
may be adjusted by one to satisfy parity while ell_i/Q has the same
limit. This would require a separately written parity/gap check before
being added as a theorem, rather than silently strengthening the source.
