Verdict
  No fatal mathematical error found in the stated exact-real-arithmetic
  theorem. The new noncommuting refinement, strong transfer, carried lift,
  and unfolded schedule are consistent. This is a mathematical review;
  the precise coverage of the Lean development is recorded separately in
  formal/VERIFICATION.txt. It must not be read as a claim that every
  algorithmic and probabilistic dependency has been checked by Lean.

  The arithmetic model is essential: arbitrary exact real operations,
  positive scalar square roots, exact comparisons, Gaussian sampling,
  and unbounded representations. This result does not establish the same
  cost in machine arithmetic, nor a stable implementable numerical method.

Proof checks

1. Support and fixed target pencil (paper Section 2)
  The companion's reduction preserves the PRODUCT support. It does not
  assert A=AWLc. On the simultaneous sketch event, M=Z Lc B,
  G=Z^T Z is positive definite and near identity, and Q0 approximates Q.
  Off-event the computed dimension cannot exceed rank(M), and Z remains
  injective. The full-support branch therefore handles exact zero tails
  before a positive eigenvalue bracket is needed.

  The same even Neumann polynomial Bm is used at every level/threshold.
  Its relative error theta/10^9 is small enough independently of depth.
  Congruence/min--max gives the stated comparison between H and Hhat;
  H, rather than Hhat, has the exact physical squared singular values.

2. Fine proxy (Section 3)
  Inverse Loewner comparison, the coarse whitening bounds, and
  theta<=h_L<=h_j imply -8h_j D <= K_j-K <= 8h_j D.
  Fine Neumann actions use the sketched factors and existing support
  maps. Identity sketches are correctly included as true-factor actions.
  No fine Gram or new support reduction is needed.

3. Relaxed refinement (Lemma 5)
  Set epsilon=h/H<=1/1024 and P=(1+epsilon)Fprev.
  The weighted error bound is E Fprev^-1 E <= 8h^2 D/H.
  Young's inequality yields the claimed kappa_P<=6. For example an
  explicit coefficient for C_P is
    (1+epsilon)C + 8*kappa*epsilon*(1+epsilon)^2,
  which is at most 2(C+1) for C>=1 and kappa<=5.

  For eta=8epsilon, f_eta(x)=sqrt(x^2+eta^2)+eta obeys
    f_eta(x)-x^2/f_eta(x)=2eta.
  Its scalar bounds produce Fstar>=128hD, Fstar+/-K>=56hD,
  and Fstar<=(5/4)P. The inverse-order comparison needed here is
    P^-1 <= (5/4) Fstar^-1.
  It gives
    Fstar-2K_j Fstar^-1 K_j <=16 C_P hD.
  Replacing K_j=K+E with parameter-one Young gives the coefficient four
  and error (16C_P+2)hD. Relative polynomial error then gives coefficient
  five and Cnext=100(C+1), with the stated floor and margins.
  None of these comparisons assumes that D, E, K or successive factors
  commute. The ordinary defect need not be small or nonnegative after
  changing its coefficient to five.

4. Finite polynomial (Lemma 4)
  The ellipse with parameter 1+eta/4 has imaginary semiaxis <=eta/4;
  hence Re(z^2+eta^2)>=15eta^2/16. This keeps both analytic square-root
  branches away from their cuts. The Chebyshev tail/alias bound gives
  degree O(eta^-1 log(1/(tau eta))). Dyadic nodes and the real-pair DCT
  can be constructed with the allowed scalar square roots and field
  operations. This is a finite construction, not a computed matrix root.

5. Threshold and physical transfer (Lemma 6)
  For beta=alpha/(1-kappa alpha^2), the crucial scalar inequality has
  exact factorization
    alpha(1-kappa c^2)-c(1-kappa alpha^2)
      =(alpha-c)(1+kappa alpha c)>=0
  on -1<=c<=alpha under the stated small-alpha condition.
  It justifies the bottom-j subspace comparison and near-threshold bound.

  Writing q=y^T K F^-1 K y>=0 and b=(1+3alpha)^2-1,
    2y^T K y >=q-b y^T F y
                >=(1-kappa b)q-b C h y^T D y
                >=-b C h y^T D y.
  This explains why the relaxed defect is sufficient and where the
  essential extra h in the final error enters.
  The physical complement is the complement of range(Z R^-T U).
  Its Rayleigh quotient gives precisely equation (13), with a positive
  denominator. The threshold/mass/physical error budgets fit comfortably
  below (1+theta)^2. Zero eigenvalues and repeated values are allowed.

6. Exact carried lift (Section 6)
  With RL=R0 S, T=I-S^T A0 S and V0=I-A0 S S^T,
    T S^T=S^T V0,
    phi(T)S^T Xi=S^T phi(V0)Xi,
    RL^-T phi(T)S^T Xi=R0^-T phi(V0)Xi.
  These identities hold for every polynomial and arbitrary noncommuting
  factors. R0^-T=J0, so the executed physical columns have the exact dual
  span required by Lemma 6. The correlated Gaussian head and tail need
  no independence: separate least-singular-value and Frobenius estimates
  followed by a union bound suffice. Nonnormal recurrence growth is a
  precision issue outside the operation-count theorem.

7. Cost and probability (Sections 7 and 8)
  The ratios in the action recurrence telescope. The scalar square-root
  schedule has terminal h_L in [sqrt(rho),1024sqrt(rho)), and
    h_j^2=h_(j-1) h0 sqrt(rho).
  Consequently true-factor work is <=32N^2 before common overhead;
  each fine term is <=N^2/h0. The overhead is
    exp(O((log log m)^2))=m^o(1),
  including all level multipliers. The multiplication overhead chi(m)
  occurs once per leaf, rather than being exponentiated by the depth.
  The raw outer width is m^(1/2+d+o(1))<m. Internal recurrences do not
  enlarge it. Output tiling uses the same fixed kernel at rank<=sqrt(m).
  Core slack can be chosen because d>1/8.

  Fresh conditional query/filter failure budgets and the joint embedding
  event give the claimed confidence. Bad-event execution uses only forward
  fine polynomials, with scalar/rank checks and capped degrees, so the
  stated outcome caps do not require failed fine factors to be invertible.

8. Corollaries and comparator
  The optimized parameters satisfy 5d*(1+d*)=1 and alpha*=1/(1+d*).
  Both comparison gaps equal s*=(7-3sqrt(5))/8=0.0364745084375788...
  The adaptive stopping argument also covers rank zero and checks full
  support before requesting quantile r+1. Its small-rank runtime is on
  the same high-probability event as its accuracy/width guarantee.
  The Appendix A LDL congruence has the correct dual orientation:
  range(Z S^-T L Eminus). It realizes the Gram comparator without an
  SVD or ordered-eigenvalue oracle. The two-level rational exponents are
  consistent with the cited rectangular multiplication formula.

Practical examples and significance

  1. Feature and cross-feature kernels. For X,Y in R^(N by m), let
     A=X and B=Y^T. This approximates the interaction/kernel matrix
     XY^T while reading the feature factors. Y=X gives the PSD Gram case.
     The general theorem is strongest for arbitrary nonsymmetric products;
     specialized PSD algorithms require a separate comparison.

  2. Compression of a composed linear map. If a model has consecutive
     dense linear layers B:R^N->R^m and A:R^m->R^N, the output factors
     give a rank-r replacement. For every input x,
       ||(AB-C)x||_2 <=(1+theta)sigma_(r+1)(AB)||x||_2.
     This controls worst-direction distortion. It does not imply a bound
     on prediction accuracy, training loss or principal-vector angles.

  3. Low-rank approximation of a factored covariance or operator. The
     guarantee remains meaningful under cancellation/ill-conditioned
     factor representations, because the error is measured against the
     tail of AB. No factor-energy or actual spectral-gap assumption is
     introduced. Numerical stability under that conditioning remains open.

  The table uses the optimized power law m=N^alpha* without integer
  rounding. 'Power ratio' is N^s*, comparing leading powers only. It
  excludes constants and subpolynomial factors and is NOT a measured
  speedup. Dense-output memory assumes eight bytes per entry, decimal GB.

        N        m (approx.)   floor sqrt(m)   theta      Power ratio   Output GB
     10^4          2,609             51       0.03476        1.40          0.8
     10^6        133,233            365       0.006479       1.66      8,000
     10^9     48,631,503          6,973       0.0005215      2.13   8,000,000,000

  More decisively, the displayed schedule accepts its first refinement
  only if sqrt(h0*h0*sqrt(rho))<=h0/1024, equivalently
    rho<=1024^-4=2^-40, or N/m>=2^40.
  At m=N^alpha*, this requires log10(N)>=82.5316114..., so its
  literal hierarchy does not activate at ordinary data sizes. Below
  that cutoff the theorem uses the companion fallback; its asymptotic
  statement supplies no useful finite-size speed guarantee for that branch.
  Constants such as A_L=10^6(C_L+1) further enlarge the proved degrees.

  The significance is an output-optimal leading exponent (N^(2+o(1)))
  for growing rank and vanishing relative excess, improving the two
  checked N^(2.0364745...+o(1)) upper bounds. The improvement is not
  literal quadratic time, a lower-bound separation, or practical speed
  evidence. If only low-rank factors are needed, the N^2 dense output is
  unnecessary, and this particular total-output comparison becomes less
  relevant. A stable implementation and wall-clock experiments are needed
  before recommending it in an application.

  For a concrete compression interpretation at N=10^4, m approximately
  2609 and r=51, suppose sigma_52(AB)=0.01. The guarantee is residual
  <=0.010347552..., so the same number bounds output error for every
  unit input vector. The two output factors store about 2Nr=1,020,000
  entries (8.16 MB at eight bytes each), while the explicit dense matrix
  stores 100,000,000 entries (800 MB). This illustrates why applications
  often prefer to retain factors. It is a guarantee/example calculation,
  not evidence that this algorithm runs efficiently at those dimensions.
