VERDICT
-------
PASS for the theorem as actually stated: fixed a>=0, b>=1, D=a+b>=3,
independent uniform simple internal/cross factors, all sufficiently large
admissible block sizes N, and the uniform hidden balanced unordered prior.
The two leading constants, ambiguity exponent, lower-order multiple-state
bound, Bayes factor one-half, and capped polynomial estimator all follow.
No defect was found in the a=0, a=1, delta=0, or exponent-one corners.

This verdict does not assert the same asymptotic after imposing graph
connectedness, for adversarial cross factors, for growing degrees, or for
extra arbitrary real eigenvectors. It is a Boolean partition theorem.

1. MINIMUM FLIPS AND WHAT IS COUNTED
-----------------------------------
Let delta=a-b. Since A1=D1 and D-delta=2b>0, an integer sign vector
satisfying A tau=delta tau is automatically balanced. Relative to the
plant it flips equally many k vertices in each old block. Global sign
identification permits k<=N/2. The difference w=(sigma-tau)/2 has entries
+1 on S_+, -1 on S_-, and zero elsewhere, and Aw=delta w.

For delta>=0, the support equation at a plus vertex gives
 delta=i-c<=k-1,
so k>=t=delta+1. Equality forces i=t-1 and c=0 at every support vertex:
two internal K_t cores and no support-cross edge. Each support vertex
has b internal and b cross edges to the exterior. The argument remains
valid when delta=0 and the two cores are singletons.

For delta=-s<0, the same equation gives -s=i-c>=-k. Thus k>=s, and
equality forces i=0,c=s: the support core is K_(s,s), with a internal
and a cross exterior edges at each support vertex.

These are simplicity arguments. The proof correctly postpones applying
them until after conditioning the configuration factors to be simple.
For fixed minimum m=t or s and N>2m, a minimum alternative unordered
partition has one orientation with k=m; its other orientation has N-m.
Consequently there is no extra factor two in the leading count.

2. THE LARGE-SUPPORT REMAINDER IS UNIFORM IN ALL PARAMETER CORNERS
----------------------------------------------------------------
At every zero coordinate of w the contributions from its nonzero
neighbors must cancel. In particular, no exterior vertex has exactly one
edge into the support. For a fixed h-vertex support in the typed
configuration, expose its incident edges. If there are e internal support
edges and B boundary edges, at most B/2 exterior vertices are touched.
At least e+B/2=Dh/2 partner choices therefore collide with the support or
previously touched exterior vertices. For h<=epsilon N, every active
typed pool retains Theta(N) stubs, so each collision has conditional
probability O(h/N). Padding by dummy noncollisions and taking a conditional
index-set union bound is legitimate even though the actual number of
exposures is adaptive. It gives
 [A(h/N)^((D-2)/2)]^h
after summing over supports. Zero-degree pools are omitted; degree-one
pools do not alter the argument.

The consecutive-ratio estimate makes the sum from h=2m+2 to epsilon N
 O(N^(-(D-2)(m+1)))=O(N^(-nu-(D-2))).
This is O(N^(-nu-1)) for every allowed D, including D=3.

For macroscopic supports the actual source uses the new all-slot identity,
not an argument that assumes an internal degree at least two. I checked
the normalization and the required symmetrization. A star law may first
be averaged over permutations WITHIN each old neighbor type: root and
pooled edge laws stay fixed, star entropy increases, and the integer
neighbor-sum constraint stays valid. The bound for the symmetrized exponent
therefore bounds the original exponent. No unproved empirical slot
exchangeability is used.

With alpha_+=alpha_-=1/2, omega_ii=a/4, omega_+-=b/2, its normalized rate is
 -Phi=(D-2)/D sum omega_ij I_ij
       +(1/D)sum_i alpha_i sum_t J_it.
The internal half-count is correct. For each omitted physical slot the
product comparison has KL divergence J_it+sum_(u!=t)I_iu. Summing at a
fixed old type costs at most 2D(D-1)(-Phi)/(alpha_i(D-2)). If V_t is the
variance of the omitted one-hot label and B_i>=0 the common root term,
the summed product-minus-actual square expectations equal
 (D-2)sum_t V_t+D B_i.
The squared functions have range at most 2D^2. Pinsker followed by
Cauchy--Schwarz gives the stated constant
 c_D=(D-2)^3/[4D^6(D-1)].
For an oriented balanced flip fraction r=k/N<=1/2, the defect from the
nearest old-block point map is r. Multiplication by total size 2N gives
the unnormalized bound -c_D N r^2. Thus the stated macroscopic error is
exponentially small, with polynomially many fixed-alphabet types and
zero-safe factorial losses. This proof does not need delta nonzero,
invertibility of the candidate frame, or any internal slot.

3. BOUNDARY PATTERNS AND THEIR CONSTANTS
---------------------------------------
Write c=b for delta>=0 and c=a for delta<0. At an exterior old-plus vertex
the zero equation requires equal counts of internal neighbors in S_+ and
cross neighbors in S_-. Their common value m_v is positive if touched;
the sum of m_v over that old side is cm. A leading pattern has cm distinct
exterior vertices on each side, all m_v=1. A collision identifies at least
one exterior vertex while preserving the number of distinct support
edges. The finitely many feasible simple patterns then lose at least one
power of N. This proves O(N^(-nu-1)) without needing a quantitative
configuration-to-simple asymptotic for those exceptional patterns.

For leading patterns, choose S_+,S_- of size m and W_+,W_- of size cm,
then four maps from the respective W sets to S sets, each support vertex
having exactly c preimages. These are functions between fixed labeled
sets. Their exact number is
 binom(N,m)^2 binom(N-m,cm)^2 [(cm)!/(c!)^m]^4.
It is asymptotic to
 N^(2m+2cm) ((cm)!)^2/[(m!)^2(c!)^(4m)].
There are no omitted automorphism factors. A graph and its flip set
determine the exterior sets and all maps uniquely. Conversely the
prescribed motif saturates the support stubs and balances the exterior
support contributions, so its occurrence indeed gives the desired flip.

The prescribed motif has e_0-v_0=nu=(D-2)m. For delta>=0,
 e_int=t delta/2+bt per internal factor, e_cross=2bt.
For delta<0,
 e_int=as per internal factor, e_cross=s^2+2as=sD.
The leading motif is connected and minimum degree at least two in all
cases: the support degrees are D>2 and exterior degrees are two.
At delta=0 it is K_(2,2a). At a=0 it is an isolated K_(b,b), with no
exterior vertices and with every motif vertex a support vertex.

For c>0 the number of physical stub assignments is
 (a!b!)^(2m)(ab)^(2cm).
The support is fully saturated on distinct required neighbors. Thus these
assignments are disjoint events, rather than an overcount of the same
configuration. One assignment has exact probability
 prod_(j=0,...,e_int-1)(aN-2j-1)^(-2) /(bN)_(e_cross).
There is no orientation factor for ordinary internal pairings and no
additional factor for the cross bijection. At a=0 the internal products
and exterior factors are directly omitted, giving (b!)^(2b) assignments.
This is a direct empty-factor calculation, not a manipulation of 0^0.

4. RESIDUAL SIMPLICITY: A RATIO, NOT JUST CONTIGUITY
---------------------------------------------------
After a physical assignment, all residual pools remain independent and
uniform. Support degrees are zero. Each W vertex has internal degree a-1
and cross degree b-1; bulk vertices retain degrees a,b. Every prescribed
edge has a saturated endpoint. Therefore a residual edge can never
duplicate a prescribed edge: full simplicity is exactly residual-factor
simplicity. This observation removes a possible constant-size forbidden
edge correction.

For every map and physical assignment the residual degree lists are the
same up to labels. Their simplicity probability is exactly the same
finite-N number, not merely uniformly comparable. I independently checked
the source's totals and normalized second factorial moments:

 delta>=0:
  L_a=aN-tD, gamma_a=(a-1)(1-bt/L_a),
  L_b=bN-2bt, gamma_b=(b-1)(1-bt/L_b);
 delta<0, a>=1:
  L_a=aN-2as, gamma_a=(a-1)(1-as/L_a),
  L_b=bN-sD, gamma_b=(b-1)(1-as/L_b).

The internal parity is valid: tD=delta(delta+1)+2bt is even in the first
case, and aN-2as is even in the second. Cross totals only have to agree
on the two sides, not be even. At a=1 the internal residual matching is
on N-2s vertices and is always simple. At a=0 the internal factors are
absent, and the cross residual graph is b-regular on N-b vertices per side.

The bounded-degree defect calculation can be applied directly: loop mean
gamma/2 and double-edge mean gamma^2/4 internally; double-edge mean
gamma_left gamma_right/2 across. Disjoint prescribed defects have the
product limiting factorial moments; feasible overlapping defects lose a
power of N. The same calculation applies to the finite residual degree
modifications. Original and residual product simplicity probabilities
therefore have the same positive limit
 exp[-1_(a>=1)(a^2-1)/2-(b-1)^2/2].
The absent a=0 factors contribute one; they must not contribute exp(1/2).
The a=1 factors also contribute one. Hence the residual/original ratio
tends to one, as required for the leading constant. Its positive limit
also gives eventual simple completability through all admissible N.
Conditioning each factor simple gives exactly the stated independent
uniform simple model because each simple factor has the same number of
labeled stub realizations.

Combining this ratio with the exact choices and assignment probabilities
gives precisely
 C_+=((bt)!)^2(a!)^(2t)/[(t!)^2(b!)^(2t)a^(t delta)],
 C_-=((as)!)^2(b!)^(2s)/[(s!)^2(a!)^(2s)b^(s^2)].
The factors a and b coming from exterior stubs cancel the boundary-edge
denominator powers. Only a^(t delta) or b^(s^2), respectively, survives.
All constants use old block size N rather than total size 2N.

5. PROPER OVERLAPS AND THE EXPONENT-ONE CASE
------------------------------------------
X counts leading flip sets. A leading motif's support is exactly its
degree-D vertices (all vertices when a=0); its exterior vertices have
degree two. Identical full vertex-and-edge images therefore mean the
same flip set. Different boundary data for the same flip set cannot
co-occur, since the support stubs are saturated. This rules out an
automorphism-sized diagonal in X(X-1).

For a connected minimum-degree-two H of excess nu and a proper subgraph
F with nonempty vertex set, including isolated common vertices,
 e(F)-v(F)<=nu-1.
If F spans, some edge was deleted. Otherwise remove r>0 vertices. If E_R
is the number of edges among removed vertices and B the boundary,
2E_R+B>=2r and B>0; hence E_R+B>=r+1. Further edge deletion only lowers
the excess. This proves the claimed inequality even when F is disconnected.

For a distinct feasible overlapping pair the union excess is thus at
least nu+1. There are finitely many typed overlap patterns; their expected
counts are O(N^(v-e)) by finite stub assignments and positive simplicity
conditioning. Incompatible prescribed unions have probability zero.
Disjoint pairs contribute O(N^(-2nu)), which is O(N^(-nu-1)) since nu>=1.
Thus
 E X(X-1)=O(N^(-nu-1)).
The unique exponent-one case is (a,b)=(1,2); its disjoint contribution
has exactly the needed N^-2 order and is not mistakenly claimed smaller.

Together with the larger-support and boundary-collision estimates, this
shows K=1+X outside an event of probability O(N^(-nu-1)). Consequently
 P(K>=2)~C N^-nu and P(K>=3)=O(N^(-nu-1)).
The leading simplicity ratio only needs to be 1+o(1); no unjustified
O(1/N) rate is used to establish the multiple-state bound.

6. DIRECT CORNER CHECKS
----------------------
(1,2): m=c=1, support core one cross edge and four boundary edges;
v=4,e=5,nu=1,C=2. Internal partners are fixed by the two matchings;
the three required cross edges have probability asymptotic to 2/N^3
for each support pair. There are N^2 support pairs. A further cross edge
between the exterior partners closes a K_4 component and can produce
additional partitions, but has excess two and belongs to the lower order.

(1,3): s=2,c=1,nu=4. The exact choice coefficient is one, the assignment
coefficient is (6)^4(3)^4, and the surviving cross denominator is 3^8.
Their ratio is 16, agreeing with the stated C.

(a,a), a>=2: t=1, nonadjacent opposite-block false twins, motif K_(2,2a).
The formula gives nu=2a-2 and C=(a!)^2, with no sign or twin-order factor.

(0,b), b>=3: the leading event is an isolated K_(b,b). Directly, its
expected count is asymptotic to
 binom(N,b)^2 (b!)^(2b)/(bN)_(b^2)
 = (b!)^(2b-2)b^(-b^2) N^(-b(b-2)).
The residual-simple ratio tends to one. Distinct saturated such components
cannot partially overlap. Independent component reorientations are
consistent with the theorem's two-state leading ambiguity mechanism.

7. POSTERIOR AND CAPPED ALGORITHM
---------------------------------
For a fixed valid planted partition, a compatible labeled graph has
likelihood 1/[G_a(N)^2 B_b(N)], independent of the partition, and has
likelihood zero for an incompatible partition. Internal/cross
decomposition is unique once that partition is fixed. G_0(N)=1 and
G_1(N) is the admissible perfect-matching count. A uniform hidden
unordered balanced plant therefore gives an exactly uniform posterior
on the K(G) valid unordered partitions. Vertex relabeling makes the
fixed-plant and hidden-prior ambiguity probabilities equal. No extra
size-bias factor is introduced at this step.

Conditional Bayes risk is 1-1/K, and
 0<=R_N-(1/2)P(K>=2)<=(1/2)P(K>=3).
This proves C/2 as the leading coefficient. The known deterministic-plant
problem is correctly excluded from this Bayes claim.

I checked the actual polynomial moment theorem used for the estimator.
With K=2,p=3, its q_*=9 and
 F_*=2+[2(2+3)/2]9^2=407,
 B_*=2*9^D+407=kappa_D.
It permits absent internal factors, degree-one matching factors, and
every finite scalar target, so it applies here without extending an old
a>=2-only theorem. Total size 2N instead of N only changes its constant.

Computing the F_3 kernel and capping its cardinality at
N^(kappa_D+nu+2) gives cap probability O(N^(-nu-2)) by Markov. On completion,
enumeration includes every true sign vector; rejecting zeros, lifting
1,2 to +1,-1, and checking the original INTEGER equation rejects modular
artifacts, including possible constant vectors when 3 divides b. Passing
vectors are automatically balanced. Deterministic elimination and a
base-three odometer give polynomial worst-case cost under the cap.

Every verified valid partition is conditionally Bayes-optimal because
the posterior is uniform. Arbitrary output on the cap event raises risk
by at most its probability, which is o(N^-nu). Thus the capped estimator
indeed attains the leading constant C/2 for all stated fixed parameters.
