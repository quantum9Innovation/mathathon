The current assembly claims: for every fixed connected simple d-regular base
B, d>=3, every fixed prime p and fixed dimension r, and every matrix R over
F_p, the independent uniform N-lift satisfies

  sup_N E #{Y in F_p^(|B|N by r): A_NY=YR}<infinity,

uniformly over R for these fixed data. The authored version includes bipartite
bases by a two-space induction. It separately gives, for nonbipartite B and
odd fixed p, scalar target a in F_p,

  E |ker_Fp(A_N-aI)|=2 p^(dim ker_Fp(B-aI))+o(1).

This file does not redo those proofs or newly certify their latest deltas.
It compares these precise claims, including their unconditioned expectation,
with primary statements actually inspected below.

FINDING

No inspected primary theorem directly subsumes the bounded raw intertwiner
moment for EVERY fixed connected simple regular BASE-LIFT ensemble. Nor did
the search locate an existing general theorem excluding all exact old/new
adjacency-spectrum collisions inside the universal-cover bulk for these lifts.
These are bounded-search findings, not proof of absence or priority.

However, bounded raw moments, vector alphabets, modular-to-real transfer,
graph-cover entropy enumeration, and Gaussian/lattice prefactor calculations
all have substantial prior art. In particular, arbitrary fixed matrix R is
already accessible from Mészáros's stronger mixing theorem in his GLOBAL
permutation/matching regular models. The candidate advance is an extension
in BASE-LIFT MODEL SCOPE, together with its boundary-sector and algorithmic
applications; it is not the first finite-field moment method for sparse
regular matrices or the first graph-cover partition-function calculation.

1. MESZAROS: A CLOSE, STRONGER MIXING INPUT ON OTHER REGULAR ENSEMBLES

András Mészáros, The distribution of sandpile groups of random regular
graphs, Trans. AMS 373 (2020), 6529-6594.
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/1806.03736v4
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1090/tran/8127
Read: model, Theorems 1.3-1.7 and Section 6 scope; relevant mixing theorem
was also inspected in prior finite-field review. Not a full-paper proof audit.

Theorems 1.5/1.6 give summed maximal-point mixing for every fixed finite
abelian alphabet: respectively sums of independent uniform permutations and
(even size) sums of independent perfect matchings. They therefore control
expectations of solution counts, not just high-probability nullity. Taking
the target to be qR and summing over q gives bounded matrix-target raw
intertwiner moments in those models: partition q by its finitely many minimal
affine cosets; each reference set is a coset power subject to a fixed number
of finite-group constraints. This is an inference from his theorem, not a
printed general-base-lift claim. His model has no arbitrary fixed simple
base with an independently permuted block above every base edge. Conditioning
on prescribed fine fibers is not a routine contiguity transfer. Also,
contiguity transfers vanishing-probability events, not unbounded expectations.

2. NGUYEN-WOOD: COKERNEL MOMENTS AND MODULAR NONSINGULARITY ARE PRIOR

Hoi H. Nguyen and Melanie Matchett Wood, Cokernels of adjacency matrices of
random r-regular graphs.
Primary full PDF: https://people.math.osu.edu/nguyen.1261/cikk/regular.pdf
arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10068
Read: Introduction, Theorems 1.3/1.5/1.6, selected moment/nonsingularity
passages in Sections 2-4, and Section 5's statement/setup for degree-dividing primes.

They calculate surjection moments and cokernel distributions for sums of
permutations and sums of perfect matchings, using Mészáros. Theorem 1.5's
standard symmetric distribution concerns odd primes coprime to the degree;
Section 5 treats new distributions caused by degree-dividing primes. They
explicitly explain why finite-prime cokernel information yields real
nonsingularity. Their independent matching/permutation ensembles are not
arbitrary fixed simple-base lifts. Ordinary cokernel surjection moments
should not be identified with the full matrix-target F_p[t]-module count
without an argument, though Mészáros's arbitrary-target mixing supplies a
route in those global models. Credit this moment/transfer framework rather
than claiming that bounded expectation is wholly new relative to their work.

3. HUANG: LOCAL CLT + LARGE DEVIATIONS FOR CONSTANT-DEGREE INVERTIBILITY

Jiaoyang Huang, Invertibility of adjacency matrices for random d-regular
graphs, Duke Math. J. 170 (2021), 3977-4032.
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/1807.06465v2
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2021-0006
Read: Introduction, configuration model, Theorems 1.1-1.3, and the stated
equidistributed/non-equidistributed proof decomposition. Not all proofs.

The paper proves finite-field expected-null-vector estimates and quantitative
real invertibility for fixed-degree directed/undirected configuration models.
Its central local CLT and large-deviation separation are close methodological
precedents. This is considerably stronger prior context than merely a generic
dense-matrix invertibility citation. The inspected statements concern the
global regular models and zero target, not matrix-target intertwiners for
every prescribed base lift. A raw expectation in the lift law requires its
own estimate; high-probability invertibility or contiguity alone is insufficient.

4. VONTOBEL: THE EXACT GRAPH-COVER TYPE ENUMERATOR IS CLASSICAL

Pascal O. Vontobel, Counting in Graph Covers: A Combinatorial Characterization
of the Bethe Entropy Function, IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory 59 (2013), 6018-6048.
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/1012.0065v2
Read: Introduction, Section VII-A through VII-D, Lemma 29, Theorems 30/33,
and the matching-count argument of Appendix A. Not the entire coding paper.

Lemma 29 gives the average configuration count in a random finite graph
cover as a product of local multinomials divided by edge multinomials.
Theorem 30 identifies its entropy exponent; Theorem 33 identifies the
exponential growth of the average partition function. Encoding a root and
its neighboring labels by edge-pair states puts the present star/edge
formula in this general normal-factor-graph framework. Thus that factorial
formula and its Bethe entropy interpretation need explicit credit. Exponent
zero gives subexponential growth, not a bounded raw moment. The inspected
theorems do not supply the complete boundary-coset estimate needed here.

5. MORI-TANAKA: CONSTANT PREFATORS ARE ALSO PRIOR, WITH A RELEVANT GAP IN SCOPE

Ryuhei Mori and Toshiyuki Tanaka, New Generalizations of the Bethe Approximation
via Asymptotic Expansion (2012).
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/1210.2592v2
Read: Introduction and Section VI-A, especially Lemmas 15/16 and their exact
hypotheses; relevant Hessian/zeta formulation. Not all loop-calculus proofs.

Lemma 16 gives constant-factor asymptotics for expected partition functions
of random covers with finitely many nondegenerate Bethe minima. It assumes
EVERY minimizing phase has strictly positive root marginals and strictly
positive mass on every legal local configuration. Point and proper-coset
phases violate those hypotheses. Thus this is a close central-prefactor
predecessor, but it is not an immediate full-moment theorem for the present
homogeneous linear systems. Applying it on a support face does not alone
control configurations leaking outside that face. The candidate's weighted
sparse-quotient/dimension induction addresses precisely this extra task.
No claim that Gaussian Hessian prefactors for covers were first developed
here would be defensible.

Mori and Tanaka, Central Approximation in Statistical Physics and Information
Theory (2012).
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/1202.0655v1
Read: Introduction and complete Section IV, Theorems 5/6 and lattice-step
discussion. Not a complete audit of the earlier dense-model calculations.

They derive central prefactors for random sparse regular factor graphs by
the method of types, including a finite lattice correction. Theorem 5
requires a unique positive root-law maximizer and a nondegeneracy condition.
This reinforces the classical status of central approximation and lattice
bookkeeping, while not replacing a classification and treatment of all
proper-coset phases in a fixed base-lift model.

6. BLOCK-MATRIX UNIVERSALITY DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY COVER PERMUTATION BLOCKS

Elia Gorokhovsky, Time-Inhomogeneous Random Walks on Finite Groups and
Cokernels of Random Integer Block Matrices, CPC 35 (2026), 329-355.
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/2405.11435v4
Published DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548325100357
Read: Theorem 1.2, Definitions 3.2/3.8 and their surrounding setup; Section 3
moment statement. Not a full proof audit.

Its blocks must be independent and balanced in their full additive matrix
spaces, with 2 alpha_1+alpha_2<1-2 beta for the block-size/balance powers.
Permutation blocks have deterministic row and column sums; deterministic
zero blocks and the transposed symmetric dependence also require attention.
Using whole N-by-N permutation blocks fails full additive balancedness and
the sublinear block-size bounds. Breaking them into small entry blocks
destroys independence. No verified reduction puts the current lift model
under this theorem. It is relevant dependence/cokernel prior art, not an
off-the-shelf subsumption merely because both matrices have block structure.

7. DENSE / INDEPENDENT-ENTRY MODULE UNIVERSALITY IS A DIFFERENT ENSEMBLE

Jiahe Shen, Sharp Threshold for Universality of Rational Canonical Forms
over a Finite Field (2026).
Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01413v2
Read: primary abstract and stated assumptions; not a proof audit.

The paper studies independent alpha_N-balanced entries, with
N alpha_N-log N tending to infinity, and uses F_p[t]-surjection moments.
It demonstrates that polynomial/module-target language and rational-canonical
form universality have substantial current precedent. A fixed-degree lift
has strongly dependent entries, deterministic zero blocks and one edge per
active type pair; it does not satisfy the displayed entry assumptions.

Gilyoung Cheong and Yifeng Huang, The cokernel of a polynomial push-forward
of a random integral matrix with concentrated residue, MPCPS 178 (2025),
229-257.
Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09491v3
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004125000064
Read: primary abstract/model, not its proofs. It concerns independent-entry
p-adic matrices X=A+pB and polynomial cokernels. It is relevant to the
algebraic framework but not the permutation-lift law. Similar independence
restrictions apply to Nguyen-Van Peski's matrix-product cokernel results;
matrix products of independent-entry matrices are not fixed-base lifts.

8. GLOBAL STRONG CONVERGENCE DOES NOT RULE OUT EXACT BULK COLLISIONS

Charles Bordenave and Benoît Collins, Eigenvalues of random lifts and
polynomials of random permutation matrices, Ann. Math. 190 (2019), 811-875.
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/1801.00876v4
Published: https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2019/190-3/p03
Read for this comparison: Introduction Sections 1.1-1.5, invariant old/new
subspaces, Theorem 2 and graph specialization. Not a new complete proof read.

The theorem gives Hausdorff spectral convergence on the orthogonal complement
of the fiber-constant space, and strong asymptotic freeness for permutation
operators. It excludes fixed new eigenvalues separated from the limiting
universal-cover spectrum. It does not forbid a new eigenvalue at a particular
old value INSIDE that spectrum: a single such point is compatible with the
Hausdorff conclusion. Complex-operator polynomials here should not be confused
with finite-field module moment estimates. A general exact collision theorem
would therefore add a different type of information in the bulk, although
outside-spectrum preservation is already covered by this spectral theory.

Jiaoyang Huang and Ruizhe Xu, Local Law and Eigenvector Delocalization for
Random Lifts (27 October 2026).
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37463v1
Read: model, Introduction, Theorems 1.2/1.3 and the stated scale/regularity
conditions. Not a full 70-page proof audit.

They give counting down to (log N)^C/N and eigenvector delocalization away
from singular energies, for fixed simple bases of minimum degree three.
Those primary statements do not assert finite-field raw moment bounds or
exclusion of a fixed exact bulk eigenvalue. A bounded number of coincident
eigenvalues is compatible with counts on those windows. Delocalization alone
also permits an extra delocalized eigenvector at the same eigenvalue. It
would be incorrect to infer exact old multiplicity from those statements.

9. ENTROPY / GAUSSIAN-WAVE PREDECESSORS AND CODING CONNECTION

Ágnes Backhausz and Balázs Szegedy, On the almost eigenvectors of random
regular graphs.
Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.04785
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/18-AOP1294
Read: primary abstract; its relevant theorem was also compared through the
explicit statements in the Dembo-McKenzie source below. No full fresh proof read.
The result concerns qualitative Gaussian-wave statistics of almost eigenvectors
in the global random regular ensemble; it is not a bounded raw modular moment.

Amir Dembo and Theo McKenzie, The Gaussian Wave for Graphs of Finite Cone Type.
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04386v1
Read in this session: Definition 1.6, Theorem 1.8 and its qualification,
Proposition 2.3, Section 3's multitype star/edge counts and Lemmas 3.1/3.2.
The multitype entropy-counting framework is close method prior art. Its
typical-process and spectral-window theorems do not estimate the total raw
finite-field intertwiner count. The window result explicitly accommodates
exceptional planted eigenvectors. Credit this lineage; do not reinterpret
Gaussian-wave convergence as exact prescribed-eigenvalue nonsingularity.

Protograph LDPC literature also counts codewords in permutation-block covers.
The review found primary enumerator work, including Yizeng Sun's 2013 thesis
https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1x66251m and the graph-cover papers above.
I read the thesis primary abstract/table of contents, not its full enumeration
chapters. Thus I do not claim a complete exclusion of coding-theory overlap.
Normalized-rank or design-rate convergence would not suffice for a bounded
raw kernel moment, and standard independent bipartite parity-check lifts do
not automatically encode the correlated symmetric adjacency equation with
arbitrary matrix R. More exhaustive coding-priority review remains worthwhile.

CLAIM LANGUAGE AND PRIORITY LIMITS

The strongest defensible candidate novelty scope is the unconditioned bounded
raw F_p[t]-intertwiner moment for all fixed connected simple regular base lifts,
including fixed small degrees and arbitrary matrix targets, with no old/new
spectral gap. The likely distinctive proof work is complete coset-phase
classification plus weighted boundary induction controlling correlation with
rare large bulk kernels. Its ingredients must be credited as above.

The scalar exact formula is separate and stronger in its dependence on p than
an unspecified fixed-p bounded moment. It supports prescribed-eigenvalue modular
transfer with N tending to infinity first. Preserving all old multiplicities
requires applying that transfer to each fixed base eigenvalue, including the
algebraic-prime reduction if it is noninteger; it is not identical to proving
simple spectrum, irreducibility of the new characteristic polynomial, or
exclusion of every possible eigenvalue depending on N. No finite-field moment
claim should silently become one of those stronger assertions.

Unknown-fiber recovery and expected near-quadratic implementation are additional
claims. The moment controls the expected size of a fixed finite candidate
enumeration, but a near-quadratic full-kernel algorithm needs its own verified
sparse-linear-algebra bound, error accounting and work model. Prior recovery
scope is reviewed separately in regular-frame-recovery-priority.txt. This file
does not certify that implementation or its historical priority.
