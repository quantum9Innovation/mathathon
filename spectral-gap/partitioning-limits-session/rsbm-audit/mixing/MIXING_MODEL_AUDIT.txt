------------------------------------------------

  generates all of the additive group F_p; hence MinC(x)=F_p.
* Source footnote2 on PDFp3 defines d MinC as a d-FOLD SET SUM. It is NOT

* Consequently the nonconstant reference law is uniform on exactly one
  hyperplane s(r)=d1 s(x), of size p^(N-1). For x=a1 the affine coset is

3. RECONSTRUCTION OF THE MOMENT CALCULATION
-----------------------------------------
All quantitative bounds needed below were independently checked.

For a fixed target shift b, a nonconstant x contributes reference mass
p^(-N+1) only when d2 s(x)=s(b); there are at most p^(N-1) such x.
A constant x=a1 contributes only when b=d2 a1, allowing at most one a.
Thus the reference affine sum is <=2, uniformly in b, and the true sum
is <=2+e_N by the source's unscaled sum of atom errors.

Independence of C and D gives the displayed product expression for the
expected finite-field kernel size. In the first replacement, for each x
the remaining sum over y is the TRUE affine sum for D, with fixed shift
-B^T x. Its bound is2+e_N. In the second replacement, for each y the
remaining x sum is the REFERENCE affine sum, with fixed shift -By, bounded
by2. Hence total error <=(2+e_N)e_N+2e_N=4e_N+e_N^2. There is no hidden
summation factor and no unjustified uniformity in the shift.

If both x,y are nonconstant, each block's hyperplane equation becomes
d2(s(x)+s(y))=0; these equations coincide, and p not dividing d2 leaves
at most p^(2N-1) pairs. Their contribution is <=p.
If x=a1,y=b1, the pointwise equations are d2(a+b)=0; exactly p constant
pairs survive. This remains true when p divides N or d1.
If only x=a1 is constant, the first pointwise requirement is
By=-d2 a1. Each a allows at most p^(N-rank_p B) vectors y, and the other
reference factor is <=p^(-N+1). Thus this orientation contributes at most
p^(2-rank_p B); transpose symmetry gives the other orientation.

The cross-matrix rank lower bound is valid over EVERY field. A selected
zero-one row is nonzero over any F_p, and rows with disjoint nonempty
supports are independent. Each row conflicts with at most
1+d2(d2-1) rows, yielding rank_p B>=N/[1+d2(d2-1)]. No condition p>d2
is needed for this step. Consequently the mixed terms vanish for every
FIXED p uniformly over simple d2-regular B.

All (2N-1)-minors vanish over the integers if real nullity>=2, so the
mod-p nullity is also>=2. Markov gives the stated limiting2/p upper bound.
Taking arbitrarily large fixed admissible primes AFTER the N limsup is
valid. No assertion that p grows with N is smuggled into the proof.

4. CONTIGUITY UNDER TWO FACTORS AND UNIFORM DETERMINISTIC B
--------------------------------------------------------
Finite independent products preserve the required direction of contiguity.
Here is a complete useful argument, since simple statements of graph
contiguity alone do not automatically justify a new paired matrix event.

If Q_N is contiguous with respect to P_N, then for every eta>0 there
are delta>0 and N0 such that P_N(E)<=delta implies Q_N(E)<=eta for all
N>=N0 and all measurable E. Otherwise choose sets along a violating
subsequence with P-probability tending0 but Q-probability bounded below.
This contradicts contiguity.

For an event E_N on two independent factors with (P_N x P_N)(E_N)->0,
set f_N(x)=P_N((E_N)_x). Markov gives P_N(f_N>delta)->0. Outside that
exceptional set, the uniform small-set assertion gives
Q_N((E_N)_x)<=eta. Integrating against P_N shows
(P_N x Q_N)(E_N)->0 after eta->0. Repeating for the other factor gives
(Q_N x Q_N)(E_N)->0. This handles arbitrary events on the pair, not just
rectangles. Independence of the two internal graphs is used here.

The event is allowed to depend on ANY deterministic sequence B_N:
contiguity is quantified over all sequences of measurable events. Applying
it to a purported sequence of worst cross matrices therefore proves the
sup_B conclusion. This is uniform failure probability, NOT a simultaneous
event for every B and NOT a guarantee for B chosen adaptively after seeing
C,D. The manuscript's theorem correctly states the former.

Adjoining a common independent cross distribution is harmless, either by
conditioning on B and the established uniform bound, or by a bounded
conditional-probability/Markov argument. Nothing in this step licenses an
adaptive dependent cross graph.

  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/(SICI)1098-2418(199705)10:3%3C305::AID-RSA1%3E3.0.CO;2-%23

The actual MRRW theorem is mutual contiguity of uniform SIMPLE regular
graphs on2n labelled vertices and the model weighted by the number of
ordered1-factorizations, equivalently a uniform ordered EDGE-DISJOINT
r-tuple of perfect matchings. It covers every fixed r>=3, explicitly
including the cubic base case. It is not literally a theorem about the
UNconditioned matching union and loopless pairing model. The manuscript
should distinguish the original theorem's model from Nguyen--Wood's
stronger stated transfer formulation when attributing its precise input.
This is citation precision, not a fatal gap in the proved conclusion.

If desired, a narrower direct transfer avoids the loopless multigraph
claim entirely: condition the independent matching union on having no
repeated edges, then use MRRW's exact simple-model contiguity. This
conditioning event has probability bounded below for fixed r (indeed
it tends to exp[-r(r-1)/4]). For completeness, successive forbidden-edge
counts give that constant: after j edge-disjoint matchings the forbidden
graph is simple j-regular; the number of its edges appearing in a new
uniform matching has fixed factorial moments tending to(j/2)^k, uniformly
over such bounded-degree forbidden graphs. Its zero probability tends to
exp(-j/2). Multiplying over j=0,...,r-1 gives the stated constant. Thus an
o(1) matching-model event remains o(1) under this conditioning, and MRRW
then transfers it to uniform-simple graphs. The same finite-product
argument applies to two internal blocks.

6. SCOPE CHECK
--------------
The graph-model and mixing argument supplies exactly the even-block,
d1>=3 theorem with qualitative o(1) failure probability. It cannot be
used as written for odd block size, d1=2, effective rates, a prime growing
with N, or a cross graph depending on the internal graph realizations.
The manuscript states these limitations rather than claiming them.

The rational-kernel recovery step is consistent with simplicity and does
not require a numerical outlier gap. It requires the parameter delta,
as the manuscript states. Fixed degrees give bounded row norms and
O(N)-bit bounds on minors via Hadamard, enough for polynomial exact
elimination. No additional model-transfer issue was found there.

Final assessment: no fatal flaw found in the audited proof. The primary
mixing theorem is precisely as strong as the new argument needs; the
symmetry tensor and p|d1 traps have both been checked explicitly.
