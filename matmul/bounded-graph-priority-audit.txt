BOUNDED GRAPH UPDATE: LIMITED PRIMARY-SOURCE PRIORITY AUDIT
=========================================================

2026-11-15 UTC. This is a scoped literature check, not a priority
certificate. It accompanies bounded-spectrum-graph-algorithm.txt and
does not change that note's independently reconstructed proof.

1. WHAT THE SCALAR LITERATURE ALREADY SUPPLIES
-------------------------------------------

Herremans, Huybrechs and Trefethen, "Resolution of Singularities by
Rational Functions," SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 61 (2023), 2580--2600,
DOI 10.1137/23M1551821:
https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/trefethen/hht_published.pdf

Their construction combines prescribed tapered exponentially clustered
poles and a polynomial of degree O(sqrt(N)). They establish the optimal
Stahl exponent for the square root and state the general x^alpha version
as Conjecture 3.1 (printed p2588): spacing 2*pi/sqrt(alpha), error
O(exp(-2*pi*sqrt(alpha*N))). Thus pole-plus-polynomial construction,
and the square-root optimal scalar upper, are prior results.

Xiang, Yang and Wu, "On the best convergence rates of lightning plus
polynomial approximations," arXiv:2312.16116v3 (2024):
https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16116
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.16116

Corollary 4.2 (PDF p27), following Theorem 1.2, proves the general
interval x^alpha optimal exponent O(exp(-2*pi*sqrt(alpha*N))) with
tapered poles and polynomial degree O(sqrt(N1)), N=N1+N2. The statement
is a concrete preassigned-pole LP upper, not merely a minimax existence
theorem. It rules out a first-general-alpha-scalar-construction claim.

Xiang, Xiang, Yang and Zhong, "Lightning Plus Polynomial Approximation:
Optimal Root-Exponential Convergence for Singular Functions in Corner
Domains," arXiv:2605.30796v5 (2026):
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30796
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30796

The abstract explicitly reports proofs of HHT Conjectures 3.1 and 5.3.
Theorems 1.1--1.2 concern optimal LP approximation of analytic factors
times z^alpha and z^alpha*log(z) on sectors, including the interval.
These are further scalar antecedents, not a graph-update theorem.

Xue and Zhang, "Shifted tapered-pole approximation on finite sectors:
an exact asymptotic and dimension control," arXiv:2610.09256v1 (2026):
https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09256
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09256

Theorem 1.1 gives shift-uniform scalar LP rates. Proposition 3.2 gives
an explicit transition-pole/polynomial compression and refines the
representation dimension to N+2*sqrt(2*alpha*lambda_sigma*N)+O(1).
Remark 3.3 (PDF pp12--13) distinguishes final representation size from
coefficient preprocessing, giving polynomial coefficient accumulation
cost and disclaiming optimal generation complexity and floating-point
stability. It is not a total graph-solver complexity theorem.

2. LOW-RANK MATRIX-FUNCTION UPDATE MECHANISM IS ALSO PRIOR
-------------------------------------------------------

Beckermann, Cortinovis, Kressner and Schweitzer, "Low-rank updates of
matrix functions II: Rational Krylov methods," SIAM J. Numer. Anal.
59 (2021), 1325--1347; accessible preprint arXiv:2008.11501:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11501
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.11501

Theorem 3.3 gives rational-update exactness for rank-ell modifications.
Remark 3.4 includes polynomial terms as infinite poles. Theorem 4.5
controls Hermitian update error through a scalar rational approximation
problem. Their introduction explicitly credits Bernstein--Van Loan's
earlier rational-update rank result and observes the strategy of first
approximating f by r and then forming r(A+D)-r(A). The introduction
also names network changes as a motivation. These mechanisms cannot
be presented as new here. The inspected theorem statements do not
include the bounded fractional graph update's sharp leading width
together with a charged nearly-linear graph algorithm.

3. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE LOCAL CLAIM
---------------------------------

The scalar results and the rational-update mechanism already imply the
bounded-spectrum sharp leading-width UPPER at an approximation level:
approximate x^alpha uniformly on [0,T] by an LP, form its two endpoint
updates, and use that each finite simple-pole correction has rank <=k
and a degree-d polynomial difference has rank <=kd. For fixed endpoint
ratio T/||E||, scaling changes log(1/epsilon) only by an additive
constant. N poles plus O(sqrt(N)) polynomial degree therefore gives

  width <= [k/(4*pi^2*alpha)+o(1)] log^2(1/epsilon).

This paragraph is our elementary composition of the cited results, not
a claim that any one cited paper states the resulting graph theorem.
It substantially limits any novelty claim for the upper bound alone.

The local result is accordingly framed as an explicitly charged graph
realization with intrinsic epsilon*||E||^alpha error: arbitrary fixed
positive vertex masses, positive changes on k edges, all returned
factor entries, expected ~O((M+N)k) arithmetic, and matching leading
width lower bound attained on a connected mass-weighted path. Its proof
uses a globally collected polynomial correction and uniform accounting
for a slowly growing subtraction order. These details establish the
local theorem; they are not themselves a historical-priority finding.

The primary statements above, plus a small targeted search for fractional
graph-Laplacian low-rank update and tapered-pole graph algorithms, did
not locate an exact matching end-to-end graph theorem with all these
guarantees. This does not prove that none exists. It also does not rule
out the possibility that standard scalar constructions, update formulas,
and graph solvers combine to give another short proof of the same upper.
No claim of first scalar optimal approximation, first LP construction,
first proof of the HHT conjecture, or certified graph priority is made.
