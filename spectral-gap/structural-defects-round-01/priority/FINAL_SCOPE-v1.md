## 1. A common attachment transition

The structural result is now broader than the scalar-port candidate examined in the initial audit. It allows a fixed vector polynomial b(x), a fixed finite symmetric internal matrix C, and one simple local resonant direction v. The calibration is a positive semidefinite local boundary matrix K_0 with kernel span(v), and the stationary coupling beta=b(d)^T v must be nonzero. The host is a fixed-degree uniform simple labelled regular graph with predetermined distinct marks.

Under those hypotheses, the result connects local attachment data to all of the following in one model:

1. The normalized top displacement tends to kappa if and only if m^3/n tends to zero, for every sequence 1<=m=o(n).
2. At m proportional to n^(1/3), the actual top m+1 eigenvalues have a collective star contribution and signed rare-pair levels. Their pair counts have a distance-phase-dependent Poisson description, with both truncation tails controlled.
3. The actual graph spectral subspaces and physical mass forms follow from a constructed lift and its Gram matrix. The constant host direction is distinguished from the whole original host.
4. In the dilute range m=o(sqrt(n)), the whole top cluster has width O_P(max(sqrt(m/n),m^2/n)) and a fixed gap below it. In n^(1/3)<<m<<sqrt(n), the top displacement has tight scale m^2/n and is bounded away from zero at that scale.
5. The lower divergence obstruction continues through m=o(n), beyond the proved upper width range. A positive pair witness survives arbitrary signs in the fixed polynomial coefficients by using the full host Green kernel outside the polynomial's finite support.

The parameters kappa and h=g_d(d)kappa^2, and the motif mass factor 1/D, summarize the first-order dependence on the local attachment. The nonseparable example shows that different motif coordinates can couple through genuinely different host polynomials. The unweighted seven-leaf-star example changes the actual internal motif and its metric. These are structural tests of a class theorem, rather than new probability mechanisms counted once per example.

## 2. The retuned cubic law

The new fine-scale theorem concerns the cubic duplication operator with host coupling AE and no added internal block. It does not extend its constants to every attachment in the structural class.

Write s=sqrt(m/n), kappa=3/sqrt(8), r=floor(log_2(1/s)), and w=(3/4)2^(-r)/s. The deterministic tuning

\[
\frac{w-\kappa+(\kappa^2/2)sr}{s}\longrightarrow\beta
\]

is resolved at physical scale s^2=m/n, centered at

\[
3-\kappa s-(\kappa^2/2)s^2r.
\]

If the number of distance-r marked pairs tends to P_0, the fine spectral measure in the negative collision window tends jointly to

\[
\delta_{\mathcal L}+P_0\delta_{33/16-\beta}.
\]

The collective coefficient L uses the same Poisson family as the pair counts. Its combined jumps are governed by

\[
J(t)=t-\frac{t^2}{\kappa+t}=\frac{\kappa t}{\kappa+t},
\]

with the explicit constant 51/32 in the frozen theorem. The raw collective fluctuation and the complementary correction separately have problematic large-jump means. Their common-process combination has bounded jumps, a finite second moment and a bounded smooth conditional density. Discarding the complementary correction gives the wrong law.

The signed collective-to-negative-pair coupling vanishes at this scale. Thus the limiting random ordering is not a nonzero two-level avoided crossing. Conditional on every fixed pair count p, the collective level is almost surely distinct from the repeated pair level. The theorem transfers a rank-one collective projection and the entire rank-p pair projection into physical graph space. It deliberately leaves internal splitting within the repeated pair group unresolved.

Every deterministic finite beta is attained by admissible integer graph sizes. The earlier distance phase alone does not determine this finer law. The proof uses a new exact energy-dependent reduction and a uniform fine-scale error, rather than dividing the older first-order cluster error by a smaller scale.

This closes the precise retuned problem left open in the input baseline. It is a local research problem solved at the stated paper scope, not a claim that a previously published open problem has been identified and settled.

## 4. Remaining scope and research value

The structural theorem has one simple local resonant channel, fixed local dimension and fixed polynomial degree. It does not cover multiple critical channels, independently oriented host ports, arbitrary irregular hosts, growing motif sizes or mark labels selected after seeing the graph. The lower and upper growth ranges remain different. The broader nonseparable residual can survive at physical order s^2, so the cubic fine constants cannot be reused there without a new analysis.

The retuned law excludes graph-adaptive tuning, conditioning on a shrinking coincidence event, and the finer splitting inside a repeated pair group. It does not assert convergence of finite-n moments merely from moments of the limiting law.

The natural mathematical perspective is a common competition between a collective host mode and geometric pair resonances, with local boundary data determining their first-order strengths. The fine cubic result additionally shows how eliminating complementary modes changes an apparent large-jump fluctuation into a bounded-jump law. These are concrete mechanisms that can guide new theorems. The current evidence does not justify presenting them as a unification of unrelated fields or as a large collection of independently solved published open problems.

The next high-value obligations are an exact publication-level priority comparison, formalization of an analytic or probabilistic bridge beyond finite matrix algebra, and a genuinely broader theorem such as multiple local resonant channels or the next physical scale for nonseparable attachments. More scalar identities or more copies of the same motif argument would not by themselves meet those obligations.
