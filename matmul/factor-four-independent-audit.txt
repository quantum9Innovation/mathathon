1. DEFINITIONS AND THE EXACT COEFFICIENTS
----------------------------------------
Throughout alpha is fixed in (0,1), beta=1-alpha, and H is a POWER
constant, namely the ratio of two power traces. For the count coefficient
c, the dimension-free moment coefficient is 2c. The values are

  PSD:          c=1/(4 pi^2 alpha beta),
  complex:      c=1/(2 pi^2 alpha beta),
  real full:    c=1/(  pi^2 alpha beta).

Thus the moment coefficients have ratio 1:2:4. This is not a ratio of
the ordinary Schatten quasi-norm constants H^(1/s).

Rank-one reduction remains valid WITHIN a fixed dimension. For PSD
endpoints, the common upper endpoint A+E_+=B+E_- and the two positive
spectral paths remain in that dimension. For Hermitian functions,
signed spectral increments and reversal of negative increments suffice.
For square absolute/polar maps use singular-value increments. Telescoping,
the s-power triangle (0<s<=1), and positive homogeneity give the exact
rank-one reduction without a rank-dependent loss.

The same argument works for a complex-valued continuous homogeneous
scalar function on Hermitian inputs: its outputs need not be Hermitian
for the Schatten s-power triangle to apply.

2. EXACT ORTHOGONAL RESOLVENT COMPRESSION
-----------------------------------------
For B=A+vv*, ||v||=1, let K contain every (A-z)^(-1)v for the selected
poles, including conjugates when needed. With P the projection onto K,
A'=PAP|K and v'=Pv, direct multiplication gives

  (A'-z)^(-1)v'=(A-z)^(-1)v,
  v'^*(A'-z)^(-1)v'=v*(A-z)^(-1)v.

Woodbury therefore reproduces each selected resolvent DIFFERENCE
exactly, after embedding the compressed operator by zero. Both its
left and right vectors lie in K because the pole set is conjugate
closed. Negative real PSD poles need no duplication.

The compressed increment has norm ||v'||^2<=1. Leaving it unnormalized
is correct: its Holder-ratio denominator is at most one. The uniform
quadrature envelope for ||E||<=1 is valid with the original universal
nodes, so both corrections are within exp(-L) of the SAME quadrature.
Hence their operator-norm distance is at most 2 exp(-L).

The available state dimensions match the sharp count constants:

  PSD, d<pi:       c_d=1/(4 pi d alpha beta),
  real, d<pi/2:    c_d=1/(2 pi d alpha beta),

with O_(alpha,d)(L^(7/4)) overhead. Fix d before letting L grow. Then
c_d tends to c at the strip boundary. Nothing requires uniform envelope
constants as d approaches that boundary.

3. THE UNIVERSAL EXTREMAL PROFILE
--------------------------------
Choose unit rank-one near-extremizers Delta_s with power trace at least
H_s-1. The starting manuscript's deficit argument proves, for EVERY
fixed x>0,

  s^2 N_(Delta_s)(x/s) -> c x^2.

It is valid even when ||Delta_s|| is bounded by M>1. The number of
singular values above one is uniformly bounded by the rank-one count
at one fixed large positive logarithmic threshold. Consequently the
contribution of the bounded negative logarithmic interval to the
scaled layer-cake moment vanishes. Unbounded ambient dimensions do
not create an uncontrolled extra term.

Equivalently one may first divide the outputs by the fixed M, apply
the deficit lemma as stated, then squeeze the fixed shift of the log
threshold between x-delta and x+delta. Both routes give the same profile.
This statement concerns a family of near-extremizers, not a Weyl law
for an individually fixed matrix or operator.

4. THE FINITE-DIMENSION CROSSOVER
---------------------------------
If n_s s^2 -> t in (0,infinity), the uniform upper count is capped by n_s.
Layer cake and x=sL give the upper limit

  integral_0^infinity exp(-x) min(t,c x^2) dx.

Values above one contribute at most n_s(M^s-1), whose product with s^2
vanishes. Dominated convergence is justified by the dimension cap
since n_s s^2 stays bounded. The uniform O(L^(7/4)) count remainder
becomes O(s^(1/4) x^(7/4)). First take s->0 with d fixed, then move d
to the strip boundary.

For the lower choose b=(1-eta)sqrt(t/c_d), eta>0, and compress at
L_s=b/s. Its state dimension is below n_s for sufficiently small s
because c_d b^2=(1-eta)^2 t<t and the overhead is o(s^-2).
At every fixed x<b, the spectral error 2 exp(-b/s) is negligible
compared with exp(-x/s). Preserving counts first at x'<x, then taking
x' up to x, gives the lower c x^2. Monotonicity supplies the cap
c b^2 at x>=b. Fatou therefore gives

  liminf s^2 H_(s,n_s) >= integral exp(-x)c min(x^2,b^2) dx.

The compressed input norm <=1 only improves this lower ratio. Let
eta->0 and then d approach the strip boundary. The upper and lower
match. Direct integration, with u=sqrt(t/c), is

  Phi_c(t)=2c[1-(1+u)exp(-u)].

Square absolute/polar maps have output rank at most n and the same
upper counts. Their Hermitian even/odd subclasses give the matching
lower dimensions, so the square-map crossovers follow. Arbitrary
rectangular aspect ratios are correctly excluded from this statement.

5. ENDPOINTS AND THE FINITE-SIZE COUNT LAW
------------------------------------------
For fixed n the positive PSD rank-one update with distinct diagonal
background and all nonzero perturbation coordinates has a strictly
positive definite correction. The resolvent integral proves this: a
vanishing quadratic form would force all residues of
v*(A+t)^(-1)z to vanish. Its power trace tends to n. The upper n M^s
matches that limit.

For n_s s^2->0 with n_s growing, take extremizers at auxiliary
exponent 1/sqrt(n), choose fixed a<b<1/sqrt(c_d) with c a^2>1-delta,
and compress at b sqrt(n). This is possible after taking d close
enough to the boundary. It preserves (1-delta)n singular values
above exp(-C_delta sqrt(n)). Their power trace is at least
(1-delta)n exp(-C_delta s sqrt(n)), proving H_(s,n)/n->1.
Bounded n_s are covered by the fixed-n argument and a finite set of n.
For n_s s^2->infinity, pad examples at n=floor(T/s^2), use the
finite-t result, then send T to infinity.

For n_L/L^2->t and fixed rank r, the upper count is the minimum of
the state cap and the uniform rank-r bound. For r=1 compress the
extremal profile at depth bL and preserve counts at aL, with
0<a<b<1 and c_d b^2<t. Taking a,b to their boundary values gives
min(t,c). If the compressed increment is smaller than one, normalize
both endpoints only AFTER this preservation argument. Homogeneity
can only increase the output singular values. For general fixed r,+r direct sums in dimensions floor(n_L/r) give min(t,r c).

6. EVERY NONZERO REAL HOMOGENEOUS FUNCTION
------------------------------------------
For f_(a,b)(x)=a x_+^alpha+b (-x)_+^alpha, put
e=(a+b)/2 and o=(a-b)/2. The upper must combine even and odd
integrands BEFORE taking node ranks. The combined node still has
two conjugate resolvent terms and rank <=2 for rank-one input.
Using separate final-function power triangles would lose a factor.

For the centered construction, the correction has diagonal blocks
o S_+ and -o S_- and self-adjoint off-diagonal blocks. When o!=0,
min--max for POSITIVE eigenvalues bounds the positive diagonal block;
min--max for NEGATIVE eigenvalues bounds the other block. These are
disjoint spectral contributions and may be added. This proves

  tr|Delta_f|^s >= |o|^s(tr S_+^s+tr S_-^s).

This is not Schatten pinching contractivity, which is false here.
Each checked C_1 channel contributes 1/(pi^2 alpha beta) to the
leading moment coefficient. The fixed |o|^s tends to one. If o=0,
the function is a nonzero multiple of the even map. Thus every
nonzero real homogeneous map has the full coefficient, including
one-sided fractional ReLU. The rank-one profile and compression then
give its stated count and dimension laws.

Minor presentation clarification: the fixed-n witness in Section 6.1
uses positive endpoints. If a=0,b!=0, f vanishes on those endpoints;
use their negative mirror instead. Its correction is b times the
negative of the PSD correction and is still full rank. This supplies
the fixed-n endpoint for all real a,b and requires no theorem change.

7. THE COMPLEX ANALYTIC BRANCH
-------------------------------
With phi=pi alpha/2, g_+=cos(phi) f_e-i sin(phi) f_o. Inserting the
checked real-map integrals cancels the +it resolvent term exactly:

  Delta_g=-i (sin(pi alpha)/pi)
             integral_0^infinity t^alpha [R_B(-it)-R_A(-it)] dt.

The sign and pole in this formula are correct. Its strip width is
pi/2, its envelope slopes are alpha,beta, and each node has rank ONE.
Consequently c_(branch,d)=1/(4 pi d alpha beta), tending to
1/(2 pi^2 alpha beta). This gives the upper moment coefficient
1/(pi^2 alpha beta).

The top-left centered correction is -i sin(phi)S_+. Singular-value
min--max for orthogonal compression gives the matching one-channel
lower. The finite-transfer argument works for this continuous complex
function as well: continuous functional calculus in a common bounded
real interval and convergence of fixed singular-value partial sums
do not require the outputs to be Hermitian. Adjoint symmetry handles
g_-. The deficit argument and direct sums give the count constants.

Minor wording clarification: the single-channel lower coefficient is
obtained after finite-matrix transfer and taking the interval radius
R->infinity FOR EACH FIXED s, then s->0 and finally delta->0. It is
not a nonzero s^2 coefficient for a fixed bounded interval. The note
references the correct inherited order, but an explicit supremum over
intervals would remove any possible ambiguity.

8. THE q+1-STATE BRANCH COMPRESSION
-----------------------------------
Let z_j all lie in one open half-plane, mu be the unit-mass spectral
measure of (A,v), and w(lambda)=prod_j |lambda-z_j|^2. The bounded
support of mu and strict positivity of w justify Gaussian quadrature
for dnu=dmu/w. A q+1-node rule is exact through degree 2q+1.
If fewer support points are available, taking the measure exactly
only reduces the state dimension.

Reweighting each Gaussian weight by w at its node preserves total
mass, since deg w=2q. It preserves m(z_j) and m(conj z_j), since
w(lambda)/(lambda-z_j) and its conjugate are polynomials of degree
2q-1. The new diagonal background and vector sqrt(b_k) are Hermitian
and UNIT NORM respectively. Thus the new rank-one perturbation has
norm exactly one, not merely a bound needing renormalization.

Both column Gram identities in (24) have the correct signs and
denominators. No derivative is needed: conjugate(z_i) and z_j are
in opposite half-planes, so the denominators cannot vanish. Woodbury
then preserves the diagonal coefficients in Q=U D V*. Equal U*U and
V*V imply identical nonzero singular values of Q and Q', by the
two polar decompositions. A common left/right isometry is unnecessary
and is correctly NOT asserted.

Both true corrections have operator-norm error <=exp(-L) relative
to their own universal quadratures. Their singular-value sequences,
with zeros appended, therefore differ by at most 2 exp(-L). This is
all that the finite-crossover lower proof needs. Its state dimension
q+1 has the branch coefficient to leading order. The proof of the
full finite-size crossover and count law for g_+/- is therefore valid.

9. SEMIFINITE MATRIX-DEGREE DICHOTOMY
-------------------------------------
For a finite-trace projection increment, and for finite-support partial
isometries in the absolute/polar cases, each quadrature sample has the
claimed support trace: tau(p), 2tau(p), or tau(p) for PSD, real full,
or complex branch respectively. Support trace is subadditive and
cannot increase under bounded multiplication. The Banach-valued
quadrature argument applies directly in M. Generalized approximation
numbers then give the same count coefficient multiplied by tau(p),
including the constant remainder. No dimension-free additive term
independent of tau(p) is introduced.

Spectral step approximants to an integrable positive difference may
be chosen below it, in operator norm, with finite support trace and
no increase in tau(E^(alpha s)). Telescoping and the trace s-power
triangle give the desired denominator. Signed PSD differences use
the common upper endpoint. General Hermitian differences use signed
steps; arbitrary differences in the absolute/polar cases use polar
steps U p_j, which are partial isometries. Continuous functional
calculus gives norm convergence of the outputs. Testing their positive
power operators on finite-trace projections and taking the supremum
justifies trace Fatou. This proves the universal upper directly in M;
no matrix-approximation or hyperfiniteness hypothesis is needed.

For unbounded matrix degree, the checked finite-trace matrix embedding
places every finite matrix example in a corner of M. The trace is
a scalar multiple of matrix trace on that corner, and the factor
cancels in the ratio. All maps vanish at zero, so corner extension
preserves the functional calculus. This gives the universal lower.

For finite degree D, the central matrix decomposition allows the
D-dimensional inequality to be applied fiberwise and integrated.
Conversely a finite-positive-trace central piece of the nonzero
D-summand carries constant D-by-D matrices and gives equality.
Therefore H_s^F(M)=H_(s,D)^F, and the limit is D. This reasoning is
valid within the note's BOUNDED-input, faithful normal semifinite
trace scope; unbounded inputs and type-III spaces are not covered.
