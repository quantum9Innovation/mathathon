                    Centered permutation products:
                sharp envelopes and uniform asymptotics


                                     November 14, 2026


                                           Abstract
          We prove attained envelopes for mixed centered permutation products, allowing
     arbitrary multiplicities and independent colors. When at most two centered singletons
     conflict with another requested cell, explicit patterns in one permutation attain the
     exact maximum for fixed support and singleton counts. A stronger bound also applies
     when several centered singletons conflict. Scalar consequences sharpen the probabilistic
     estimate used in random-lift and biregular spectral-gap arguments. For distinct diagonal
     cells, the leading terms of Collins and Nagatsu are relatively accurate exactly when
     k 3 /N → 0, answering their published optimal-scale question. The critical limits are
     parity-dependent hyperbolic functions. A positive partition expansion and an elementary
     saddle estimate give a uniform approximation throughout k = o(N ), a sharp hierarchy
     of correction thresholds, and an exact-denominator formula for every 2 ≤ k ≤ N . For
     the latter formula we determine the full parity error profile and the optimal uniform
     rate C∗ N −1/3 + o(N −1/3 ), with C∗ ≈ 0.39549627. For the shorter quartically corrected
     hyperbolic approximation on bounded square-root ranges, we determine the signed
     first error and the sharp constant in the uniform N −1/4 rate. We explain the spectral
     application and isolate two failures in an intermediate conditioning formula without
     asserting a new graph eigenvalue theorem.

1     The question and the proof mechanism
A permutation matrix has one nonzero entry in every row and column. Its entries are therefore
strongly dependent, even though each entry has mean 1/N . This dependence has a useful
consequence. Products of suitably separated centered entries are far smaller in expectation
than one would predict by taking absolute values first.
    For a uniform permutation matrix M of size N , two distinct diagonal cells satisfy

Two cells in the same row instead have centered product expectation −N −2 . The pattern of
requested cells matters. The smaller scale in (1) is cancellation, not independence.
    The mixed-product estimate in Bordenave’s Proposition 28 [1] exploits this distinction. It
also appears in Bordenave and Collins [5, Proposition 22]. Brito, Dumitriu and Harris import
it as Lemma 7 in their preprint, numbered Lemma 5.6 in the published article [2]. We give a
proof built from three operations:
    1. Keep the isolated centered singletons together.
    2. Condition only on a compatible set of prescribed matches. The remaining matrix is
       exactly a smaller uniform permutation matrix.
    3. Use its row sum to obtain a positive recurrence, and only then expand the remaining
       factors.
This order prevents the triangle inequality from destroying the cancellation we want to
estimate.

8                              Where the estimate enters spectral-gap arguments
This section identifies the application of Theorem 2.1 and explains the surrounding mechanism.

8.1                              Random lifts: one permutation per base edge
In Bordenave’s model, choose one orientation of each undirected edge of a finite base graph.
Attach an independent uniform permutation of [n] to each chosen edge. Its reverse orientation
carries the inverse permutation. A lift edge between fibers is represented by a matrix entry
of this permutation, and reversing the edge transposes the same entry.

Base edges become colors in (3). Distinct requested lift edges become distinct cells. Their
multiplicities and consistency conditions are unchanged. The parameter a1 of [1, Proposition

8.3   Why isolated centered factors help a trace expansion
The non-backtracking matrix is indexed by oriented graph edges. Its entry from an oriented
edge e to an oriented edge f is one exactly when the head of e is the tail of f and f is not
the reverse of e. This definition permits a return along a different parallel edge. In a simple
graph, it reduces to v = x and y ≠ u for consecutive edges u → v and x → y. Thus powers of
the matrix count walks that never immediately reverse the same edge.
    The matrix is generally not normal. A safe norm estimate uses

                                     ∥C∥2s ≤ tr (CC ∗ )s .
                                                           


Expanding the right side for a centered path matrix produces products of matching entries. A
product that uses a distinct matches pstarts at scale L−a . Each consistent centered singleton
gives an additional factor of order a/L in our bound. Repeated cells and conflicting
singletons have different costs and must be counted separately.
    Choosing locations for a circuit contributes positive powers of the graph size. Matching
probabilities cancel most of them. Excess cycles and isolated centered factors supply the
savings that remain. The combinatorial argument must sum these estimates over all allowed
circuit shapes. The mixed-product theorem handles each fixed pattern, but does not perform
this sum.
    Short neighborhoods are typically tree-like, with a controlled allowance for a single cycle.
The papers restrict to such paths and estimate the remainder from more complicated local
tangles. Non-backtracking walks make this separation useful because immediate reversals
would create many large, uninformative contributions even on a tree.

10     Contribution, limitations and further questions
The mixed-product theorems identify attained maxima when there are zero, one or two
inconsistent centered singletons. Their proofs keep the exact depleted moment, merge colors
through positive partition weights, and handle arbitrary multiplicities through affine indicator
identities. For one distinguished inconsistent singleton, an exact kernel difference exchanges
a missing conflicting neighbor for one extra prescribed match. This also improves the general
inconsistent-singleton bound. The scalar consequences recover the spectral estimate with a
smaller singleton factor and an optimal universal prefactor. The diagonal results determine
the original cubic threshold, the parity transition, a hierarchy of further corrections and sharp
first uniform errors for both the square-root and full-range approximations.
     The base recurrence, its normalized parity form and the leading coefficients belong to
Collins and Nagatsu [3]. The mixed estimate itself is already part of Bordenave’s spectral-gap
method [1]. The special-function identities and the saddle method are classical. The attained
envelope at fixed support and singleton counts is the principal finite extremal result. The
diagonal theorems give explicit uniform consequences and elementary proofs in the probabilis-
tic normalization, including the necessity and bounded-comparability characterization of the
cubic scale. Classical uniform asymptotic theory supplies another route to the corrections.
We do not claim a new special-function method or base historical novelty on a mismatch
with only one prior parameter regime.                                                    √
