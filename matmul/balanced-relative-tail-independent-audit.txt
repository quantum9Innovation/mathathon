1. Nonzero forms and a common explicit witness
---------------------------------------------
The Gram identity W_c=J+lambda P_c gives rank(Wbar)<=ell<m, so
Tbar=Wbar-I is nonzero for every realization. Tbar is symmetric,
has zero diagonal and annihilates 1. Thus its nonzero restriction to
1-perp is a nonzero quadratic form. Direct substitution confirms

  Tbar_ij=n_ij/[d(m-q)],
  n_ij=(m-1)K_ij-d(q-1), i!=j,

where K_ij is the integer number of colors in which that pair collides.
Not all n_ij vanish, and |n_ij|<=C0=d(m-1).

For X=C0+2 and z_i=X^(2^i), the exponents 2^i+2^j, i<j, are all
different. Therefore R(X)=sum_(i<j)n_ij X^(2^i+2^j) is the value of
a nonzero integer polynomial with coefficients bounded by C0. If D
is the highest occupied exponent, its leading term has magnitude >=X^D,
while every lower term together has magnitude at most
C0*(X^D-1)/(X-1)<X^D. Hence |R(X)|>=1.

Centering z does not change its quadratic value because Tbar*1=0.
For x=(z-mean(z)1)/||z-mean(z)1|| this proves the same positive bound

  |x^T Tbar x|>=2/[d(m-q)||z-mean(z)1||^2]=mu

for all realizations. Its enormous coordinate size is disclosed; this
is a fixed witness, not a practical data-preparation or bit-cost claim.
