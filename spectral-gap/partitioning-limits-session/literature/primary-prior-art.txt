PRIMARY LITERATURE AUDIT -- 15 November 2026
==========================================

Scope: targeted searches and the specified sections below, not an exhaustive
bibliographic search or certification of historical priority.

1. Guattery--Miller, On the Quality of Spectral Separators (1998;
   extended abstract SODA 1995).
   https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/GuatteryMiller98.pdf

   Read abstract/introduction, algorithm definitions, Theorem 6.3 proof,
   and Section 7 through Theorem 7.1 proof. Bounded-degree planar graphs
   already give median Fiedler bisections of size Theta(n) despite an
   O(1) bisection. Their tree-cross-path family gives the asymptotically
   worst Cheeger ratio for the best threshold cut. Their generalized
   algorithm permits an arbitrary scalar function of each vertex's first
   k eigenvector coordinates, then thresholding; identical coordinate
   fibers defeat every such method. This includes fixed k, and they
   analyze mildly growing k. Do not claim arbitrary graph algorithms
   given eigenvectors are covered: graph-aware refinements may split fibers.

2. d'Orsi--Jones--Ruotolo--Vadhan--Zhang, Sparsest cut and eigenvalue
   multiplicities on low degree Abelian Cayley graphs, arXiv:2412.17115v3,
   30 September 2025; APPROX/RANDOM 2025 version.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17115v3
   https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX/RANDOM.2025.16

   Read introduction/results, formal solution-dimension definition,
   Theorems 1.7/1.10/1.11, and appendix headings/statements. Solution
   dimension measures the number of first nontrivial eigenvectors needed
   to approximate SOME nearoptimal centered normalized cut. Basic
   Rayleigh spectral-tail control approximates ALL sparse cuts in an
   eigenvalue threshold space; small threshold rank gives exponential
   nets. Theorem 1.7 combines enumeration with cut improvement in time
   poly(n) exp{SD_(eps^3,c) O(log(1/eps))}. Abelian Cayley graphs have
   much smaller solution dimension than naive threshold-rank predictions;
   all their sparsest cuts are approximable by boundedly many low modes
   at fixed degree/accuracy. Thus the bare all-cutspace/net observation
   is prior art, and solution dimension is a current useful comparison.

3. Peng--Yoshida, Average Sensitivity of Spectral Clustering, KDD 2020.
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04094

   Read abstract/introduction and located its separate unnormalized,
   normalized, and k-way theorem sections. It studies expected change
   after RANDOM edge deletions, not adversarial single-edge doubling.
   Guarantees depend on higher eigenvalues/cluster structure. The authors
   explicitly motivate averaging using adversarial dangling-set failures.
   Our dense flat example excludes dangling-set localization but should
   be positioned as a worst-case obstruction, not a refutation of these
   averaged estimates.

4. Noschese--Reichel, Sensitivity of Perron and Fiedler eigenpairs to
   structural perturbations of a network, NLA 2025, arXiv:2509.16024v1.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16024v1

   Read introduction and located Proposition 5. It explicitly studies
   one-edge weight perturbations, classical eigenvalue derivatives,
   rank-one interlacing, and eigenvector condition numbers. Proposition 5
   is the familiar reciprocal spectral-separation condition number for a
   normal-matrix simple eigendirection. No inspected statement gives
   arbitrary-many nearly orthogonal Fiedler directions along one monotone
   edge path, or a flat optimal-bisection counterexample with vanishing
   relative fractional response. This is a bounded search outcome only.

5. Schuster--Liskiewicz, New Abilities and Limitations of Spectral Graph
   Bisection, ESA 2017, arXiv:1701.01337.
   https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol087-esa2017/LIPIcs.ESA.2017.66/LIPIcs.ESA.2017.66.pdf

   Search hit and abstract/conclusion excerpts checked, not full proof
   read. The subject is Boppana's diagonally optimized spectral bisection
   bound and certification, including structural obstructions. A generic
   PSD certificate for exact bisection is not a new idea; Boppana (1987),
   Coja-Oghlan (2005/2006), and SDP dual-certificate work are essential
   comparisons if proposing a certificate that repairs Fiedler failures.

OTHER PRIMARY LEADS
-------------------
* Guglielmi--Sicilia, A low rank ODE for spectral clustering stability,
  arXiv:2306.04596 (2023): structured distance to a vanishing eigengap;
  a rank-four ODE. Abstract checked. Not a gap-free stability theorem.
* Urschel--Zikatanov, On the Maximal Error of Spectral Approximation of
  Graph Bisection, arXiv:1512.06325: quadratic-order error in unbounded
  degree graphs. Abstract checked.
* Haynsworth/Aronszajn--Krein/secular-equation literature is the natural
  prior-art family for a many-direction rank-one eigenvector-path theorem.
  A geometric method for eigenvalue problems with low-rank perturbations
  (2017), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5627089/ , explicitly
  parametrizes low-rank eigenvalue bifurcations through resolvents.
  Targeted search did not find a theorem bounding or realizing arbitrarily
  many nearly orthogonal lowest eigendirections on one positive rank-one
  path. No absence-of-literature claim is justified by that search.

PROMISING TARGET AND LIMITS
---------------------------
The dense-edge-doubling-instability.tex theorem gives an exact elementary
obstruction: one weight doubled, relative fractional response O(1/n),
unique mutually orthogonal and uniformly flat Fiedler vectors, and unique
optimal bisections separated by n/2 vertices. It rules out trying to turn
the new sharp relative-rank theorem into a gap-free eigenvector/partition
stability theorem by merely adding delocalization or sign margins.
The construction itself uses familiar clustered-eigenvalue mechanisms;
it is not offered as a breakthrough or historically new result.

A much stronger target is an unbounded metric entropy of the Fiedler
trajectory along one monotone edge-weight interval while the TOTAL relative
perturbation tends to zero. A diagonal-plus-rank-one matrix model can make
many low positive spectral scales dominate in succession; the challenge
is a graph-edge realization retaining balanced and delocalized directions,
and separating this from classical secular-equation consequences.

UPDATE: CLOSER PRIMARY RANK-ONE TRAJECTORY COMPARISONS
--------------------------------------------------
* Soda--Kempf, Exact Diagonalization of Sums of Hamiltonians and Products
  of Unitaries, arXiv:2206.09927v2, 6 June 2025 (v1 title Newton cradle
  spectra). https://arxiv.org/html/2206.09927v2 . Read main-text rank-one
  sections through Equation (15). Equation (13) explicitly gives all
  rank-one eigenvector coordinates as normalized resolvent entries. This
  is the direct formula underlying the proposed geometric-scale mechanism;
  the matrix mechanism should be credited to classical secular calculus.
* Gabbassov--Kempf, Adiabatic Dynamics of Entanglement, QST10 045054
  (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18301 . Current v4 October2026 changes
  abstract only according to version comments; inspected v2 introduction,
  Section II.1, references, and Appendix A/B. It describes nearorthogonal
  exchanges at avoided crossings and cascades transporting a direction
  through successive adjacent eigenvalue indices.
* Gabbassov--Singh--Kempf, Principles of Quantum Optimization for
  Constrained Problems, arXiv:2607.14227v1 July2026,
  https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14227v1 . Read Section VI.2 Theorem1 and
  Corollary1.1. Same adjacent-index cascade stated explicitly.
* Distinction for the parent trajectory theorem: a FIXED LOWEST index
  visits many almostorthogonal directions inside its FIRST interlacing
  interval, with a genuine graph edge, balanced flat rounded cuts, and
  vanishing total relative norm. The inspected sources do not state
  those combined features. This is still a narrow novelty boundary, not
  historical priority or a new general perturbation technique.
