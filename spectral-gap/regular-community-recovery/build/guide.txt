            Reading guide: exact recovery in regular block models
                  November 15, 2026        •   Main paper: regular-community-recovery.pdf

Main result. For every fixed pair of integers a > b > 0, two equal classes with independent uniform
simple a-regular internal graphs and an independent uniform simple b-regular cross graph admit deter-
ministic polynomial-time exact recovery, up to exchanging the classes, with probability 1 − Oa,b (N −2 ).
This holds at every admissible class size N , including odd N when a is even. The degree parameters are
supplied to the algorithm. No planted-eigenvalue outlier inequality is imposed.
Write n = 2N , D = a + b, δ = a − b. The planted signs satisfy Aσ = δσ. Recovery follows from two
ingredients:

The second exponent is at least two. These statements do not require the real eigenspace to be one-
dimensional.
Read the main proof in this order. Section 2 counts configurations by three aggregate edge tables.
Uniform-fiber entropy bounds every collection of local profiles at once, avoiding a degree-dependent count
of those profiles. Pairing weights cancel the entropy exponent. Only polynomially many aggregate tables
remain, giving the universal binary exponent 24.
Section 3 proves sign uniqueness. A conditional-variance/Pinsker estimate gives the explicit entropy
deficit Φ ≤ −(2b2 /a4 )α2 (1 − α)2 at overlap α. It works even at (a, b) = (2, 1). Small overlaps are
excluded by exposing the support of the difference of two sign eigenvectors: every exterior neighbor must
receive at least two support edges.
Section 4 turns the estimates into an algorithm. Solve (A + DI)z = (b mod 2)1 over F2 , cap the affine
solution count at n27 , enumerate, and check exactly b opposite neighbors at every vertex over the integers.
The cap fails with probability Oa,b (N −3 ). The bounded algorithm uses OD (n28 ) elementary work and
O(n2 ) storage; the uncut enumerator uses expected Oa,b (n25 ) work. These are conservative proof bounds,
not practical runtime estimates.
Model transfer. For this main theorem, conditioning the three configuration factors on simplicity
has positive limiting probability and yields exactly the requested independent uniform-simple laws. No
matching-sum contiguity, local central limit theorem, even-size restriction beyond admissibility, or real-
corank-one assertion is used.

For the fixed-frame theorem, the matching-model moment is 2pℓp + o(1) at each fixed odd prime. Integer
minors, Bézout identities, and elementary residue-root primes transfer real extra multiplicity to a modular
event. The limit in size is taken before arbitrarily large fixed primes. Contiguity transfers the vanishing
event, not the unbounded kernel expectation. This argument gives no effective convergence rate or
numerical eigengap.
Faster certified algorithm: Section 7. For a specified integer eigenvalue and a one-dimensional
balanced sign kernel, an exact randomized algorithm returns certified signs or abstains. Expected bit
work is O eD (n2 log(1/η)); success is at least 1 − η under the promise. A shifted Wiedemann recurrence
certifies a pinned minor’s nonsingularity, and integer checks certify the signs. There are no false accepted
certificates. Prime generation is charged and terminates almost surely with finite expectation. This
runtime applies to the stronger spectral regimes above; it is not the bound established for every parameter
of the main theorem.
Credit and limits. The paper credits Brito et al.’s RSBM recovery and rigidity results, Barucca’s full-
range proposal, and Zhu–Zhu’s already stated planted-eigenvalue simplicity lemma. It supplies complete
arguments and precise quantifiers, without claiming historical firstness. Finite-field mixing, contiguity,
entropy, Pinsker, configuration conditioning, and sparse exact linear algebra are classical inputs. The
results do not establish noise stability, an effective spectral gap, growing degrees/classes, unequal classes,
unknown general frames, or adaptive graph factors. Section 8 gives the full scope comparison.
