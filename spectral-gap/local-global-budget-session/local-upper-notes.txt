COMPULSORY LOCAL BUDGET AND THE BRIDGE UPPER COEFFICIENT
Independent research proof, 2026-11-15. No frozen manuscript edited.

Claim proved below (Step 3 includes the needed Fredholm identity):

Let 0<alpha<1 and d>=3. Let W^{bridge}_{d,alpha}(epsilon) be the
supremum of the strict correction count over finite connected simple
unit-weight hosts of maximum degree <=d, where the doubled edge is a
BRIDGE. Then

 limsup_{epsilon downarrow0} epsilon W^{bridge}_{d,alpha}(epsilon)
       <= alpha log(2d/(d+2)).

For d=3 this is alpha log(6/5). This proves an upper bound, not its
attainability. The entire local determinant is compulsory. In fact,
each infinite rooted limit gives its own sharper residual budget
alpha log 2-alpha log(1+tau_wired).

1. ROOTED LIMITS AND CONTINUOUS FUNCTIONAL CALCULUS

Orient every edited edge (u_n,v_n). Bounded-degree edge-rooted graphs
are sequentially compact in the topology of rooted balls. A subsequence
converges to a connected finite or countably infinite rooted graph G.
The limiting edited edge e=(u,v) remains a bridge: any alternative u-v
path is finite and would occur in all sufficiently large rooted balls
of G_n, contradicting the bridge assumption.

Write A_n=L(G_n), B_n=A_n+b_n b_n^*, and A=L(G), B=A+bb^*.
The old Laplacians have norm <=2d, the updated ones norm <=2d+2.
For finitely supported test functions f_i on G, transport them into
G_n after the supporting rooted ball is identified, writing f_{i,n}.
For every polynomial p, every matrix entry

 <f_{i,n},p(A_n)f_{j,n}> and <f_{i,n},p(B_n)f_{j,n}>

equals its limiting entry eventually: a polynomial of degree k only
uses a fixed larger rooted ball. Uniform polynomial approximation of
x^alpha on [0,2d+2] then gives

 Q_n(i,j)=<f_{i,n},A_n^alpha f_{j,n}>
                  -> Q(i,j)=<f_i,A^alpha f_j>,
 D_n(i,j)=<f_{i,n},(B_n^alpha-A_n^alpha)f_{j,n}>
                  -> D(i,j)=<f_i,(B^alpha-A^alpha)f_j>.

There is no inverse power in these convergent quantities.

If G is infinite, A is injective on ell^2(VG). Indeed zero energy
forces a function to be constant on connected G, and an ell^2 constant
on an infinite graph is zero. Thus for linearly independent f_i,
Q is strictly positive definite, and Q_n is eventually positive
definite. The generalized Ritz matrices

                    Q_n^{-1/2} D_n Q_n^{-1/2}

converge in norm to Q^{-1/2} D Q^{-1/2}.

Finite-graph constants cause no difficulty. Replacing f_{i,n} by
P_n f_{i,n}, where P_n projects onto constants-perpendicular, leaves
both Q_n and D_n unchanged; Q_n>0 guarantees these projected test
vectors are independent. Therefore finite generalized Ritz eigenvalues
are bounded above by the ordered eigenvalues of the actual finite
relative correction C_n.

If the limit G is finite, local convergence implies G_n is eventually
isomorphic to G: use a rooted radius strictly larger than its diameter,
which exposes all vertex degrees. In that case all relevant counts
are bounded by |VG|, hence epsilon times the counts tends to zero.

2. GENERALIZED RITZ EXHAUSTS THE ENTIRE LOCAL RESPONSE

The single edge term obeys bb^*<=A. Hence b belongs to Ran A^{1/2},
tau=||A^{-1/2}b||^2<=1, and B<=2A. Operator monotonicity yields

             A^alpha <= B^alpha <= 2^alpha A^alpha.

Consequently the relative form defines a unique bounded positive
operator C on ell^2(VG) satisfying

 <A^{alpha/2}f,C A^{alpha/2}g>
              =<f,(B^alpha-A^alpha)g>.

The defining range is dense. More particularly,

                 A^{alpha/2}(c_00(VG))

is dense in ell^2(VG): c_00 is dense, A^{alpha/2} is bounded, and
Ran A^{alpha/2} is dense since ker A=0.

Assume C is compact (Step 3 gives trace class). Let lambda_j(C) be
its decreasing eigenvalues, with zero padding. For each fixed k and
eta>0, approximate orthonormal top eigenvectors of C by vectors
A^{alpha/2}f_i, f_i finitely supported. The resulting k-dimensional
compression has jth eigenvalue at least lambda_j(C)-eta, j<=k,
once the approximations are sufficiently close. Step 1 transports
this compression to G_n. Ordinary compression min-max gives

                 liminf_n lambda_j(C_n)>=lambda_j(C)

for every fixed j. This proves compulsory capture of every fixed
number of local eigenvalues. Neither strong convergence of C_n nor
convergence of A_n^{-alpha/2} is needed.

3. INFINITE-DIMENSIONAL TRACE CLASS / FREDHOLM INPUT

The exact identity needed in Step 4 is

 log det_F(I+C)=Tr log(I+C)=alpha log(1+tau),
               tau=||A^{-1/2}b||^2.

This identity applies to any bounded injective A>=0 and b with finite
tau, including inf spec A=0. The following regularization proof was
independently suggested by the Fredholm subtask and avoids unbounded
inverse-form manipulations.

For B_s=A+sbb^*, m(t)=<b,(A+t)^{-1}b>,
w_t=A^{-alpha/2}(A+t)^{-1}b, and c_alpha=sin(pi alpha)/pi,

 C_s=c_alpha int_0^infinity t^alpha [s/(1+s m(t))] w_t w_t^* dt.

For fixed t>0 the vector w_t exists: its squared norm is the spectral
integral of lambda^{-alpha}(lambda+t)^{-2} against d<b,E_A(lambda)b>,
which is bounded by a constant depending on t times tau. The positive
integral H with denominator removed has

 Tr H=c_alpha int_0^infinity t^alpha||w_t||^2 dt=alpha tau,

by Tonelli and
c_alpha int_0^infinity t^alpha/(lambda+t)^2 dt=alpha lambda^{alpha-1}.
Thus the integral defining C_s is trace class. Its pairing with
A^{alpha/2}f agrees with the usual bounded-operator fractional power
difference by Sherman--Morrison, so it is precisely the relative form
operator from Step 2.

Now put A_delta=A+delta I, B_{delta,s}=A_delta+sbb^*, and define
C_{delta,s} by the same formulas with A replaced by A_delta. All
negative powers are bounded. Let R_{delta,s}=I+C_{delta,s}. The
resolvent formula gives trace-norm differentiability in s. Bounded
cyclicity and the scalar beta integral show

 Tr(R_{delta,s}^{-1} C_{delta,s}')
    =Tr(B_{delta,s}^{-alpha} (d/ds)B_{delta,s}^alpha)
    =c_alpha int_0^infinity t^alpha
        <b,(B_{delta,s}+t)^{-1}B_{delta,s}^{-alpha}
                         (B_{delta,s}+t)^{-1}b> dt
    =alpha <b,B_{delta,s}^{-1}b>
    =alpha tau_delta/(1+s tau_delta),

where tau_delta=<b,A_delta^{-1}b>. The Fredholm determinant derivative
and integration over 0<=s<=1 therefore give

                  log det_F(I+C_{delta,1})
                            =alpha log(1+tau_delta).

For fixed t>0, w_{delta,t}->w_t in norm by spectral calculus, and
||w_{delta,t}||<=||w_t||. Also m_delta(t)->m(t). Hence the rank-one
integrands for C_{delta,1} converge in trace norm pointwise in t and
are dominated by t^alpha||w_t||^2, an integrable function as above.
Trace-norm dominated convergence gives C_{delta,1}->C in trace norm.
As tau_delta increases to tau, continuity of the Fredholm determinant
in trace norm proves the exact identity. This argument works without
any positive lower bound on A.

4. SUBTRACTION FROM THE FINITE BRIDGE DETERMINANT

Every finite edited bridge has leverage one, so exactly

                  sum_j log(1+lambda_j(C_n))=alpha log 2.

Let epsilon_n downarrow0, q_n=N_{C_n}(epsilon_n), and pass to a
rooted convergent subsequence. For each fixed k the elementary estimate

 max(q_n-k,0) log(1+epsilon_n)
       <= alpha log 2 - sum_{j=1}^k log(1+lambda_j(C_n))

holds with eigenvalue zero padding. If q_n>=k it follows by counting
the remaining q_n-k strict eigenvalues; if q_n<k the left side is
zero and the right side is nonnegative because the first-k sum is
at most the entire determinant budget.

As epsilon/log(1+epsilon)->1, Step 2 implies

 limsup_n epsilon_n q_n
       <= alpha log 2 - sum_{j=1}^k log(1+lambda_j(C)).

Let k->infinity and use Step 3:

 limsup_n epsilon_n q_n
               <= alpha log[2/(1+tau_wired(G,e))].

This is the useful local/global budget inequality. In particular a
local limit with tau_wired=1 leaves zero inverse-epsilon coefficient.

5. THE WIRED BRIDGE RESISTANCE AT MAXIMUM DEGREE d

For a half H of G-e rooted at its endpoint o, define its capacity by

 cap_H(o)=inf { E_H(f): f finitely supported in H, f(o)=1 },
 r_H=1/cap_H(o), with 1/0=infinity.

If H is finite, its constant function is permitted, so cap=0 and
r=infinity. For every infinite H the root degree is <=d-1, and
every nonroot vertex has at most d-1 neighbors farther from the root.
Set q=d-1. The number of edges crossing distance levels h,h+1 is
at most q^{h+1}. The finitely supported radial trial function

 f_R(x)=(q^{-dist(o,x)}-q^{-R})/(1-q^{-R})  if dist(o,x)<R,
         0                                              otherwise

has energy at most

 sum_{h=0}^{R-1} q^{h+1}
        [(q^{-h}-q^{-h-1})/(1-q^{-R})]^2
                    =(q-1)/(1-q^{-R}).

Edges within a distance level contribute zero. Taking R->infinity
gives cap_H(o)<=d-2, hence

                           r_H>=1/(d-2).

This direct trial-function proof avoids any regularity, transience,
or tree assumption. Equivalently it is the usual cutset/flow bound.

Let r_+,r_- denote the two half resistances. The whole-graph wired
edge resistance, defined by the ell^2 inverse quadratic form or by
finitely supported energy tests, is

 tau_wired=sup_{f in c_00} |f(u)-f(v)|^2/E_G(f)
           =(r_++r_-)/(1+r_++r_-),

with value one if either r is infinite. To see the formula, hold
f(u)=a and f(v)=c. The infimum of half energies is a^2/r_+ +c^2/r_-;
add the bridge energy (a-c)^2 and minimize subject to a-c=1.
The minimum total energy is 1+1/(r_++r_-). The half infima can be
approached independently by finite-support functions, proving equality
even when capacities vanish. Equality with ||A^{-1/2}b||^2 is the
spectral-theorem variational characterization, using that c_00 is an
energy core because A is bounded.

Consequently

                         tau_wired>=2/d.

For the two (d-1)-ary regular tree halves, r_+=r_-=1/(d-2), so this
geometric lower bound is exact. This alone does not prove the spectral
count coefficient is attained by finite graphs.

6. UNIFORM SUPREMAL UPPER BOUND

The finite determinant budget makes W^{bridge}_{d,alpha}(epsilon)
finite for every positive epsilon. The strict count is integer valued,
so its finite supremum is attained among the nonempty host class.
Choose epsilon_n realizing any candidate limiting upper coefficient,
and maximizing hosts. Extract a rooted convergent subsequence. A finite
limit gives coefficient zero by Step 1. An infinite limit is covered
by Steps 4 and 5, which yield

 limsup epsilon_n W^{bridge}_{d,alpha}(epsilon_n)
       <=alpha log[2/(1+2/d)]=alpha log(2d/(d+2)).

This proves the claimed bound. It applies only to bridge edits; a
nonbridge local limit need not split into two independent half graphs.

PROOF STATUS / SHARPNESS

The upper argument by itself does not give a matching finite-graph
lower construction. All upper steps above are explicit and require
no positive spectral gap. No assertion that high response vanishes
in a reservoir limit is used here. A later companion lower construction
was independently audited in the addendum below.

ADDENDUM: INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF THE REPEATED-BLOCK LOWER PLAN

The separate sharpness-notes.txt was subsequently completed. Its
multiport construction repairs the one-port leaf obstruction and was
checked against the following potential failure points; no gap was
found in this audit.

* Each of the two complete binary top trees has access impedance
  1-1/Q, because layer h contributes J^2/2^h. Together with the old
  bridge resistance one this gives 3-2/Q; the doubled bridge instead
  gives 5/2-2/Q. There is no missing factor of two.

* The ideal circuit has an automorphism group transitive on the Q
  copied bottom trees and preserving each leaf color. Uniqueness of
  the ideal current solution therefore gives j_i/Q at every port of
  target reservoir i, for arbitrary signed j. The bottom-tree energy
  is j^T R_B j/Q and neck energy is sum_i ell_i j_i^2/Q.

* Mean-coordinate Thomson duality is valid. Injecting j_i into the
  reservoir mean coordinate means uniform source j_i/n_i at all its
  vertices. A port current vector a_i with total j_i leaves the
  internal zero-sum load P_i a_i-(j_i/n_i)1, with minimum internal
  energy a_i^T H_i a_i, H_i=P_i^T L_i^dagger P_i. Thus dropping
  contacts gives the ideal lower impedance bound; the symmetric ideal
  flow is an admissible actual trial flow, costing at most
  (R/Q)(sum_i j_i^2+J^2). The uniform matrix error bound follows.

* Subdividing Q distinct expander edges once preserves a gap uniformly
  in Q: each old vertex can be assigned at most three new ports,
  yielding total variance <=(2+14/gamma_0) times energy. Hence
  0<=H_i<=R I with R independent of Q or volume. For fixed Q,
  reservoir sizes old-even-plus-Q approximate arbitrary fixed masses.

* The bridge carries J in every admissible flow, irrespective of
  contact asymmetry. Exactly Z'(H)=Z(H)-(1/2)11^T. Thus the terminal
  stiffness change is positive rank one uniformly over all contacts.
  The limit pair (diag(t)+3 11^T)^-1 and
  (diag(t)+(5/2)11^T)^-1 is correct.

* The inverse of f(g)=(2/5+g)/(1/3+g) is
  g=(2/5-r/3)/(r-1). For r_i=(6/5)exp(-is/m), s<log(6/5),
  g_0=0 and all increments q_i=g_i-g_{i-1} are finite and positive.
  Setting t_i=1/q_i gives every reverse logarithmic pivot exactly s/m.

* A full binary bottom tree exists for every integer m>=2; requiring
  Q to be a power of two places no restriction on the final count m.
  Choosing m just below alpha(s-eta)/log(1+epsilon) therefore avoids
  a geometric-sequence loss in the leading coefficient.

* The graph is simple and has maximum degree three: root bridge
  endpoints have two children; a top right leaf identified with a
  bottom root has one parent and two children; reservoir ports have
  two internal neighbors and one external edge. All target reservoirs
  lie on the right and only the anchor reservoir lies on the left,
  so the edited edge remains a bridge.

* Q is fixed before mass separation and reservoir volume limits.
  Therefore the core is finite during the existing reservoir-limit
  argument. Its constants may depend on Q,m,delta; no uniformity in
  those parameters is claimed or needed. Compact contact-family
  uniformity permits choosing mass separation and anchor mass before
  knowing the actual large-volume contact matrices.

Thus the later matching lower construction, together with the upper
proved here, supports the exact cubic bridge coefficient

             lim_{epsilon downarrow0} epsilon
                      W^{bridge}_{3,alpha}(epsilon)
                           =alpha log(6/5).

The lower proof still depends on the separated-mass and relative
reservoir lemmas in the frozen manuscript; its multiport modification
is explicitly justified in the companion notes. This audit does not
claim external peer review or formal verification.
