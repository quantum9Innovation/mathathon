1. ALL-DEGREE LOWER CONSTRUCTION

Set q=d-1 and Q=q^h. A full depth-h q-ary tree contributes
J^2 sum_{k=1}^h q^{-k}=J^2(1-1/Q)/(d-2), exactly as stated. Two
such trees and bridge resistance r_b yield the common impedance
r_b+2(1-1/Q)/(d-2). Thus the target common resistances are
R_d=d/(d-2) and R'_d=(d+2)/(2(d-2)), whose ratio is 2d/(d+2).

The top trees have degree at most q+1=d. A top-right leaf identified
with a binary bottom root has one parent and two children, so degree
three. Reservoir ports have internal degree two and one external
attachment. The same cubic reservoirs therefore work for all d>=3.

Q being a power of q does not restrict the arbitrary integer target
count m. Bottom trees may have any m>=2 leaves. For fixed m and target
neck impedances t_i>0, integer ell_i can satisfy ell_i/Q->t_i along
Q=q^h. The exact f(g)-inverse tuning and reverse pivot formulas remain
valid after replacing cubic common resistances by R_d,R'_d.

2. BIPARTITE RESERVOIRS: GAP AND COLORING

The bipartite double cover of a connected nonbipartite cubic graph
with two-sided nontrivial adjacency gap is connected and cubic. Its
adjacency eigenvalues are plus/minus the original ones; the new -3 is
simple. The positive Laplacian gap remains uniformly bounded below.

Replacing Q distinct edges by paths of length three preserves the
bipartition and supplies two new vertices of opposite colors per edge.
The old graph energy is <=3 times the new energy. Assigning each new
vertex to its adjacent old endpoint assigns at most three new vertices
to each old vertex, so

 V_new <=2 E+6 V_old,
 V_all <=2 E+7 V_old <=(2+21/gamma_0) E.

Hence the displayed uniform gap is correct, independently of Q and
reservoir volume. For fixed Q, sizes 4k+2Q approximate any prescribed
positive mass ratios under common scaling, and enough selected edges
exist when k is large.

All anchor attachment vertices occur at one depth. For any target
label i, the binary bottom leaf depth is the same in every repeated
copy, and all necks ell_i have the same length. Thus all ports used by
a given reservoir require the same color. On each length-three
subdivided edge either required port color is available. This produces
a consistent coloring of the complete host; varying depths across
different labels create no problem because labels use different
reservoirs. Adjusting neck parity by O(1) also preserves the limit.

3. REPLACEMENT RESERVOIR REALIZATION LEMMA

The new compressed-resolvent argument is valid and removes the need
to estimate leakage into a high spectral subspace. With fixed core
and fixed port count,

 A_a=O(a^-1), B_a=O(a^-1/2), F_a>=xi I.

Uniform internal gaps control mean-zero reservoir norms. The finite
core's Dirichlet problem with reservoir pins zero controls its norm
by external edge energy plus fixed port deviations. Connected fixed
incidence gives this positive Dirichlet stiffness and the stated
one-dimensional common kernel. This assumption holds in the actual
construction and is now explicit in the standalone lemma.

Block inversion gives

 G_a(t)=[aS_a+t+
 aB_a(F_a^-1-(F_a+t/a)^-1)B_a^*]^-1.

For each fixed t>0 the correction is O(t/a), since B_a=O(a^-1/2).
Thus G_a(t)->(H+t)^-1. The first moment P_a^*aL_aP_a=aA_a is
uniformly bounded. The fractional operator integral therefore has
the a-independent envelope

                  t^(alpha-1) min(1,C/t),

integrable at both endpoints for 0<alpha<1. Dominated convergence
proves compressed positive fractional-power convergence for both
endpoint graph Laplacians. No uniform bound on aL_a is required;
no inverse-power convergence or vanishing high response is asserted.

Taking V into the limiting common-kernel complement yields a strictly
positive limiting fractional-energy Gram matrix. The finite constant
direction need not equal its limit: projecting test vectors off finite
constants changes neither generalized Gram matrix, and limiting
positivity gives eventual independence. Generalized Ritz convergence
and min-max then transfer the required positive relative eigenvalues.

The compact contact-family step is valid because Q, all core and port
dimensions, and all masses are fixed before reservoir volumes grow.

4. ALL-EDGE FREE/WIRED EXTENSION

The shell exhaustion works for connected or disconnected H=G-e;
every component contains u or v. Free conductances increase. The
shell-zero admissible families increase when extended by zero,
proving the decreasing wired/capacity limits. Exhausted finite
components have capacity zero and cause no exception.

Retaining individual shell vertices before Kron elimination is
essential and is done correctly. Nonnegative star elimination gives
direct uv conductance c and terminal-shell totals a,b. Therefore

 Cf_R>=c,
 Cw_R=c+ab/(a+b),
 p_R=a+cb/(c+b)>=a,
 q_R=b+ca/(c+a)>=b.

The zero-denominator cases obey the stated convention. Passing to
the limit yields Cw_H-Cf_H<=pq/(p+q). The radial trial applies to
each root of H even if H is connected, since each root has degree
at most d-1; hence p,q<=d-2. Restoring e adds one to both
conductances, proving w>=r/[1+(d-2)r/2]. The ratio upper bound is
increasing for 0<=r<=1 and reaches 2d/(d+2) at one.

For finite approximants, Rayleigh monotonicity on every embedded fixed
finite connected subgraph gives lim tau_n<=Rf_G(u,v). The local
Fredholm/Ritz budget uses wired resistance. Subtracting these budgets
and applying the electrical comparison is correct.

5. RIGIDITY CONSEQUENCE

Root's rigidity deduction checks. Equality of
(1+r)/(1+w)=2d/(d+2), d>=3, forces r=1 by strict monotonicity of
the scalar upper bound. An alternative finite u-v path would force
r<1, so e is a bridge. Its half capacities must both equal d-2.
If any BFS cut has fewer than (d-1)^(h+1) edges, the radial trial's
limiting energy is strictly below d-2 by the fixed deficit times
the squared radial drop. All cuts are therefore maximal. The degree
constraints then force one parent and d-1 distinct children at each
nonroot vertex, with no same-level edges. The halves are complete
(d-1)-ary trees.

For a sequence saturating the spectral coefficient, refine any local
subsequence until finite leverages converge. Equality throughout the
budget chain forces the electrical ratio to be maximal. Thus only
the d-regular tree can occur as an edge-rooted local limit, and also
tau_n->1. Finite local limits are excluded by their zero scaled
counts. This proves qualitative rigidity, not a quantitative rate.
