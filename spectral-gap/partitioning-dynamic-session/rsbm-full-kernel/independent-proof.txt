FULL BASE-FIELD KERNEL IN EXPECTED NEAR-QUADRATIC TIMES CORANK WORK
Independent proof audit; November 15, 2026

This note writes only in the authorized rsbm-full-kernel directory.
It audits the proposal in rsbm-nearquadratic-full-kernel-root.txt.
The linear algebra ingredients are classical. The RSBM moment bound
is an INPUT to the averaging corollary, not proved in this note.

VERDICT
-------
The full-kernel algorithm works for EVERY fixed sparse symmetric
matrix M over one fixed odd prime field, including M=0 and full-rank
M. It is Las Vegas, never produces an incorrect basis or rank, and
has expected soft-O(n^2(r+1)) BIT cost at fixed row sparsity, where
r is the actual BASE-FIELD corank. There is no real-corank-one
promise, spectral gap, or exceptional-graph fallback.

The essential certification remains the shifted-sequence inequality
ell<=rank(M). Collecting n-ell independent, verified kernel vectors
supplies the matching upper bound. Bad preconditioning or projection
can delay this agreement but cannot make a false certificate pass.

A fixed base-field moment E p0^r=O(1) then suffices for unconditional
expected soft-O(n^2) kernel construction AND exact listing of all
admissible integer sign assignments. Enumeration must use incremental
updates, rather than spending O(n r) work separately per candidate;
otherwise E p0^r alone would not directly bound that extra factor r.

1. PRECISE THEOREM AND MODEL
------------------------------
Fix an odd prime p0 and a positive row-sparsity bound Delta. Let
M be any symmetric n-by-n matrix over F_p0, with at most Delta n
nonzero entries. Put

  k=rank_Fp0 M,       r=n-k.

There is a randomized algorithm returning:

  * a basis B0 in F_p0^{n x r} of ker_Fp0 M;
  * the exact rank k;
  * a noninteractive certificate deterministically checkable within
    soft-O(n^2(r+1)) bit operations.

It always returns a correct result, terminates almost surely, and
uses expected soft-O(n^2(r+1)) bit operations. The suppressed factors
are logarithmic in n, with constants allowed to depend on fixed p0
and Delta. Storage can be O(n(r+1) log n) bits up to logarithmic
factors; the output itself has n r base-field entries.

The statement is for base-field rank. If M is an integer matrix
reduced modulo p0, its rational rank need not equal its base-field
rank. Exact INTEGER sign verification in the final enumeration step
removes spurious modular solutions. No modular rank is silently
identified with a rational rank.

2. FINITE EXTENSION AND ITS FULL COST
--------------------------------------
Choose s minimal such that

  q=p0^s >= 64(n+1)^6.

Then q<64 p0(n+1)^6 and s=O(log n). Work in

  F_q = F_p0[X]/(h(X)),

where h is an irreducible monic polynomial of degree s. Rank does
not change on extending the field, so rank_Fq M=k and its corank
remains r.

Construct h by sampling monic degree-s polynomials and testing
irreducibility EXACTLY. For s>=2 the irreducible fraction is at least
1/(2s). An elementary proof is

  p0^s=sum_{d|s} d I_p0(d),
  sum_{d|s,d<s} d I_p0(d)
       <= sum_{j=1}^{floor(s/2)} p0^j <= p0^s/2,

where I_p0(d) counts monic degree-d irreducibles; p0>=3 is enough.
For s=1 every monic linear polynomial is irreducible.

One deterministic test computes z_j=X^{p0^j} mod h for j=1,...,s,
requires z_s=X mod h, and for every prime divisor a of s requires

  gcd(h,z_{s/a}-X)=1.

The first test forces all factors to be distinct and of degrees
dividing s; the remaining tests force their degrees to be exactly s.
Thus the test is exact. Factoring the small integer s, polynomial
exponentiation, and gcd computation all take poly(s) base-field work
with elementary algorithms. Expected O(s) candidate tests therefore
cost polylog(n) bits. No irreducibility oracle is free.

Naive polynomial multiplication, reduction, and inversion modulo h
cost poly(s) base-field operations. Since p0 is fixed, every F_q
operation costs polylog(n) bits. A uniform F_q element is sampled by
s independent uniform base-field coefficients. Nonzero samples use
rejection of zero, of expected cost q/(q-1). All random choices below
have this charged representation. Construction of the field is done
once, outside the retry loop.

3. THE TWO PRIMARY PRECONDITIONING THEOREMS
--------------------------------------------
Primary source actually inspected:
Li Chen, Wayne Eberly, Erich Kaltofen, B. David Saunders,
William J. Turner, Gilles Villard,
  Efficient matrix preconditioners for black box linear algebra,
  Linear Algebra and its Applications 343--344 (2002), 119--146.
https://kaltofen.math.ncsu.edu/bibliography/02/CEKSTV02.pdf

Use independently uniform nonzero diagonal entries of D over F_q
and T=DM. Their Theorem 4.2 gives cyclicity of the nonzero part with
failure probability at most n(n-1)/(2(q-1)). Their Theorem 4.5, for
SYMMETRIC M, gives zero semisimplicity with failure probability at
most r/(q-1). Both apply over arbitrary fields, with NO restriction
that the characteristic exceed n and NO leading-minor hypothesis.
The characteristic restriction in their squarefree Theorem 4.3 is
irrelevant here.

Consequently, except with probability at most

  epsilon_D = [n(n-1)/2+r]/(q-1),                         (1)

there is a direct sum decomposition over F_q

  F_q^n = ker T direct-sum im T,
  T similar to diag(0_r,C),

where C is an invertible CYCLIC k-by-k matrix. In particular its
minimal polynomial has degree k and nonzero constant coefficient.
For k=0 this nonzero-part polynomial is 1. Left preconditioning
preserves the actual kernel: ker T=ker M because D is invertible.

4. SHIFTED KRYLOV PROBING AND AN UNCONDITIONAL LOWER CERTIFICATE
---------------------------------------------------------------
Choose u,v independently uniformly in F_q^n. Form 2n terms

  a_j = u^T T^{j+1} v,      j=0,...,2n-1.                (2)

This requires exactly 2n sparse T-matvecs and O(n^2) further field
work. Run exact Berlekamp--Massey, obtaining the actual minimum
finite-sequence recurrence LENGTH ell and its monic forward
recurrence polynomial g of degree ell. Do not use a degree padded
with zero coefficients as a substitute for the minimum length.

ALWAYS-VALID LOWER-RANK CERTIFICATE:

  ell <= rank T = k.                                    (3)

Proof. T v belongs to the invariant space im T of dimension k.
Cayley--Hamilton for T restricted to that space gives a recurrence
of order at most k for the entire sequence (2). In particular its
first 2n terms have a recurrence of order at most k. Exact minimality
of Berlekamp--Massey gives (3). This proof works for every T,u,v,
including singular matrices with nilpotent zero blocks. QED.

On the good preconditioner event in Section 3, T|_{im T}=C is
invertible and cyclic of dimension k. The projected start T v is
uniform on im T and u restricts to a uniform functional there.
The two k-dimensional controllability/observability determinants
are nonzero polynomials of degree k in these projections. Thus
Schwartz--Zippel gives joint failure probability at most 2k/q.
When both determinants are nonzero, the leading k-by-k scalar
Hankel matrix is nonsingular. Therefore the first 2n>=2k terms
have shortest recurrence length k, and their degree-k recurrence
is uniquely the minimal polynomial of C. In particular

  ell=k,       g(0)!=0,       g(C)=0.                    (4)

Let G denote this combined good preconditioner/projection event.
Its probability satisfies

  P(G^c) <= epsilon
    := [n(n-1)/2+r]/(q-1) + 2k/q = O(n^2/q).             (5)

The bound is uniform over every fixed symmetric M. With our q,
it is much smaller than a fixed constant, even in small dimension.
The k=0 case has an empty nonzero part, ell=0, and g=1 without any
projection failure.

The polynomial assertion in (4) is exact, not a guessed global
matrix identity. Full scalar complexity k forces both reachable
and observable nonzero-part spaces to have dimension k. Their
leading Hankel matrix is invertible, so its k equations determine
the monic recurrence uniquely. The actual minimal polynomial of C
satisfies those same equations, proving equality. Extra terms up
to 2n do not alter this unique recurrence.

5. ONE TRIAL: VERIFY EVERY CANDIDATE AND CAP THE PROBES
-------------------------------------------------------
One trial consists of fresh D,u,v and (2)--(4), followed by:

  (a) If ell=n, return the empty kernel basis immediately. By (3)
      the matrix is full rank, deterministically.
  (b) If g(0)=0, declare this trial unsuccessful and restart.
  (c) Otherwise set target dimension t=n-ell. Draw at most n+1
      fresh uniform vectors z in F_q^n. For each compute

        x=g(T)z

      by Horner evaluation using at most ell T-matvecs and O(n ell)
      vector field operations. VERIFY Mx=0, and retain x only if
      this exact check passes AND x is independent of the previously
      retained candidates, over F_q.
  (d) Stop successfully only after t independent verified candidates
      have been retained; otherwise restart after n+1 probes.

A successful trial ALWAYS certifies the exact kernel and rank.
Indeed (3) gives rank M>=ell, while the t=n-ell independent verified
kernel vectors give rank M<=n-t=ell. Thus rank M=ell and t=r.
This argument remains sound even if the preconditioner or projection
was bad. An underestimated ell cannot pass: it asks for more than r
independent actual kernel vectors. No random choice can fabricate
them. All comparisons, residual checks, and independence tests are
exact finite-field computations.

On event G,

  g(T) = g(0) P,

where P is the projection onto ker T along im T. The decomposition
in Section 3 makes this identity immediate: g acts by g(0) on the
zero part and by zero on C. Since P maps onto the r-dimensional
kernel and g(0)!=0, each fresh x=g(T)z is UNIFORM on ker M; samples
are independent. They all pass the residual check.

For r>=1, the expected number of uniform r-dimensional vectors
needed to span is

  sum_{j=0}^{r-1} 1/(1-q^{j-r})
    =r+sum_{i=1}^r 1/(q^i-1)
    <=r+q/(q-1)^2 <=r+2.                               (6)

For r=0 no probes are needed. After n+1 samples, failure to span
has probability at most

  1-prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(1-q^{j-(n+1)})
    <=sum_{j=0}^{r-1} q^{j-(n+1)}
    <=q^{r-n-1}/(q-1)
    <=1/[q(q-1)].                                       (7)

This is at most the probability bound for r=n. A union bound over
all nonzero linear functionals, modulo scalar, gives the same bound.
The cap is therefore safe on good trials, and controls arbitrary
bad trials without any unproved stopping heuristic.

6. COMPLETE EXPECTED COST ON EVERY FIXED INPUT
-----------------------------------------------
Let m0=nnz(M)=O(n). One application of T=D M costs O(m0+n)=O(n)
F_q operations. The sequence and recurrence phase costs O(n^2).
A polynomial probe has degree at most n and costs O(n^2) field
operations in the worst case. Testing Mx=0 costs O(n), and inserting
one candidate into a maintained echelon basis of j vectors costs
O(n(j+1)) field operations. This charges all candidate independence
work and storage, rather than treating a rank oracle as free.

On G, the expected O(r+1) probes from (6), even with the cap, cost
O(n^2(r+1)) field operations in total. The more detailed independence
cost is O(n r^2) plus rejected-dependent probes; this is bounded by
O(n^2(r+1)) because r<=n. Randomness costs O(n(r+1) log q) bits on
these good trials and is included in that bound.

On G^c, the fixed cap bounds the ENTIRE trial by O(n^3) field
operations, including all n+1 polynomial actions and independence
tests. Therefore an unconditional single-trial expectation is

  E cost(trial)
     <= O(n^2(r+1)) + O(n^3) P(G^c)
     <= O(n^2(r+1)) + O(n^5/q)
     = O(n^2(r+1)).                                     (8)

The last equality uses q>=64(n+1)^6. Thus rare bad draws cannot
contribute an uncontrolled cubic expected cost. The probability
of successful termination in a trial is at least

  (1-epsilon)(1-1/[q(q-1)]),                             (9)

which is uniformly greater than 1/2 with this q. Trials are fresh
and independent conditional on the fixed M. A renewal identity,
valid even when trial cost and success are correlated, yields

  E total cost = E cost(trial) / P(trial succeeds)
               = O(n^2(r+1)) field operations.          (10)

Every F_q operation costs polylog(n) bits, and extension construction
costs only polylog(n) bits. Hence (10) is the asserted expected
soft-O(n^2(r+1)) bitwork. It also proves almost-sure termination.
No quantitative graph-probability estimate enters this all-input
algorithmic claim.

Boundary checks:

  * If M is full rank, r=0; a good trial has ell=n and returns the
    empty basis after O(n^2) work.
  * If M=0, r=n; every sequence is zero, ell=0, g=1, and probes are
    ordinary uniform F_q^n vectors. Their basis construction may
    cost O(n^3), exactly consistent with n^2(r+1).
  * If a bad projection understates rank, target n-ell exceeds r,
    so verified independence prevents false acceptance; the cap
    prevents infinite looping within that trial.

7. DESCENT TO F_p0, FULLY CHARGED
----------------------------------
A successful trial gives r verified F_q-independent columns
v_1,...,v_r spanning ker_Fq M. Write each in the chosen polynomial
basis of F_q/F_p0:

  v_i = sum_{a=0}^{s-1} theta^a v_{i,a},
  v_{i,a} in F_p0^n.

Because M has base-field coefficients, Mv_i=0 implies
Mv_{i,a}=0 separately for every component. The r s component
vectors therefore all lie in ker_Fp0 M. Their F_q span contains
the original v_i, so their base-field rank is at least r; field
extension preserves ranks. Their base-field rank is at most r
because the base kernel has dimension r. Hence it is exactly r.

Select an F_p0 basis by ordinary incremental elimination. Coefficient
extraction costs O(n r s) base-field operations. Selecting from r s
candidates costs O(n r^2 s), and stores O(n r) base-field basis
entries, in addition to the extension-field basis if retained.
Since r<=n and s=O(log n), the bit cost is soft-O(n^2(r+1)).
The output has exactly r BASE-FIELD vectors, not extension vectors
whose enormous F_q kernel would later be enumerated.

An alternative is to choose r pivot rows I of the extension basis
V, set C=V(V_I)^{-1}, and observe that C has base-field entries:
Frobenius C^{p0} has the same kernel columns and identity pivot
coordinates as C; their unique coordinates in this kernel basis
force C^{p0}=C. Matrix inversion and multiplication cost
O(r^3+n r^2)<=O(n^2 r) F_q operations. Component extraction avoids
needing this alternative argument in an implementation.

8. DETERMINISTIC OUTPUT VERIFIER
---------------------------------
Output the base-field basis B0 and the successful trial data
(h,D,u,v), where h defines the extension field. A checker:

  * verifies the prescribed extension degree/size, monicity, and
    irreducibility of h;
  * verifies all residue encodings and each D_ii!=0;
  * recomputes the 2n shifted terms and exact recurrence length ell;
  * verifies M B0=0 over F_p0;
  * computes the base-field column rank of B0 and requires it to be
    exactly r=n-ell, with B0 containing r columns.

The shifted-sequence argument gives rank_Fq M>=ell. The independent
base-field kernel columns give rank_Fq M<=n-r=ell. Field extension
preserves the rank of M, so the asserted BASE-FIELD rank and full
kernel are certified exactly. The checker need not trust g(T),
preconditioning success, any claimed probability, or how the basis
was generated. Its cost is soft-O(n^2+n r^2), within
soft-O(n^2(r+1)). The successful trial data have O(n log n) bits;
the output basis has O(n r) bits at fixed p0.

9. EXPECTED RSBM COST FROM ONE RAW BASE-FIELD KERNEL MOMENT
-----------------------------------------------------------
Suppose an ensemble of fixed-degree integer graph matrices M_Z
satisfies, for one fixed odd prime p0,

  E p0^r <= C,
  r=dim_Fp0 ker(M_Z mod p0),                             (11)

uniformly in n. The all-input algorithm above has a constant-factor
bound uniform over each fixed reduced matrix. Since r+1<=p0^r,
iterated expectation gives

  E(kernel construction bitwork)
       <=soft-O(n^2) E(r+1)=soft-O(n^2).                 (12)

A bounded configuration-model raw moment transfers through
conditioning on simplicity: if the simplicity probability is at
least c>0, the conditioned moment is at most C/c. This uses actual
conditioning, not contiguity of an unbounded random variable.
Whether the proposed direct all-parity/cubic RSBM calculation proves
(11) must be audited separately; this note assumes it only here.

Enumerate the p0^r base-field kernel vectors with an odometer or
p0-ary Gray code. Maintain the current vector explicitly. Increasing
a digit, including wrapping p0-1 to zero, changes its coefficient by
+1 modulo p0, so update by adding the corresponding basis vector.
The total number of digit changes in a complete odometer pass is
at most

  p0^r sum_{j>=0}p0^{-j} = p0^{r+1}/(p0-1).

Thus enumeration costs O(n p0^r), with NO multiplicative r factor.
For each candidate, require all coordinates to be 1 or -1 modulo
p0, convert uniquely to integer signs (p0 is odd), normalize one
fixed sign if desired, and verify M_Z sigma=0 over the INTEGERS.
Check the requested balance and model degrees too. At fixed degree
these checks cost soft-O(n) bits per candidate. Therefore

  E(enumeration and exact checking) <=soft-O(n) E p0^r
                                   =soft-O(n).          (13)

Every integer sign solution reduces to a base-field kernel vector,
and +1/-1 are distinct modulo p0, so the enumeration misses none.
Spurious modular solutions fail integer verification. In a regular
RSBM with known d1,d2, the equation A sigma=(d1-d2)sigma and total
degree d1+d2 forces exactly d1 same-sign and d2 opposite-sign
neighbors at every vertex; exact balance supplies the equal block
sizes. Thus this procedure lists ALL admissible planted-parameter
partitions, up to global exchange. It does not select arbitrarily
among statistically indistinguishable multiple partitions.

Combining (12) and (13) gives unconditional expected near-quadratic
BIT work whenever the raw moment input (11) has been proved. The
only source of recovery nonuniqueness is then actual ambiguity in
the integer sign list. A high-nullity base-field event is handled
within the algorithm's expected cost; no exact dense fallback or
real-eigenvalue gap is used.

10. CLAIM BOUNDARIES
----------------------
This is a classical black-box-kernel specialization with explicit
one-sided rank certification and charged field descent. It is not a
new matrix preconditioner, a proof of the proposed RSBM moment, a
proof of its ambiguity probability, or a historical-priority claim.
The result holds in fixed odd characteristic because the exact
preconditioner inputs are Theorems 4.2 and 4.5; substituting the
squarefree Theorem 4.3 would incorrectly introduce a characteristic
restriction and would break the fixed-base-field argument.
