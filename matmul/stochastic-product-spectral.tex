\begin{abstract}
For dense real \(A\in\R^{n\times m}\), \(B\in\R^{m\times p}\), with
\(m\leq\min(n,p)\), write \(I=m(n+p)\). We construct a symmetric,
unbiased stochastic oracle for a signed spectral-threshold pencil using
only batches of original input rows. Constant-distortion whitening makes
its normalized second moment bounded, independently of cancellations
and factor conditioning; an oracle action costs \(O(m^2)\).
Published nonconvex-component SVRG, proximal acceleration, and
shift-and-invert PCA theorems then give rank-\(r\) relative spectral
approximation in
\[
 \tO\bigl(Ir+m^{\omega+\zeta}
            +r I^{3/4}m^{1/2}/\sqrt\theta+\MM(n,r,p)\bigr).
\]
The probability guarantee is \(1-\delta\) for every fixed input.
A tail-mass Nystr\"om lemma avoids a polynomial loss in \(r\) or a
singular-value condition number. All output entries are included.
Explicit regimes improve the checked general-product Gram and Krylov
bounds, including growing target rank. The optimization and PCA tools
are established; potential novelty is the factor-access oracle and its
total-cost consequences. Global priority remains under review.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and model}
Let \(M=AB\), \(k=\rank M\), and \(1\leq r\leq m\).
Eigenvalues and singular values beyond a matrix's dimension are zero.
All costs count real arithmetic operations, square roots, comparisons,
and Gaussian sampling in the usual randomized linear-algebra model.
We use the square multiplication bound \(\omega\leq9/4\) from
\cite{amanov}, with arbitrary fixed exponent slack \(\zeta>0\).
Tildes suppress only logarithms in dimensions, inverse accuracy, and
inverse failure probability. No input-condition-number logarithm is hidden.
No floating-point stability or bit-complexity theorem is claimed.

\begin{theorem}[Total-time spectral approximation]\label{thm:main}
For \(0<\theta\leq1/16\) and \(0<\delta<1\), an algorithm returns
factors of width at most \(r\) and explicitly forms their product \(C\),
such that
\[
 \Pr\{\norm{M-C}_2\leq(1+\theta)\sigma_{r+1}(M)\}\geq1-\delta .
\]
For \(\ell=\min(r,k)\), its total cost is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 \tO\!\left(I(1+\ell)+m^{\omega+\zeta}
    +\frac{\ell I^{3/4}(mk)^{1/4}}{\sqrt\theta}
    +np+\MM(n,\ell,p)\right).
\end{equation}
The product rank need not be supplied. In particular \(k\leq m\)
gives the bound in the abstract. Zero products, zero optimal tails,
rank-deficient factors, repeated singular values, and arbitrary
cancellations are covered.
\end{theorem}

Every iteration budget is capped in advance. Required positive norms,
nonzero denominators, and valid brackets are checked; a failed check
returns the current factors. These exits do not occur on the good
events in the proof. The computed rank used to cap the algorithm never
exceeds \(k\), even when a sketch fails, as shown next. The separate
\(np\) term includes explicitly writing zero when \(k=0\).

\section{Constant sketches and product support}
Set \(P=A^\top A\), \(Q=BB^\top\), and fix \(\xi=1/64\).
Fast subspace embeddings \cite{cnw}, with
\(\widetilde O(m)\) rows, produce explicit \(\bar P,\bar Q\) satisfying
\[
 (1-\xi)P\preceq\bar P\preceq(1+\xi)P,\qquad
 (1-\xi)Q\preceq\bar Q\preceq(1+\xi)Q
\]
on a common event of probability \(1-O(\delta)\).
Application and Gram construction cost
\(\widetilde O(I+m^{\omega+\zeta})\).

Let \(a=\rank\bar P\). By rank-revealing elimination and small-core
Cholesky algebra, compute \(W_0\in\R^{m\times a}\) with
\(\range W_0=\range\bar P\), \(W_0^\top\bar P W_0=I\),
and \(L_0=W_0^\dagger\). All this costs
\(\widetilde O(m^{\omega+\zeta})\); no tall transformed factor is formed.
On the good event, \(A=AW_0L_0\).
Let \(\bar Q_c=L_0\bar QL_0^\top\), compute an orthonormal basis \(U\)
for its range, and put
\[
 \widehat k=\rank\bar Q_c,\qquad
 W=W_0U,\quad L=U^\top L_0,\quad Z=AW,\quad
 G=W^\top PW,\quad Q_c=LQL^\top .
\]
Here \(Z,G,Q_c\) are used only as operators.
On the good event \(\widehat k=k\): the Loewner bounds identify the
range of \(\bar Q_c\) with that of \(L_0B\).
Consequently
\[
 M=ZLB,\qquad
 \frac1{1+\xi}I\preceq G\preceq\frac1{1-\xi}I,\qquad
 (1-\xi)Q_c\preceq L\bar QL^\top\preceq(1+\xi)Q_c .
\]
The spectra of \(MM^\top\) and \(G^{1/2}Q_cG^{1/2}\) agree after
padding with zeros. The algorithm uses neither \(G^{1/2}\) nor a
materialized \(AW\) or \(LB\).

The partial support does not give \(A=AWL\). Only \(M=AWLB\) is
needed. It persists under every right projection: the columns of
\(L_0B(I-VV^\top)\) remain in \(\range U\).

There is also a deterministic rank upper bound off the good event.
Always \(\range W_0\subseteq\range P\), since \(\bar P\) is a
sketched Gram. Thus
\[
 \widehat k=\rank(L_0BT^\top)
 \leq\rank(\Pi_{\range P}B)=\rank(AB)=k ,
\]
where \(T\) is the \(B\)-side embedding.
The algorithm caps deflations at \(\min(r,\widehat k)\).
When \(\widehat k=0\), it writes zero and exits.
Every implemented dimension, minibatch size, regularization parameter,
and iteration cap uses the computed \(\widehat k\). In the good-event
analysis below we write \(k=\widehat k\); the true rank \(k\) only
parametrizes the bound, since \(\widehat k\leq k\) always.

\section{A cheap symmetric stochastic pencil}
Write \(c\) for a current squared-norm scale comparable to
\(\lambda=\norm{M}_2^2>0\). Its construction is given below.
For \(1/2\leq\lambda/c\leq1\), the relevant normalized pencil is
\[
 D_s=sG-\frac{GQ_cG}{c}.
\]
Every exact action costs \(O(I)\). Safe shifts satisfy
\(0<s\leq2\); when \(s-\lambda/c\geq\gamma/60\), this pencil is
positive definite with minimum eigenvalue at least a constant times
\(\gamma\).

Let \(a_i\) be the transpose of row \(i\) of \(A\), and \(b_j\) column
\(j\) of \(B\). The analytical features
\(u_i=W^\top a_i\), \(v_j=Lb_j\) satisfy
\(G=\sum_i u_iu_i^\top\), \(Q_c=\sum_jv_jv_j^\top\).
Estimate every squared feature length within factors \(1/2\) and \(2\)
using fresh Gaussian JL matrices with \(O(\log((n+p)/\delta))\)
columns. Compute
\[
 A(W\Omega_A),\qquad B^\top(L^\top\Omega_B)
\]
in \(\widetilde O(I)\) time. This avoids transforming each row.
The resulting sampling distributions obey
\[
 p_i\geq\frac{\norm{u_i}_2^2}{4\tr G},\qquad
 q_j\geq\frac{\norm{v_j}_2^2}{4\tr Q_c}.
\]
Zero features contribute nothing. Scores and sampling tables require
no factor-energy promise.

\begin{lemma}[Minibatch moment and action cost]\label{lem:sample}
With \(k\) independent length-weighted samples, define
\[
 \widehat G=\frac1k\sum_{t=1}^k
        \frac{u_{i_t}u_{i_t}^\top}{p_{i_t}},\qquad
 \widehat Q=\frac1k\sum_{t=1}^k
        \frac{v_{j_t}v_{j_t}^\top}{q_{j_t}}.
\]
Conditional on the JL event, these are unbiased and
\[
 \mathbb E\widehat G^2\preceq20I,\qquad
 \mathbb E(\widehat Q/c)^2\preceq20I .
\]
Each action costs \(O(mk)\), including sample generation and input access.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For one \(G\)-sample \(R=u_iu_i^\top/p_i\),
\(\mathbb ER^2\preceq4(\tr G)G\). Hence
\[
 \mathbb E\widehat G^2
 =(1-1/k)G^2+k^{-1}\mathbb ER^2\preceq20I,
\]
using \(\tr G\leq2k\), \(\norm G_2\leq2\).
Likewise \(\norm{Q_c/c}_2\leq2\) and
\(\tr(Q_c/c)\leq2k\).
For an action, compute \(Wx\) once, aggregate \(k\) raw outer-product
actions \(a_i(a_i^\top Wx)/p_i\) in the original \(m\) coordinates,
then apply \(W^\top\) once. This costs \(O(mk)\).
The \(Q\)-action uses \(L^\top x\), raw \(b_j\), and \(L\).
No transformed row is computed separately.
\end{proof}

Draw independent \(\widehat G_0,\widehat G_1,\widehat G_2,\widehat Q\)
and set
\[
 \widehat D_s=s\widehat G_0-
   \frac{\widehat G_1\widehat Q\widehat G_2+
         \widehat G_2\widehat Q\widehat G_1}{2c}.
\]
Each realization is symmetric and linear, with
\(\mathbb E\widehat D_s=D_s\). Successive conditioning on the independent
factors bounds the second moment of each product by \(20^3I\).
Convexity of squared norm handles symmetrization, even though the
reversed product reuses the same samples. Since \(s\leq2\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance}
 \mathbb E\norm{\widehat D_s x}_2^2\leq V\norm x_2^2
\end{equation}
for a universal constant, for example \(V=20000\).
An action still costs \(O(mk)\). Independence of the three \(G\)-roles
and the \(Q\)-role is essential.

\section{SVRG and accelerated positive-shift solves}
Let \(\mu I\preceq D_s\preceq CI\) and
\(f(x)=x^\top D_sx/2-b^\top x\). At snapshot \(w\), use
\[
 g=\widehat D_s(x-w)+(D_sw-b),\qquad x^+=x-\eta g,
\]
with fresh samples for each step. The snapshot gradient costs \(O(I)\).
This is the usual descent update, not a replacement by a sampled mean.

The variance assumption in nonconvex-component SVRG
\cite[Theorem 9]{garber} is met with \(S=V/\mu\):
for the minimizer \(x_*\), \eqref{eq:variance} gives
\[
 \mathbb E\norm{\widehat D_s(x-x_*)}_2^2
 \leq V\norm{x-x_*}_2^2
 \leq\frac{2V}{\mu}(f(x)-f(x_*)).
\]
For clarity this conclusion can be proved directly. Unbiasedness and
the second moment give
\[
 \mathbb E\norm{x^+-x_*}_2^2
 \leq\norm{x-x_*}_2^2
 -2\eta(1-2\eta S)(f(x)-f(x_*))
 +4\eta^2S(f(w)-f(x_*)).
\]
With \(\eta=1/(8S)\), averaging an epoch of
\(\lceil64S/\mu\rceil\) iterates contracts expected objective error
by at least a factor two. Thus one contraction costs
\(\widetilde O(I+mk/\mu^2)\).
One may formally index components by all finite minibatch outcomes;
their probabilities cancel in the sampled gradient difference.
Neither enumeration nor a logarithm of the number of outcomes appears.

Add a proximal regularizer \(\rho\norm{x-w}_2^2/2\).
The sample Hessian is \(\widehat D_s+\rho I\), whose second moment is
bounded when \(\rho\) is bounded, and strong convexity is at least
\(\mu+\rho\).
Accelerated approximate proximal point \cite[Theorem 1.1]{frostig}
requires only strong convexity of the aggregate objective and a
linearly convergent regularized solver. It applies to these indefinite
sampled Hessians and gives cost
\[
 \tO\!\left(\sqrt{\rho/\mu}
               \left(I+\frac{mk}{\rho^2}\right)\right).
\]
Take \(\rho\) a sufficiently large constant multiple of
\(\sqrt{mk/I}\) when \(\mu\) is smaller. When \(\mu\) is larger,
unaccelerated SVRG costs \(O(I)\).
Since \(mk\leq I\), this proves the solve bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solve}
 \tO\!\left(I+\frac{I^{3/4}(mk)^{1/4}}{\sqrt\mu}\right).
\end{equation}
Repeated contractions and Markov's inequality give any prescribed
Euclidean solve accuracy and failure probability at logarithmic cost.
For zero-start solves the initial objective gap is at most
\(\norm b_2^2/(2\mu)\); the dependence on right-hand-side norm is
logarithmic, not a polynomial omitted from \eqref{eq:solve}.

\section{A global correlation-PCA wrapper}
A normalized positive scale is available without a high-accuracy
true-factor norm computation. Let \(\bar Q_c=L\bar QL^\top\).
Random-start power iteration on this explicit small core obtains an
actual quotient \(\nu\) between
\(0.9\norm{\bar Q_c}_2\) and \(\norm{\bar Q_c}_2\) with the allocated
confidence, in \(\widetilde O(k^2)\) work.
Take \(c=\nu/[0.9(1-\xi)^2]\). The Loewner bounds imply
\[
 \tfrac12<0.9\left(\frac{1-\xi}{1+\xi}\right)^2
                  \leq \lambda/c\leq1 .
\]
The conceptual PSD matrix
\(\mathcal H=G^{1/2}Q_cG^{1/2}/c\) has norm in that interval.

Use the robust AppxPCA theorem \cite[Theorem 3.1]{lazysvd}.
For cutoff \(\gamma\in(0,1/2]\) and leakage \(\varepsilon_{\rm pca}>0\),
it needs only logarithmically many approximate shifted inverses.
All shifts satisfy \(s\leq2\),
\(s-\lambda_1(\mathcal H)\geq\gamma\lambda_1(\mathcal H)/12\);
it returns a unit vector with squared mass at most
\(\varepsilon_{\rm pca}\) below eigenvalue
\((1-\gamma)\lambda_1(\mathcal H)\).
In our coefficients the inverse call is exactly
\[
 x=D_s^{-1}Gv.
\]
All vector inner products and norms are taken in the \(G\) metric,
using true input actions. These simulate the conceptual Euclidean
operations without computing \(G^{1/2}\).
Each system has \(\mu\geq c_0\gamma\), for a fixed positive constant
\(c_0\), so \eqref{eq:solve} applies.

The literal uniform-sphere initialization can also be simulated.
Using the fixed known spectral interval of \(G\), a polynomial
\(J_G\) approximates \(G^{-1/2}\) with relative error
\(\eta\leq p_*/(100k)\) in \(O(\log(k/p_*))\) true \(G\)-actions.
Here is an explicit construction. Write \(lI\preceq G\preceq uI\),
put \(a=l/u\), \(\rho=(1-\sqrt a)/(1+\sqrt a)\),
\(c_a=(1+\sqrt a)^2/4\), and
\(T=((1+a)I-2G/u)/(1-a)\). Then
\[
 J_G=u^{-1/2}c_a^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{q}\rho^jP_j(T)
\]
has relative error at most \(\rho^{q+1}/\sqrt a\), by the Legendre
generating function and \(\norm{P_j(T)}_2\leq1\).
Since \(a\) is bounded below by an absolute constant, the degree is
\(O(\log(1/\eta))\). The three-term recurrence uses arithmetic,
square roots, and \(G\)-actions only; see also \cite{thresholdnote}.
Draw \(g\sim N(0,I_k)\), set \(x_0=J_Gg\), and normalize in the
\(G\) norm. Analytically \(G^{1/2}x_0\) before normalization is a
Gaussian whose covariance eigenvalues are in
\([(1-\eta)^2,(1+\eta)^2]\). Its KL divergence from a standard Gaussian
is at most \(8k\eta^2\), hence total variation is at most
\(2\sqrt{k}\eta<p_*/10\). Normalization preserves this upper bound.
The primary uniform-start theorem therefore loses only this allocated
failure probability, including under subsequent adaptive oracle calls.

The AppxPCA phases have unnormalized intermediate inverse iterates.
Their norms grow by at most \((C/\gamma)^{O(\log(k/(p_*^2
\varepsilon_{\rm pca})))}\) on the good events.
Use a deterministic upper bound \(\Gamma\) of this form, with a
sufficiently large fixed constant \(C\), to cap right-hand-side norms
and to set every solver epoch budget in advance. Abort to current
factors if that bound is exceeded.
Choose inverse tolerances at least as strict as
\((\gamma/C)^{C\log(k/(p_*^2\varepsilon_{\rm pca}))}\);
these may be stricter than the published absolute constants.
Their inverse logs and the right-hand-side bounds are polylogarithmic.
Euclidean and \(G\)-norm errors are uniformly equivalent.
Thus random solvers satisfy each requested oracle error with its
allocated conditional failure probability using \eqref{eq:solve}.
Induction over past accurate calls justifies applying their SPD guarantee;
a union bound covers all calls.

Mapping the final coefficient vector to \(z=Zx/\sqrt{x^\top Gx}\)
costs \(O(I)\) and gives the following primitive for \(H=MM^\top\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cpca}
 \norm z_2=1,\qquad
 \sum_{\lambda_j(H)\leq(1-\gamma)\lambda_1(H)}
           |\langle z,u_j\rangle|^2\leq\varepsilon_{\rm pca},
\end{equation}
at cost
\(\widetilde O(I+I^{3/4}(mk)^{1/4}/\sqrt\gamma)\).
Neither the true top eigenvalue nor a gap is supplied.

\section{Tail-mass Nystr\"om deflation}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tail}
Let \(H\succeq0\), \(\norm z_2=1\), \(s=z^\top Hz>0\), and
\[
 R=H-\frac{Hzz^\top H}{s}.
\]
Let \(t=\lambda_{j+1}(H)\), and let \(w\) be the squared mass of
\(z\) in a bottom \(d-j\) eigenvector subspace of \(H\).
If \(w<1\), then
\[
 \lambda_j(R)\leq
 \frac{\lambda_1(H)t}{\lambda_1(H)(1-w)+tw}
 \leq\frac{t}{1-w}.
\]
Also \(R\succeq0\), \(R\preceq H\), and
\(\rank R=\rank H-1\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First take \(H\succ0\). In an orthonormal basis starting with \(z\),
the positive block of \(R\) is a Schur complement. Its inverse is the
compression of \(H^{-1}\) to \(z^\perp\).
Write \(z=z_{\rm head}+z_{\rm tail}\). For any vector \(y\) in the
bottom \(d-j\) eigenspace, the vector
\[
 x=y-\frac{\langle z_{\rm tail},y\rangle}{1-w}\,z_{\rm head}
\]
is perpendicular to \(z\). These vectors span a \(d-j\) dimensional
space. Put
\(v=|\langle z_{\rm tail},y\rangle|^2/
((1-w)\norm y_2^2)\leq w/(1-w)\).
Its inverse quadratic quotient is bounded below by
\[
 \frac{x^\top H^{-1}x}{\norm x_2^2}
 \geq\frac{1/t+v/\lambda_1(H)}{1+v}
 \geq\frac{1-w}{t}+\frac{w}{\lambda_1(H)}.
\]
Min--max on the compressed inverse and inversion of eigenvalue order
give the claim. Replace \(H\) by \(H+\epsilon I\) and let
\(\epsilon\downarrow0\) for the PSD case.
Finally \(R=H^{1/2}(I-qq^\top)H^{1/2}\), with
\(q=H^{1/2}z/\sqrt s\), proves positivity, monotonicity, and rank drop.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Correlation-PCA counting]\label{lem:count}
Suppose each of \(r\) successive Nystr\"om steps satisfies
\eqref{eq:cpca} with cutoff \(\gamma\) and leakage \(\varepsilon\).
Then
\[
 \lambda_1(H_r)\leq
 \frac{\lambda_{r+1}(H_0)}
      {(1-\gamma)(1-\varepsilon)^r}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set \(t_i=(1-\varepsilon)^{-i}\lambda_{r+1}(H_0)\).
Inductively track \(\lambda_{r-i+1}(H_i)\leq t_i\).
If \(\lambda_1(H_i)\leq t_i/(1-\gamma)\) at any stage, later
PSD downdates cannot increase the norm, proving the stated final bound.
Otherwise \(t_i<(1-\gamma)\lambda_1(H_i)\), and the relevant bottom
subspace lies entirely below the cutoff.
Its witness mass is at most \(\varepsilon\), so Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}
gives the next tracked inequality.
After \(r\) such steps the bound is even \(\lambda_1(H_r)\leq t_r\).
This also covers a zero optimal tail. Ties can be included in the
cutoff subspace; if the head is small the monotonicity branch applies.
\end{proof}

\section{Rank updates, cost, and probability}
For the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, set
\(\gamma=\theta/4\), \(\varepsilon=\theta/(4r)\).
Then \((1-\varepsilon)^r\geq1-\theta/4\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:count}
gives a spectral error factor at most
\((1-\theta/4)^{-1}\leq1+\theta\).
Only leakage precision depends on \(r\), and it enters logarithmically.
If \(r\geq k\), the exact rank drop instead terminates after \(k\)
steps with zero residual, giving the required guarantee directly.

Let \(\mathcal B=LB\), maintained implicitly, and let \(V_i\) contain
the orthonormal right vectors. In compressed coordinates write
\[
 V_i=\mathcal B^\top C_i,\quad
 D_i=\mathcal BV_i=Q_c C_i,\quad
 S_i=I_k-D_iC_i^\top,\quad
 M_i=Z S_i\mathcal B.
\]
At a step take \(z_i\) from \eqref{eq:cpca}, set
\(s_i=\norm{M_i^\top z_i}_2^2\), and
\[
 c=S_i^\top Z^\top z_i/\sqrt{s_i},\quad
 v=\mathcal B^\top c,\quad d=\mathcal Bv.
\]
The vector \(v\) is unit and perpendicular to previous right vectors,
and \(c^\top D_i=0\). Therefore
\[
 S_{i+1}=S_i-dc^\top=(I_k-dc^\top)S_i.
\]
The residual covariance is \(Q_{c,i}=S_iQ_cS_i^\top\).
The initial coarse Loewner event implies a simultaneous proxy for
every such adaptive congruence. With \(\bar Q_i\) that proxy and
\(w=\bar Q_i c\), its update is
\[
 \bar Q_{i+1}=\bar Q_i-dw^\top-wd^\top
                     +(c^\top\bar Q_i c)dd^\top .
\]
It costs \(O(k^2)\). A zero proxy detects a zero residual on the good
event and terminates. Otherwise the constant-accuracy small-core norm
estimate gives the next scale \(c\).

The \(G\)-side row probabilities remain valid throughout.
At every stage recompute the \(Q\)-side length probabilities from
\[
 B^\top(L^\top S_i^\top\Omega_i),
\]
with a fresh JL matrix conditional on the past, in
\(\widetilde O(I)\) time. Reusing the initial probabilities is
unjustified for the oblique \(S_i\).
A residual minibatch action uses \(S_iL\) and its transpose, plus
\(k\) raw columns of \(B\); its cost is still \(O(mk)\).
Apply \(S_i=I-D_iC_i^\top\) in \(O(ki)\), with \(i\leq k\).
Every exact residual pencil action costs \(O(I)\).
All physical vectors and updates cost \(O(I)\) per step.
After at most \(\ell\) rank drops, form \(MV_\ell\) in \(O(I\ell)\)
work and expand \(MV_\ell V_\ell^\top\) at the charged multiplication
cost. Early recovery returns fewer columns.

Allocate failure probability among the two initial embeddings and all
fresh norm estimates, JL batches, uniform-start approximations, and
inverse calls. Condition on their past at each call and use fresh
randomness. The robust shift guarantees hold whenever all earlier
calls are accurate. The capped union bound yields total failure at
most \(\delta\). The number of stages is at most
\(\min(r,\widehat k)\leq\ell\) even off the good events.
Summing \eqref{eq:solve} and the stated setup and output costs proves
\eqref{eq:main}.

\section{Adaptive absolute error}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:absolute}
For a supplied tolerance \(e_{\rm abs}>0\), the rank need not be given.
With probability at least \(1-\delta\), an adaptive version returns
\(\norm{M-C}_2\leq e_{\rm abs}\) and width
\[
 R\leq r_e:=\min\{0\leq r\leq k:
                         \sigma_{r+1}(M)\leq e_{\rm abs}/(1+\theta)\}.
\]
For each realized stopping width, the cost is
\[
 \tO\!\left(I(R+1)+m^{\omega+\zeta}
  +(R+1)I^{3/4}(mk)^{1/4}/\sqrt\theta
  +np+\MM(n,R,p)\right).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use cutoff \(\gamma=\theta/8\) at every stage and leakage
\(\varepsilon_i=\theta/[32(i+1)(i+2)]\).
Their sum is \(\theta/32\). The actual Rayleigh value satisfies
\(s_i\geq(1-\gamma)(1-\varepsilon_i)\lambda_1(H_i)\).
Stop when
\(U_i=s_i/((1-\gamma)(1-\varepsilon_i))\leq e_{\rm abs}^2\).
The variable-leakage proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:count}, applied to the
first \(i\) stages, gives
\[
 U_i\leq
 \frac{\sigma_{i+1}(M)^2}
 {(1-\gamma)^2\prod_{j=0}^{i}(1-\varepsilon_j)}
 \leq\frac{\sigma_{i+1}(M)^2}{1-9\theta/32}.
\]
For \(i=0\) the same bound is valid directly.
Since \((1+\theta)^2(1-9\theta/32)>1\), acceptance occurs by \(r_e\).
Zero residuals stop earlier. Costs add linearly in the number of stages;
smaller leakage changes only logarithms.
Use summable confidence budgets and return the current factors on a
failed primitive. The small-width cost controlled by \(r_e\) holds on
the same successful event; rare failures may use additional stages
within the fixed rank cap.
\end{proof}

\section{Total-time consequences and comparison}
\begin{corollary}[The current fixed-rank regime]\label{cor:example}
Let \(n=p=N\), \(m=\lceil N^{50/59}\rceil\),
\(\theta=m^{-56/125}\), and \(\delta=1/100\).
For every fixed \(r\), construction costs
\(N^{2-1/1180+o(1)}\), and total explicit-output time is \(O(N^2)\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For the worst case \(k=m\), the accelerated term has \(m\)-exponent
\[
 \tfrac34\cdot\tfrac{109}{50}+\tfrac12+
                    \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{56}{125}
 =\tfrac{2359}{1000}<\tfrac{59}{25}.
\]
Core and input exponents \(9/4\) and \(109/50\) are smaller.
Choose fixed multiplication slack below their strict margins.
Conventional fixed-rank expansion costs \(O(N^2)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[An open output-optimal family]\label{cor:family}
Let \(N=m^{1+d+o(1)}\), \(\theta=m^{-b}\), with
\[
 \frac18<d<\frac{\sqrt2-1}{2},\qquad 2d<b<\frac52d .
\]
For fixed target rank and confidence, total explicit-output time is
\(O(N^2)\), whereas the checked Gram and ordinary-Krylov bounds both
have exponent strictly above two.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The new construction exponent in powers of \(m\) is
\[
 \max\{9/4,\ 2+d,\ 2+3d/4+b/2\}<2+2d.
\]
The Gram comparison from \cite{amanov} has exponent
\(2+d+1/(4(1+d))\), above output exactly when
\(d<(\sqrt2-1)/2\).
The fixed-rank Krylov comparison is \(2+d+b/2\), above output
exactly when \(b>2d\). Core exponent slack and logarithms fit
inside the strict margins.
\end{proof}

One can optimize these upper-bound comparisons explicitly. Write
\[
 \alpha_*=\frac{3\sqrt5-5}{2},\qquad
 s_*=\frac{7-3\sqrt5}{8}\approx0.0364745084 .
\]
For any fixed \(0<\eta<s_*\), set
\[
 m=\lceil N^{\alpha_*}\rceil,\qquad
 \theta=N^{-(10s_*-2\eta)}.
\]
The new construction is \(N^{2-\eta+o(1)}\) and total time is
\(O(N^2)\). The better of the named Gram and Krylov bounds is
\(N^{2+s_*-\eta+o(1)}\).
Indeed the Gram exponent is \(2+s_*\) and the Krylov exponent
\(2+s_*-\eta\), using \(\alpha_*^2+5\alpha_*-5=0\).
The saving can approach \(N^{s_*-o(1)}\); \(s_*\) is a supremum
for the strict-quadratic comparison, not an attained positive-margin
construction at its boundary.

There is also a growing-rank consequence. For
\[
 N=m^{7/6+o(1)},\qquad r=\lceil m^{7/200}\rceil,\qquad
 \theta=m^{-17/50},
\]
construction costs \(N^{699/350+o(1)}=N^{2-1/350+o(1)}\).
Fast rectangular expansion gives total \(N^{2+o(1)}\), whereas
fast-batched block Krylov and exact Gram have respective bounds
\(N^{701/350+o(1)}\) and \(N^{100/49+o(1)}\).
Here the construction exponents are
\(9/4,\ 1321/600,\ 233/100\) in powers of \(m\).
For the fair block comparison, Krylov uses
\(\widetilde O(m^{17/100})\) iterations and block width \(m^{7/200}\).
Each factor-block multiplication costs only
\(\widetilde O(I)=m^{13/6+o(1)}\), rather than \(Ir\), by
\cite{amanov}. The basis width is \(\widetilde O(m^{41/200})\).
Conventional tall QR, followed by fast projected-product construction
and small-core processing, stays below the resulting
\(m^{701/300+o(1)}\) iteration cost.
The output exponent is two because \(r<N^{1/2}\), using the
general rectangular bound in \cite{amanov}.
For growing rank this is an exponent-two output statement, rather
than the literal \(O(N^2)\) expansion bound used for fixed rank.

Product rank also needs an exact-compression comparison.
The computed \(W,L\) give exact skinny factors \(AW,LB\) on the
good event. An available materialized-factor bound includes
\[
 \MM(n,m,k)+\MM(k,m,p)+\MM(k,n,k)+\MM(k,p,k)+k^{\omega+\zeta},
\]
followed by lifting and output expansion.
For sufficiently small \(k\), this route can already be output optimal;
one should use the better bound in each regime.
A separate example for the stochastic rank dependence is
\[
 N=m^{59/50+o(1)},\qquad k\leq m^{24/25},\qquad
 \theta=m^{-469/1000},
\]
with fixed target rank. The stochastic construction exponent is at
most \(4719/2000\) in powers of \(m\), giving
\(N^{2-1/2360+o(1)}\) and total \(O(N^2)\).
The named exact-skinny materialization bound has exponent
\(16736281/7080000>59/25\), by the full asymmetric rectangular
formula in \cite{amanov}; the bound using the full-rank stochastic
term instead has exponent \(4739/2000>59/25\).
These are comparisons of upper bounds, not materialization lower bounds.

These are arithmetic, asymptotic comparisons to the checked
general-product literature. They are not finite-size speed measurements
or lower bounds against all algorithms.
At the original \(N=m^{9/8}\), \(\theta=1/m\) target, the new
accelerated term is \(m^{83/32}\), worse than exact Gram.
That stronger target remains open.

\section{Prior-work scope}
The stochastic optimization theorems \cite{garber,frostig} and
AppxPCA \cite{lazysvd} are existing results. Constant Gram sketches,
JL length estimates, and Nystr\"om deflation are existing tools.
Independent-copy unbiasing of covariance products, including signed
polynomials, is also established prior art \cite{lijordan,gemp}.
The proposed new contribution is the controlled \(O(mk)\)
signed-product oracle from raw factors, the gap-free rank accounting,
and their combined total cost. No theorem in the checked sources was
found to state or subsume this separate-factor runtime; this is a
targeted novelty assessment, not an exhaustive priority certificate.

The accelerated covariance scale in \cite{garber} already matches
the displayed worst-case expression for supplied covariance features.
We make no covariance-dominance claim. Our estimator removes the
need to supply or form a true Gram root for the general product.
Likewise multilevel sketched preconditioning \cite{msp} includes
implicit-Gram algorithms for supplied positive features.
The signed pencil here is not supplied in that form.
The preceding threshold hierarchy \cite{thresholdnote} provides
another valid route to nearby exponents; the present solver matches
its limiting envelope without increasing recursion depth.
The accompanying audit files retain the checked product, sampling,
finite-Schatten, and oversampled-Krylov comparisons and their limits.
