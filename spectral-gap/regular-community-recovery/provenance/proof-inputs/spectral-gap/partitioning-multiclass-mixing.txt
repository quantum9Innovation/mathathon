MULTICLASS FIXED-EIGENVALUE EXCLUSION FROM BLOCKWISE STRONG MIXING
Independent working proof and audit, November 15, 2026.
Authoring agent: graph_breakthrough. Only this file is written by this task.

Outcome and status
------------------
The proposed fixed-k extension works under the stated even-size and
internal-degree-at-least-three hypotheses. In the independent internal
perfect-matching model, its modular first moment is in fact

  E |ker_Fp(A-aI)| = 2 p^{ell_p} + o_{p,Q}(1),                 (1)

where ell_p = nullity_Fp(Q-aI), uniformly over every permitted collection
of deterministic cross matrices and over a in F_p. This is a consequence
of Meszaros's established mixing theorem, not a new imported mixing law.
The two contributions of size p^{ell_p} come from vectors nonconstant
on every block and vectors constant on every block. All intermediate
patterns are negligible.

Classical contiguity transfers the resulting HIGH-PROBABILITY real
statement to independent uniform simple internal regular graphs. It
does not transfer the unbounded expectation (1). The real conclusion
holds for every fixed real algebraic frame eigenvalue, not only integer
ones; Section 6 proves the required elementary prime argument. Given
the fixed frame Q, exact rational elimination then recovers all k blocks
without an outlier or numerical spectral-gap hypothesis.

This note is a complete derived argument with explicit primary inputs.
It is not a historical novelty certificate. It supplies no effective
failure rate and makes no growing-k, odd-N, internal-degree-two, or
adaptive-cross-matrix claim.

1. Precise model and theorem
---------------------------
Fix an integer k >= 1 and a symmetric k-by-k nonnegative integer matrix

  Q = (d_ij),                    d_ii >= 3 for all i.

Assume the off-diagonal frame graph, with edge ij whenever i != j and
d_ij > 0, is connected. For k=1 the connectedness convention is vacuous.
All entries of Q, and k itself, are fixed as N tends to infinity.

There are k labelled blocks, each of the same EVEN size N. For i<j,
choose any deterministic N-by-N zero-one matrix B_ij having all row
and column sums equal to d_ij; set B_ji=B_ij^T. If d_ij=0 then B_ij=0.
These matrices may depend on N, with no expansion or randomness
assumption. They are simple biregular biadjacency matrices.

The diagonal adjacency blocks C_i are independent uniform simple
d_ii-regular graphs on their respective labelled vertex sets. Define
the symmetric adjacency matrix A by A_ii=C_i and A_ij=B_ij.
This is an ordinary simple unweighted graph. It need not be globally
regular when Q has unequal row sums.

Let J_N:R^k -> R^{kN} be the block-constant injection

  J_N c = (c_1 1_N, ..., c_k 1_N).

For every realization, A J_N = J_N Q. The old block-constant spectral
space is W_old = im J_N; the old lambda-eigenspace is

  E_old(lambda) = J_N ker_R(Q-lambda I).

THEOREM A. For every fixed real number lambda, put

  ell = nullity_R(Q-lambda I).

Then, along even N,

  sup_{(B_ij)} P[ ker_R(A-lambda I) != E_old(lambda) ] -> 0.  (2)

If lambda is not a frame eigenvalue, the right-hand space is zero;
thus (2) asserts absence of that fixed eigenvalue with high probability.
If lambda is a frame eigenvalue, it asserts that its full eigenspace
is exactly its old eigenspace, including when the old multiplicity
ell exceeds one. A finite union permits any fixed finite collection
of targets simultaneously, in particular all eigenvalues of Q.

The same uniform conclusion holds if all cross matrices have any joint
law independent of all internal blocks; the cross matrices need not
be independent of one another. The proof does not permit choosing them
after inspecting the internal random graphs.

All probabilities in (2) use the uniform SIMPLE internal graph model.
Sections 2--6 first prove (2) in the independent matching-sum model;
Section 7 transfers the event, with its stated quantifiers.


2. Imported finite-field mixing statement
---------------------------------------
Fix an odd prime p. Initially C_i is the sum of d_i=d_ii independent
uniform perfect-matching adjacency matrices on the even set of size N.
All these matchings, across all blocks, are independent. Repeated
internal edges are allowed in this intermediate model. Write
s(x)=1_N^T x in F_p.

For x,r in F_p^N define the reference kernel q_i(x,r) by

  q_i(x,r) = 1_{r=d_i c 1_N}                  if x=c 1_N,
           = p^{-(N-1)} 1_{s(r)=d_i s(x)}     if x nonconstant.

Define

  e_i(x) = sup_{r in F_p^N} |P(C_i x=r)-q_i(x,r)|,
  epsilon_N = max_i sum_x e_i(x).

Meszaros, Theorem 1.6, implies epsilon_N -> 0 for each FIXED p and
fixed Q. Its finite abelian group is F_p. A nonconstant vector has
minimal affine coset F_p. For odd p the symmetric tensor restriction
is vacuous: the subgroup generated by a tensor b+b tensor a is all
of F_p tensor_Z F_p, since 2 is invertible. A constant vector has its
deterministic image. Extending both masses by zero outside their exact
support justifies the supremum over all r above.

There is no need to assume p does not divide d_i or N. All scalar sum
functionals on F_p^N remain nonzero, also when p divides N. No prime
growing with N will be used.


3. Uniform affine bound for every hybrid of actual/reference blocks
-------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the main replacement issue for k>2. A reference kernel q_i is
not presumed to arise from a single random linear matrix. In particular
one must NOT justify its affine bound by saying that every inhomogeneous
solution set is an affine copy of a random kernel.

For 0<=j<=k let M_j(N,p) be the supremum of

  sum_{x_1,...,x_j} product_{i=1}^j K_i(x_i,
                 a_i x_i + b_i - sum_{r != i} D_ir x_r),    (3)

over all of the following choices: j internal types from the fixed
list of degrees; arbitrary scalars a_i in F_p; arbitrary deterministic
N-vectors b_i; arbitrary deterministic N-by-N matrices D_ir over F_p;
and any choice at each index of K_i=P(C_i x_i=.) or K_i=q_i.
Allowing arbitrary D_ir, without symmetry or degree constraints, is
deliberate: the class is closed when one fixes one vector x_i and
absorbs its contribution into all remaining right-hand sides.
Set M_0=1. The expression is just a nonnegative finite sum; for hybrid
kernels it need not count the solutions of a joint random linear map.

If every block is reference, the expression is <=(2p)^j. Indeed, for
any prescribed pattern of constant/nonconstant vectors, a constant
block has at most p choices and factor <=1. A nonconstant block has
at most p^N choices and factor <=p^{-(N-1)}, giving at most p as well.
Ignore all constraints and sum the 2^j patterns.

Now replace one actual factor by its reference. For fixed x_i, the
remaining sum is a (j-1)-block affine hybrid of the form (3). Therefore
the total absolute replacement error is at most

  sum_{x_i} e_i(x_i) M_{j-1} <= epsilon_N M_{j-1}.

Telescoping all actual factors to reference gives

  M_j <= (2p)^j + j epsilon_N M_{j-1}.                       (4)

This argument uses induction on j, not an assumed bound on M_j itself.
For sufficiently large N, epsilon_N<=1. Define finite constants

  D_0=1,                  D_j=(2p)^j+j D_{j-1}.

Induction in (4) gives M_j<=D_j for all j<=k. Consequently the actual
homogeneous k-block expected kernel size differs from its all-reference
sum by at most

  k epsilon_N D_{k-1} = o_{p,Q}(1),                         (5)

uniformly over every cross matrix and scalar target. This is the
needed bounded affine replacement. Its constants may depend on fixed
p and k; they need not be uniform as p grows, since the N limit is
always taken first.

There is an even shorter direct alternative to this induction. The
summed maximum atoms satisfy

  sum_x sup_r q_i(x,r)
    = p+(p^N-p)p^{-(N-1)} <= 2p,
  sum_x sup_r P(C_i x=r) <= 2p+epsilon_N.                  (5a)

For any hybrid and ANY target functions r_i(x_1,...,x_j), bound each
factor by f_i(x_i)=sup_r K_i(x_i,r). The unrestricted sum of their
product factorizes as product_i sum_{x_i} f_i(x_i). Hence the whole
j-block sum is at most (2p+epsilon_N)^j, even without affine targets.
Likewise one replacement error is at most
epsilon_N(2p+epsilon_N)^{k-1}, by bounding the other factors this way.
Thus the sharper total error

  k epsilon_N(2p+epsilon_N)^{k-1} = o_{p,Q}(1)              (5b)

may replace (5). This direct maximum-atom argument is sufficient by
itself. The induction above is retained to explicitly check the
proposed affine-hybrid route; neither route invokes affine-copy
linearity of reference kernels.


4. The exact all-reference count
--------------------------------
Fix a in F_p and let ell_p=nullity_Fp(Q-aI). The actual expected kernel
size is

  sum_{x_1,...,x_k} product_i P(C_i x_i =
                            a x_i - sum_{j != i} B_ij x_j).

Replace all factors using (5), and call the reference sum T_N. Write
u=p^{-(N-1)}. Separate the following three types of vector tuples.

4a. Every block nonconstant.

Let s_i=s(x_i). Because every column sum of B_ij equals d_ij, the i-th
reference hyperplane condition is exactly

  d_ii s_i = a s_i - sum_{j != i} d_ij s_j.

Thus the complete collection of scalar conditions is

  (Q-aI)s=0.                                               (6)

For any prescribed sum s_i, a fiber of s:F_p^N->F_p has exactly
p^{N-1} vectors. At most p of those vectors are constant, independently
of whether p divides N. If h_N(s_i) denotes its nonconstant count, then

  p^{N-1}-p <= h_N(s_i) <= p^{N-1}.

There are exactly p^{ell_p} choices of s obeying (6). Hence this part
of T_N is

  u^k sum_{s in ker(Q-aI)} product_i h_N(s_i)

and lies between

  p^{ell_p}(1-p^{2-N})^k   and   p^{ell_p}                   (7)

for N>=2. It equals p^{ell_p}+o(1), with error at most
k p^{ell_p} p^{2-N}. In particular p|N causes no problem.

4b. Every block constant.

Write x_i=c_i 1_N. Every factor is deterministic. The full system
reduces exactly to (Q-aI)c=0, so this contribution is exactly

  p^{ell_p}.                                               (8)

The injection c -> (c_i 1_N)_i is injective over F_p for every N;
it does not require that N be invertible modulo p.

4c. A nonempty proper set of blocks nonconstant.

Let S be the nonconstant blocks and T its nonempty complement. The
connected frame graph has an edge ij with i in T, j in S, d_ij>0.
The equation at this constant block i forces

  sum_{r in S} B_ir x_r
    = [(a-d_ii)c_i - sum_{r in T, r != i} d_ir c_r] 1_N.    (9)

For a simple d-biregular matrix B with d>=1, greedy row selection
gives, over EVERY field,

  rank B >= N/[1+d(d-1)].                                  (10)

Proof: a row has d nonzero columns. Its support meets at most d(d-1)
other rows, as each such column belongs to d-1 other rows. Select
rows with mutually disjoint nonempty supports. At least N/[1+d(d-1)]
can be selected, and they are linearly independent over any field.
The selected rows contain entries 1, so no modular cancellation
occurs. In particular p>d is not required for this rank bound.

The concatenated coefficient matrix [B_ir]_{r in S} in (9) contains
B_ij as a column submatrix, so its rank is at least that of B_ij.
Define

  kappa = min_{i<j: d_ij>0} 1/[1+d_ij(d_ij-1)] > 0

when k>1. For fixed constants (c_i)_{i in T}, equation (9) permits
at most p^{|S|N-kappa N} unrestricted choices of the vectors x_S.
Requiring them to be nonconstant only decreases that count. All
nonconstant factors are at most u, and all constant factors at most 1.
Thus this whole pattern contributes at most

  p^{|T|} p^{|S|N-kappa N} u^{|S|}
     = p^{k-kappa N}.                                     (11)

There are 2^k-2 such patterns. For k=1 there are none, and their bound
is interpreted as zero. Notice that we ignored every equation except
(9) when counting the mixed pattern. Any additional affine sum
constraints can only decrease its contribution.

Combining (5), (7), (8), and (11), for k>1,

 |E|ker_Fp(A-aI)|-2p^{ell_p}|
   <= k p^{ell_p}p^{2-N}
       +(2^k-2)p^{k-kappa N}+k epsilon_N D_{k-1}.            (12)

For k=1 omit the middle term. This proves (1), uniformly in all
permitted cross matrices and a in F_p. For each fixed p, ell_p<=k,
so all displayed errors tend to zero uniformly even over the scalar a.


5. Integer targets and the order of limits
-----------------------------------------
First suppose lambda is an integer. Let ell=nullity_Q(Q-lambda I).
Outside a finite set of primes the quotient rank is preserved, so
ell_p=ell for a=lambda mod p. To see this directly, one nonzero
(k-ell)-minor remains nonzero modulo every prime not dividing it;
all larger minors vanish as integers.

For every realization, the old block-constant modular kernel has
dimension ell. If the real nullity of A-lambda I is at least ell+1,
all integer minors of order kN-ell vanish. Its modular nullity K_p
is then at least ell+1 as well. Since K_p>=ell deterministically,

  P(K_p>=ell+1)
    <= [E p^{K_p}-p^{ell}]/[p^{ell+1}-p^{ell}].             (13)

For each fixed admissible odd prime p, (12) implies

  limsup_{N even -> infinity} sup_B
    P[ nullity_R(A-lambda I)>=ell+1 ] <= 1/(p-1).           (14)

There are arbitrarily large primes avoiding the fixed finite bad set.
Taking them after the limsup in N proves the integer version of (2)
in the matching-sum model. This also covers ell=0: it excludes a fixed
integer lambda that is not a frame eigenvalue. It asserts nothing
about the probability of modular simplicity at a fixed small prime.


6. Algebraic targets: reduction at primes with a linear residue root
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The integer restriction is unnecessary. This section avoids both
extension-field mixing and a prime-distribution theorem.

LEMMA 6.1 (elementary polynomial prime-divisor lemma).
If f in Z[t] is nonconstant, there are arbitrarily large primes p
for which f has a root in F_p. They can avoid any fixed finite set.

Proof. Let S be any finite set of primes, enlarge it to contain 2,
choose an integer b with c=f(b)!=0, and enlarge S to contain the
prime divisors of c. Put M=product_{p in S}p. Expansion at b gives

  f(b+c M t) = c h(t),

where h in Z[t] is nonconstant and h(t)=1 mod every prime in S.
Choose an integer t with |h(t)|>1. Some prime p divides h(t); it
does not belong to S, since gcd(h(t),M)=1. Consequently p divides
f(b+cMt), and b+cMt modulo p is a root. Since the initial set S was
arbitrary, the lemma follows. This is the usual elementary Schur
prime-divisor argument. No quantitative density is asserted. QED.

LEMMA 6.2 (good reductions preserve the quotient multiplicity).
Suppose lambda is a real algebraic integer, f in Z[t] is its monic
irreducible minimal polynomial, and

  ell = nullity_R(Q-lambda I).

There is a nonzero integer m depending only on Q and lambda such
that whenever p does not divide m and a in F_p satisfies f(a)=0,

  nullity_Fp(Q-aI)=ell.                                    (15)

Proof. Every minor of Q-tI is a polynomial in Z[t]. Every minor of
order k-ell+1 vanishes at lambda, hence is divisible by f in Z[t].
(For ell=0 there are no minors of order k+1 and this upper-rank
assertion is vacuous.) Choose a minor g(t) of order k-ell that does
not vanish at lambda. For k-ell=0 use the empty minor g=1.
The irreducibility of f implies gcd(f,g)=1 over Q[t]. Clearing
denominators in a polynomial Bezout identity gives

  U(t)f(t)+V(t)g(t)=m != 0,       U,V in Z[t].

At a root a of f mod p with p not dividing m, this implies g(a)!=0.
The larger minors vanish at a, while this rank-(k-ell) minor does
not. This proves (15). No discriminant condition or p not dividing
N condition is needed. QED.

For a fixed integer matrix A of size kN, if

  nullity_R(A-lambda I)>=ell+1,

then EVERY minor of order kN-ell of A-tI vanishes at lambda. Each
is divisible by f in Z[t], because f is monic and minimal. Thus at
EVERY root a mod p of f one has

  nullity_Fp(A-aI)>=ell+1.                                 (16)

The old block-constant modular kernel has dimension exactly ell
for the good primes in Lemma 6.2. Combine (12), (13), (15), and (16).
For each fixed good root prime the same bound (14) holds. Lemma 6.1
supplies arbitrarily large such odd primes. Hence the real extra
lambda-eigenspace is absent with high probability.

This proves Theorem A for all real algebraic integers, whether or
not they are eigenvalues of Q. For a real number lambda that is not
an algebraic integer, no integer matrix can have lambda as an
eigenvalue, since its characteristic polynomial is monic in Z[t].
Such a lambda is also not an eigenvalue of Q, so Theorem A is then
deterministic. This covers every fixed real lambda.

All matrices here are symmetric. Their real geometric multiplicities
equal their algebraic multiplicities. The minor argument itself is
a nullity argument and does not silently assume a Jordan form over
a residue field. In particular, an algebraic conjugate that also
appears in Q causes no difficulty: Lemma 6.2 controls every root of
f modulo every selected good prime.

7. Transfer of the event to uniform simple internal graphs
-----------------------------------------------------------
The matching-sum graph, even conditioned on being simple, is NOT
asserted to be a uniform simple regular graph. Its weights depend
on the number of perfect-matching decompositions.

The established model relation is as follows. For each fixed d>=3
and even N, the union of d independent uniform perfect matchings
is mutually contiguous with the d-regular configuration model
conditioned on having no loops. This follows directly by taking
all summand degrees equal to one in Greenhill--Janson--Kim--Wormald,
Theorem 1.3, in its stated loopless exception. Their Corollary 1.1
also gives the corresponding simplicity-conditioned contiguity.
The same relation is cited in Nguyen--Wood, Section 4, with the
earlier Janson and Molloy--Robalewska--Robinson--Wormald sources.

For completeness, the finite-product use of contiguity is legitimate
even though the failure event couples all blocks. If Q_N is contiguous
with respect to P_N, then for any measurable 0<=g_N<=1,

  E_{P_N}g_N ->0   implies   E_{Q_N}g_N ->0.                (17)

For each fixed eta>0, use Markov to get
P_N(g_N>eta)->0, transfer this event by contiguity, and bound
E_Q g_N<=eta+Q_N(g_N>eta); then let eta tend to zero.
Apply (17) to sections of a product event, replacing the k independent
coordinate laws successively. This proves that fixed finite products
preserve the required direction of contiguity. The degrees d_i can
differ, since k and the whole degree list are fixed.

For any deterministic cross-matrix sequence B_N, Theorem A's failure
event has matching-model probability o(1). Product contiguity therefore
gives probability o(1) for k independent loopless configuration blocks.
For fixed degrees, the probability that all these blocks are simple
is bounded below by a positive constant. Conditioning on that event
costs a bounded factor and yields exactly k independent uniform simple
regular graphs. Thus their failure probability is o(1).

The uniform supremum over deterministic cross matrices follows by a
subsequence argument, not by an unstated uniform contiguity rate. If
the supremum in (2) did not tend to zero, choose a subsequence and a
violating deterministic matrix collection at each of its sizes, and
extend it arbitrarily to the other sufficiently large admissible
sizes. Its matching failure still tends to zero uniformly by (12),
contradicting the transfer just proved for every such sequence.
Finally any independent random collection of cross matrices satisfies
the same conclusion by averaging the uniform bound.

Only a probability-o(1) event was transferred. The equality of
expectations (1), whose variable can be as large as p^{kN}, is NOT
claimed for the uniform simple model on the basis of contiguity.


8. Exact recovery of the whole partition by one rational nullspace
------------------------------------------------------------------
Let m_Q(t) be the monic minimal polynomial of the known fixed frame
Q over Q. Since Q is a symmetric integer matrix, m_Q is squarefree
and belongs to Z[t]. Equivalently,

  m_Q(t)=char_Q(t)/gcd(char_Q(t),char_Q'(t))

with the gcd monic. Its degree is at most k, its coefficients are
fixed integers, and its roots are exactly the distinct real frame
eigenvalues.

Always m_Q(A)J_N=J_Nm_Q(Q)=0. Apply Theorem A simultaneously to all
the finitely many roots of m_Q. Since A is real symmetric, it is
diagonalizable, and with probability 1-o(1), uniformly in the cross
matrices,

  ker_R m_Q(A)
    = direct_sum_{lambda in spec Q} ker_R(A-lambda I)
    = W_old.                                              (18)

Its dimension is exactly k. Crucially this statement does not require
the frame to have distinct eigenvalues: whole repeated old eigenspaces
are included. Nor is a numerical distance from the new spectrum used.

Given A and Q, compute the rational nullspace of the integer matrix
m_Q(A), and place any rational basis as columns of a (kN)-by-k matrix
V. On event (18) there is an invertible k-by-k matrix T with

  V=J_N T.

The rows of V are therefore equal exactly within each planted block.
Rows associated with two distinct blocks cannot be equal, because
two rows of an invertible matrix T cannot be equal. Grouping equal
rational rows recovers all k classes, up to their unavoidable common
permutation. Computing individual irrational eigenvalues is unnecessary.

This is a polynomial-time exact algorithm in the bit model, with
the fixed frame supplied as input. Indeed the maximum total degree
of A is a fixed constant D depending on Q. If
m_Q(t)=sum_{r=0}^s c_r t^r, every row of m_Q(A) has absolute row sum
at most sum_r |c_r|D^r, a constant depending only on Q. Hadamard's
inequality bounds its minors by exp(O_Q(kN)); fraction-free rational
elimination has polynomial arithmetic and bit complexity. Forming
the fixed-degree matrix polynomial also takes polynomial time.
No nearly-linear runtime or numerical stability guarantee is claimed.

The familiar homogeneous k-class RSBM has

  Q=(a-b)I_k+b 1_k1_k^T,       a>=3, b>=1 fixed.

It has eigenvalue a+(k-1)b once and a-b with multiplicity k-1.
Its polynomial is (t-a-(k-1)b)(t-a+b). The theorem gives exact
recovery for even block sizes without an assortativity condition
a>b or any outlier condition. In this homogeneous case one may
also recover from span{1,ker(A-(a-b)I)}. More generally (18) handles
any connected symmetric integer frame with d_ii>=3, including frames
with irrational eigenvalues.

There is also an event certificate within the promised model:
if exact computation finds nullity_Q m_Q(A)=k, then the deterministic
inclusion W_old subset ker m_Q(A) already forces equality. This is
a certificate of exact recovery, not an estimate of its failure rate.

The source two-block proof already develops a special case of the
reference count and cross-matrix disjoint-row-support rank argument.
The present note's additional work is the uniformly bounded affine
hybrid induction for fixed k, the all-pattern reference count,
the algebraic residue-root reduction, and their joint old-space
recovery consequence. These are derived statements; a claim that
they have not appeared elsewhere would need a separate literature
and priority audit.

The exact limitations are:

  * N is even. Odd block sizes with even internal degrees are not
    supplied by the imported perfect-matching mixing theorem.
  * Every d_ii>=3. Degree-two internal blocks are not covered.
  * k and the complete degree frame Q are fixed. The error bounds
    and the prime-selection argument supply no growing-k theorem.
  * All cross blocks are simple, have fixed bounded degrees, and
    are fixed independently of the internal graphs. General weighted
    matrices can lose the uniform linear modular rank used in (10).
  * The theorem concerns fixed real target eigenvalues. It does not
    assert simple new spectrum, nor a uniform statement for a target
    chosen after inspecting A or for targets moving with N.
  * The matrix-polynomial recovery algorithm is given Q. Unknown-frame
    identifiability and algorithms are not established here.
  * The N limit precedes the choice of arbitrarily large fixed primes.
    Mixing constants and contiguity were not made quantitative.
  * The proof excludes exact collisions. It gives no positive lower
    bound on the distance from an old eigenvalue to nearby new ones.

No frozen manuscript or earlier audit was edited in producing this note.
