Positive-shift access barriers for relative fractional updates
Independent dynamic session, 2026-11-15

Status and scope
----------------
This file proves restricted access barriers. It proves no impossibility
for algorithms given the explicit graph, arbitrary-right-hand-side solves,
an exact shifted-solve oracle, or an unshifted vector solve. In particular,
it supplies no lower bound against all nearly-linear graph algorithms.
The input notes' log-determinant identity is a rank/existence statement;
none of the arguments below strengthens that identity to a graph
computational lower bound.

1. A finite positive-shift quadrature misses a hidden relative scale
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Let A_a=diag(a,1), u_a=(sqrt(a z_1),sqrt(z_2)), with fixed z_1,z_2>0,
z_1+z_2=tau, and a down to zero. Put B_a=A_a+u_a u_a^T. Then

  C_a=A_a^{-alpha/2}(B_a^alpha-A_a^alpha)A_a^{-alpha/2}

has (1,1) entry converging to

  c=((1+tau)/(1+z_2))^alpha-1 > 0.

This is the n=2 separated-scale lemma of the relative-response note.
For any fixed finite collection t_j>0 and finite weights omega_j,
the resolvent quadrature correction

  F_a=sum_j omega_j (A_a+t_j I)^{-1}u_a u_a^T(A_a+t_j I)^{-1}
                    /[1+u_a^T(A_a+t_j I)^{-1}u_a]

has relative (1,1) entry

  a^{1-alpha} z_1 sum_j omega_j/[(a+t_j)^2(1+h_a(t_j))] -> 0.

Consequently its relative norm error has liminf at least c. This also
applies to shifts and weights scaled by ||u_a||^2, provided their
normalized finite limits have positive shifts and finite weights: that
scale tends to z_2. An absolute-error rule normalized at the edit norm
therefore does not automatically provide relative error.

This is a failure of finite predetermined scales, not a lower bound on
adaptive rational Krylov construction. Tiny raw components can be
amplified by exact arithmetic and adaptively placed poles. A finite
relative approximation rank is not itself contradicted.

2. Adaptive noisy positive-shift oracle: a genuine restricted barrier
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix alpha in (0,1), tau>0, s=tau/2, and u=sqrt(s)e_1 in R^2. The
algorithm knows n=2, alpha, tau, u, ||A||=1, and ||u||^2=s. It does
not receive entries of A. It may make adaptive queries of either form:

 (a) endpoint H=A or H=B=A+uu^T, shift t>0, tolerance eta>0:
     obtain a vector y with
       ||y-(H+tI)^{-1}u||_{H+tI}
           <= eta ||(H+tI)^{-1}u||_{H+tI};

 (b) exact scalar leverage u^T A^{-1}u=tau.

The oracle may choose any admissible answers. No query has eta=0 or
t=0 in (a). The output is one fixed explicit symmetric matrix F, or
fixed explicit factors whose matrix product is F. For a two-factor
nonsymmetric output, replace it by its symmetric part; this cannot
increase the norm error to the symmetric target.

THEOREM. Write

  c=((1+tau)/(1+tau/2))^alpha-1.

For every 0<eps<c, no deterministic algorithm with a uniform finite
query bound q can guarantee, on all SPD inputs in this model and all
admissible oracle answers,

  ||A^{-alpha/2}(B^alpha-A^alpha-F)A^{-alpha/2}|| <= eps.

This also excludes the stronger one-sided condition
0 <= B^alpha-A^alpha-F <= eps A^alpha. The statement allows arbitrary
computation between queries, adaptive shifts and adaptive positive
tolerances. Therefore positive tolerance chosen extremely small at a
query does not repair this access model uniformly across all scales.

Proof. For 0<a<1/2 set p_a=a/(1-a), and put

  w_a=sqrt(p_a)e_1+sqrt(1-p_a)e_2,
  A_a=I-(1-a)w_a w_a^T,
  B_a=A_a+uu^T.

The eigenvalues of A_a are a and 1, and ||A_a||=1. Directly,

  u^T A_a^{-1}u=s[p_a/a+(1-p_a)]=2s=tau.

Thus all disclosed data are exactly the same for every a. In the
eigenbasis (w_a,w_a-perp), the squared update components are

  a z_1(a), z_2(a),
  z_1(a)=s/(1-a), z_2(a)=s(1-2a)/(1-a).

Here z_1(a)+z_2(a)=tau and both tend to s. The same dominated
convergence proof as the separated-scale lemma gives

  w_a^T(B_a^alpha-A_a^alpha)w_a/a^alpha -> c,

and the off-diagonal entry of that raw fractional difference in this
eigenbasis tends to zero. In fact the relative off-diagonal is
O_alpha,tau(a^{(1-alpha)/2}).

Use the following common transcript, independent of a:

  at A,t return y_A^0(t)=u/(1+t),
  at B,t return y_B^0(t)=u/(1+s+t),
  at the scalar leverage query return tau.

For A, let h_a(t)=u^T(A_a+tI)^{-1}u. Exact calculation gives

  h_a(t)=s/(1+t)+s a/[(a+t)(1+t)],

and the squared relative energy error of y_A^0 is exactly

  ||y_A^0-(A_a+tI)^{-1}u||_{A_a+tI}^2 / h_a(t)
       = a(1-a)/[(1+t)(t+2a)] -> 0

for every fixed t>0. Therefore every fixed positive tolerance eventually
permits the common A answer.

For B, Sherman-Morrison gives

  (B_a+tI)^{-1}u=(A_a+tI)^{-1}u/(1+h_a(t)).

The right side converges in Euclidean norm to u/(1+s+t). Since
||B_a+tI|| <= 1+s+t, its squared error in (B_a+tI)-energy tends to
zero. Its exact squared solution energy is h_a(t)/(1+h_a(t)), tending
to s/(1+s+t)>0. Hence every fixed positive tolerance eventually permits
the common B answer too. The scalar leverage answer is exact.

Run the deterministic algorithm on this transcript. Every finite prefix
has positive queried shifts and tolerances, so every answer in that
prefix is admissible for every sufficiently small a. If the algorithm
did not stop by query q, its first q+1 actions would contradict its
uniform bound on one such valid A_a input. It therefore stops by q and
returns some fixed F. All its queries and output are now fixed; choose
a sufficiently small to make their common answers admissible.

It remains to prove that any fixed F fails for all sufficiently small
a. Suppose to the contrary that the desired relative norm inequality
holds along a sequence a down to zero. Testing the low eigenvector
forces F_22=0, since the raw target low quadratic form and eps a^alpha
both tend to zero, and w_a -> e_2. Testing the cross entry between
the low and high eigenvectors forces F_12=0: the allowed raw cross
error is eps a^{alpha/2} -> 0, and the target raw cross entry tends to
zero. It follows that

  w_a^T F w_a/a^alpha=p_a F_11/a^alpha -> 0,

because alpha<1. The target low quadratic form divided by a^alpha
tends to c, contradicting eps<c. Thus for this fixed F there is a
positive a_F below which all inputs fail. Choose a below a_F and below
the finitely many oracle-admissibility thresholds. This establishes the
claimed failure on one genuine SPD input. QED.

What precisely bypasses the theorem?

* Exact vector queries have eta=0 and can read the tiny raw component.
* A t=0 vector query along u has an e_2 component of order a^{-1/2},
  exposing the hidden scale; its energy error ratio under the common
  answer does not tend to zero. Known scalar leverage is much weaker.
* Arbitrary right-hand sides, e.g. e_2, also expose the low eigenvalue.
* Explicit input entries reveal a. So do sufficiently informative static
  data structures prepared from A; their cost must be separately charged.
* The matrices are SPD, not asserted unit-mass graph Laplacians. Their
  spectral path realizations do not preserve an indistinguishable oracle
  transcript or fixed disclosed u. Such a transfer would need a new proof.

The theorem is a deterministic bounded-query statement against permitted
oracle errors. It does not assert a lower bound against randomized
algorithms with an exact oracle, and does not manufacture a log(kappa)
lower bound. The proved conclusion is that this restricted approximate
oracle admits no bound uniform over all positive a.

3. Standard energy-solve accuracy alone cannot certify relative factors
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
This obstruction occurs already for ordinary unit-mass graphs, although
it is not a computational lower bound.

Let L_a be the 3-vertex path with conductances w_12=1,w_23=a, and
increase edge 12 by d=tau>0. Then u=sqrt(d)(e_1-e_2) and
u^T L_a^dagger u=tau exactly because edge 12 is a bridge. The smaller
positive eigenvalue lambda_a tends to zero. Let w_a be a corresponding
unit eigenvector in 1-perp. Fix t=1, v_a=(L_a+I)^{-1}u, and eta>0.
The approximate solution

  vhat_a=v_a+eta ||v_a||_{L_a+I}/sqrt(1+lambda_a) w_a

has exactly the permitted relative (L_a+I)-energy error eta. Moreover,
||v_a||_{L_a+I} stays bounded above and bounded away from zero.

As a -> 0, w_a tends to the weak-cut vector proportional to (1,1,-2),
which is orthogonal to u, and hence w_a^T v_a -> 0. Therefore, for
every fixed positive weight omega,

  w_a^T (L_a^dagger)^{alpha/2}
      [omega vhat_a vhat_a^T]
          (L_a^dagger)^{alpha/2} w_a -> infinity.

The true relative update norm is bounded by (1+tau)^alpha-1, by
L_a+uu^T <= (1+tau)L_a on the support and operator monotonicity.
So treating an arbitrary energy-accurate shifted solution as a raw
factor cannot give a gap-independent relative factor guarantee at
fixed eta. A valid algorithm needs a stronger error structure, a filter,
or a separate certification argument. This shows insufficiency of one
solver contract, not impossibility for graph solvers as algorithms.

4. Cost and prior art checks
---------------------------
Explicit factors with n rows and r columns require Omega(nr) output
writes under that output convention. The determinant sharpness result
forces r of order alpha log(1+tau)/eps in the worst case. A claimed
nearly-linear update cost must therefore specify whether it means
roughly (m+n)r up to logarithms, m+nr up to logarithms, an implicit
representation, or an amortized maintenance cost. The output-size fact
does not force a graph gap or conductance condition-number logarithm.

Primary sources consulted:

* Beckermann, Cortinovis, Kressner, Schweitzer, Low-rank updates of
  matrix functions II: Rational Krylov methods (2021),
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11501 . The paper develops rational Krylov
  update methods and rational exactness/convergence analysis. It is not
  being cited as a relative graph construction lower bound.
* Massei and Robol, Rational Krylov for Stieltjes matrix functions:
  convergence and pole selection,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02032 . The paper analyzes pole selection
  for actions of Stieltjes functions of SPD matrices. Its upper bounds
  do not prove condition dependence necessary for every access model.
* Koutis, Miller, Peng, A nearly-mlogn time solver for SDD systems,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.4842 . The solver's stated accuracy is
  relative system-energy error. Section 3 above explains why this
  contract cannot silently be substituted for relative fractional
  factor accuracy.

No claim of historical priority is made for these elementary barriers.
