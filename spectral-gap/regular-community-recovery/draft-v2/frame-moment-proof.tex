\section{Bounded intertwiner moments for regular frames}\label{sec:frame-moment-proof}

Fix a connected nonnegative integer frame $Q=(d_{ij})_{i,j\le k}$
whose row sums all equal $d\ge3$. Choose its primitive positive
integer size vector $(c_i)$, satisfying $c_id_{ij}=c_jd_{ji}$, and put
$N_i=c_iN$, $n=\sum_iN_i$. This represents every compatible integer
size vector: connectedness fixes the ratios, and primitivity makes the
scale an integer. A type-$i$ vertex has $d_{ij}$ distinguished stubs
aimed at type $j$. Independently pair each present internal stub pool
uniformly, and match the two sides of each present cross pool by a
uniform bijection. Omit absent factors. Scales are admissible when
$N_id_{ii}$ is even. Loops and parallel edges are retained, and a loop
contributes two to adjacency and degree. Write $A_N$ for this
configuration adjacency matrix.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded regular-frame moment]\label{thm:frame-bounded-moment}
For every fixed prime $p$ and integer $r\ge0$ there is a finite constant
$C(Q,c,p,r)$ such that, uniformly in $R\in\mathbb F_p^{r\times r}$,
\[
 \sup_{N\text{ admissible}}\mathbb E Z_R(A_N)\le C(Q,c,p,r),
 \qquad
 Z_R(A)=|\{Y\in\mathbb F_p^{n\times r}:AY=YR\}|.
\]
The same conclusion holds for independent uniform simple internal
regular and cross biregular factors, at all admissible scales where
those simple factors exist. Internal degrees may be zero or one;
individual class sizes may differ. No condition on the rank or Jordan
form of $R$, or on $p$ dividing a degree, is imposed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Bounded moments for regular lifts]\label{thm:lift-bounded-moment}
If $B$ is a fixed connected simple $d$-regular base, $d\ge3$, and
$A_N$ is the adjacency matrix of its independent uniform random
$N$-lift, then for every fixed $p,r$,
\[
 \sup_{N\ge1}\sup_{R\in\mathbb F_p^{r\times r}}
                  \mathbb E Z_R(A_N)<\infty.
\]
Both bipartite bases and every positive lift size are included.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Take $Q=A_B$ and $c_i=1$. Each present pool is a cross matching with
one stub per vertex, exactly the random-lift law. All factors are
automatically simple and every $N$ is admissible.
\end{proof}

We prove a larger homogeneous class to close dimension induction.
Regard $d_{ii}>0$ as a loop in the old-type support. If that support
is nonbipartite, use a common space $V=\mathbb F_p^r$ and arbitrary
type-dependent maps $T_i:V\to V$. If it is bipartite with sides
$L,R_0$, use spaces $V_L,V_{R_0}$, allowing zero dimensions, and maps
$T_i:V_{\mathrm{side}(i)}\to V_{\mathrm{opposite}(i)}$. The equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-system}
 \sum_{\text{stubs }vu}Y_u=T_i(Y_v),\qquad \mathrm{type}(v)=i.
\end{equation}
Let $V_i$ be its root space, $U_i$ its common neighbor space, and put
$u_i=|V_i|$, $v_i=|U_i|$. In the two-space case there are no internal
factors. Reversibility and regularity give equal total physical sizes
on its sides:
\[
 d\sum_{i\in L}N_i=\sum_{i\in L,j\in R_0}N_id_{ij}
                         =d\sum_{j\in R_0}N_j.
\]
The number of types on each side and the individual $N_i$ need not
agree. We prove bounded moments uniformly over the finitely many
maps in these fixed spaces. The all-zero-dimensional system has one
assignment and needs no local limit argument.

\subsection{Exact counts, repeated slots, and all zero phases}
A legal star at type $i$ records its root $x\in V_i$ and all $d$
ordered physical neighbor labels in $U_i$, with sum $T_i(x)$. It has
$u_iv_i^{d-1}$ possibilities. For star counts $t_i(s)$, let $m_{ij}$
pool the root--neighbor counts over the $d_{ij}$ slots aimed at $j$.
Cross tables reverse, and an internal table is symmetric with even
diagonal. Their exact expected contribution is
\begin{align}\label{eq:fmp-exact-types}
 &\prod_i\frac{N_i!}{\prod_s t_i(s)!}
 \prod_{i<j:d_{ij}>0}\frac{\prod_{x,y}m_{ij}(x,y)!}{(N_id_{ij})!}
 \prod_{i:d_{ii}>0}
 \frac{\prod_{x<y}m_{ii}(x,y)!\prod_x(m_{ii}(x,x)-1)!!}
 {(N_id_{ii}-1)!!}.
\end{align}
Indeed the multinomials assign stars, including roots, to labeled
vertices. Pairing each requested category with its reverse gives the
factorial ratios. Every configuration and root labeling determine
the stars uniquely, including at loops. There is no further root
multinomial. We use $(-1)!!=1$ for empty categories.

For $P_i=t_i/N_i$, write $\mu_i$ for the root law and
$\nu_{ij}=m_{ij}/(N_id_{ij})$ for each present pooled pair law. Its
marginals are $\mu_i,\mu_j$. Zero-safe factorial bounds give
\eqref{eq:fmp-exact-types} as $\exp\{N\Phi+O(\log(N+1))\}$,
uniformly over all types, where
\[
 \Phi=\sum_i c_iH(P_i)-\sum_{i<j}c_id_{ij}H(\nu_{ij})
                         -\sum_i\frac{c_id_{ii}}2H(\nu_{ii}).
\]
Inactive terms are omitted. Only polynomially many empirical types
occur, because all data except $N$ are fixed.

Average each $P_i$ over permutations of physical slots aimed at the
same old type. This preserves pooled tables and legal support and
increases entropy. Strict concavity shows that equality in this
step requires $P_i$ itself to be invariant. For the averaged laws,
let $I_{ij}$ be the pooled pair mutual information and $J_{it}$ the
conditional total correlation of all but physical slot $t$, given
the root. Every deletion is lossless. Averaging those identities and
using regularity and reversibility gives exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-entropy}
 \Phi=-\frac{d-2}{d}
 \left[\sum_{i<j}c_id_{ij}I_{ij}+\sum_i\frac{c_id_{ii}}2I_{ii}\right]
                    -\frac1d\sum_i c_i\sum_tJ_{it}\le0.
\end{equation}
The coefficients are real entropy coefficients; no degree is divided
by in the finite field. An original entropy zero must have zero
symmetrization loss and satisfy this identity at equality.

We recall the elementary equality argument in full. If $d$ variables
in a finite vector space have fixed sum and every retained $d-1$
tuple has its product marginal law, fix two coordinates and condition
the other $d-2$ to support values. The retained-product property
makes the event positive and preserves both remaining marginals.
Their fixed sum makes their laws reflected translates, so their
support-difference spaces coincide. Applying this to every pair gives
one space $H$. Varying one conditioned coordinate, available since
$d\ge3$, makes the reflection identities imply translation
invariance by all generators of $H$. Each marginal is uniform on
its full coset of $H$.

At equality in \eqref{eq:fmp-entropy}, all present pair laws are
products and every retained tuple is a root-independent product.
Apply the argument conditionally on each root. Every old type occurs
as a neighbor, so each root law is uniform on a coset $b_i+H_i$.
All physical neighbors at any type have the same subspace. Thus the
$H_i$ are constant along even walks in the old support. Repeated
slots do not change this relation; an internal factor supplies a
self-incidence and an odd closed walk. Consequently the possibilities
are a common $H$ in the nonbipartite case, or $H_L,H_{R_0}$ in the
bipartite case, subject to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-phase}
 T_i(H_i)\subseteq K_i,\qquad
 T_i(b_i)-\sum_jd_{ij}b_j\in K_i,
\end{equation}
where $K_i$ is the common neighbor subspace. Conversely these conditions
give a uniform root and any $d-1$ independent uniform neighbor cosets,
with the last label inferred in its required coset. This is the
unique uniform legal star law for those cosets and gives zero entropy.
There are finitely many such phases. Except for the full uniform
phase, their common subspace, or at least one side subspace, is proper.

\subsection{The full physical-slot comparison and exact reference law}
For the central argument retain a table $M_{it}(x,y)$ for each
physical slot $t$, even when several target the same old type. Pool
these tables only to form $m_{ij}$ in the factorial weight $W$ given
by the last two products of \eqref{eq:fmp-exact-types}; put $W=0$
when pooled compatibility or internal parity fails.

Under $\mathsf P$ sample uniform legal stars independently at every
vertex. Under $\mathsf Q$ sample the root and all $d$ neighbors
independently uniformly. Put
$K_N=\prod_i(u_iv_i^{d-1})^{N_i}$. Exact counting gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-normalizers}
 \mathbb E Z=K_N\mathbb E_{\mathsf P}W(M),\qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathsf Q}W(M)=\prod_i v_i^{-dN_i},\qquad
 K_N\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q}W(M)=1.
\end{equation}
For the middle identity, each configuration and unrestricted root
assignment determine all physical neighbor labels, so summing the
weight over unrestricted stars counts the roots once. The last
identity is immediate in one space. In two spaces it uses the equal
total physical sizes proved above. The normalized measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-reference}
 \rho_N(m)=K_N\mathbb P_{\mathsf Q}(M=m)W(m)
\end{equation}
is exactly the law obtained by sampling the configuration first and
then independent uniform root labels. This is an identity of laws,
not a permutation approximation to the configuration model.

Let $\overline m_N=(N_i\mathbf1/(u_iv_i))_{i,t}$ and
$\Delta=M-\overline m_N$. At type $i$ the real table-direction space
$\mathcal H_i$ consists of $d$ rectangular tables with total zero in
each and identical root-row margins. It has dimension
$(u_i-1)+du_i(v_i-1)$. Both local laws have the same uniform mean and
the same positive covariance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-covariance}
 \Sigma_i=(u_iv_i)^{-1}[I+(d-1)\Pi_i],
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_i$ projects onto common row-constant modes. Conditional on
the root, any two distinct neighbor labels are independent uniform
under either law: a third physical slot absorbs the sum constraint.
Conditional variances give eigenvalue $1/(u_iv_i)$ on row-centered
modes, and the common root gives $d/(u_iv_i)$ on the $u_i-1$ common
row modes. These span the entire declared real space, including when
an alphabet has size one.

Let $\Lambda_{0i}$ be the full integer common-row lattice. Differences
of unrestricted stars generate it by within-row transfers and common
root changes. The legal difference lattice $\Lambda_i$ is exactly
its sublattice specified by one neighbor-space congruence per root:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-lattice}
 \sum_t\sum_y y\,m_{it}(x,y)-T_i(x)r_i(x)=0\quad\hbox{in }U_i,
\end{equation}
where $r_i(x)$ is the common row margin. Here is completeness of these
periods. A circle character constant on local legal stars is a sum
of functions $\theta_t(x,y)$. At fixed $x$, varying two entries while
a third completes the prescribed sum forces
$\theta_t(x,y)=\alpha_t(x)+\chi_x(y)$ with one common additive
character $\chi_x$ of $U_i$. Root variation forces
$\sum_t\alpha_t(x)+\chi_x(T_i(x))$ to be constant. Modulo characters
already constant on unrestricted stars, these are exactly the
characters of \eqref{eq:fmp-lattice}. A within-row transfer changes
one root's congruence arbitrarily, so all are independent. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-index}
 [\Lambda_{0i}:\Lambda_i]=v_i^{u_i},\qquad I_*:=\prod_i v_i^{u_i}.
\end{equation}
The legal affine lattice hull at every size lies inside the
unrestricted one, since every legal star is an unrestricted star.
Pooled pairing compatibility may fail in a residue class; then its
weight is zero. No residue hypothesis is needed for an upper bound.

\subsection{A relative local estimate on a fixed neighborhood}
We provide the finite-support argument needed beyond a shrinking
central window. For either local law let $X$ be its star table vector,
with mean $\mu$, maximal lattice $\Lambda$, and positive covariance
on its real span. Its analytic log moment generating function
$\mathcal L(\theta)=\log\mathbb E e^{\langle\theta,X\rangle}$ has
positive Hessian. The inverse function theorem gives an analytic
mean-matching tilt $\theta(z)$ for all $z$ in a fixed neighborhood
of $\mu$. Shrink to compact neighborhoods for both laws. Tilted
support weights stay bounded below, covariance eigenvalues stay
positive, and derivatives remain uniformly bounded.

For $m/\ell=z$ in that neighborhood and
$m\in\ell x_0+\Lambda$, tilting is exact:
\[
 \mathbb P(S_\ell=m)
 =e^{-\ell J(z)}\mathbb P_{\theta(z)}(S_\ell=m),\qquad
 J(z)=\langle\theta(z),z\rangle-\mathcal L(\theta(z)).
\]
At the tilted mean, Fourier inversion on the maximal-lattice torus
has one modulus-one maximum. Off a fixed neighborhood of it, the
modulus is uniformly at most a fixed number less than one. Near it,
the centered logarithm is its covariance quadratic plus a uniform
cubic error and has a uniform Gaussian modulus bound. Rescaling
$\eta=\sqrt\ell\,\xi$ and integrating the cubic error against that
Gaussian gives $O(\ell^{-1/2})$; the region
$\|\eta\|>\ell^{1/12}$ and the outer torus are exponentially small.
Consequently, uniformly over this fixed tilt neighborhood,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-tilted-density}
 \mathbb P_{\theta(z)}(S_\ell=m)=
 \frac{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}
 {(2\pi\ell)^{h/2}(\det\Sigma_z)^{1/2}}
 [1+O(\ell^{-1/2})],
\end{equation}
with determinants on the real span of dimension $h$. In particular
every such affine lattice point has positive probability eventually;
no support-saturation assumption has been made.

The two local means and covariances agree, so their rate functions
have the same terms through quadratic order. Their tilted determinant
ratio is bounded on the fixed neighborhood. Apply
\eqref{eq:fmp-tilted-density} at $\ell=N_i=c_iN$ and multiply over
types. For some fixed $\varepsilon_0,C>0$, on the legal affine lattice,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-fixed-ratio}
 \frac{\mathbb P_{\mathsf P}(M=m)}{\mathbb P_{\mathsf Q}(M=m)}
 \le CI_*\exp\!\left(C\frac{\|\Delta\|^3}{N^2}\right)
 \quad(\|\Delta\|\le\varepsilon_0N).
\end{equation}
On $\|\Delta\|\le\sqrt N\log N$ the determinant ratio tends to one
and the rate error is $O(\log^3N/\sqrt N)=o(1)$, giving ratio
$I_*(1+o(1))$ uniformly. Off the legal lattice the numerator is zero.
Affine containment and the local estimate make the denominator
positive at all relevant central points for large $N$.

\subsection{Concentration under the actual weighted reference law}
Represent each internal uniform pairing by a uniform permutation of
its stubs, pairing consecutive positions. Each pairing has exactly
$2^{L/2}(L/2)!$ such representations. Represent every cross bijection
by a uniform permutation as well. Together with the independent root
labels these provide $O(N)$ permutation-image and label exposures.

A permutation transposition changes at most two edges and four
physical incidences, so a scalar full-slot table entry changes by
at most four. Changing one vertex label affects its $d$ own
incidences and at most $d$ reverse incidences, giving bound $2d$.
These bounds include loops and repeated endpoints. Expose permutation
images sequentially. Two possible next images have completions coupled
by a single transposition preserving earlier images, so the conditional
expectations differ by at most four. Then expose the vertex labels,
whose increments have range at most $2d$. The usual exponential-moment
bound for bounded-range martingale differences, with sum of squared
ranges $O(N)$, gives for each scalar entry
$\rho_N(|M_j-\mathbb E_{\rho_N}M_j|\ge t)\le2e^{-ct^2/N}$.
A union bound over the fixed number of coordinates gives the same
form for a norm, after changing constants.

Its mean differs from $\overline m_N$ by only $O(1)$. Cross partners
are distinct physical vertices, hence have independent labels. For
an internal physical stub at type $i$, its partner lies at its own
vertex with probability
\[
 \gamma_i=\frac{d_{ii}-1}{N_id_{ii}-1}.
\]
Otherwise the two labels are independent. Thus a slot-table entry
has mean $N_i[(1-\gamma_i)/q^2+
\gamma_i\mathbf1_{x=y}/q]$ in a one-space alphabet of size $q$.
Its displacement from $N_i/q^2$ is bounded. Absent factors are omitted,
and $d_{ii}=1$ gives zero loop bias. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-reference-tail}
 \rho_N(\|\Delta\|\ge t)\le C_0e^{-c_0t^2/N}
                  \qquad(t\ge C_1\sqrt N),
\end{equation}
valid for every larger $t$, not only inside the local neighborhood.

By \eqref{eq:fmp-normalizers}--\eqref{eq:fmp-reference}, restricted
contributions to $\mathbb E Z$ are expectations under $\rho_N$ of
the likelihood ratio. The logarithmic window is at most $I_*+o(1)$.
Choose $\varepsilon>0$ so small that \eqref{eq:fmp-fixed-ratio}
holds and $\eta=C\varepsilon<c_0$. On $T=\|\Delta\|\le\varepsilon N$,
the exponential in that ratio is at most $e^{\eta T^2/N}$. For
$L=\sqrt N\log N$, integration of \eqref{eq:fmp-reference-tail} gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E_{\rho_N}[e^{\eta T^2/N}\mathbf1_{T\ge L}]
 &\le C e^{-(c_0-\eta)L^2/N}
   +C\int_L^\infty\frac{2\eta t}{N}e^{-(c_0-\eta)t^2/N}\,dt\\
 &\le C'e^{-c'\log^2N}=o(1).
\end{align*}
Extending the majorant past $\varepsilon N$ only enlarges the upper
bound; the reference tail is valid along the whole integral. Thus an
open fixed neighborhood in the \emph{full physical-slot tables} has
bounded contribution, with limsup at most $I_*$. No pooled Gaussian
precision assertion is needed.

\subsection{Proper phases and exact internal forcing}
Fix a proper phase $b_i+H_i$ and let $M_H(N)$ be the moment of its
homogeneous restricted system. Quotient by these subspaces and
subtract the type-constant quotient assignment. Conditions
\eqref{eq:fmp-phase} make the resulting defect a homogeneous quotient
solution on every configuration, including loops and parallel edges.
For any fixed quotient assignment its lifts form an affine system,
empty or of cardinality exactly the restricted kernel size $Z_H(G)$.
The representatives need not solve the ambient base equations. Thus
zero defects contribute at most $M_H(N)$.

Let $S$ be a nonzero quotient support of size $s$. An exterior zero
vertex cannot have exactly one incidence from $S$, counting edges
with multiplicity. If $a$ edges lie in $S$, $b=ds-2a$ leave it, and
$w$ exterior vertices are touched, the certificate of all edges
incident to $S$ has
\[
 w\le b/2,\quad E=a+b\le ds,\quad V=s+w,\quad
 E-V\ge\alpha s,\qquad \alpha=(d-2)/2.
\]
Loops count once in $a$ and twice in the degree sum. The certificate
prescribes every stub at $S$, so it needs no absent-edge condition.

We control its correlation with $Z_H$ by exact conditional forcing.
For a cross bijection with $m$ unused stubs on each side, force
$u\mapsto v$ by swapping the images of $u$ and the current preimage
of $v$. Earlier prescriptions remain fixed; every permitted output
has exactly $m$ preimages. For an internal pairing with $m$ free
stubs, leave a prescribed pair $\{u,v\}$ alone if already present;
otherwise replace $\{u,x\},\{v,y\}$ by $\{u,v\},\{x,y\}$.
Every output has exactly
\[
 1+2\frac{m-2}{2}=m-1
\]
preimages: itself, and two inverse orientations for every other free
pair. All involved stubs avoid earlier prescriptions. These constant
fiber counts prove the uniform conditional law at every step, also
when several stubs have the same physical owner. Applying the forcing
separately to the independent factors gives a coupled $G^{\mathcal C}$
with exactly the law conditional on the certificate.

At most $E$ switches are used, each changing at most four physical
equation rows. With $r_*$ the ambient dimension, or the sum of side
dimensions, the restricted operators satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(L_H(G^{\mathcal C})-L_H(G))\le4r_*E,
 \qquad Z_H(G^{\mathcal C})\le p^{4r_*E}Z_H(G).
\]
This holds for rectangular two-space operators too, since the
variable space and on-site maps stay fixed. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-conditional-kernel}
 \mathbb E[Z_H(G)\mid\mathcal C]\le p^{4r_*E}M_H(N).
\end{equation}

Every active pool has at least $\lambda n$ stubs for a fixed
$\lambda>0$. For $s\le\lambda n/(4d)$ all prescribed internal or
cross pairing denominators are at least $\lambda n/2$. Hence
$\mathbb P(\mathcal C)\le(C_Q/n)^E$. Choose $S$ and its exterior
vertices in at most $n^{s+w}/(s!w!)$ ways, quotient labels in at most
$p^{r_*s}$ ways, exterior stubs in at most $2^{dw}$ ways, and pair
the selected stubs in at most $(2ds)^E$ ways. Invalid typed
prescriptions only overcount. Multiplying their probabilities by
\eqref{eq:fmp-conditional-kernel} and absorbing fixed factors gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-support-count}
 \mathbb E\#\{Y:D_b(Y)=s\}
 \le M_H(N)\,ds\,L^s(s/n)^{\alpha s},
\end{equation}
where $D_b$ counts vertices outside their phase cosets. The size-power
calculation is
\[
 \frac{s^E n^{s+w-E}}{s!w!}
 =(s/n)^{E-s-w}\frac{s^s}{s!}\frac{s^w}{w!}
 \le(s/n)^{\alpha s}\frac{s^s}{s!}\frac{s^w}{w!}.
\]
Use $s^s/s!\le e^s$, sum $s^w/w!$ by $e^s$, and allow at most $ds$
choices of $E$. The rank factor is exponential in $s$, since $E\le ds$,
and is absorbed in $L^s$.

Unlike a simple-base lift, the configuration may have singleton
supports. The sum must start at $s=1$. For a sufficiently small
fixed $\varepsilon$, consecutive terms on the right of
\eqref{eq:fmp-support-count} have ratio at most
$2Le^\alpha((s+1)/n)^\alpha\le1/2$ through $s\le\varepsilon n$.
Their sum is $O(n^{-\alpha})M_H(N)$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fmp-boundary}
 \mathbb E\#\{Y:D_b(Y)\le\varepsilon n\}
             \le[1+O(n^{-(d-2)/2})]M_H(N).
\end{equation}
No independence between a sparse defect event and a bulk kernel
moment has been assumed.

\subsection{Induction, all admissible sizes, and the simple law}
Induct on $r$ for nonbipartite support, and on $r_L+r_{R_0}$ in
the bipartite class. Every proper phase restricts to lower dimension,
with well-defined type-dependent maps in the same homogeneous class.
Zero-dimensional sides cause no exception; the total-zero-dimensional
system has one assignment. Hence \eqref{eq:fmp-boundary} and the
induction hypothesis bound every proper-phase neighborhood.

There are finitely many phases. Their open root-coset neighborhoods
and the open full physical-slot neighborhood above contain every
entropy zero. At the full uniform zero all physical slot tables,
not just the pooled tables, are products. The complementary feasible
star-law set is compact and its continuous original entropy exponent
is strictly negative. Uniform factorial errors and polynomially many
types in \eqref{eq:fmp-exact-types} bound its contribution by
$\operatorname{poly}(N)e^{-cN}$. This proves a finite limsup of the
whole moment. There are finitely many maps at fixed dimensions, and
finitely many earlier admissible scales, so their maxima give the
claimed uniform supremum.

Finally fixed-degree internal and cross-biregular configuration
factors have positive limiting simplicity probability. More precisely
the independent product tends to $e^{-\Lambda}>0$, where
\[
 \Lambda=\sum_{i:d_{ii}>0}\frac{d_{ii}^2-1}{4}
       +\sum_{i<j:d_{ij}>0}\frac{(d_{ij}-1)(d_{ji}-1)}2.
\]
The internal loop and double-edge means are $(d_{ii}-1)/2$ and
$(d_{ii}-1)^2/4$, and the cross double-edge mean is the displayed
cross summand. Fixed factorial moments follow by choosing the
prescribed stubs: disjoint bad patterns give the product limits,
and overlapping distinct patterns have edge excess and contribute
$O(N^{-1})$. Finite Bonferroni bounds for the zero event then give
the exponential limit. Absent factors contribute one; internal degree
one and cross factors with a degree-one side are already simple.
Thus joint simplicity has a positive lower bound along all sufficiently
large admissible scales. Every simple internal graph has
$(d_{ii}!)^{N_i}$ distinguished-stub realizations, and a simple cross
graph has $(d_{ij}!)^{N_i}(d_{ji}!)^{N_j}$. Conditioning therefore
gives exactly the asserted independent uniform simple factors, and
\[
 \mathbb E[Z_R\mid\text{all factors simple}]
 \le\frac{\mathbb E Z_R}{\mathbb P(\text{all factors simple})}.
\]
This transfers the raw expectation directly. Finitely many earlier
scales at which simple factors exist are absorbed into the constant.
It proves Theorem~\ref{thm:frame-bounded-moment}.

Every fixed power is also bounded: for a fixed integer $s\ge1$,
$Z_R(A)^s=Z_{\operatorname{diag}(R,\ldots,R)}(A)$, so apply the
theorem at dimension $sr$. The same observation controls products
over a fixed finite target family. All frame, degree, field and
dimension parameters are fixed. This proof makes no exact scalar
moment, real-corank, growing-parameter or noisy-recovery assertion.
