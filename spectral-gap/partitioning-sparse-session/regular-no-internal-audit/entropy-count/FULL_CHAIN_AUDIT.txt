1. Exact theorem and quantifiers
-------------------------------
Fix supplied constants K>=1 and D>=3. The true fixed integer nonnegative
frame Q has1<=k<=K nonempty old classes, connected off-diagonal support
(one class allowed), and all row sums equal the SAME d,3<=d<=D. Internal
degrees ai are allowed to be0or1. Class sizes satisfy edge balance and
Ni*ai even. All nonzero internal regular and cross biregular factors are
independently uniform SIMPLE factors; zero factors are omitted.

The conclusion that passes is: with high probability along every
admissible size sequence, every equitable partition into at most K
nonempty classes is a compatible coarsening of the old classes. Hence
the old partition is the unique finest equitable partition WITHIN that
bounded class family. With only the bounds K,D, the specified exact
algorithm recovers k, the partition and Q with high probability, has
unconditional expected polynomial bit work, and has a polynomial cap
with abstention and worst-case polynomial work.

This does not assert global fineness among all equitable partitions:
the n-singleton partition always exists. It does not cover arbitrary
irregular total degrees by merely bounding them. The previous theorem
with all internal degrees>=2 and the current regular-total-degree theorem
have distinct overlapping assumptions, as the composition explicitly says.

2. Count and entropy bridge
--------------------------
The exact labeled-stub factorial formula is valid with absent internal
factors and internal perfect matchings. It assigns ordered root stars
once, counts internal symmetric/even-diagonal categories by factorials
and double factorials, and counts reversed cross categories by factorials.
Loops contribute two to adjacency and degree. The number of legal stars
over Fp^r is exactly q^d because any physical slot is determined by the
root and d-1 retained labels with coefficient one.

The uniform factorial remainder includes all zero counts. There are at
most(n+1)^(K*q_*^d) types, and each remainder is bounded by
F[log(dn+1)+1], F=K+[K(K+3)/2]*q_*^2, q_*=p^K. No boundary-type
regularity assumption or local central limit theorem is hidden here.

Within-type slot symmetrization preserves every pooled pair law, root
law, matching constraint and local sum identity, while increasing star
entropy. It never exchanges different neighbor types. The all-slot
identity is exact for the symmetrized laws; the original exponent is no
larger. The composition states this distinction correctly.

With alpha_i=Ni/n, the normalized identity is
  L=-Phi=(d-2)/d *E+J/d,
  E=sum_(i<=j)w_ij I_ij,
  J=sum_i alpha_i sum_t J_it,
  w_ii=alpha_i*ai/2, w_ij=alpha_i*dij(i<j).
The factor one-half for internal edges is correct. Reversibility and
COMMON row sum d cancel all root entropy terms exactly. This gives
nonpositive entropy for every candidate R over every fixed field,
without an internal slot or a matrix inverse.

The corresponding unnormalized exponent in the exact count is Psi=n*Phi.
Therefore the real gap below contributes exp(-constant*n) to the actual
candidate count. No normalization factor or cross-edge factor is lost.

For real one-hot candidates, the same exact category count applies with
the INTEGER local equation. A legal omitted label is unique, so there
are at most r^d legal stars. One may pad the one-hot label alphabet with
unused symbols to use the generous q_*=2^K uniform remainder/type bound.
The case r=1 has no positive root defect and is harmless. This justifies
the bridge from the finite-field moment to integer partition counting;
the variance argument itself is not performed modulo two.

3. Complete real one-hot rigidity calculation
--------------------------------------------
After within-type symmetrization, for each i and omission t the auxiliary
law keeps the root law and independently samples all retained neighbors
from their root-independent marginal laws. Actual positive tuples have
positive auxiliary probability, so their relative entropy is finite.
Direct entropy expansion gives
  kappa_it=J_it+sum_(s!=t) I(X_i;Y_is).
For a FIXED old type i, alpha_i times its sum of slot mutual informations
counts an internal edge twice and every incident cross edge once, so it
is at most2E. Also alpha_i sum_t J_it<=J. Thus
  alpha_i sum_t kappa_it <=J+2(d-1)E
                         <=2d(d-1)/(d-2)*L.
The last comparison uses the exact coefficients of E and J in L; it
does not assume local divergences at different vertices are disjoint.

Let mu_s be the mean one-hot vector for slot s, and Vs=1-||mu_s||^2.
For each omission define
  f_t=||R_X-sum_(s!=t)e_(Y_s)-mu_t||^2.
Under the actual retained law, the inferred omitted vector equals its
true one-hot vector, so E_P f_t=Vt, regardless of root correlations.
Under the auxiliary law the retained vectors are independent of each
other and of the root. Conditional variance plus squared bias gives
  E_Q f_t=sum_(s!=t)Vs+B_i,
  B_i=E||R_X-sum_s mu_s||^2>=0,
with the SAME B_i for every omission. Consequently, for
Delta_t=E_Q f_t-E_P f_t,
  sum_t Delta_t=(d-2)sum_sVs+d B_i >=(d-2)sum_sVs.
Individual Delta_t need not be nonnegative; the proof only uses their
sum followed by absolute-value upper bounds. No error is introduced by
that distinction.

Every occupied row R_X is a nonnegative vector of mass d, since its
supported stars satisfy the exact integer equation. The retained sum
plus mu_t is another nonnegative vector of mass d under BOTH actual
and auxiliary laws. Hence0<=f_t<=2d^2 even on auxiliary tuples which
are not legal stars. Range-bounded total variation and Pinsker give
  |Delta_t|<=2d^2 sqrt(kappa_it/2).
Summing, applying Cauchy--Schwarz, and substituting the divergence bound
gives exactly
  L>=alpha_i(d-2)^3/[4d^6(d-1)] *(sum_sVs)^2.
All constants and inequality directions were independently checked.

Define delta=sum_i alpha_i(1-max_x rho_i(x)). Some old type j has root
variance Vj=1-||rho_j||^2>=delta. Every old type has positive total
degree. Reversibility supplies a slot of some old type i pointing to j,
or it is an internal slot at i=j. Therefore sum_sVs at that i is at
least delta, without requiring connectedness for this local observation.
The global gap is
  Phi<=-alpha_min(d-2)^3 delta^2/[4d^6(d-1)]
      <=-alpha_min delta^2/(8D^6).
For integer d>=3,(d-2)^3/(d-1)>=1/2, giving the second bound. The
argument also works for k=1, where all d slots are internal. It requires
neither distinct candidate rows, injectivity nor invertibility of R.

At equality delta=0, all old root laws are point masses. Matching
marginals then force every neighbor label to its old-type point, and
the integer equation is QT=TR. Conversely compatible point maps have
deterministic stars and zero entropy. Unused candidate labels impose
no lower-mass condition because only occupied R rows were used.

4. Macroscopic exclusion and finite candidate family
---------------------------------------------------
The quantity n*delta is EXACTLY the minimum number of row changes from
the candidate Y to any old-block point map JT: choose a most frequent
label in each old class separately. T may merge classes or leave labels
unused. No surjectivity restriction is imposed at this stage.

The gap and exact count give expected number of candidates at distance
at least eta*n from every point map at most
  polynomial(n)*exp[-alpha_min*eta^2*n/(8D^6)].
The count exponent is n*Phi, not Phi alone. There are finitely many
integer quotient candidates with r<=K and row sums<=D. They include the
quotient of EVERY equitable partition into at most K nonempty classes
in a degree-d graph. Markov and that finite union exclude all such
macroscopic alternatives simultaneously with exponentially high
probability, after retaining the harmless fixed polynomial prefactor.

Candidate quotients can be disconnected; the proof only uses the old
frame for its uniform old-class proportions. Tiny candidate classes and
near-point maps omitting a label are explicitly covered. Neither a
candidate class-size bound nor compactness is needed.

5. Arbitrary-support estimate reconstructed
------------------------------------------
For a bad set S of size s with outside neighbors W of size w, let a,b
count its internal and boundary edges, e=a+b. Loops count once in a,
twice in the stub degree equation. Then
  2a+b=d*s, b>=2w, e<=D*s,
so e-s-w>=((d-2)/2)s. This is an integer and is at least its ceiling.
No balanced composition of S across old classes is assumed.

Every active typed pool has size at least rho*n, rho=1/[K D^(K-1)].
For s<=rho*n/(4D), prescribing e stub pairs has probability at most
(2/(rho*n))^e: each internal or cross denominator is at least rho*n/2,
and the factors are independent. Incompatible typed prescriptions have
probability zero and can be included in an upper bound.

The witness count
  binom(n,s)binom(n,w)*2^(D*w)*(2D*s)^e
overcounts all choices of S,W, selected W stubs and pairings. It even
includes invalid type pairings and W-W edges, which is harmless. Its
algebraic rearrangement has factor
  (s/n)^(e-s-w)*(s/w)^w,
and(s/w)^w<=exp(s/euler). Bounds w<=D*s/2,e<=D*s and the polynomial
number of e,w pairs give the explicit C^s(s/n)^ceil(c*s), c=(d-2)/2.
The chosen eta makes u_(s+1)/u_s<=C exp(c)eta^c<=1/2 for s>=2.
Hence the sum over s>=2 is at most2C^2(2/n)^(d-2).

Internal degree-zero factors contribute simplicity probability one;
the corrected simplicity exponent omits them. Degree-one internal and
cross perfect matchings are always simple. Conditioning produces exactly
independent uniform simple factors by their constant stub multiplicities.
A singleton cannot be bad in a simple positive-degree graph, so the
small-bad-set failure probability is O(n^(-(d-2))) after conditioning.

On the complement of this single graph event, no nonzero matrix V over
ANY field can satisfy AV=VR with at most eta*n nonzero rows, for any R
or dimension. An outside vertex with exactly one incident edge into the
support would produce one nonzero row in AV and zero in VR. This avoids
any uncountable union over real coefficients or any R-dependent event.

6. Exact near-point closure, k=1 and uniformity
--------------------------------------------
Changed rows S and all their neighbors occupy at most(D+1)|S| vertices.
Choosing eta<=rho/[2(D+1)] supplies in EVERY old class a vertex whose
own row and all neighbor rows are unchanged. Evaluating verified integer
AY=YR at one such vertex in old class i gives(QT)_i=(TR)_i exactly.
It follows that V=Y-JT satisfies AV=VR with row support S. The support
event forces V=0. This includes non-onto point maps and tiny new labels.

Intersecting macro and support events proves the complete structural
claim, rather than merely classifying zero-entropy limiting laws. The
same argument covers k=1, a uniform simple d-regular graph. No positive
cross degree is needed there because d>=3 supplies the support bound
and the all-slot variance calculation supplies the gap.

Connectedness plus integer balance gives alpha_min>=rho by following
paths of length at most K-1; it is immediate for k=1. Every consistent
connected fixed frame determines its positive proportions. There are
only finitely many such frames under supplied fixed K,D. Hence entropy
gap, support constants, simplicity constants and eventual thresholds can
all be uniform over this family. The algorithm never needs to find
these proof constants or to know Q or the class sizes beforehand.

7. Binary enumeration, memory, expected work and cap
---------------------------------------------------
Vectorization gives the binary operator I_r tensor A-R^T tensor I_n
with nr<=Kn variables. Its kernel has exactly2^h_R vectors. Standard
basis one-hot rows remain distinct over F_2^r; this is not the scalar
sign encoding which collapses the two signs. Every actual integer
equitable solution belongs to the corresponding binary kernel.

There are at most sum_(r=1)^K(D+1)^(r^2) quotient candidates. Dense
binary elimination uses O_K(n^3) bit operations and O_K(n^2) bits.
One kernel basis uses O_K(n^2) bits. Enumerating one basis combination,
checking one-hot rows/nonempty classes, and verifying INTEGER AY=YR
fits O_(K,D)(n^2) bit work per vector. Candidate entries and degree
counters have bounded bit length; the full exponential list need not
be stored. One current vector and one best partition suffice.

The unconditional simple-model moment includes rare graphs with large
nullity. Thus, BEFORE conditioning on successful recovery,
  E work<=C(K,D)[n^3+n^2(n+1)^B]<=C'(K,D)n^(B+3),
  B=K*2^(KD)+K+[K(K+3)/2]*2^(2K).
The displayed exponent is deliberately loose and absorbs routine
enumeration bookkeeping. It is not a practical complexity claim.

A deterministic n^(B+5) bit-work budget interrupts with probability
O_(K,D)(n^-2) by Markov. Bounded-size work chunks and counters of O(log n)
bits implement the cap within the loose bounds above. Space remains
O_K(n^2). CAP SEMANTICS: return an explicit FAILURE/ABSTENTION flag on
interruption; never certify the partial maximum from unfinished search.

On completing enumeration and on the structural event, every accepted
Y=JT with nonempty candidate classes makes T onto, so r<=k. The actual
planted(J,Q) candidate is always present with r=k. Thus maximum r=k,
and every maximizing T is a permutation. This recovers the partition
up to names, k, and Q by exact neighbor counts. Intersecting structural
success with noninterruption proves the claimed high-probability recovery
for the worst-case polynomial truncated algorithm.

8. Random-lift interpretation and boundaries
-------------------------------------------
For a fixed connected SIMPLE d-regular base H, Q=A_H, ai=0 and all
positive dij=dji=1. Equal fiber sizes and one independent uniform perfect
matching per base edge are EXACTLY the random N-lift model up to vertex
names. Every such graph is simple automatically because H has neither
loops nor parallel edges. Every N, odd or even, is admissible. Therefore
the theorem recovers its fiber partition and unknown bounded base frame
without an old/new adjacency-eigenvalue gap.

Distinct coarse equitable partitions of H do not contradict this result:
they lift to coarsenings of the recovered fiber partition. The proof does
not assert absence of exact old/new eigenvalue collisions or simple real
eigenspaces. It makes no claim about arbitrary deterministic lifts,
growing base order, degree-two covers, noise/edits, or numerical spectral
stability. A transitive deck group is not assumed; the covering-theory
meaning of a 'regular cover' is not silently substituted for a regular
base graph.
