1. Claims actually reviewed

For 0<alpha<1 and a connected host Laplacian L, the metric is

 C_alpha=(L^dagger)^(alpha/2)(L'^alpha-L^alpha)(L^dagger)^(alpha/2).

The shared constant kernel contributes zero. On its orthogonal
complement, I+C_alpha is the relative matrix for the pair
(L'^alpha,L^alpha). This is a base-energy relative norm, not the
absolute norm of L'^alpha-L^alpha or relative error divided by its norm.

The following are different graph models and must remain distinguished.

A. Unrestricted degree: BOTH endpoints are simple unweighted connected
   bipartite graphs; one previously absent edge is INSERTED, and its
   host effective resistance is exactly one at every finite stage.
   Every finite q_i>=1 with product 2^alpha is in the padded relative
   spectral closure. Extra correction eigenvalues tend to zero.
   Consequently the exact strict-count supremum is
     max(0,ceil[alpha log 2/log(1+epsilon)]-1).

B. Degree two/three comparison: the HOST is simple, connected,
   unweighted and has unit vertex masses; ONE EXISTING UNIT EDGE IS
   DOUBLED. The updated graph has this one conductance equal to two.
   Its combinatorial maximum degree remains that of the host.
   Worst strict count is Theta_alpha(log^2(1/epsilon)) at degree two,
   and Theta_alpha(1/epsilon) at degree three. This statement does not
   say both endpoints are unweighted, and it is not an insertion claim.

Verdict on the reviewed mathematical mechanisms: PASS, with the exact
scope above. No algorithmic graph-size, runtime or finite-precision
claim follows. The degree-three leading constant has not been optimized.

3. Independent checks of the unrestricted-degree transfer

The complete-bipartite cells and fixed rational resistor gadgets are
simple, unweighted and bipartite. All external edges of a cell use
one port. The distinguished connector is three internally disjoint
length-three paths, with no existing direct edge between the terminals.
The insertion adds that direct unit edge. Each dangling part attaches
to a single connector terminal, hence carries no current under the
terminal-to-terminal resistance test. Host resistance is exactly one.

With A_s=sL_s and normalized cell-indicator isometry J_s, eliminating
the cell-mean-zero block and then the fixed gadget block yields
 J_s^T(A_s+t)^-1 J_s -> (H+t)^-1
for every t>0. The stated block orders are consistent: FF>=cs^2,
PF=O(s^1/2), ZF=O(s), and PP is uniformly bounded.

The power transfer is valid even though full high eigenvalues diverge.
Compression of inverses gives
 0<=J_s^T A_s(A_s+t)^-1 J_s<=min(1,M/t) I.
Multiplying by t^(alpha-1) supplies one integrable dominator. Thus
 J_s^T A_s^alpha J_s -> H^alpha.
There is no invalid replacement of a fractional power by a Schur
complement's power. The proof applies fractional calculus first.

The chosen cell-constant test vectors are exactly orthogonal to the
physical constant vector even in the presence of gadget vertices.
The generalized Ritz relative eigenvalues therefore converge to all
macro relative eigenvalues. Their log sum exhausts the exact physical
budget alpha log 2. Positivity makes every remaining log contribution
nonnegative; hence no additional relative eigenvalue can stay away
from one. This last step justifies full padded spectral convergence,
not merely convergence of selected compressed eigenvalues.

4. Degree-two path/cycle check and the classical sharp asymptotic

For a unit path on n vertices, the leverage measure of edge p is
 z_k=(2/n)sin^2(k pi p/n), lambda_k=4sin^2(k pi/(2n)).
Consequently nu([0,delta])<=sqrt(delta), uniformly in n and p.
The positive relative derivative majorizes C_alpha and has diagonal
alpha z_k. Removing the low block at delta proportional to epsilon^2
by PSD pinching is valid. Rational approximation on the remaining
block gives the uniform log^2 count. Its displayed upper coefficient
is explicitly nonsharp. The rational rank identity is classical.

For unit cycles, lambda_k=4sin^2(pi k/n), z_k=1/n, so the analogous
bound is nu([0,delta])<=sqrt(delta)/2. This covers the entire connected
simple maximum-degree-two class, with the trivial singleton excluded
when there is no edge to double.

For centered paths of size 2N, the active odd modes have z_k=1/N.
The leverage measure tends to the arcsine measure on (0,4). The
exact response is between one half and one times the relative
derivative. In logarithmic coordinates the derivative has kernel
 sqrt(r(x)r(y)) sinh(alpha(x-y)/2)/sinh((x-y)/2),
with r(x) asymptotic to a positive multiple of exp(x/2) at -infinity.
The Fourier symbol comparison with alpha=1/2 is valid for fixed
alpha. The change u=exp(x/2) gives the classical weighted Cauchy
kernel 2sqrt(uv)/(u+v) on (0,1). This verifies the use of Laptev's
theorem and the constant 1/(2pi^2) for the centered limiting operator.
The bounded step kernels yield Hilbert--Schmidt convergence, and
strict eigenvalue counts transfer to sufficiently long finite paths.
This does not identify the optimal leading coefficient uniformly
over every finite length and edge location.

PRIMARY CLASSICAL INPUT: A. A. Laptev, Spectral asymptotic behavior
of a class of integral operators, Math. Notes 16 (1974), 1038--1043,
Theorem (3), p.1039. For x^beta y^gamma/(x+y)^a on L^2(0,1),
N(epsilon)~log^2(1/epsilon)/(2pi^2 rho), rho=beta+gamma-a+1>0,
with a>0 and beta,gamma>-1/2. The theorem applies to beta=gamma=1/2,
a=1. It explicitly credits Widom's 1966 Theorem (3.3) for a=1 and
beta=gamma. Root-exponential weighted-Cauchy decay is classical.
Full primary PDF was read as extracted text (six pages); the theorem,
conditions and coefficient were independently checked in a rendered
image of p.1039. This is not a reconstruction of every detail of its
proof from the poorly recognized mathematical OCR.
 https://www.ma.ic.ac.uk/~alaptev/Papers/MatZam74.pdf
 https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01149794

H. Stahl, Best uniform rational approximation of x^alpha on [0,1],

5. Degree-three reservoir check

The graph proof correctly uses a uniformly gapped port reservoir,
not a perfectly conducting finite graph. The exact static contact
resistance is r=e_p^T L_H^dagger e_p to the reservoir MEAN; this
differs from pinning an individual vertex. Series neck resistance
ell+r and anchor resistance 1+r (or 1/2+r) follow from constrained
mean-zero energy minimization.

The binary tree's new grounded leaf admittance increments satisfy
g_j-g_(j-1)>=1/(4Km). The retained-terminal determinant ratio uses
f(g_(i-1))/f(g_m), so adjacent reverse-pivot ratios are
f(g_(i-1))/f(g_i). The ordering, direction and constants check.
The mass-separation lemma is proved without assuming a special tree
matrix: elementary symmetric polynomials isolate the trailing minors,
resolvent responses identify leverage weights, and an integrable
fractional identity plus off-diagonal scale separation gives the
relative diagonal limit. Uniformity on compact contact-parameter sets
follows from its convergent-family version.

For ordinary unit-mass reservoirs, the fixed complementary block
is uniformly coercive; its resolvent correction on the low block is
O(lambda/s). Low subspaces approach the indicator space at angle
O(s^-1/2). The high fractional part contributes O(s^-1) after
compression, and multiplication by s^alpha makes this vanish since
alpha<1. The baseline inverse powers are handled on the shared
positive support, with the constant kernel retained separately.
The sequential choices (mass separation, anchor mass, reservoir size)
are legitimate; they are not efficient-size bounds.

Reservoir existence is a classical consequence of Friedman/Bordenave:
every sufficiently large even size admits simple cubic graphs with
both nontrivial adjacency extremes below 3 in absolute value. The
actual Bordenave TeX source (2040 lines) and bibliography (183 lines)
were read earlier in this agent's signing audit; that coverage is
logged in signing-frontier-2026-11-15.txt. Here the needed source
input is specifically Theorem 1 and its uniform simple-graph model.
 https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04482

6. A proved bipartite strengthening of the degree-three construction

The degree-three lower construction can also be required to have a
BIPARTITE host (and hence bipartite updated weighted graph). This is
a change in the reservoir choice only; no finite-pair universality
or exact leading-coefficient claim is implied.

Proof. Choose simple cubic graphs F of every sufficiently large even
size with all adjacency eigenvalues other than 3 bounded in absolute
value by r<3. Their bipartite double covers have spectrum consisting
of both signs of the original spectrum. The only adjacency value 3
is simple, so the covers are connected, and their Laplacian gap is
at least gamma_0=3-r. They are simple cubic bipartite graphs of sizes
divisible by four.

Replace one edge a--b by the path a--p--q--b, adding TWO vertices,
and use p as the port. This preserves simplicity and bipartiteness.
For the new graph energy Q and the mean mu on the old vertices,
the old energy is at most 3Q, and
 sum_old (x_v-mu)^2 <= 3Q/gamma_0.
Each new vertex is adjacent to one old endpoint, so its squared
deviation from mu is at most (2+6/gamma_0)Q. Total variance about
the actual mean is consequently at most (4+15/gamma_0)Q.
Thus the subdivided reservoir has uniform gap
 gamma >= 1/(4+15/gamma_0).
Its port has internal degree two and can accept its one external
edge without exceeding degree three. Sizes 4k+2 approximate every
fixed positive target mass ratio with O(1) rounding error.

The external tree and necks join separate reservoirs through bridges.
Flip the bipartition independently in each reservoir to match the
tree attachment. All cycles then remain inside bipartite reservoirs.
Every subsequent static, mass-separation and relative fractional
argument in the audited degree-three proof applies with the modified
gap constant. Therefore its Theta_alpha(1/epsilon) lower holds already
for bipartite unweighted hosts. QED.

7. Mandatory outlier: independent review of the new exact inequality

For A>0, B=A+uu^T, eta=||u||^2 and a=u^TAu/eta, the reviewed lemma is
 lambda_max(C_alpha)>=(1+eta/a)^alpha-1.
Its proof is correct. Scalar Jensen gives
 z(t)=u^T(A+t)^-1u>=eta/(a+t).
Since z^2/(1+z) is increasing, the rank-one positive fractional integral
gives u^T(B^alpha-A^alpha)u>=eta[(a+eta)^alpha-a^alpha].
Scalar concavity gives u^TA^alpha u<=eta a^alpha. Testing x=u in
the generalized Rayleigh quotient gives the assertion. Equality when
u is an A eigenvector confirms sharpness as a general matrix lemma.

The residual logarithmic budget argument is also correct uniformly
over graph families, including nonbridge doublings with varying tau.
One always has S<=alpha log2 for an existing unit-edge doubling and
a common degree-only lower bound on the largest correction eigenvalue.
Thus taking a supremum before the epsilon->0 limsup is legitimate.

For host maximum degree Delta, the resulting leading-count upper
coefficients are
 R_eff=1 insertion: alpha log[2Delta/(Delta+2)],
 any existing-edge doubling: alpha log[2(Delta+1)/(Delta+3)].
These are upper bounds, not claimed sharp graph constants. At Delta=3
they are alpha log(6/5) and alpha log(4/3), respectively. In particular
the degree-three lower's order does not imply the unrestricted-degree
coefficient alpha log2. Fixed degree precludes full determinant-
admissible closure because a nonvanishing outlier is compulsory.
