1. The imported mixing theorem is exactly strong enough
---------------------------------------------------------
The primary undirected model is the sum of d1 independent uniform perfect
matchings on EVEN N. Theorem 1.6 states that the SUM over all vectors of
the maximum point-probability discrepancy tends to zero. There is NO
normalizing factor p^-N and no hidden multiplication by the support size.
The proof here correctly uses that unscaled aggregate error.

For the additive group V=F_p and p odd, V tensor_Z V is cyclic of order p.
Its subgroup I2 generated by a tensor b+b tensor a contains twice a
generator, hence is the entire tensor group. Thus the symmetric-tensor
restriction is genuinely vacuous. A nonconstant vector has minimal affine
coset all of F_p, since a nonzero coordinate difference generates its
additive group. Its reference distribution is therefore uniform on the
single hyperplane sum r=d1 sum x. A constant a*1 has deterministic
reference d1*a*1, exactly its true image. No assumption p not dividing d1
is needed. Extending the probabilities by zero outside their shared support
is legitimate and preserves the point-error bound.

2. Single-block affine estimates
----------------------------------
Let Delta=d1-d2 and choose fixed odd p with p not dividing d2.
Constants solve (C-Delta I)x=0 only for x=0, because their residual is
d2*a*1. A nonconstant vector can contribute under the reference only if
sum x=0, in which case its probability is p^(-N+1). There are at most
p^(N-1) such vectors. Hence the reference expected kernel size is <=2,
and the actual expectation is <=2+epsilon_N.

For every fixed realization C and every right side b, its solution set
for (C-Delta I)x=b is either empty or a coset of the same kernel.
Taking expectations gives the UNIFORM affine bound <=2+epsilon_N.
Replacing its probabilities by the reference costs at most epsilon_N
again, giving <=2+2epsilon_N. The latter loose bound is enough; no
uncounted p^N factor is introduced. These statements hold also along
subsequences p divides N; excluding constant vectors from the sum-zero
hyperplane only lowers its size.

3. Two independent internal blocks: the summed-error products
--------------------------------------------------------------
Condition on any deterministic cross matrix B with row and column sums d2.
The two internal matching sums are independent, so the expected number of
kernel vectors is the product-probability sum displayed in the proof.
When replacing its first factor, fix x and bound its pointwise discrepancy
by e_x. The sum over y of the remaining second factor is an affine
solution expectation <=2+epsilon_N, independent of the possibly complicated
right side -B^T x. Summing e_x gives error at most
(2+epsilon_N)epsilon_N. After that replacement, replacing the second
factor costs at most (2+2epsilon_N)epsilon_N by the reference affine
bound. Total error <=4epsilon_N+3epsilon_N^2 is correct uniformly in B.
Neither total variation nor entrywise errors summed over p^(2N) targets
are substituted for the actual theorem's aggregate error.

For two nonconstant vectors, the two sum constraints are the SAME:
 sum x+sum y=0,
since p does not divide d2. There are at most p^(2N-1) such ordered
pairs, each with probability p^(-2N+2), giving contribution <=p.
For constants x=a*1,y=b*1, both deterministic equations reduce to a+b=0,
giving exactly p. If exactly one vector is constant, its deterministic
block equation forces By=-d2*a*1 (or the transposed equation). At most
p^(N-rank_p B) solutions for each a, times p possible a and at most
p^(-N+1) for the other nonconstant reference probability, give
p^(2-rank_p B) in each orientation. The stated total upper bound follows.
Indeed the nonconstant/nonconstant contribution is p-o(1) at fixed p,
so with linear rank B the expectation actually tends to 2p; the proof
only needs its upper bound.

4. Cross rank, including independence and uniformity
------------------------------------------------------
For a simple d2-regular bipartite biadjacency B, selecting a row excludes
at most 1+d2(d2-1) rows sharing a support coordinate. Greedy selection
therefore supplies at least N/[1+d2(d2-1)] disjoint nonzero row supports.
Their row vectors are linearly independent over EVERY field, since their
supports are disjoint and entries are one. Thus the linear rank lower
bound is correct; choosing p>d2 is sufficient for the needed prime
restriction but not itself needed for this rank argument.
The mixed reference contribution tends to zero uniformly over all such B.

The proof is valid conditional on every deterministic simple regular B.
Sampling B by ANY law independent of the two internal blocks preserves
all matching-model probability bounds. It does not authorize B to be
chosen after inspecting the internal graphs.

5. Lifting the real-rank event and the order of limits
-------------------------------------------------------
The planted vector (1,-1) is always in the integer kernel of A-Delta I.
If the real kernel dimension is at least two, all integer minors of order
2N-1 vanish. Reducing them modulo p proves that the F_p kernel dimension
is at least two, with at least p^2 vectors. Markov therefore yields the
claimed limsup probability <=2/p, uniformly in simple regular B, for each
fixed admissible p. A slightly stronger bound follows by subtracting the
always-present p planted vectors, but is unnecessary.

First take N->infinity along even N with p fixed. Only THEN take p through
arbitrarily large odd primes not dividing d2. This proves real nullity one
with high probability and does not request any mixing estimate uniform in
a growing prime. No outlier inequality enters the argument. For symmetric
real A, geometric multiplicity equals algebraic multiplicity, so this is
indeed simplicity of the planted eigenvalue d1-d2.

6. Conditioning on simplicity and the precise contiguity transfer
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The matching union conditioned to be simple is generally NOT uniform over
simple regular graphs: its probability is proportional to the number of
ordered 1-factorizations. This distinction is correctly acknowledged.
MRRW's actual Theorem 3 asserts MUTUAL contiguity of this distribution
and the uniform simple r-regular distribution on an even number of vertices,
for every fixed r>=3. In particular it has the direction required here.

The conditioning event has probability bounded away from zero at fixed r.
One direct check is to count coinciding edges over unordered pairs of
matching colors. Its factorial moments converge to those of a Poisson
variable with mean r(r-1)/4: vertex-disjoint collision events provide the
leading contribution, and any overlapping pattern either is impossible
within a matching or loses a power of N. Thus the probability of no
coincidences tends to exp[-r(r-1)/4]>0. No matching has loops, and no
coincidences is exactly simplicity. Independent internal blocks give the
product of two such positive limiting probabilities. Hence conditioning
both internal graphs preserves the already-proved o(1) bad-event bound,
uniformly in B.

For completeness, ordinary one-factor contiguity DOES transfer this joint
event, but a product argument must be supplied rather than an assertion
of total-variation equivalence. Let Q_N be uniform simple and P_N the
conditioned matching law, so Q_N is contiguous with respect to P_N.
If f_N is any measurable function in [0,1] and E_P f_N->0, then
E_Q f_N->0: apply Markov to P(f_N>sqrt(E_P f_N)), contiguity to that
event, and bound the expectation on its complement. Now if an event E_N
has probability o(1) under P_N tensor P_N, apply this bounded-function
principle first to f(x)=P_y(E_x), then to g(y)=Q_x(E_y). It follows that
its probability is o(1) under Q_N tensor Q_N.

Uniformity over deterministic B is retained: if the supremum bad
probability under Q tensor Q failed to tend to zero, choose a deterministic
worst (or near-worst) B_N for each N. The conditioned matching probability
is uniformly o(1), and the preceding product-contiguity argument applies
to that very event sequence, contradiction. Finally averaging over any
independent cross-graph law gives the desired uniform-simple RSBM.
Equivalently one can tensor with that independent law and apply the same
bounded-function argument. This verifies the joint transfer without assuming
contiguity has an unjustified quantitative rate.

Importantly, contiguity is applied AFTER the matching-model bad probability
has been shown to tend to zero by the fixed-p then p->infinity argument.
It does not transfer the numerical finite-p bound 2/p to the uniform-simple
model. The source correctly promises no such quantitative rate.

7. Scope and what has actually been proved
-------------------------------------------
For every fixed d1>=3,d2>0, on even admissible block sizes, the planted
adjacency eigenvalue d1-d2 is simple with high probability in the uniform
simple regular two-block model, independently of whether it is separated
from the bulk. The stated assortative specialization d1>d2 is included.
The algebra itself does not require that inequality. The conclusion is
uniform over independently supplied simple d2-biregular cross matrices.
A randomly chosen planted partition follows by relabeling invariance.

For odd d1, even block size is already required by admissibility. For even
d1>=4, admissible ODD block sizes are not covered by this matching theorem.
The internal degree d1=2 (in particular (2,1)) is not covered. Nothing in
this audit replaces those missing cases with a claim from another model.
The result supplies no eigenvalue-gap rate, finite-precision exact-recovery
algorithm, or proof of historical novelty. It also does not permit a cross
matrix chosen adaptively after the internal graphs.

No unresolved mathematical defect was found at this precise scope.
