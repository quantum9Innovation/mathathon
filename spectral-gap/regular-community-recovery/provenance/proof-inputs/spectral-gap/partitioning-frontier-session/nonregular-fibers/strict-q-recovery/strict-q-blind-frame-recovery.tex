\begin{abstract}
We prove recovery of an unknown bounded reversible degree frame,
its type count and its planted partition when the frame admits positive
symmetric omission weights strictly below one half. Total degrees may
differ and internal degrees may be zero or one. Every equitable partition
with at most the supplied bounded number of classes is, with high
probability, a compatible coarsening of the planted partition. A binary
intertwiner enumeration has unconditional expected polynomial bit work;
a deterministic polynomial cap gives worst-case polynomial work with
explicit abstention. The proof includes a uniform polynomial moment,
an explicit real one-hot entropy gap, a mixed-degree small-support
argument and the full algorithmic composition. No spectral separation,
candidate-class mass bound or knowledge of the omission certificate is
required. Historical novelty and practical efficiency are not claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model, strict hypothesis and complete conclusion}
Fix supplied constants $K\ge1$ and $D\ge3$. An unknown fixed frame
$Q=(d_{ij})_{i,j\in[k]}$ has $1\le k\le K$, nonnegative integer entries,
and row sums $d_i=\sum_jd_{ij}\le D$. Its off-diagonal support is
connected, allowing the one-type case. Positive class sizes $n_i$ satisfy
\[
 n_id_{ij}=n_jd_{ji},\qquad n_id_{ii}\text{ even},
 \qquad n=\sum_i n_i,\qquad \alpha_i=n_i/n.
\]
Along a frame's admissible sequence, connectedness fixes these positive
proportions. Independently sample uniform simple internal $d_{ii}$-regular
graphs and uniform simple $(d_{ij},d_{ji})$-biregular cross factors.
Omit degree-zero factors. Let $A$ be the resulting adjacency matrix and
$J$ the $n$-by-$k$ planted indicator matrix. Then $AJ=JQ$.

\paragraph{Strict symmetric omission certificate.}
On every active type factor assume there are real weights
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 q_{ij}=q_{ji},\qquad 0<q_{ij}<\tfrac12,
 \qquad \sum_jd_{ij}q_{ij}=1\quad(i\in[k]).
\end{equation}
For an internal factor this means $0<q_{ii}<1/2$ per physical slot.
The certificate is a hypothesis on the true frame, not an algorithmic
input. Since each of the $d_i$ slot weights is below $1/2$ and they sum
to one, \eqref{eq:q} implies $d_i\ge3$. Set
\[
 d_{\min}=\min_i d_i,\quad
 q_* =\min_{\text{active }ij}q_{ij}>0,\quad
 c_* =\min_{\text{active }ij}(1-2q_{ij})>0,\quad
 C_\kappa=\max\{q_*^{-1},\ 2(D-1)c_*^{-1}\}.
\]

An equitable partition with $r$ nonempty classes has one-hot indicator
$Y\in\{0,1\}^{n\times r}$ and an integer quotient $R\ge0$ satisfying
$AY=YR$. Its quotient rows have sums at most $D$. A compatible
coarsening of the planted partition has $Y=JT$ for a one-hot map
$T\in\{0,1\}^{k\times r}$ and satisfies $QT=TR$.

\begin{theorem}[Full strict-weight recovery]\label{thm:main}
Under these hypotheses, along every admissible size sequence, with
probability tending to one, every equitable partition into at most $K$
nonempty classes is a compatible coarsening of the planted partition.
Consequently the planted partition is the unique finest partition in
this bounded class family, up to class names.

Given only $A,K,D$, the exact algorithm in Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}
recovers the planted partition, $k$ and $Q$, up to class permutation,
with high probability. Its untruncated expected bit work is polynomial,
including rare graphs. It has $O_K(n^2)$ bit space. A deterministic
polynomial work cap with explicit failure output gives worst-case
polynomial work and high-probability recovery.
Neither $Q$, the sizes, the certificate, nor an old/new eigenvalue gap
is supplied to the algorithm.
\end{theorem}

The bounded-family qualification is essential: the $n$-singleton
partition always exists. All results below concern fixed $K,D,Q$;
uniform constants over the finite family of qualifying connected frames
are explained at the end. No boundary weight $q_{ij}=0$ or $1/2$ is
included in this theorem.

\section{Exact type count and unconditional modular moment}
Initially use the typed configuration model, with labelled physical
stubs. A loop contributes two to degree and adjacency. Fix $r\le K$
and any matrix $R\in\F^{r\times r}$. Labels lie in the vector
alphabet $\mathcal A=\F^r$, of cardinality $a=2^r$. A legal ordered
star at old type $i$ is $(x,z_1,\ldots,z_{d_i})$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:legal}
 \sum_{s=1}^{d_i}z_s=xR.
\end{equation}
The slots retain their prescribed old neighbor types. Root plus any
$d_i-1$ neighbor labels determine the omitted label uniquely, so there
are exactly $a^{d_i}$ legal stars.

Let $T_i(s)$ be legal-star counts at old type $i$. Let $m_{ij}(x,y)$
count pooled stubs from root label $x$ to requested neighbor label $y$
in factor $ij$. Compatible tables reverse across a cross factor;
internal tables are symmetric with even diagonal. Write
$M_{ii}=n_id_{ii}$ and $M_{ij}=n_id_{ij}$ for $i<j$, and
$P(u)=(u-1)!!$ for even $u\ge0$, with $P(0)=1$.
The exact contribution of a compatible type is
\begin{align}\label{eq:count}
 W(T)={}&\prod_i\frac{n_i!}{\prod_sT_i(s)!}
 \prod_{i:d_{ii}>0}
 \frac{\prod_{x<y}m_{ii}(x,y)!\prod_xP(m_{ii}(x,x))}{P(M_{ii})}
 \nonumber\\
 &\hspace{12mm}\times\prod_{i<j:M_{ij}>0}
        \frac{\prod_{x,y}m_{ij}(x,y)!}{M_{ij}!}.
\end{align}
The vertex multinomial already chooses root labels. After those stars
are assigned to labelled vertices, each internal category is paired to
its reversal and each cross category bijected to its reversal. The
denominators count all uniform configurations. Conversely a configuration
and a root labeling determine every ordered star, including at loops.
This proves \eqref{eq:count} without an extra root multinomial.

Write $P_i=T_i/n_i$ for a star law, $\rho_i$ for its root marginal,
and $\nu_{ij}=m_{ij}/M_{ij}$ for pooled pair laws. These have marginals
$\rho_i,\rho_j$. Put
\[
 w_{ii}=\alpha_id_{ii}/2,\qquad
 w_{ij}=\alpha_id_{ij}\ (i<j),\qquad
 \Phi(P)=\sum_i\alpha_i\HH(P_i)-\sum_{i\le j}w_{ij}\HH(\nu_{ij}).
\]
All entropies use natural logarithms. Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constants}
 a_*=2^K,\qquad F=K+\frac{K(K+3)}2a_*^2,
 \qquad B=Ka_*^D+F.
\end{equation}
Uniform factorial bounds give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factorial}
 W(T)\le\exp\{n\Phi(P)+F[\log(Dn+1)+1]\}.
\end{equation}
Here is an explicit remainder check. For integer $u\ge0$, with
$f(u)=u\log u-u$ and $f(0)=0$,
\[
 0\le\log(u!)-f(u)\le\log(u+1)+1.
\]
Also $|\log P(u)-f(u)/2|\le2[\log(u+1)+1]$.
A root multinomial has upper error at most one such unit; a cross
factor at most $a^2$ units; an internal factor at most $2a^2$ units.
There are at most $K$ roots, $K$ internal and $K(K-1)/2$ cross factors,
giving precisely the conservative $F$ in \eqref{eq:constants}.
Zero entries and absent factors cause no loss of uniformity.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted entropy identity]\label{lem:entropy}
Symmetrize $P_i$ only within slots having the same old neighbor type,
writing $\overline P_i$. For a physical slot $t$, let
\[
 J_{it}=\sum_{s\ne t}\HH(Z_{is}\mid X_i)
        -\HH((Z_{is})_{s\ne t}\mid X_i),
 \qquad I_{ij}=\II(\nu_{ij}).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy}
 \Phi(P)\le\Phi(\overline P)
 =-\sum_{i\le j}w_{ij}(1-2q_{ij})I_{ij}
  -\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_tq_{it}J_{it}\le0,
\end{equation}
where a slot toward old type $j$ has $q_{it}=q_{ij}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Within-type permutations preserve legal support and pooled pair laws;
entropy concavity increases star entropy. Lossless omission of slot $t$
and averaging with its weight, whose sum over slots is one, give
\[
 \HH(\overline P_i)=\HH(\rho_i)
   +\sum_s(1-q_{is})\HH(Z_{is}\mid X_i)-\sum_tq_{it}J_{it}.
\]
Replace conditional entropy by pair entropy minus root entropy and then
pair entropy by $\HH(\rho_i)+\HH(\rho_j)-I_{ij}$.
Reversibility gives the edge coefficient $1-q_{ij}-q_{ji}$,
including $1-2q_{ii}$ internally. The root coefficient is
\[
 \alpha_i\left(1-\sum_jd_{ij}q_{ji}\right)=0
\]
by symmetry and \eqref{eq:q}. This proves \eqref{eq:entropy}.
The divisions and omission weights operate on real entropies, not on
field elements; characteristic two is harmless.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial moment]\label{lem:moment}
For every $R\in\F^{r\times r}$ and every admissible configuration size,
\[
 \E_{\rm config}|\{Y:AY=YR\}|
 \le(3D)^F(n+1)^B.
\]
In the intended independent uniform simple-factor model, eventually
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
 \E_{\rm simple}2^{h_R}\le C(K,D)(n+1)^B,
\end{equation}
where $h_R$ is the binary intertwiner nullity. The bound is unconditional
on structural recovery and uniform over all candidate $R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At most $(n+1)^{Ka_*^D}$ star-count types occur. Apply
\eqref{eq:factorial}--\eqref{eq:entropy} and
$Dn+1\le D(n+1)$, $e<3$.

For fixed degrees, each internal degree $a\ge1$ has limiting simplicity
probability $\exp[-(a^2-1)/4]$; degree zero is absent. A cross $(u,v)$
factor has limit $\exp[-(u-1)(v-1)/2]$. These formulae also give
probability one for internal matchings and cross factors with a degree
one side. To recall a direct justification, loops and pairs of parallel
edges have the displayed Poisson means. Every fixed joint factorial
moment is dominated by disjoint bad patterns; consistent overlaps have
union edge excess at least one and contribute $O(1/\min_i n_i)$.
Finite Bonferroni bounds followed by growing truncation give the
zero-pattern probability. There are finitely many independent factors,
with total mean at most $K^2D^2$, so their joint simplicity probability
is eventually at least $e^{-K^2D^2}/2$.
Conditioning costs its reciprocal. Every simple factor has a constant
number of labelled-stub realizations, namely $(a!)^{n_i}$ internally
and $(u!)^{n_i}(v!)^{n_j}$ across types. Thus conditioning gives exactly
the intended independent uniform simple factors and proves
\eqref{eq:moment}.
\end{proof}

\section{A quantitative real one-hot gap}
For this section labels are the ordinary unit vectors in $\mathbb R^r$,
and every star obeys the original integer equation
\[
 \sum_s e_{Y_{is}}=R_{X_i,*},
\]
with a fixed nonnegative integer candidate $R$. Define
\[
 \delta(P)=\sum_i\alpha_i(1-\max_a\rho_i(a)).
\]
For empirical candidate indicators this is exactly the minimum normalized
number of changed rows to an old-type point map $JT$. Unused labels and
non-surjective maps are allowed.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit strict-weight gap]\label{lem:gap}
For every compatible legal one-hot collection,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 \Phi(P)\le-\gamma_Q\delta(P)^2,
 \qquad \gamma_Q=\frac{9}{2D^5C_\kappa}>0.
\end{equation}
In particular zero entropy forces every old root law to be a point mass.
No candidate class is assumed to have positive limiting mass.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Symmetrization preserves root laws and increases $\Phi$, so assume it has
been done. Put $L=-\Phi\ge0$,
\[
 E=\sum_{i\le j}w_{ij}I_{ij},\qquad
 J_0=\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_tJ_{it}.
\]
For retained data at $(i,t)$, let $\mathsf Q_{it}$ keep the root law
but draw all retained neighbor labels independently of each other and
the root, using their old-type marginals. Its divergence from the actual
retained law is
\[
 \kappa_{it}=J_{it}+\sum_{s\ne t}I(X_i;Y_{is}).
\]
It is finite, including on boundary distributions, because the product
of marginals is positive on every positive actual tuple. The exact
weighted identity implies
\begin{align*}
 \sum_i\alpha_i\sum_t\kappa_{it}
 &=J_0+\sum_i\alpha_i(d_i-1)\sum_sI(X_i;Y_{is})\\
 &\le J_0+2(D-1)E\le C_\kappa L.
\end{align*}
The equality $\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_s I(X_i;Y_{is})=2E$ includes
the internal half-edge factor.

Write $\mu_{is}=\E e_{Y_{is}}$,
$V_{is}=1-\|\mu_{is}\|_2^2$, $S_i=\sum_sV_{is}$, and
\[
 f_{it}=\left\|R_{X_i,*}-\sum_{s\ne t}e_{Y_{is}}-\mu_{it}\right\|_2^2.
\]
Its actual expectation is $V_{it}$. Under $\mathsf Q_{it}$ it is
$\sum_{s\ne t}V_{is}+B_i$, where
$B_i=\E\|R_{X_i,*}-\sum_s\mu_{is}\|_2^2\ge0$ does not depend on $t$.
Every occupied row $R_{X_i,*}$ and the other vector inside the norm
are nonnegative vectors of total mass $d_i$ under both laws. Hence
$0\le f_{it}\le2D^2$. Range total variation and Pinsker therefore give
\[
 |\E_{\mathsf Q_{it}}f_{it}-\E f_{it}|
       \le\sqrt2D^2\sqrt{\kappa_{it}}.
\]
Sum first over omissions and then types; Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\begin{align*}
 \sum_i\alpha_i(d_i-2)S_i
 &\le\sqrt2D^2\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_t\sqrt{\kappa_{it}}\\
 &\le D^2\sqrt{2D C_\kappa L}.
\end{align*}
On the other hand, reversibility and $d_i\ge3$ give
\[
 \sum_i\alpha_i(d_i-2)S_i
 \ge\sum_i\alpha_iS_i
 =\sum_j\alpha_jd_j(1-\|\rho_j\|_2^2)
 \ge3\delta(P).
\]
Square the resulting inequality to obtain \eqref{eq:gap}.
It remains valid before symmetrization. This quantitative estimate also
settles the compactness boundary: unused labels and arbitrarily small
candidate classes do not weaken its constant.
\end{proof}

For an empirical one-hot type, the exact count \eqref{eq:count} and
factorial bound remain valid. Legal one-hot stars number at most $r^{d_i}$,
by lossless deletion. Padding the alphabet by zero-mass labels to size
$a_*$ preserves entropy and gives the same conservative remainder even
when $r=1$. Thus for fixed $\eta>0$, uniformly over the finite
bounded candidate family,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:macro}
 \E_{\rm simple}\#\{Y:AY=YR,\ \delta(Y)\ge\eta\}
 \le C(K,D)(n+1)^B e^{-\gamma_Q\eta^2 n}.
\end{equation}
This is an integer one-hot count, separate from the binary moment.
If $\delta=0$, matching marginals make every star deterministic and
the exact legal equation is $QT=TR$; conversely compatible point maps
have entropy zero.

\section{Mixed-degree small supports and exact local closure}
Connectedness and balance give
\[
 \alpha_{\min}\ge\rho:=1/[K D^{K-1}].
\]
Indeed a path of at most $K-1$ active factors changes a size ratio by
at most $D^{K-1}$. Every active stub pool therefore has at least $\rho n$
stubs. All constants below are uniform over the bounded frame family.

\begin{lemma}[No small unsupported row set]\label{lem:support}
There is $\eta=\eta(K,D)>0$ such that, in the intended simple model,
\[
 \Prob(\exists\,\varnothing\ne S,\ |S|\le\eta n:
       \text{no outside vertex has exactly one neighbor in }S)
       =O_{K,D}(n^{-(d_{\min}-2)}).
\]
On the complementary event, no nonzero matrix $V$ with at most $\eta n$
nonzero rows can satisfy $AV=VR$, simultaneously over every field,
matrix width and square matrix $R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In a configuration, let $S$ have $s$ vertices and outside neighbor set
$W$ of size $w$. Write $a$ for its internal edges, loops counted once,
$b$ for its edges to $W$, and $e=a+b$. A bad set has
\[
 2a+b=\sum_{v\in S}d_v\ge d_{\min}s,\quad b\ge2w,
 \quad e\le Ds,\quad w\le Ds/2.
\]
Consequently $e-s-w\ge cs$, $c=(d_{\min}-2)/2\ge1/2$.
A witness prescribes $S,W$, all stubs at $S$, selected stubs at $W$,
and the $e$ pairs. If $s\le\rho n/(4D)$, every prescribed valid pair
in an internal pool costs a denominator at least $\rho n/2$, as does
every pair of a cross bijection. Thus its probability is at most
$(2/(\rho n))^e$. For fixed $s,w,e$, at most
\[
 \binom ns\binom nw\,2^{Dw}(2Ds)^e
\]
prescriptions occur. This overcounts invalid types safely. Using
$(s/w)^w\le \exp(s/\mathrm e)$, with value one at $w=0$, and summing the
polynomially many $w,e$ possibilities bounds the expected number of
bad $s$-sets by
\[
 C^s(s/n)^{\lceil cs\rceil},
\]
where one conservative choice is
\[
 C=4(D+1)^2\exp(1+D/2+1/\mathrm e)\,2^{D^2/2}(4D/\rho)^D.
\]
Choose
\[
 \eta\le\min\{\rho/(4D),\ 1/(4\mathrm e C^2),\ \rho/[2(D+1)]\}.
\]
For $s\ge2$ the upper bounds $u_s=C^s(s/n)^{cs}$ have successive
ratio at most $C\mathrm e^c\eta^c\le1/2$. Their sum is therefore
$O_{K,D}(n^{-2c})$. Conditioning on simplicity costs a bounded factor,
as in Lemma~\ref{lem:moment}. Singleton sets are never bad in a simple
graph of positive minimum degree. This proves the probability bound.

If $V$ has nonzero row support $S$ and an outside vertex has exactly one
edge into $S$, the corresponding row of $AV$ is one nonzero row of $V$,
whereas that row of $VR$ is zero. This contradiction applies over every
field and for every coefficient matrix, on the same graph event.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Structural part of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
There are at most
$M=\sum_{r=1}^K(D+1)^{r^2}$ possible integer candidate matrices,
a constant. A finite union of \eqref{eq:macro} and Markov's inequality
excludes every candidate with $\delta\ge\eta$ with failure probability
at most $C(K,D)(n+1)^B e^{-\gamma_Q\eta^2n}$.
Intersect this event with Lemma~\ref{lem:support}'s good event.

For any remaining equitable indicator $Y$, choose a one-hot old-type
map $T$ minimizing disagreement and put
$S=\{v:Y_v\ne(JT)_v\}$. Then $|S|<\eta n$.
Changed vertices and their neighbors occupy at most $(D+1)|S|$ vertices.
Our choice of $\eta$ leaves in every old class a vertex $v_i$ whose
own row and all neighbor rows are unchanged. Its exact integer equation
gives
\[
 (QT)_i=(AJT)_{v_i}=(AY)_{v_i}=(YR)_{v_i}=(TR)_i.
\]
Thus $QT=TR$ over the integers. Now $V=Y-JT$ satisfies $AV=VR$ and
has support $S$. Lemma~\ref{lem:support} forces $V=0$.
Every equitable candidate is therefore a compatible coarsening.
\end{proof}

\section{Unknown-frame algorithm and charged bit work}\label{sec:algorithm}
Enumerate all $r\le K$ and all nonnegative integer $r$-by-$r$ matrices
whose row sums are at most $D$. For each, compute a binary basis of
\[
 \ker_{\F}(I_r\otimes A-R^T\otimes I_n).
\]
Enumerate its $2^{h_R}$ vectors as $n$-by-$r$ matrices $Y$. Accept only
one-hot rows, nonempty candidate classes and the \emph{original integer}
identity $AY=YR$. Keep an accepted partition of maximum class count.
The true $(J,Q)$ is included, with no certificate supplied to the
algorithm.

On the structural event every accepted partition is $JT$ with an onto
map to its $r$ nonempty classes; hence $r\le k$. The planted partition
gives $r=k$. At a maximum, $T$ is an onto map between two $k$-element
sets and is a permutation. The returned partition is therefore exactly
the planted one up to class names. Its integer neighbor counts recover
$Q$, and its class count recovers $k$.

Binary elimination costs $O_K(n^3)$ bit work. Forming basis combinations,
testing rows and masses, and verifying integer neighbor counts cost at
most $O_{K,D}(n^2)$ bit operations per vector. Degrees and candidate
entries are bounded, so neighbor-count integers have $O_{K,D}(1)$ bits.
Class-size counters have $O(\log n)$ bits; the stated bound includes both.
Lemma~\ref{lem:moment} therefore yields the \emph{unconditional} base-work bound
\[
 \E[\text{base bit work}]\le C(K,D)n^{B+2}.
\]
This already counts the untruncated algorithm's basis iteration and
integer checks. In particular the following conservative unconditional
estimate holds as well:
\[
 \E[\text{total bit work}]\le C(K,D)n^{B+3},
\]
including elimination and rare large kernels.
A binary basis and a current vector, together with the best partition,
use $O_K(n^2)$ bits; a full list of solutions is never stored.

For the capped version, instrument each base bit operation with a binary
clock and stop after $n^{B+4}$ base bit operations. The counter has
$O_{K,D}(\log n)$ bits. Its overhead gives a
deterministic total-work bound $O_{K,D}(n^{B+5})$. If interrupted, return
\texttt{FAILURE/ABSTENTION}, not an unfinished maximum. Markov applied
to the base-work expectation bounds interruption by $O_{K,D}(n^{-2})$.
Hence the capped algorithm has worst-case polynomial bit work and
high-probability recovery, with total
failure bounded by
\[
 O_{K,D}(n^{-(d_{\min}-2)}+n^{-2})
       +C(K,D)(n+1)^B e^{-\gamma_Q\eta^2n}.
\]
This completes the algorithmic part of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Uniformity, examples and boundaries}
For fixed $K,D$ there are finitely many integer connected frames.
Every consistent one fixes its proportions, and each strictly feasible
frame admits a choice of positive strict weights. Choose one certificate
for each such frame. Finiteness makes the minimum of their positive
$q_*,c_*$ positive, so the entropy rate and eventual simplicity
threshold can be made uniform over this finite qualifying family.
The exponent $B$ and the algorithm never depend on that choice.

For $K_5$ minus one edge, the two endpoints of the deleted edge have
degree three and the other three vertices have degree four. Give weight
$1/3$ to an edge between these groups and $1/6$ to an edge inside the
degree-four group. Every vertex weight sum is one and every weight lies
strictly between zero and one half. Thus blind fiber recovery follows
for its independent matching $N$-lifts, at every positive $N$, despite
the absence of internal edges and the unequal total degrees.

The hypothesis is substantive. The separate $K_{3,12}$ counting
obstruction proves that minimum degree three alone does not imply
blind fiber recoverability, even with substantial genie information.
That base admits no such omission weights. Non-strict boundary
certificates do not supply the positive $c_*,q_*$ used here and are
outside this theorem. No result for growing $K$, growing degree,
arbitrary deterministic lifts, noise, numerical eigenvector stability,
or eigenvalue simplicity is asserted. The polynomial exponent is very
large and supplies no practical efficiency claim.
