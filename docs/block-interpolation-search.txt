1. A self-contained independent-Gaussian-row lemma

Let N be b-by-b, with independent centered Gaussian rows n_i. Their row
covariance matrices may differ, but suppose each is at least a^2 I for
some a>0. Then for every t>0,

  Pr{sigma_min(N)<=a t} <= sqrt(2/pi) b^(3/2) t
                         <= b^(3/2)t.                     (1)

Proof. N is nonsingular almost surely. Let d_i be the distance from row
i to the span of the other rows. Conditional on those other rows, a unit
normal to their span has scalar product with n_i a centered Gaussian of
variance at least a^2. Therefore

  Pr{d_i<=u | other rows} <= sqrt(2/pi) u/a.

The normal can be chosen measurably outside the null event of deficient
other-row rank. For an invertible N, its inverse column i has norm1/d_i;
thus ||N^-1||_F^2=sum_i d_i^-2. If every d_i>sqrt(b) a t, then
||N^-1||<=||N^-1||_F<1/(a t), so sigma_min(N)>a t. A union bound with
u=sqrt(b) a t proves(1). The same statement holds conditionally whenever
the independent-row covariance lower bound is conditionally fixed.

No identical-distribution assumption, covariance upper bound, isotropy,
entrywise independence within a row, or independence of the row distances
is used. The elementary b^(3/2) factor is deliberately unoptimized.

2. Positive result: the full prescribed bound for d=2 and every block size

Fix arbitrary real diagonal b-by-b Lambda_1,Lambda_2 with disjoint spectra.
Let Omega_1,Omega_2 be independent standard real Gaussian matrices, and

  B_i=Omega_i^-1 Lambda_i Omega_i,
  Delta=min_{lambda in spec Lambda_1,eta in spec Lambda_2}|lambda-eta|>0.

Use exactly the two orderings and the two constants in KE-05. With d=2,

  chi_mono <= max(kappa(Omega_1),kappa(Omega_2)),
  chi_coef = Delta ||(B_1-B_2)^-1||.                        (2)

The first assertion follows by conjugating each endpoint shift; the scalar
0/0 convention gives1<=kappa. The second follows directly from S_12;
reversing the ordering just negates B_1-B_2.

THEOREM. For every 0<beta<1, set

  L=b sqrt(5/beta), u=beta/(5b^(3/2)),
  K=L/u=5sqrt(5)b^(5/2) beta^(-3/2).

For every fixed admissible pair Lambda_1,Lambda_2,

  Pr{chi_mono chi_coef<=K^3} >=1-beta.                      (3)

In particular the constants are independent of all within-block and
cross-block gaps. The polynomial dependence on b,beta is not optimized.

Proof. Conditional on Omega_1, put

  N=Omega_2(B_1-B_2)=Omega_2 B_1-Lambda_2 Omega_2.

If eta_i is the i-th diagonal entry of Lambda_2 and omega_i^T is row i
of Omega_2, then row i of N is omega_i^T(B_1-eta_i I). The rows are
independent conditional on Omega_1. Moreover

  sigma_min(B_1-eta_i I) >= Delta/kappa(Omega_1),

because B_1-eta_i I=Omega_1^-1(Lambda_1-eta_i I)Omega_1.
Their covariance matrices are consequently at least
(Delta/kappa(Omega_1))^2 I. By(1), conditional probability at least
1-beta/5 gives

  sigma_min(N) >= (Delta/kappa(Omega_1))u.                  (4)

For each i=1,2, Markov's inequality and E||Omega_i||_F^2=b^2 give
Pr{||Omega_i||>L}<=beta/5. Applying(1) to each standard Gaussian
Omega_i gives Pr{sigma_min(Omega_i)<u}<=beta/5. These four events
and(4) have total failure at most beta, without assuming their mutual
independence. On their intersection kappa(Omega_i)<=K, and

  Delta ||(B_1-B_2)^-1||
    =Delta ||N^-1 Omega_2||
    <= kappa(Omega_1)L/u <=K^2.

Equation(2) now proves(3). The proof handles within-block repetitions,
every b>=1, and the exact prescribed separate-per-fixed-input probability
quantifiers. In particular it does not require one Gaussian draw to work
for all possible spectra simultaneously.

3. An ordered-interval counterexample with ALL eigenvalues simple

This is an extension of the known three-block nilpotent-limit mechanism,
not a claim to have newly refuted KE-05. It answers a strictly narrower
possible repair: impose ordered, disjoint block intervals but retain the
literal prescribed first-root orderings and the same constants.

For 0<epsilon<1/4, take b=2,d=3 and, in descending block order,

  Lambda_top=diag(1+epsilon,1+2epsilon),
  Lambda_mid=diag(0,1),
  Lambda_bottom=diag(-1,-1-epsilon).                        (5)

All six eigenvalues are distinct. Every eigenvalue of Lambda_top is
above every eigenvalue of Lambda_mid, which is above every eigenvalue
of Lambda_bottom. Thus there is no interlacing or within-block repetition.

Write X=Omega_top^-1 diag(1,2)Omega_top,
P=Omega_mid^-1 diag(0,1)Omega_mid,
Y=Omega_bottom^-1 diag(0,1)Omega_bottom. The similarities are

  B_top=I+epsilon X, B_mid=P, B_bottom=-I-epsilon Y.

Select the REQUIRED ordering k=bottom, namely(bottom,top,mid). Since
P^2=P and tr P=1, the second-to-last interpolation step is

  S=(I-P)+epsilon X,
  det S=epsilon(kappa+2epsilon), kappa=tr(PX),
  adj S=P+epsilon adj X.

The updated top block therefore satisfies the exact identity

  Bhat_top
    =I+[(P+epsilon adj X)X((I-P)+epsilon X)]/(kappa+2epsilon)
    --> I+N,  N=PX(I-P)/kappa.                              (6)

The scalar kappa is nonzero and N is nonzero almost surely. To verify
the nonzero-polynomial claim directly, take Omega_top=I and
Omega_mid=[[1,1],[1,2]]. Then

  P=[[-1,-2],[1,2]], kappa=3,
  PX(I-P)=[[2,2],[-2,-2]].

The corresponding rational-function numerators are nonzero polynomials
in the independent Gaussian entries; their zero sets have measure zero.
Also N^2=0, since(I-P)P=0. None of these assertions depends on Omega_bottom.

The global maximum endpoint is c=1+2epsilon, and

  ||cI-Lambda_top||=epsilon,
  ||cI-Bhat_top|| --> ||N||>0 almost surely.                (7)

Thus the prescribed chi_mono diverges at least as1/epsilon. Its coefficient
factor cannot cancel the divergence. In the selected ordering, B_bottom
tends to -I and the literal recurrence gives

  S_12 --> -2I-N,
  S_13 --> (-2I-N)(-I-P).                                 (8)

The limit in(8) is invertible: its two factors have determinants4 and2.
The minimum cross-gap from the bottom block to either other block is
exactly1. Consequently the selected ordering's chi_coef tends to the
strictly positive finite random quantity

  ||[(-2I-N)(-I-P)]^-1||^(1/2).

For any fixed sequence epsilon_j down to0, all required orderings are
defined almost surely simultaneously: the recurrence is rational in the
Gaussian entries, and setting all Omega_i=I makes each required determinant
nonzero because the block spectra are disjoint. A countable union of
the resulting null exceptional sets is null. Coupling the same Gaussian
matrices over the sequence, equations(7)--(8) give

  chi_mono(epsilon_j)chi_coef(epsilon_j) --> infinity a.s.

Dominated convergence of the fixed-threshold indicators proves that its
probability of being at most any fixed finite constant tends to0. This
retains the separate-per-input probability interpretation; the deterministic
input spectra never depend on the Gaussian sample.

This rules out the ordered-interval repair with the SAME prescribed
all-root orderings, even if every within-block eigenvalue is simple. It
does not rule out choosing a better solvent-factor order separately for
each root, bounding the fundamental polynomial directly, or proving the
claimed small-block Krylov convergence by another argument. The original
known counterexample already demonstrates that individual-factor growth
can be a poor proxy for actual interpolation behavior.

4. Next stronger proof obligation (partly resolved by Section5 below)

Try the ORIGINAL block Vandermonde/interpolation system, avoiding the
updated nonnormal solvents. Its rows are independent Gaussian vectors
restricted to rank-b subspaces of the bd-dimensional coefficient space.
Lemma(1) cannot be applied there directly: those row covariances have
zero minimum eigenvalue. A proof must lower-bound the projection of the
conditional row-normal vector into its particular degree-(d-1) evaluation
subspace. Simply replacing that covariance by a full-rank Gaussian one
would assume the desired interpolation bound.

The d=2 left multiplication above works because subtraction/elimination
leaves a b-dimensional full-covariance Gaussian problem conditional on
one independent block. For d>=3, analogous elimination coefficients depend
on the same random blocks whose rows would be used for the next small-ball
step. Preserving the needed conditional independence, or proving a new
deterministic projection bound from the block cross-gaps, is the concrete
remaining task. An unproved factorization/independence step is not a result.

5. Direct three-block interpolation: a positive repair of the convergence bound

The obstacle just described can be bypassed for d=3, without trying to
bound the disproved solvent-factor constants. The result below is for
EVERY b, with arbitrary disjoint real diagonal spectra, including all
within-block repetitions. It directly controls the fundamental matrix
polynomials, so the ordered negative example in Section3 does not conflict
with it.

5.1 A homogeneous two-node polynomial and its resolvent identity

Let B_i,B_j be any b-by-b matrices with D=B_i-B_j invertible. Define the
matrix polynomial with coefficients to the RIGHT of its argument by its
scalar-variable expression

  R_ij(z)=(zI-B_i)D^-1(zI-B_j)
         =z^2 D^-1-z(B_iD^-1+D^-1B_j)+B_iD^-1B_j.           (9)

Then R_ij(B_i)=R_ij(B_j)=0. The first identity follows by immediate
coefficient cancellation. For the second, the first two terms combine
to -B_j D D^-1=-B_j and the remaining two to D D^-1 B_j=B_j.
This check is necessary: arbitrary product-factor evaluation at a matrix
argument would not preserve the scalar-variable factorization in(9).

For any scalar z outside both spectra, the exact resolvent identity is

  R_ij(z)^-1
    =(zI-B_j)^-1 D (zI-B_i)^-1
    =(zI-B_i)^-1-(zI-B_j)^-1.                             (10)

The second equality uses D=(zI-B_j)-(zI-B_i), keeping the order of all
factors. Thus no condition number of an UPDATED similarity is introduced.

5.2 Independent Gaussian rows return at the remaining block

Fix three diagonal blocks Lambda_1,Lambda_2,Lambda_3 with pairwise disjoint
spectra, and independent standard Gaussian Omega_1,Omega_2,Omega_3. Let
B_i=Omega_i^-1 Lambda_i Omega_i. Put

  Delta_ij=min distance(spec Lambda_i,spec Lambda_j),
  Delta=min_{i<j}Delta_ij,
  delta_k=min_{i!=k}Delta_ik,
  kappa_i=kappa(Omega_i).

For root k, take the other two indices i,j in either fixed order, and
use their R_ij. Conditional on Omega_i,Omega_j, let

  N_k=Omega_k R_ij(B_k).

If eta_l is the l-th entry of Lambda_k and omega_l^T is row l of Omega_k,
then row l of N_k is exactly omega_l^T R_ij(eta_l). These rows are
independent conditional on the other two blocks. From(10) and diagonal
similarity,

  ||R_ij(eta_l)^-1|| <= (kappa_i+kappa_j)/delta_k.

Consequently the row covariances of N_k are at least
[delta_k/(kappa_i+kappa_j)]^2 I. Lemma(1) gives a spectrum-uniform
conditional smallest-singular-value tail for N_k. This conditional step
uses ORIGINAL blocks only, so the forbidden dependence on the root's
own Gaussian matrix is absent.

Define

  F_k(z)=R_ij(z) N_k^-1 Omega_k.                            (11)

Then F_k(B_i)=F_k(B_j)=0 and F_k(B_k)=I. To check the latter, multiply
on the left by Omega_k and use N_k's definition. Thus(11) is the actual
degree-two fundamental interpolant. The three constructed polynomials
prove surjectivity, hence invertibility, of the square interpolation map;
there is no need to assume an unproved noncommuting product formula.
All needed inverses exist almost surely by the conditional row lemma.

5.3 Spectrum-uniform theorem, with an explicit confidence constant

THEOREM. Let [a,c] contain all three spectra and let L_spec=c-a>0. For
0<beta<1, set

  L_g=b sqrt(12/beta), u=beta/(12b^(3/2)), K=L_g/u.

With probability at least1-beta,

  max_k sup_{z in[a,c]} ||F_k(z)||
       <=2K^6 (L_spec/Delta)^2.                            (12)

The constant depends only on b,beta, not on within-block or cross-block
eigenvalues or gaps. In particular this proves the conjectured DIRECT
interpolation growth exponent d-1=2 for d=3, although the original
factorwise chi_mono chi_coef conjecture is false even at b=2,d=3.

Proof with all event counts. Allocate beta/12 to each of twelve events:

  (a) three bounds ||Omega_i||<=L_g, by Frobenius Markov;
  (b) three bounds sigma_min(Omega_i)>=u, by Lemma(1);
  (c) three pair bounds obtained from
      sigma_min(Omega_j(B_i-B_j)) >=Delta_ij u/kappa_i,
      conditional on Omega_i, for one orientation of each unordered pair;
  (d) three bounds sigma_min(N_k)>=delta_k u/(kappa_i+kappa_j),
      conditional on the two other blocks.

Each failure probability is at most beta/12, regardless of dependence
between these events. On their intersection kappa_i<=K. The pair event
and the norm of Omega_j imply

  ||(B_i-B_j)^-1|| <=K^2/Delta_ij.

For z in[a,c], diagonal similarity yields ||zI-B_i||<=kappa_i L_spec.
Thus (9) gives

  ||R_ij(z)|| <=K^4 L_spec^2/Delta_ij.

The root event gives

  ||N_k^-1 Omega_k||
      <=(kappa_i+kappa_j)L_g/(delta_k u) <=2K^2/delta_k.

Their product is at most
2K^6 L_spec^2/(Delta_ij delta_k)<=2K^6(L_spec/Delta)^2,
simultaneously for all k,z. This proves(12).

5.4 Direct small-block Krylov consequence

Let a fixed real symmetric A have a target invariant subspace Q of
dimension3b. Group its target eigenvalues into three b-by-b diagonal
blocks with pairwise disjoint spectra. Let Delta be their minimum
CROSS-BLOCK gap; within-block repetitions or arbitrarily small gaps
are allowed. Let L_spec=lambda_max(A)-lambda_min(A)>0 and
relgap_b=Delta/L_spec. For a standard Gaussian n-by-b initial Omega,

  K_3(A,Omega)=range[Omega,A Omega,A^2 Omega].

For every0<beta<1 there is a finite C(n,b,beta), independent of the
eigenvalues and all their gaps, such that

  tan(angle(Q,K_3(A,Omega))) <= C(n,b,beta) relgap_b^-2

with probability at least1-beta. The angle is the largest principal
angle. The assertion covers interior or exterior targets; it does not
need an ordered-block assumption. If n=3b the target is the whole space
and the assertion is trivial almost surely.

For completeness, this consequence does not need to trust an additional
solvent-factor theorem. In eigen-coordinates partition the first3b rows
of Omega into Omega_1,Omega_2,Omega_3 and call the remaining rows Omega_T.
The head Krylov matrix K_H is square and invertible on the event above.
For any head vector partitioned into y_k, its interpolation polynomial is

  p_y(z)=sum_{k=1}^3 F_k(z) Omega_k^-1 y_k.

It reproduces the head values and hence generates K_H^-1 y as its
coefficient vector. If G=max_k sup_{z in spectral interval}||F_k(z)||,
each tail row obeys

  |omega_l^T p_y(lambda_l)|
     <=||omega_l|| sqrt(3) G max_k||Omega_k^-1|| ||y||.

Therefore

  ||K_T K_H^-1||
     <=sqrt(3)||Omega_T||_F G max_k||Omega_k^-1||.            (13)

The graph-subspace formula identifies this norm with the stated tangent.
Apply the head theorem with failure beta/2 and use its u to bound each
head inverse by1/u. Markov bounds the tail Frobenius norm by
sqrt(2b(n-3b)/beta) except beta/2. An explicit admissible constant is

  C(n,b,beta)=2sqrt(6b(n-3b)/beta) K(beta/2)^6/u(beta/2).

Its poor dimension/confidence exponents are not optimized. Only its
spectrum independence matters for this partial convergence assertion.

Relation to the original target. This validates the desired small-block
cluster-robust exponent for THREE target blocks directly, rather than
resurrecting the false intermediate constants. It is a theoretical result
about standard block Krylov analysis, not a new fast construction of a
long unknown raw-factor Krylov sequence or a solution to the original
rank-one theta=1/m product-runtime goal. A bounded primary-source search
did not locate this exact three-block result; novelty still needs a
proper literature review and independent proof audit.

6. General-depth obligation, subsequently addressed in Section7

For four or more blocks, R_ij in(9) alone is insufficient. Sequentially
adding further vanishing constraints by updated similarities would
reintroduce the conditional dependence and potentially extra powers of
the smallest cross-gap. A general homogeneous interpolation basis whose
inverse at a fresh block has a gap-linear bound like(10) would extend
the argument. Such a basis or an alternative direct probability bound
has not been proved here. The growing-d conjectured exponent remains
open in this note; the original chi-product conjecture remains false.

The preceding paragraph records the state immediately after the direct
three-block proof. Section7 below supersedes its unproved-depth status.

7. Symmetric raw-factor induction: direct interpolation for EVERY depth

This construction avoids updated nonnormal solvents entirely. It uses
orthogonal polynomials of the ORIGINAL symmetric diagonal matrix formed
by the other blocks. The crucial inverse identity is an end-to-end
resolvent compression. The proof below is self-contained; it does not
resurrect the false chi_mono chi_coef statement.

7.1 Statement and an explicit recursive confidence constant

Let d>=1,b>=1 be fixed. Let Lambda_1,...,Lambda_d be real diagonal
b-by-b matrices with pairwise disjoint spectra, and let independent
standard real Gaussian Omega_i define B_i=Omega_i^-1 Lambda_i Omega_i.
For d>=2, let

  Delta=min_{i!=j} distance(spec Lambda_i,spec Lambda_j)>0,
  L_spec=c-a>0,

where [a,c] contains all spectra. Let F_i be the right-coefficient
degree-(d-1) fundamental polynomials: F_i(B_j)=delta_ij I.

THEOREM. For every0<beta<1 there is a finite explicit C_d(b,beta),
independent of all eigenvalues and gaps, such that

  Pr{max_i sup_{z in[a,c]}||F_i(z)||
           <= C_d(b,beta)(L_spec/Delta)^(d-1)} >=1-beta.    (14)

One unoptimized choice is C_1=1 and, for d>=2,

 C_d(b,beta)=324(d-1)^(3/2)d^3 b^5 beta^-3
                     C_(d-1)(b,beta/(3d)).                 (15)

For d=1 take F_1=I and omit Delta. These constants are intentionally
loose. Their logarithms grow O(d^2 log(d+1)+d log(b/beta)); the theorem
asserts the spectrum-uniform exponent d-1, not optimal b,d,beta constants.

7.2 Generic raw block Krylov bases need no spectral oracle

For q blocks of the original data, form the ANALYTICAL symmetric diagonal
matrix A_0=diag(Lambda_i) of order qb and raw Gaussian stack
Gamma=[Omega_i] of sizeqb-by-b. The square raw Krylov matrix

  [Gamma,A_0 Gamma,...,A_0^(q-1)Gamma]

is invertible almost surely. Its determinant is a polynomial in the
Gaussian entries and is nonzero when all Omega_i=I: after a row/column
permutation the witness is a direct sum of b scalar q-node Vandermonde
matrices, with distinct nodes by cross-block disjointness. All relevant
subsets of a fixed d-block family share this property simultaneously,
outside a finite union of null sets.

Block Gram--Schmidt consequently produces orthonormal qb-by-b blocks
V_0,...,V_(q-1) with

  range[V_0,...,V_j]=range[Gamma,...,A_0^j Gamma],
  V_j=sum_{l=0}^j A_0^l Gamma C_(l,j).

The leading C_(j,j) is invertible. Define the b-by-b right-coefficient
polynomial P_j(z)=sum_l z^l C_(l,j). For each selected original block i,
the block row of V_j is Omega_i P_j(B_i).

Symmetry of A_0 makes its orthogonal-basis representation block
tridiagonal: ordinary block Arnoldi is block Lanczos here. In particular
the last recurrence has the form

 A_0 V_(q-1)=V_(q-2)H+V_(q-1)T,

where the first term is absent if q=1. Set

 R_q(z)=z P_(q-1)(z)-P_(q-2)(z)H-P_(q-1)(z)T.             (16)

This polynomial has degree q and annihilates EVERY selected B_i under
left evaluation. No product of scalar-variable factors is evaluated at
noncommuting matrix arguments. If the normalized spectrum is in[0,1],
then ||H||,||T||<=1, because they are compressions of A_0.

7.3 The raw symmetric resolvent controls the inverse exactly

For z outside spec(A_0), define

  W_q(z)=V_(q-1)^T (zI-A_0)^-1 Gamma.                       (17)

Then

  W_q(z)R_q(z)=I.                                         (18)

Proof. Write R_q(z)=sum_{l=0}^q z^l C_l. Its annihilation means
sum_l A_0^l Gamma C_l=0. Polynomial division with right coefficients gives

 (zI-A_0)^-1 Gamma R_q(z)
   =sum_{l=1}^q sum_{t=0}^{l-1} z^(l-1-t) A_0^t Gamma C_l.

Left multiplication by V_(q-1)^T kills every term with t<q-1. The only
surviving term is V_(q-1)^T A_0^(q-1)Gamma C_q=I, since C_q is the
leading coefficient of P_(q-1) and V_(q-1) is its orthonormal raw
polynomial realization. This proves(18), including the q=1 case.

As all factors are square, R_q(z) is invertible and its inverse is W_q(z).
In particular, for a scalar eta separated from spec(A_0) by delta>0,

  ||R_q(eta)^-1|| <= ||Gamma||/delta.                       (19)

This bound uses only an ordinary symmetric resolvent. There is no factor
involving nonnormal updated similarities or an extra internal cross-gap.

7.4 Induction with the fresh original Gaussian block

Normalize all diagonal input spectra affinely into[0,1], so L_spec=1;
the original interval and gap are restored at the end. Assume(14) for
q=d-1 blocks. For root k, construct(16) from the OTHER q original blocks
only. Let G_q denote the maximum norm of their q-node fundamental
polynomials on[0,1]. Interpolation of the degree<=q-1 polynomials P_j gives

 P_j(z)=sum_{i!=k}F_i^(others)(z) P_j(B_i),
 ||P_j(B_i)||=||Omega_i^-1(V_j)_i||<=||Omega_i^-1||.

Therefore, with M=max_i||Omega_i^-1||,

  sup_z ||P_j(z)||<=q G_q M,
  sup_z ||R_q(z)||<=3q G_q M.                              (20)

Let delta_k be the smallest gap between the root spectrum and the spectra
of all other blocks. Conditional on the other Gaussian blocks,

  N_k=Omega_k R_q(B_k)

has independent rows omega_(k,l)^T R_q(eta_(k,l)). By(19), their
covariances are at least[delta_k/||Gamma||]^2 I. Lemma(1) thus applies
conditionally with no loss of independence. Finally

  F_k(z)=R_q(z)N_k^-1 Omega_k                              (21)

has the required value I at B_k and zero at all other blocks. Constructing
all d such polynomials gives surjectivity and hence invertibility of the
full square interpolation map; these are the actual unique fundamental
polynomials. Independence is needed only between the fresh root Gaussian
and the construction using all OTHER blocks, which is explicit here.

7.5 Quantiles and recursion bookkeeping

Allocate beta/3 to each of three collections of events:

  (i) For each omitted root k, the inductive q-block interpolation bound
      with failure beta/(3d). Their cross-gap is at least the full Delta,
      so every G_q<=C_q(b,beta/(3d))Delta^(-(q-1)).

  (ii) For all d Gaussian blocks, use
       L_g=b sqrt(6d/beta), u=beta/(6d b^(3/2)),
      to bound ||Omega_i||<=L_g and sigma_min(Omega_i)>=u.
      The d Markov failures and d applications of(1) total at most beta/3.

  (iii) For every root k, conditional on the other blocks, use
        v=beta/(3d b^(3/2))
      in Lemma(1) to bound
      sigma_min(N_k)>=delta_k v/||Gamma||.
      The d conditional failures total at most beta/3.

No independence between the events themselves is claimed or needed.
On their intersection M<=1/u, ||Gamma||<=sqrt(q)L_g, and

  ||N_k^-1 Omega_k||<=sqrt(q)L_g^2/(delta_k v).

Equations(20)--(21) yield

 max_k sup_z ||F_k(z)||
   <=[3q^(3/2)L_g^2/(u v)] C_q(b,beta/(3d)) Delta^-q.

The bracketed factor equals324 q^(3/2)d^3 b^5 beta^-3, proving(15).
Affine rescaling restores(L_spec/Delta)^q. All exceptional rank/inverse
events are Gaussian null sets already covered by Section7.2. This proves
the theorem for each fixed d and all deterministic admissible spectra.

7.6 Full small-block convergence consequence and limits

The graph argument in Section5.4 works for any d, replacing sqrt(3) by
sqrt(d). It gives, for a target db-dimensional invariant subspace and a
standard Gaussian n-by-b start,

 tan(angle(Q,K_d(A,Omega)))
   <=sqrt(d)||Omega_tail||_F max_i||Omega_i^-1|| G_d.

Combining(14), its Gaussian inverse bounds, and a tail Frobenius Markov
bound proves a spectrum-uniform C(n,b,d,beta) relgap_b^(1-d) bound.
This is the DIRECT convergence statement that the disproved chi-product
conjecture was meant to imply. It allows arbitrarily close or repeated
eigenvalues WITHIN a block, and requires only disjoint block spectra.
Subsequent convergence to a particular invariant subspace still needs its
appropriate external spectral separation; this is an initial subspace
angle bound, exactly the structural quantity considered in the source.

This is a candidate full proof of that direct statement, independently
derived in this search. It has not yet completed independent review or
an exhaustive novelty audit. It is not a proof of the original false
intermediate chi-product bound. It also does not construct a raw long
Krylov sequence more cheaply, change the standard Krylov arithmetic cost,
or settle the original product-output-optimal runtime goal. Its potential
contribution is the missing spectrum-uniform cluster-robust convergence
analysis via symmetric raw orthogonal polynomials and fresh-row small-ball
conditioning.

8. Fractional moments sharpen the growing-depth constants

The parent supplied the following strengthening, independently checked
here. It removes the O(d^2 log d) confidence-recursion overhead from(15).
The polynomial constructions, spectrum normalization and exact identities
of Section7 are unchanged.

For any nonempty subset S of the original d blocks and root k in S with
|S|>=2, construct R_(S\k) from the other q=|S|-1 blocks exactly as in
Section7. Let Gamma_(S\k) be their raw Gaussian stack, let delta_(S,k)
be the root-to-others cross-gap, and define

 Z_(S,k)=delta_(S,k)||N_(S,k)^-1||/||Gamma_(S\k)||,
 N_(S,k)=Omega_k R_(S\k)(B_k).

All these objects are measurable deterministic functions of the indicated
original Gaussian blocks: use fixed QR sign choices for the raw bases.
Null exceptional ranks can be totalized arbitrarily. The conditional
row lemma implies

 Pr{Z_(S,k)>z | Omega_(S\k)} <=min(1,b^(3/2)/z),
 E[Z_(S,k)^(1/2) | Omega_(S\k)] <=2b^(3/4).                (22)

The moment follows by integrating the tail, splitting at z=b^(3/2).

Let L_0=b sqrt(4d/beta) and U_0=4d b^(3/2)/beta. With probability at
least1-beta/2, all d blocks simultaneously satisfy

  ||Omega_i||<=L_0, ||Omega_i^-1||<=U_0.                    (23)

Indeed d Frobenius-Markov norm tails and d row-lemma inverse tails each
cost beta/4 in total. There is no conditioning on this event in(22).

On(23), the deterministic recursion for the actual fundamental norm G(S)
is

 G(S)<=max_{k in S}
     [3(|S|-1)^(3/2)U_0 L_0^2/Delta] Z_(S,k) G(S\k),     (24)

where Delta is the full fixed cross-gap after normalization to[0,1].
Every local cross-gap is at least Delta. Expanding(24) reaches a singleton
after d-1 eliminations and considers at most d! nested elimination paths.

For a fixed path, later Z factors depend only on the blocks remaining
after the current root is removed. Tower conditioning in that order and
(22) therefore give

  E[(product_path Z)^(1/2)] <=(2b^(3/4))^(d-1).

This step does NOT assume independence of the Z factors. Markov followed
by a union bound over d! paths shows, except probability beta/2,

 max_path product Z
   <=[2d!(2b^(3/4))^(d-1)/beta]^2.                         (25)

Union the failure of(23) and(25); their dependence is irrelevant. Since
the nonrandom prefactors from(24) multiply to

 [3U_0 L_0^2/Delta]^(d-1)((d-1)!)^(3/2),

equations(23)--(25) prove

 G_d <=4 beta^-2(d!)^2((d-1)!)^(3/2)
                   [192d^2 b^5 beta^-2/Delta]^(d-1).       (26)

For every d>=2 this is at most

  [768d^8 b^5 beta^-4/Delta]^(d-1).                       (27)

For the simplification, use(d!)^2<=d^(4(d-1)),
((d-1)!)^(3/2)<=d^(3(d-1)/2), and
4beta^-2<=(4beta^-2)^(d-1). The resulting d exponent7.5 is at most8.
The constant1000 can replace768 for a cleaner theorem statement.
Undo affine normalization by replacing1/Delta with L_spec/Delta.

Thus the direct polynomial bound has base polynomial in b,d,1/beta,
with total logarithm O(d log(db/beta)); it does not hide an exponential
growth of depth inside every logarithm. This strengthens the direct
small-block Krylov angle consequence, while retaining its exact stated
partition and probability scope.

Partition caution. Positive cross-gap refers to the SUPPLIED partition
into d blocks of size b. It cannot be replaced without proof by the
condition that every eigenvalue has multiplicity at most b. For example
b=3,d=2 and three distinct values each of multiplicity2 cannot fill two
size3 blocks without splitting one repeated value across blocks. This
note claims only the partition-specific theorem and its corresponding
Shao relgap_b parameter. Unequal-size or padded extensions are separate.

10. Padding removes the equal-block partition restriction for an ADDITIVE gap

This extension was proposed independently by the research-runs agent and
the parent; the projection argument and packing bounds are audited here.
It does not by itself remove a relative-gap condition number.

Let the k target eigenvalues, with multiplicity, be sorted as
mu_1<=...<=mu_k. Assume k>b and define the additive b-th-neighbor gap

  g=min_i [b-th smallest of {|mu_i-mu_j|:j!=i}]>0.          (28)

Cut the sorted list wherever an adjacent gap is at least h=g/b. Every
remaining component has size at most b. Otherwise its first b+1 values
span less than b h=g, giving its first value b neighbors at distance
less than g, contrary to(28). This argument permits repetitions within
components and needs no gap between individual values in a component.

Greedily pack WHOLE components, in order, into blocks of capacity b.
Let their positive integer weights be w_1,...,w_D. Any consecutive two
finished blocks have combined weight greater than b: the first component
of the next block could not fit in the previous one. Pairing adjacent
blocks then proves

  D<=2ceil(k/b)-1<=ceil(2k/b).                             (29)

For example, even D=2t implies k>tb; odd D=2t+1 gives the same strict
inequality after pairing the first2t blocks. A cross-block pair of values
belongs to different components, so its gap is at least h.

Pad each block to size b by repeating one of its OWN eigenvalues, using
independent fictitious Gaussian rows for all dummy coordinates. This
creates an augmented diagonal problem A_ext of size

  n_ext=n+Db-k,

with augmented target dimension Db. The full spectral interval remains
unchanged; its target cross-gap is at least g/b. Apply the d=D theorem
to this fixed padded input. The original algorithm does not have to know
the component partition, eigenvalues, or dummy samples: they are devices
for proving a property of its ordinary D-depth Krylov space.

10.1 Projection to the original space is a rigorous graph restriction

In eigen-coordinates, put the original k target coordinates first and
the dummy target coordinates next. On the augmented good event let K_H
be its invertible Db-by-Db head Krylov matrix and K_T its original-tail
Krylov matrix. For any y in R^k, prescribe augmented head values

  y_ext=(y,0_dummy),  c=K_H^-1 y_ext.

The augmented Krylov vector has original head y, dummy head0, and original
tail K_T K_H^-1 E y, with E the isometric inclusion into the original
head coordinates. Projecting away all dummy coordinates gives a vector
in ORIGINAL K_D(A,Omega), because the original rows of every augmented
power A_ext^j Omega_ext are exactly A^j Omega. Thus original K_D contains
the k-dimensional graph

  { (y,T y): y in R^k }, T=K_T K_H^-1 E,
  ||T||<=||K_T K_H^-1||.                                  (30)

The original largest target-to-Krylov angle is no larger than the angle
to this contained graph. Hence the augmented angle bound transfers with
no projection-condition factor. This remains valid when Db>n: original
K_D may have dimension less than Db, but it contains the displayed
k-dimensional graph.

The desired original-angle event depends only on the ORIGINAL Gaussian
Omega. It is implied by the joint augmented good event, so integrating
out the fictitious rows gives original success probability at least1-beta.
The coefficient vector c may depend on dummy samples in the proof; its
existence is sufficient for a subspace containment claim. The algorithm
never needs to construct it.

Together with(29), this yields a uniform additive-gap angle bound at
depth D<=ceil(2k/b), with cross-gap parameter at least g/b. Running the
larger fixed depth ceil(2k/b) removes the need to know D. For k<=b use
the usual one-block Gaussian overlap bound instead; the b-th-neighbor
quantity in(28) is then not defined and must not be queried.

10.2 Forward gaps and the unresolved relative-gap distinction

The SAME component argument works with the additive forward gap

  g_forward=min_{1<=i<=k-b}(mu_(i+b)-mu_i)>0

and cutoff g_forward/b. A b+1-point component would violate that minimum
directly. This is often a more convenient parameter for sorted spectra.

This does NOT yet prove the complete gap replacement proposed in Chen
et al., arXiv:2508.06486,Section5. Their stated conjecture uses the
RELATIVE forward gap (lambda_i-lambda_(i+b))/lambda_i and removes the
rank condition number. Nor should(28) be identified with the relative
b-th-nearest definition from Meyer--Musco--Musco without conversion.
A global additive gap divided by the full spectral diameter can still
be tiny when the relevant eigenvalues themselves are tiny, even if
their relative gaps are constant. Our present theorem may then retain
a logarithmic condition-number dependence. A scale-sensitive interpolation
or good-starting-block argument is needed to remove it; padding alone
does not establish that stronger relative-gap assertion.

11. A scale-sensitive identity: relative-tail growth with NO condition number

The missing scale-sensitive step can be proved. The parent and this agent
independently obtained the identity below; the raw evaluation-functional
proof here avoids any need to justify polynomial residue cancellation.

Assume the fixed d-block target spectra are NONNEGATIVE. Define their
symmetric relative cross-gap

  g=min_{i!=j,lambda in Lambda_i,eta in Lambda_j}
                    |lambda-eta|/max(lambda,eta)>0,         (31)

with a zero/zero cross-pair giving gap0. Thus two different blocks never
both contain zero. Let a=min of all target eigenvalues. Then for every
0<beta<1, the candidate uniform-tail theorem is

 Pr{max_i sup_{0<=z<=a}||F_i(z)||
       <=[20000d^12 b^5/(beta^4 g)]^(d-1)} >=1-beta.        (32)

No global spectral diameter or target condition number occurs. This is a
different statement from a condition-number-free lower bound on a raw
MONOMIAL Krylov matrix's smallest singular value; the latter is false in
general. The theorem concerns actual tail interpolation and convergence.

11.1 Evaluation-functional derivation of the relative identity

Use the terminal R and W of Section7 on a q-block subset S. They exist
for an arbitrary symmetric A_0, without normalizing its spectral norm;
the norm bounds on recurrence coefficients are not needed in this proof.
For scalar z outside spec(A_0), define the b-by-qb row operator

  L_z=R(z)V_last^T(zI-A_0)^-1.

For every0<=t<=q-1, resolvent polynomial division and orthogonality give

  L_z A_0^t Gamma=z^t I.

Indeed all polynomial remainder terms have powers less than q-1 and are
killed by V_last^T, while R(z)W(z)=I. Thus L_z is EXACTLY the
evaluation-at-z map on the square raw Krylov coefficient space. By the
fundamental interpolation identity, its i-th block row is

  (L_z)_i=F_i^S(z)Omega_i^-1.

For a fresh root eigenvalue eta outside all other block spectra, it follows
that

 R(z)R(eta)^-1
   =L_z(zI-A_0)(eta I-A_0)^-1 Gamma
   =sum_{i in S}F_i^S(z)Omega_i^-1
                (zI-Lambda_i)(eta I-Lambda_i)^-1 Omega_i.   (33)

Every multiplication order in(33) matters. It is not obtained by
substituting matrices into a scalar-variable product factorization.

If0<=z<a and eta is in the omitted root block, then for every other
lambda>=a,

  |z-lambda|/|eta-lambda|<=lambda/|eta-lambda|<=1/g.

Writing G(S,z)=max_i||F_i^S(z)|| and kappa_max=max_i kappa(Omega_i),
equation(33) consequently gives

  ||R(z)R(eta)^-1||<=q kappa_max G(S,z)/g.                 (34)

11.2 Fresh rows, pointwise recursion and final finite-point union

For root k of T=S union{k}, set

  Ntilde_(T,k)(z)=Omega_k R(B_k)R(z)^-1.

Conditional on all OTHER blocks, this matrix has independent Gaussian
rows. Equation(34) bounds its row covariance below by
[g/(q kappa_max G(S,z))]^2 I. The normalization is positive: constant
polynomial interpolation implies sum_i F_i^S(z)=I, so G(S,z)>=1/q.
Moreover

  F_k^T(z)=Ntilde_(T,k)(z)^-1 Omega_k.

Define the normalized inverse Z by multiplying ||Ntilde^-1|| by
g/(q kappa_max G(S,z)). Its conditional tail and half-moment are exactly
those of Section8, independent of the relative spectral scales.

On the same global Gaussian event(23), the pointwise deterministic
recursion is now

  G(T,z)<= [q U_0 L_0^2/g]
                   max_k{Z_(T,k)(z)G(T\k,z)}.             (35)

There is NO sqrt(q) or additional absolute-gap factor in(35).
For N deterministic evaluation points in[0,a), carry out the final
fractional-moment Markov union over N times d! paths, keeping the points
out of every intermediate confidence allocation. This yields

 max_point G_d(z)
 <=4N^2 beta^-2(d!)^2(d-1)!
                   [64d^2b^5 beta^-2/g]^(d-1)             (36)

with probability at least1-beta. The N^2 factor is OUTSIDE the depth
power. In particular finite tail eigenvalues do not introduce d log n
through repeated confidence splitting.

11.3 A deterministic grid gives a uniform tail interval

For a matrix polynomial F of degree p=d-1 on[0,a], scalarizing u^T F v
and applying the scalar Markov derivative inequality gives

  sup||F'|| <=(2p^2/a) sup||F||.

Take N=4p^2+1 and grid nodes z_j=j*a/N for j=0,...,N-1. Every node is
STRICTLY below a; no normalization at a head eigenvalue occurs. The
distance from any point of[0,a] to a left grid node is at most a/N,
which is less than a/(4p^2). The derivative bound therefore gives
sup||F||<=2 max_grid||F||, including the right endpoint without a limit.
This applies to the simultaneous maximum of all F_i too. There are
N<=5d^2 points. Multiplying(36) by2 proves(32); the constant20000 and
d exponent12 are conservative. This endpoint-omitting grid was supplied
by the fresh independent auditor after the first proof draft.

If a=0, the interval is one point. Approach it by z=-epsilon. The ratio
in(33) is then bounded by

  1/g+epsilon/Delta_abs,

where Delta_abs>0 is the fixed absolute cross-gap. Apply the pointwise
argument with g_epsilon=(1/g+epsilon/Delta_abs)^-1. As epsilon decreases
to0, g_epsilon tends to g and F_i(-epsilon) tends to F_i(0). The closed
probability bound therefore passes to the limit with the SAME final
constant. Delta_abs is only a proof device and does not occur in(32).
This handles zero head minimum without pretending that a nonnegative
point can lie strictly below zero.

11.4 Relative-forward packing and the resulting genuine convergence scope

Let target eigenvalues be sorted lambda_1>=...>=lambda_k>=0, k>b, with
positive relative forward b-th gap

  gamma=min_{1<=i<=k-b}(lambda_i-lambda_(i+b))/lambda_i>0.   (37)

A zero/zero term is taken to give0. Cut adjacent values when their
relative gap (lambda_i-lambda_(i+1))/lambda_i is at least gamma/b.
Every remaining component has size at most b. Otherwise a b-edge chain
would give

  lambda_(i+b)/lambda_i
     >(1-gamma/b)^b >=1-gamma,

by Bernoulli's inequality, contradicting(37). Any two values in different
components have symmetric relative distance at least gamma/b: one cut
between them already has that relative gap, and extending the endpoints
can only increase it.

Pack whole components and pad exactly as in Section10. Then
D<=2ceil(k/b)-1, and the augmented partition has relative cross-gap
g>=gamma/b. Applying(32), followed by the proved graph projection, gives
a condition-number-free startup angle bound for ORIGINAL ordinary
K_D(A,Omega), with only dimension/confidence polynomials and the factor

  [poly(D,b,1/beta)/gamma]^(D-1).

The simultaneous Gaussian inverse event from the theorem's proof and a
tail Frobenius bound complete the graph prefactor. Its logarithm is
O(D log(Db/(beta gamma))+log(n/beta)). No dummy samples or spectral
partition have to be known by the actual standard Krylov iteration.

Combining this startup bound with an appropriate EXISTING good-start
low-rank approximation lemma can give ordinary RBKI convergence with
relative b-th gap dependence and no target condition number, up to the
explicit additional logarithmic factors above. The exact sharp formula
in Chen et al.'s conjecture must not be claimed without checking those
logarithms and the imported extraction hypotheses. The conjecture's
raw-monomial singular-value lower-bound clause is a separate statement
and is not proved by this argument. No new raw-factor multiplication
runtime follows merely from these convergence bounds.

13. Does the terminal normalization itself reduce raw operator passes?

No such runtime conclusion follows. The exact terminal identity in the
proof is obtained on the ANALYTICAL restriction to q other spectral
blocks, of dimension exactly qb. Its q-block Krylov basis fills that
space, so the last recurrence has no next-block residual. The spectral
restriction and its diagonal data are not supplied in an implicit raw
factor-product problem.

For an implementable short q-step prefix on a larger full operator H,
the terminal recurrence instead contains V_q E_q. Its polynomial obeys

  sum_l H^l Gamma C_l = V_q E_q,

and the same polynomial-division calculation gives

  W_q(z)R_q(z)
    =I+V_(q-1)^T(zI-H)^-1 V_q E_q.                        (38)

Dropping this additional term would assume the important part of the
approximation problem. In particular a compressed resolvent of a short
prefix is not exactly the inverse of its terminal polynomial.

An explicit scalar-block check is H=diag(1,2), Gamma=(1,1)^T, q=1.
Then V_0=Gamma/sqrt(2), its Rayleigh value is3/2, and

  R_1(z)=(z-3/2)/sqrt(2),
  W_1(z)=(1/sqrt(2))[(z-1)^-1+(z-2)^-1],
  W_1(z)R_1(z)=(z-3/2)^2/[(z-1)(z-2)].                   (39)

This differs from1 even though the matrix is tiny and positive definite.
Equation(39) specifically falsifies applying the analytical full-terminal
inverse identity to a shorter actual raw prefix. It is not a lower bound
on other raw-factor algorithms or on accurately approximating(38).

The scalar Chebyshev acceleration layer also retains its ordinary
accuracy dependence. A degree-q polynomial with value1 at1+theta and
supremum at most eta on[0,1] must satisfy

  q >= arcosh(1/eta)/arcosh(1+2theta),                     (40)

by the Chebyshev extremal inequality. This is
Omega(theta^-1/2 log(1/eta)) for small theta and eta. Thus improving
the startup/interpolation constant does not remove theta^-1/2 from this
particular gap filter. Data-dependent or rational algorithms may escape
the scope of(40); no claim about all factor-aware arithmetic is made.

The original hard total-output case still needs a new input-action
mechanism, such as the OPEN order-sensitive raw partial-Krylov bound in
factor-algebra-search.txt, or a different solver/preconditioner whose full
raw-input work is proved. The new convergence theorem is a real theoretical
advance if its novelty survives review, but it does not by itself supply
that missing computational mechanism.
