EXACT MODULAR RECOVERY WITH A DETERMINISTIC QUADRATIC RANK CERTIFICATE
Independent algorithm audit; November 15, 2026

Written only in the authorized rsbm-exact-algorithm directory.
Read the even-block RSBM baseline and deterministic-reductions notes.
This is a specialization of classical exact black-box linear algebra,
not a new planted-eigenvector identity or a new nonsingularity theorem.

OUTCOME
-------
The rank-certification difficulty has a simple solution. Use a SHIFTED
scalar Krylov sequence, starting with Qv rather than v. If exact
Berlekamp--Massey on 2k terms returns linear complexity k for a k-by-k
matrix Q, Q is CERTAINLY nonsingular. Indeed the whole sequence lives
on the invariant space im(Q), whose dimension is rank(Q), so its
linear complexity cannot exceed rank(Q). Computing the sequence and
its exact shortest recurrence costs O(k) sparse matvecs plus O(k^2)
field operations. No cubic elimination, global matrix-polynomial
identity test, or probabilistic verification of the rank is needed.

Random diagonal preconditioning makes this certificate available with
constant probability for every nonsingular sparse matrix over a field
of polynomial size. The appropriate primary theorem has NO generic
leading-principal-minor hypothesis and NO characteristic restriction.

For every promised integer input M=A-lambda I of corank one with
kernel spanned by an all-nonzero sign vector, this gives a Las Vegas
algorithm using expected soft-O(n^2) BIT operations at fixed degree.
Every returned sign vector AND the claimed rational rank have a
fully deterministic soft-O(n^2)-bit verifier.

For arbitrary inputs, use a bounded number of trials and allow FAIL.
This gives a total one-sided recovery algorithm with soft-O(n^2)
expected bitwork on EVERY graph and high-probability recovery when
the qualitative RSBM simplicity theorem applies. If instead a full
exact answer on exceptional high-nullity graphs is required, a dense
exact fallback is possible, but the existing qualitative o(1) graph
failure rate does not by itself preserve an unconditional expected
soft-O(n^2) runtime for that fallback algorithm.

1. INPUT, THE PRINCIPAL MINOR, AND NORMALIZATION
------------------------------------------------
Let n denote the TOTAL matrix order, not one RSBM block size. Let

  M=A-lambda I in Z^{n x n},

where A is a simple graph adjacency matrix of maximum degree d and
lambda is a known integer. In the RSBM, d=d1+d2 and lambda=d1-d2.
Fix d and lambda for the soft-O(n^2) bit statement. Symmetry is used
only to guarantee that the chosen principal minor is nonsingular
under the promise; the modular Krylov procedure works for arbitrary
square matrices.

Promise:

  rank_Q M=n-1,    ker_Q M=span_Q{sigma},
  sigma in {+1,-1}^n.

Normalize sigma_n=1; this fixes the unavoidable global sign. Delete
the last coordinate, put k=n-1, and write

  M = [ H   a ],       h=-a.
      [ a^T c ]

Every principal (n-1)-minor is nonzero over Q. One proof is that the
adjugate of a symmetric corank-one matrix is c0 sigma sigma^T with
c0 != 0; each diagonal cofactor is c0 sigma_i^2=c0. Thus H is
nonsingular. The unique rational solution of Hx=h is exactly
(sigma_1,...,sigma_k), with no large denominators to reconstruct.

For a prime p not dividing det(H), reduce modulo p, solve Hx=h,
read residues +1 and -1 as integer signs, append sigma_n=1, and
verify M sigma=0 OVER THE INTEGERS. If balance is part of the target,
verify sum sigma_i=0 too. One successful modular nonsingularity
certificate proves rank_Q M>=n-1; the verified integer kernel vector
proves rank_Q M<=n-1. Together they certify the exact rank and the
unique sign line. Verification of signs alone would not certify rank.

2. THE SHIFTED-SEQUENCE RANK CERTIFICATE: FULL PROOF
----------------------------------------------------
Let Q be any k-by-k matrix over a field F and u,v in F^k. Form

  s_j = u^T Q^{j+1} v,     j=0,...,2k-1.                 (1)

Run the exact Berlekamp--Massey algorithm to determine the MINIMUM
linear recurrence order L of this finite sequence. An order ell
means coefficients c1,...,c_ell with

  s_j+c1 s_{j-1}+...+c_ell s_{j-ell}=0
  for every ell<=j<=2k-1.

Leading/trailing zero coefficients must be handled using the actual
shortest-register LENGTH L returned by Berlekamp--Massey. Merely
printing a padded degree-k polynomial is not a certificate.

CERTIFICATE LEMMA. If L=k, then rank_F Q=k, deterministically.

Proof. W=im(Q) is invariant under Q. Set r=dim W=rank Q and w=Qv.
Then w belongs to W, and the characteristic polynomial of Q|_W has
degree r and annihilates Q|_W by Cayley--Hamilton. Applying u^T to
that identity and all its powers gives a recurrence of order at most
r for the ENTIRE infinite sequence u^T Q^j w, j>=0. Its first 2k
terms consequently have a recurrence of order at most r. Exact
minimality of Berlekamp--Massey gives L<=r. Hence L=k forces r=k.
QED.

This argument is sound on every input, including singular matrices,
unlucky primes, bad random diagonals, zero test vectors, and high
rational nullity. Randomness affects availability, never validity.

A short noninteractive certificate for the original matrix consists
of (p,D,u,v,sigma), with p prime, D nonsingular diagonal, and
Q=D(H mod p). A deterministic checker:

  * checks that p lies in the prescribed Theta(n^2) interval, checks
    primality, residue encodings, and nonzero diagonal entries of D;
  * recomputes (1) with exact modular matvecs;
  * runs exact Berlekamp--Massey and requires L=k;
  * checks sigma in {+1,-1}^n, sigma_n=1, M sigma=0 over Z,
    and balance when requested.

Since D is invertible, the rank lemma proves H invertible modulo p.
Thus the checker proves rational rank n-1, not just some modular
kernel witness. The certificate has O(n log p) bits, and the checker
uses O(k(nnz(H)+k)) field operations and O(n) field storage.

3. WHY RANDOM DIAGONALS AND PROJECTIONS SUCCEED
-----------------------------------------------
PRIMARY INPUT (precise scope; actually inspected):
Li Chen, Wayne Eberly, Erich Kaltofen, B. David Saunders,
William J. Turner, and Gilles Villard,
  Efficient matrix preconditioners for black box linear algebra,
  Linear Algebra and its Applications 343--344 (2002), 119--146.
  https://kaltofen.math.ncsu.edu/bibliography/02/CEKSTV02.pdf
Theorem 4.2, journal p.130 / PDF p.12, states: for ANY matrix over
ANY field and diagonal entries independently uniform in a finite
set S, diagonal left multiplication is cyclic up to nilpotent blocks
with probability at least 1-k(k-1)/(2|S|). For nonsingular H and
S=F_p^*, there is no nilpotent part and Q=DH is cyclic. There is NO
leading-minor assumption, symmetry assumption, or restriction p>k.
The characteristic restriction in their Theorem 4.3 concerns an
additional squarefree conclusion, which we do not need.

Thus, conditional on det(H) != 0 modulo p,

  P_D[Q not cyclic] <= k(k-1)/(2(p-1)).                  (2)

Here cyclic means its matrix minimal polynomial has degree k.
For cyclic Q, the Krylov determinant

  det[v,Qv,...,Q^{k-1}v]

is a nonzero polynomial of total degree k in v. Its transpose analogue
in u is also nonzero. Schwartz--Zippel gives failure at most 2k/p for
independent uniform u,v in F_p^k. If both Krylov matrices are full
rank, Q invertible makes w=Qv cyclic too; the k-by-k Hankel matrix

  (s_{i+j})_{i,j=0}^{k-1}
  = [u^T;u^TQ;...;u^TQ^{k-1}] [w,Qw,...,Q^{k-1}w]

is nonsingular. No recurrence of order below k can hold on its first
2k terms, because that would make its columns dependent. Since a
k-dimensional recurrence always exists, Berlekamp--Massey returns
L=k. Consequently

  P[L<k | H invertible mod p]
  <= k(k-1)/(2(p-1)) + 2k/p.                             (3)

This proves a concrete constant trial success probability whenever
p is a sufficiently large constant times n^2. No assertion that a
fixed small prime works for a random RSBM is being used.

4. RECOVERING THE MODULAR SOLUTION FROM THE SAME RECURRENCE
-----------------------------------------------------------
Suppose the rank certificate succeeds, L=k. The monic recurrence
polynomial f(t)=f0+f1 t+...+f_k t^k, f_k=1, recovered from (1) is
then the characteristic polynomial of Q; in particular f0 != 0.
One may check f0 != 0 as an implementation consistency guard.

Here is a proof that this is exact, not a probabilistic minpoly guess.
The shortest INFINITE scalar recurrence has degree at most k, so a
finite-prefix shortest degree k forces its infinite degree to be k.
Both the controllability and observability Krylov spaces must have
full dimension k: otherwise restriction to the reachable subspace or
quotient by the unobservable invariant subspace would give a scalar
recurrence of degree below k. Their leading k-by-k Hankel matrix is
therefore nonsingular. Its k equations determine a monic degree-k
recurrence uniquely, so the Berlekamp--Massey polynomial agrees with
the infinite recurrence. That recurrence is the degree-k minimal
polynomial of Q and hence its characteristic polynomial.

Set y=D h. Cayley--Hamilton gives

  Q^{-1}y = -f0^{-1}(f1 y+f2 Qy+...+f_k Q^{k-1}y).        (4)

Evaluate the right side by Horner's rule in k-1 further sparse
matvecs, obtaining x. Check Hx=h modulo p. Check every x_i is either
1 or p-1, convert to integer signs, append 1, and verify M sigma=0
exactly. The integer verification remains mandatory even though the
promise would already imply the answer on good inputs. This ensures
one-sided soundness on arbitrary inputs as well.

For reference, recurrence-based sparse finite-field solving is
classical Wiedemann linear algebra:
Douglas H. Wiedemann, Solving sparse linear equations over finite
fields, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 32(1) (1986), 54--62.
https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1986.1057137
Primary paper PDF:
https://www.enseignement.polytechnique.fr/profs/informatique/Francois.Morain/Master1/Crypto/projects/Wiedemann86.pdf
The polynomial inverse formula and quadratic Berlekamp--Massey cost
are already in that paper. No novelty claim is made for (4).

5. DETERMINANT SIZE AND GOOD-PRIME PROBABILITY
----------------------------------------------
Put R^2=d+lambda^2. Each row of H has Euclidean norm at most R.
Under the promise, the nonzero integer T=det H satisfies

  1<=|T|<=R^k=(d+lambda^2)^{k/2},
  B:=log|T| <= (k/2) log(d+lambda^2).                     (5)

Let Q0=C n^2, and sample a prime p uniformly from [Q0,2Q0]. Here C
may be a sufficiently large constant depending on the fixed d and
lambda. For large enough n, the interval contains at least
Q0/(3 log Q0) primes (any usual quantitative prime-density bound, or
just its asymptotic consequence, suffices). At most B/log Q0 of these
primes divide T: the product of distinct dividing primes is at most
|T|. Hence

  P_p[p divides det H] <= 3B/Q0
       <= 3k log(d+lambda^2)/(2 C n^2).                 (6)

Combining (3) and (6), one trial succeeds with probability at least

  1 - 3B/Q0 - k(k-1)/(2(Q0-1)) - 2k/Q0.                (7)

For fixed degree and sufficiently large C this is at least 3/4 for
all sufficiently large n. The finitely many smaller sizes may use
exact elimination without altering the fixed-degree asymptotic bound.
All primes have O(log n) bits. There is no need to reconstruct an
O(n)-bit determinant or to use a prime larger than that determinant.

Primes dividing 2d2 in the RSBM cause a forced modular quotient-mode
collision and are automatically bad when H is the chosen cofactor.
The large-prime construction eventually avoids those fixed divisors
anyway. One can exclude them explicitly; this changes no bound.

6. PRIME GENERATION IS CHARGED
-------------------------------
One must not silently sieve up to n^4 and call that near quadratic.
The p=Theta(n^2) choice above makes several simple options legitimate.

Option A, low memory and elementary: draw uniform integers from the
specified interval and retain exactly those that are prime, using
DETERMINISTIC trial division up to sqrt(2Q0)=O_{d,lambda}(n).
Each candidate costs soft-O(n) bit operations; prime density gives
O(log n) expected candidates. Rejection sampling yields exactly the
uniform distribution on interval primes. Thus prime generation costs
expected soft-O(n) bit operations and logarithmic working memory,
well below the main quadratic work. A deterministic polynomial-log
primality test could be used instead, but is unnecessary here.

Option B: sieve through 2Q0=O_{d,lambda}(n^2), store the interval
prime list, and select uniformly. Ordinary sieve bookkeeping costs
soft-O(n^2) bit operations and O(n^2) bits. This is ALSO within the
time target, although its space is larger than the Krylov algorithm.
No primality oracle is free in either description.

For a fixed explicit outer trial budget, prime generation by rejection
still terminates almost surely and has the stated expected cost on
every input; it is independent of graph simplicity. If a strict
worst-case budget is preferred, cap candidate draws at O(log^2 n)
and return FAIL on exhaustion, at polynomially small extra failure
probability. Do not declare an untested pseudoprime to be a field.

7. BIT COMPLEXITY, STORAGE, AND TERMINATION GUARANTEES
------------------------------------------------------
For one trial:

  * exactly 2k Q-matvecs form the shifted sequence;
  * Berlekamp--Massey costs O(k^2) field operations;
  * at most k-1 additional Q-matvecs evaluate (4);
  * diagonal scaling, inner products, and updates cost O(k^2);
  * modular residual and integer sign checks are linear at fixed d;
  * prime generation is charged as above.

More generally the field cost is O(k nnz(H)+k^2). For fixed d and
lambda, nnz(H)=O(n), p has O(log n) bits, and each field operation,
including inversions by the Euclidean algorithm, costs polylog(n)
bit operations. Thus each trial costs soft-O(n^2) bits and O(n)
field elements of working storage, besides the sparse input and
short output certificate. All costs are independent of any analytic
eigenvalue separation or numerical condition number.

PROMISE LAS VEGAS VERSION: repeat with fresh p,D,u,v until the rank
certificate and exact sign check succeed. By (7), every promised
input has a constant expected number of trials, so expected bitwork
is soft-O(n^2). The output is always certified correct. On a matrix
of rational nullity at least two, this loop can NEVER succeed and
must not be advertised as an all-input terminating algorithm.

TOTAL ONE-SIDED VERSION: run t=ceil(c log n) independent trials and
return FAIL if none succeeds. On EVERY graph it has soft-O(n^2)
expected bitwork (the logarithmic number of trials is suppressed)
and every non-FAIL answer is certified. On every promised input its
failure probability is at most (1/4)^t. If the RSBM baseline proves
the promise with probability 1-q_n, q_n=o(1), the overall recovery
probability is at least

  1-q_n-(1/4)^t = 1-o(1).

This conclusion needs no quantitative q_n rate and has a valid total
algorithm. It must be phrased as a possibly-FAIL exact recovery
algorithm, not a Las Vegas rank-decider on all exceptional graphs.

TOTAL FULL-ANSWER FALLBACK VERSION: after t trials, run an exact
fraction-free nullspace/rank computation, with polynomial bit cost
F(n), on every remaining input. It is always correct and terminating.
On promised inputs, choose t large enough that F(n)(1/4)^t is at
most n^2, obtaining conditional expected soft-O(n^2) work. But over
an RSBM ensemble with only qualitative q_n=o(1), its unconditional
expected work can include q_n F(n). This need not be soft-O(n^2).
A quantitative graph failure bound, a faster exceptional-input
algorithm, or permission to return FAIL is necessary to remove that
limitation. An analytic eigengap is not necessary.

8. WHAT CAN HONESTLY BE CLAIMED FOR THE EXISTING RSBM BASELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------
For d1>=3, even block size, and the precise independent uniform
simple regular-block model of the baseline, the derived qualitative
simplicity theorem plus the TOTAL ONE-SIDED algorithm gives certified
exact recovery, up to global exchange, with probability 1-o(1), using
soft-O(n^2) expected bit operations at fixed degree. A returned
answer includes a deterministic soft-O(n^2) rank and kernel verifier.
The random prime is allowed to grow because the ALGORITHMIC
Hadamard argument (5)--(6), not a uniform-in-p RSBM mixing theorem,
controls its badness on each fixed good integer instance.

This does not resolve odd block sizes for even d1, the exact (2,1)
real-kernel question, or novelty/priority of regular-block recovery.
The algebraic ingredients are classical. The useful audit improvement
is that a claimed near-quadratic recovery algorithm need not leave
rank verification cubic or silently assume a small fixed prime works.

INDEPENDENT FINITE-FIELD SANITY CHECK
-------------------------------------
An ephemeral JavaScript check implemented modular Gaussian rank,
shifted-sequence Berlekamp--Massey, and the polynomial inverse over
F_101 for 2,000 matrices of orders 1 through 10. Half the draws were
forced singular by a duplicate row (or zero in order one). It found
963 full-complexity certificates and 1,011 singular rejections, with
zero false rank certificates and zero inverse-polynomial residual
failures. This checks implementation conventions and polynomial
orientation on small examples; the proofs above, not these samples,
establish the asymptotic and all-input soundness statements.
