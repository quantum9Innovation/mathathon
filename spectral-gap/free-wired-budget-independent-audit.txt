1. The exhaustion and all disconnected deletion cases
-------------------------------------------------------
For infinite connected G, deleting one edge gives H with at most two
components, each containing u or v. The union of the two radius-R H-balls
is finite, exhausts H and has every component attached to a retained root.
Completely exhausted finite components are correctly allowed to have no
shell vertex. No independent component without a retained vertex appears
in the elimination problem.

The free finite conductance Cf_R is nondecreasing under enlargement:
adding edges/vertices cannot reduce the minimum energy at prescribed
terminal difference one. Equivalently its reciprocal free resistance is
nonincreasing. These quantities remain bounded by the root degrees.
Their limit is the ordinary free conductance, including Cf=0 if H
separates the two roots. The equivalent flow characterization is useful:
Rf is the infimum of energy among finitely supported feasible unit flows;
every such flow is contained in one exhaustion member, and each finite
member has its usual energy-minimizing flow.

For the wired and singleton-capacity problems, a shell-zero function
extends by zero with exactly the same energy. Any edge leaving the ball
has its inside endpoint in the shell, so it contributes zero. The finite
admissible classes embed increasingly, and every finitely supported
function eventually vanishes on the shell. Thus the claimed decreasing
limits of Cw_R,p_R,q_R are correct. Optimizing the common terminal voltage
shift in Cw_R is essential; the source does so by fixing only U-V=1.
These statements also apply when one deletion component is finite, both
roots are in the same component, or a component has capacity zero.

2. Finite Kron reduction and the escape-conductance bound
----------------------------------------------------------
The one-vertex star elimination formula is correct and proves the needed
Kron reduction without an external theorem. Every elimination retains a
nonnegative-conductance Laplacian and its minimum energy on remaining
vertices. Retaining u,v and EACH shell vertex first is the right order.

In the reduced graph, let c be direct uv conductance and a,b the total
root-to-shell conductances. Free energy has the nonnegative direct term
c(U-V)^2, hence Cf_R>=c. Wired shell values zero leave exactly
 c(U-V)^2+aU^2+bV^2.
Minimizing at U-V=1 gives Cw_R=c+ab/(a+b). Fixing U=1 and minimizing
V gives p_R=a+cb/(c+b), and the analogous q_R=b+ca/(c+a).
All formulas extend continuously with every 0/0 term defined as zero.
They remain valid for an isolated retained root/finite deletion half,
for a=b=0, or for c=0 when H disconnects u,v.

The harmonic function h(x,y)=xy/(x+y) is increasing in each nonnegative
argument and continuous at (0,0). Therefore
 Cw_R-Cf_R<=h(a,b)<=h(p_R,q_R).
Passing to the established limits proves exactly
 Cw(H)-Cf(H)<=h(cap_H(u),cap_H(v)).
No convergence of the intermediate coefficients a,b,c is required.
This resolves the main potential exhaustion-consistency issue.

3. Degree control of capacities and restoration of the edge
-------------------------------------------------------------
Both roots have degree <=d-1 in H. For distance levels from either root,
with Q=d-1, the number of edges crossing h,h+1 is <=Q^(h+1). Every
positive-distance vertex has a parent and hence at most Q outward
neighbors; within-level edges contribute no radial energy. The source's
truncated radial trial function has energy at most
 (Q-1)/(1-Q^-N),
by a direct geometric sum. Consequently each singleton capacity is
<=d-2. A finite component has capacity zero and is automatically included.
Monotonicity of h then gives
 Cw(H)-Cf(H)<=(d-2)/2.

Restoring the existing unit edge adds exactly one to every energy with
terminal difference one, in both free and wired classes. Hence
 Cf(G)=1+Cf(H), Cw(G)=1+Cw(H).
With r=Rf(G), w=Rw(G), k=(d-2)/2, this gives
 1/w<=1/r+k,  0<r<=1,
so
 w>=r/(1+kr)>=(2/d)r.
The ratio calculation is also exact:
 (1+r)/(1+w)<=1+kr^2/[1+(k+1)r].
Its derivative is kr[2+(k+1)r]/[1+(k+1)r]^2>=0, so the maximum on
[0,1] is 2d/(d+2). This verifies both stronger and weaker resistance
claims. The regular tree equality case is correct: free resistance of
its existing edge is one, and the alternate wired path through infinity
has resistance 2/(d-2), giving wired edge resistance 2/d.

For d=2, both endpoint capacities in the deleted graph vanish by linear
radial trials, so w=r=1 for the infinite connected ray/path possibilities.
The refined actual-capacity statement follows by keeping h(cap_u,cap_v)
in place of the universal k. No bridge assumption enters these arguments.

4. Finite leverage versus the limiting FREE resistance
--------------------------------------------------------
For every fixed finite connected subgraph F of the rooted limit G
containing e, local convergence embeds F into all sufficiently large G_n.
A unit electrical flow in F extends by zero to a feasible flow in G_n;
its divergence remains exactly the prescribed endpoint demand. Thus
 tau_n=R_eff(G_n;e)<=R_eff(F;e).
Taking a cofinal exhaustion by F and using the free flow characterization
gives limsup tau_n<=Rf(G;e)=r. This argument is correct even when the
finite approximants are bridged and their limit has separate infinite
halves. Substituting wired resistance here would be false, as noted in
the source.

One may also verify the complementary consistency check, not needed for
the displayed upper argument: any finitely supported potential on G
transports with exactly the same energy once the ball includes all edges
incident to its support, so liminf tau_n>=Rw(G;e)=w. Thus every leverage
subsequential limit ell lies in [w,r], and the final residual log budget
is nonnegative. No inverse convergence is being assumed.

5. Compulsory WIRED local spectrum and the all-host count bound
---------------------------------------------------------------
The previously proved local Fredholm identity gives the positive trace-
class response C with Tr log(I+C)=alpha log(1+w), including spectral
bottom zero. Finite-support positive-power Gram convergence and generalized
Ritz min--max give lambda_j(C)<=liminf lambda_j(C_n) for fixed j.
Neither statement needs the bridge property.

The finite total budget is now variable:
 sum log(1+lambda_j(C_n))=alpha log(1+tau_n).
Pass to a leverage-convergent subsequence tau_n->ell<=r and subtract the
first k modes. The source's (q_n-k)_+ count inequality is valid also for
q_n<k because its right side remains nonnegative. Multiply by
 epsilon_n/log(1+epsilon_n)->1,
use the fixed-j liminf, and then send k to infinity. This proves

 limsup epsilon_n N_(epsilon_n)(C_n)
 <=alpha log[(1+ell)/(1+w)]
 <=alpha log[(1+r)/(1+w)]
 <=alpha log[2d/(d+2)].

Every finite existing unit edge has tau_n<=1, so the determinant budget
uniformly bounds all strict counts by a finite integer. The supremum count
over the nonempty finite host class is therefore attained as an integer
value. Choosing maximizing hosts at an epsilon sequence and extracting
rooted and leverage subsequences is legitimate. A finite local limit
forces eventual graph isomorphism once a ball beyond its diameter is
exposed, hence bounded counts and zero epsilon-scaled limit. Infinite
limits are covered above. Thus the full uniform conclusion is proved:

 limsup_(epsilon->0) epsilon W^all_(d,alpha)(epsilon)
                      <=alpha log[2d/(d+2)].

This is an upper theorem for doubling ANY existing unit edge of an
unweighted finite host of maximum degree <=d. The updated graph has its
one edge weight two, as intended. It does not assert this formula for
unit insertion at a nonedge, arbitrary weights, or exact finite counts.
A matching lower construction in a bridge subclass would transfer to this
larger class immediately, but that lower is outside this audit's decision.
