Horn and the real field

Li--Mathias Theorem 4.1 explicitly realizes real lower block-triangular
matrices with specified singular values and conjugation-closed
eigenvalues in prescribed order. When all prescribed eigenvalues are
positive real, every block has size one. Transposition then gives the
real upper-triangular R used in the manuscript. Repeated singular or
diagonal values are allowed. Thus the exact real-field step is valid;
it is not merely a complex Weyl--Horn argument. A pinpoint citation to
Theorem 4.1 would be slightly stronger than the present generic
Sections 3--4 citation, but the current reference is adequate.
  https://cklixx.people.wm.edu/higham.pdf

Mathematical checks of the exact rank-one theorem

1. Cyclic decomposition. The cyclic dimension is exactly the number of
   distinct eigenspaces of A with a nonzero projection of u. On each
   such eigenspace it contributes one basis direction. Hence A is
   diagonal with distinct theta_i and all u_i nonzero on the active
   subspace; basis signs make u_i positive. Its orthogonal complement
   reduces both endpoints and all their powers. The correction vanishes
   there exactly.

2. Integral formula. After the A^(-alpha/2) congruence, the entries are
   the claimed positive diagonal scaling of the Gram kernel
   integral w(t)/((theta_i+t)(theta_j+t)) dt. Its entries are finite
   for 0<alpha<1. There is no missing power of t or endpoint factor.

3. Strict total positivity. For increasing theta and t, the Cauchy
   determinant is positive, not alternating in sign. Continuous
   Cauchy--Binet over the ordered chamber needs no 1/k! factor;
   the unrestricted integral would need it. Truncation to [a,b],
   followed by a->0 and b->infinity, is legitimate: all determinant
   integrands are nonnegative, entries converge, and determinants
   are continuous. Every minor is strictly positive because every
   nonempty compact ordered chamber has positive integrand.

4. Positive eigenvalues and simplicity. Total positivity includes all
   principal minors, yielding SPD. Equivalently the active rational
   functions are independent in a positive-density measure. Every
   compound has positive entries, so Perron--Frobenius yields the
   strict spectral gaps claimed. If lambda_k=lambda_(k+1), the top
   compound eigenvalue occurs for two distinct subsets and cannot be
   simple. This excludes every nonunit collision at finite A.

5. Exact inverse existence. The parameter ball lies entirely in the
   strictly decreasing positive chamber of the fixed-sum affine space.
   The family S(x)=I+sqrt(z(x))sqrt(z(x))^T is compact SPD, and in fact
   has the fixed spectrum (1+tau,1,...,1). Uniform convergence of the
   general pivot lemma therefore applies. Ordered eigenvalues and their
   logarithms are continuous; no differentiability or eigenvector
   continuity is needed. The determinant identity puts F_delta in the
   same affine space. Uniform error below the radius makes
   x*+x-F_delta(x) a self-map of the ball. Brouwer gives exactly
   F_delta(x)=x*, at finite delta>0. The m=1 exception is handled.
   Padding by an inactive SPD block supplies any trailing unit values.

Uniformity of the general pivot lemma

The proof's final compactness sentence is valid. A compact SPD family
has uniform lower and upper spectral bounds. Every trailing principal
determinant is bounded away from zero. All nonleading terms of the
elementary symmetric polynomials carry a uniform positive power of
delta, while eigenvalue separation is uniform by min--max. The
eigenvector bounds use only uniform bounds for S and S^(-1). Therefore
the normalized power convergence is uniform, as required by Brouwer.

Spectral envelopes and strict counts

For x=log q and y=alpha log r, x<=_maj y yields
x_k <= (y_1+...+y_k)/k, and
x_(n-k+1) >= (y_(n-k+1)+...+y_n)/k.
Averaging exactly the first or last k coordinates of y preserves
decreasing order and is majorized by y, proving both sharp envelopes.
These arguments allow mixed-sign y without alteration.

For strict count >s, k eigenvalues above s force the top-k average
bound strictly above log s. Conversely strictness gives a nonzero
margin at the averaged closure target, preserved by a finite witness.
Thus the maximum over k satisfying the stated strict prefix inequality
is exact, including k=n and equality thresholds. The bottom-tail
formula for <s is the symmetric argument. No hidden attainment of a
repeated target is used. The rank-one ceiling-minus-one convention is
therefore correct.

Graph consequences

The nonunit relative input spectrum is precisely 1+gamma_i from
U^T L^dagger U. Congruencing the rank-k spectral truncation of C gives
the claimed Loewner residual bound. Endpoint-edge cyclicity on a path
is correct: v_1=v_2 and the endpoint equation imply both vanish, then
the tridiagonal recurrence forces the whole eigenvector to vanish.
Hence every positive path eigenvalue contributes to the active rank.

Optional wording improvement: use "rank at most k correction" instead
of "rank-k correction" in the graph approximation statement, since
the truncation may have smaller rank when C has fewer than k positive
eigenvalues. This is a precision issue, not a change to the bound.
