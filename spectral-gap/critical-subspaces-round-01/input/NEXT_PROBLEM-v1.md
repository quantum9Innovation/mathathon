# Proposed next target: critical spectral subspaces and resonance

Status: proposed research target, not an established result or a background execution instruction.

The eigenvalue law is now explicit. The next structural question is how the corresponding eigenvectors occupy the collective host direction and the neighbourhoods of close marked pairs.

Work first in the critical cubic zero-interaction model. For a fixed spectral level separated from zero, prove a quantitative approximation of its spectral subspace by the span of the relevant pair modes and the collective mode when present. State the result using spectral projectors or invariant subspaces, since equal pair amplitudes can have positive limiting multiplicity. A claim that every eigenvector localizes on one pair could fail under these degeneracies.

Treat the resonance phase at which a pair amplitude equals 3/sqrt(8) separately. Determine the next scale of collective-to-pair mixing, including which finite corrections and host randomness select the eigenvectors. The current decoupling norm only proves that this mixing vanishes at the eigenvalue scale. It does not identify the basis inside a limiting degenerate subspace.

Success evidence is a precise subspace theorem, or a counterexample to the proposed individual-mode interpretation, with a separate review and finite formal steps tied to actual graph objects. Preserve the current eigenvalue law and its finite formal packet. Priority must be compared with established resonant tunnelling and sparse-graph localization results before any novelty claim.
