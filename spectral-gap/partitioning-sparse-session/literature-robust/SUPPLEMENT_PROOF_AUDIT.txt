Perron--Frobenius plus Galois proposition
----------------------------------------
The equitable partition embeds the two-dimensional quotient invariant
subspace into the full adjacency space by lifting each quotient coordinate
to a constant on its class. Positive cross degrees make the nonnegative
quotient irreducible. Its Perron eigenvector is strictly positive and lifts
to a strictly positive adjacency eigenvector. Connectedness makes A
irreducible, so this lifted eigenvalue is the simple top adjacency root.

For nonsquare positive integer discriminant D, the two quotient roots
share the monic irreducible polynomial
 t^2-(a1+a2)t+a1*a2-b1*b2 in Z[t].
Each occurrence of either root in det(tI-A) belongs to a power of that
irreducible factor over Q; hence the two roots have identical algebraic
multiplicity. Simplicity of the larger root forces simplicity of the
smaller. Because A is real symmetric, its eigenspaces have those same
dimensions. No spectral separation or parity assumption occurs here.

The inequality sqrt((a1-a2)^2+4b1b2)>|a1-a2| gives
lambda_minus<min(a1,a2). Therefore(b1,-(a1-lambda_minus)) has opposite,
nonzero signs and lifts to precisely the two classes. A one-dimensional
eigenspace determines this sign partition up to an overall sign. The
proposition does not claim that all equitable partitions using different
quotients or eigenvalues are excluded.

The cube counterexample to extending this logic to integer roots is
correct. A square discriminant gives rational algebraic integers, hence
integer roots; their irreducible minimal polynomials are separate linear
factors, so Perron simplicity does not force multiplicity of the other.
For equal total degree d the roots are exactly d and d-b1-b2, confirming
that the difficult nontrivial equal-total-degree result really uses the
finite-field argument. If total degrees differ, vertex degrees identify
the classes already; the source appropriately separates this fact from
the spectral simplicity conclusion.

Connectivity input and odd block sizes
---------------------------------------
Independently inspected the actual author PDF:
 N.C.Wormald, Models of random regular graphs,
 https://users.monash.edu/~nwormald/papers/regsurvey.pdf
The model convention on PDF page3 is uniform labeled simple d-regular
graphs with nd even. Page5 restricts n to even only when d is odd.
Section2.6, PDF page13, states that fixed d>=3 gives d-connectedness
asymptotically almost surely and attributes the original results to
Bollobas and Wormald. The article also reproduces a proof and an
O(n^(2-d)) bound for fixed degree.

Thus each internal ai-regular graph with fixed ai>=3 is connected with
probability tending to one along all of its admissible sizes, including
odd sizes when ai is even. If both internal graphs are connected, any
cross graph with positive prescribed degrees supplies an edge between
them, so the whole graph is connected. This event is independent of
any geometric properties of B. The nonsquare-discriminant corollary
therefore extends correctly to all admissible sizes. For both sizes even,
the preceding integer-mode result handles integer roots, while this
proposition handles irrational roots. Since there are only two roots,
individual high-probability simplicity statements hold simultaneously
by a finite union bound.

Algebraic modular reduction
---------------------------
Take a fixed algebraic integer lambda with monic irreducible minimal
polynomial f in Z[t]. The note concerns real adjacency eigenvalues and
an integer m in the meaningful range0<=m<N. If real nullity is at least
m+1, every(N-m)-minor of A-tI is an integer polynomial g(t) with
g(lambda)=0. Minimality gives f|g in Q[t]. Polynomial division by a
monic integer f preserves integer coefficients and has zero remainder,
so in fact f|g in Z[t]. At any root lambda_p of f modulo p, reduction
therefore annihilates every such minor. The finite-field matrix has
rank<=N-m-1 and nullity>=m+1. This is a valid implication for every
realization, and never attempts to reduce an irrational real number
directly modulo p.

The Schur argument is correct. For irreducible degree>1, c=f(0) is
nonzero and there are no integer roots. If all prime divisors of all
nonzero values lay in a finite set with product P, then for integer k
 f(cPk)/c
is an integer congruent to1 modulo P. Its absolute value is greater
than one for sufficiently large |k|, and its prime divisors divide
f(cPk), hence would all belong to the finite set. Congruence1 rules out
all those primes, a contradiction. Every prime divisor of a nonzero
value f(x) gives the root x modulo p. Removing any fixed finite bad-prime
set retains unbounded root primes. Complete splitting is unnecessary.

If a separately established fixed-prime moment estimate is
E|ker_Fp(A-lambda_p I)|<=C*p^m+o_N(1), then Markov at p^(m+1)
gives limiting real-failure probability<=C/p. Sending fixed eligible
primes to infinity only after the size limit proves the stated
conclusion, provided C is independent of p as explicitly required.
This is a reduction lemma, not a proof of that moment bound for an
unrestricted frame model. The source states that limitation correctly.

Unequal diagnostic code
-----------------------
The random cross sampler is configuration rejection with prescribed
left/right degrees, so conditional on simplicity it is uniform. The
structured version is precisely a disjoint union of K_(b2,b1): every
left row has b1 neighbors and every right column has b2 neighbors.
It is selected before the internal randomness. Independent internal
regular samplers were audited previously. Relabeling preserves all
equitable relations and the planted two-level eigenvector.

The certifier fixes coordinate0 to one via an invertible principal
minor modulo one of the default primes. It tries both possible original
values at coordinate0, normalizes the two known levels accordingly,
and avoids primes that merge those residues or make their denominators
zero. An accepted two-level integer candidate has the prescribed counts
and exact full residual zero. A nonzero modular principal minor proves
real rank at leastN-1; this verified nonzero integer nullvector proves
rank at mostN-1. Thus any acceptance is an exact real nullity-one
certificate for the supplied integer input. It is not an approximate
rank or density calculation.

All supplied48 cases have at most72 vertices, small fixed degrees and
level magnitudes<=4. For p<=1019, elimination products are at most
1018^2 and dot sums below72*1018^2<7.5e7; graph/candidate residuals
are also far inside int64. The imported determinant solver's arithmetic
and pivot/sign tracking were independently checked in the previous
audit. The planted-vector comparison does not require an arbitrary sign
choice here: each case has unequal class sizes, and the certifier uses
the positive/negative counts to select the designated orientation.

Independent rerun and extra exact example
------------------------------------------
audit_supplements.py imports the actual unmodified diagnostic functions
and redirects only their output directory into this audit directory.
The rerun returned48/48 certified, and both resulting JSON files are
byte-identical to the supplied artifacts.

It also constructs a connected21-vertex simple example with block sizes
14 and7, internal circulants of degree4, cross degrees1 and2, and
Q=[[4,1],[2,4]]. The quotient polynomial is(t-4)^2-2. Using Python
integer Faddeev--LeVerrier recurrence, it computes the full exact integer
characteristic polynomial and checks that this irreducible quadratic
divides exactly once. This corroborates the proposition on a host with
an odd block size. Exact coefficients and division remainders are kept
in SUPPLEMENT_CHECKS.json. These checks do not replace the proofs or
establish historical novelty.
