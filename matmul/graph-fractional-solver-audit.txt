FRACTIONAL GRAPH UPDATES: SOLVER TRANSFER AND PRIORITY AUDIT
=========================================================

Scope: a mathematical synthesis of the existing fractional matrix-update
quadrature with nearly-linear graph Laplacian solvers. This note proves
the sparse two-endpoint implementation, including signed edge changes,
without treating an approximate linear solver as a symmetric linear map.
It does not certify novelty, finite-word stability, or the general matrix
multiplication goal. Sources were inspected on November 14--15, 2026.

1. INPUT AND PRECISE GUARANTEE
=============================
Let L and L' be weighted undirected graph Laplacians on the same N vertices.
The endpoint edge weights are nonnegative. Either endpoint may be
disconnected. Merge parallel edges and duplicate changes first, and let
k>=1 count the distinct unordered edges whose weights change by d_e!=0.
Both additions and deletions are allowed. Let M count edges in the UNION
of the two endpoints, and put E=L'-L. For fixed 0<alpha<1, beta=1-alpha,
the algorithm constructs factors P,Q with

  ||P Q^T - (L'^alpha-L^alpha)|| <= epsilon ||E||^alpha

with probability >=1-delta, in EXPECTED arithmetic work

  ~O_alpha((M+N) k),

and width O_alpha(k log^2(2k/epsilon)). Suppressed logarithms include
N,k,1/epsilon,1/delta. The main P Q^T output need not be exactly symmetric.
An exactly symmetric signed factor has at most twice that width and the
same expected arithmetic order. Dense expansion costs an additional
O(N^2 k log^2(2k/epsilon)). No base function L^alpha is supplied or
computed: the deliverable is its CORRECTION. Producing L'^alpha itself
requires a base representation or an additional initial computation.

Here k is an edge-count bound, not necessarily rank(E). For example, many
changes on a small vertex set can give rank(E)<<k. No sharp lower bound
for this unweighted-vertex-mass graph class is established in this note.

Costs use exact real arithmetic and scalar square roots. Alpha=1/2
uses only these operations together with comparisons and discrete
randomness; general fixed alpha needs scalar-power evaluation charged
as in sharp-matrix-function-updates.tex. This is not a bit-complexity or
finite-precision result. Expected work and success probability are
different assertions; the source solver does not, by its theorem alone,
give a deterministic runtime cap on the complete algorithm.

2. INTRINSIC SPARSE NORMALIZATION DESPITE CANCELLATION
===================================================
For e={u,v}, write b_e=e_u-e_v, choose either orientation, and set

  U_e=sqrt(|d_e|) b_e,  J=diag(sign d_e),  E=U J U^T.

Let c_v=sum_{e incident to v} d_e. The actual perturbation's squared
Frobenius norm is computable directly from sparse changes:

  a^2:=||E||_F^2 = sum_v c_v^2 + 2 sum_e d_e^2.

This identity includes cancellation between signed diagonal changes.
After duplicate edges are merged, every changed off-diagonal entry has
only one d_e; thus a>0 whenever k>0. Put

  b:=||U||_F^2=2 sum_e |d_e|.

Cauchy--Schwarz and the off-diagonal contribution give

  b <=2 sqrt(k) sqrt(sum_e d_e^2) <=sqrt(2k) a.

Also rank(E)<=k, hence

  ||E|| <= a <=sqrt(k)||E||.

Only O(N+k) sparse arithmetic is needed once distinct changes are sorted
or indexed; ordinary deterministic sorting adds O(k log k). No dense
QR, Gram product, eigenvalue computation, or cancellation ratio enters.

3. EXACT TWO-ENDPOINT RESOLVENT FACTORIZATION
===========================================
For every t>0 define

  R_t=(L+tI)^-1, R'_t=(L'+tI)^-1,
  V_t=R_t U, W_t=R'_t U.

The resolvent identity gives

  D_t:=R_t-R'_t=R_t E R'_t=V_t J W_t^T.                 (1)

Although its displayed factors differ, D_t is symmetric. Both endpoints
being PSD gives

  ||R_t||,||R'_t|| <=1/t,
  ||D_t|| <= min(1/t, ||E||/t^2) <= min(1/t,a/t^2).    (2)

The first term follows from -I/t <=R_t-R'_t<=I/t, not from subtracting
two independent upper norm bounds. The update's sign and endpoint
nullspaces are irrelevant. In particular an edge deletion that creates
a new connected component is allowed.

The PSD functional calculus representation gives the convergent norm
integral

  L'^alpha-L^alpha = c_alpha int_0^infty t^alpha D_t dt,
  c_alpha=sin(pi alpha)/pi>0.                          (3)

The integrand behaves as t^(alpha-1) at zero and t^(alpha-2) at infinity
by (2), so no positive lower eigenvalue or spectral norm of L is used.

4. ACTUAL COLUMN SOLVES AND A NODE ERROR BOUND
============================================
For a symmetric positive definite A=L+tI, an energy-relative solver on
right-hand side u returns vhat with

  ||vhat-A^-1u||_A <=eta ||A^-1u||_A.

Since A>=tI and u^T A^-1 u<=||u||^2/t,

  ||vhat-A^-1u||_2 <=eta ||u||/t.                      (4)

Apply the guarantee separately to every column of U at BOTH endpoints.
No shared randomness, common inverse operator, symmetry, or linearity of
the numerical solver is required. On the event that all calls succeed,

  ||Vhat-V|| <=||Vhat-V||_F <=eta sqrt(b)/t,
  ||What-W|| <=eta sqrt(b)/t,
  ||V||,||W|| <=sqrt(b)/t.

Let Dhat=Vhat J What^T. Because ||J||=1, for eta<=1,

  ||Dhat-D|| <=(2eta+eta^2)b/t^2 <=3eta b/t^2.         (5)

Indeed expand (V+dV)J(W+dW)^T. This is the entire solve perturbation
argument. There is no Woodbury core inverse, no near-singular small
matrix, and no computation of U^T R U. Hence no O(Nk^2+k^3) core setup
or signed-factor transformation is hidden in the main construction.

5. QUADRATURE AND ACCURACY BUDGET
===============================
Normalize t=a exp(y). Define

  F(y)=c_alpha (a exp(y))^(alpha+1) D_(a exp(y)).

For any fixed d<pi this is analytic in |Im y|<d. If t=re^(i theta)
with |theta|<=d, then |lambda+t|>=c_d(lambda+r), lambda>=0,
where c_d>0 depends only on d. Resolvent bounds and their difference
identity therefore give the uniform complex-strip envelope

  ||F(y)|| <=C_(alpha,d) a^alpha
       min(exp(alpha Re y),exp(-beta Re y)).            (6)

This analyticity and envelope are the same hypotheses used in the
existing fractional-update quadrature. A logarithmic trapezoid with
spacing h=Theta_alpha(1/log(1/rho)), retaining nodes between

  t_min/a=Theta_alpha(rho^(1/alpha)),
  t_max/a=Theta_alpha(rho^(-1/beta)),

has q=O_alpha(log^2(1/rho)) nodes and exact-resolvent quadrature error
at most C_alpha rho a^alpha. For completeness: the analytic-strip
trapezoid error is bounded by the integrals on the two boundary lines,
divided by exp(2pi d/h)-1; (6) bounds those integrals by C a^alpha.
The two retained-grid tails are geometric series and are bounded by
C a^alpha exp(alpha y_min) and C a^alpha exp(-beta y_max).
Choose h and the two integer cutoffs with constant budget slack.

For the same positive physical weights w_j=c_alpha h t_j^(alpha+1),
(5) bounds the solve contribution by

  sum_j w_j ||Dhat_j-D_j||
   <=3c_alpha eta b h sum_j t_j^(alpha-1)
   <=C_alpha eta (b/a) a^alpha rho^(-beta/alpha).

The last inequality is another geometric-series estimate. Taking

  eta <=c_alpha' rho^(1/alpha)/sqrt(2k)                 (7)

makes this at most C_alpha rho a^alpha, by Section 2. In particular

  log(1/eta)=O_alpha(log(2k/epsilon))

after choosing rho=epsilon/[C_alpha''(k+1)]. Since
a^alpha<=k^(alpha/2)||E||^alpha and
k^(alpha/2)/(k+1)<=1, all quadrature and solve error budgets can be
chosen to sum to epsilon||E||^alpha.

Actual node/weight realization uses the constructive scalar procedures
of sharp-matrix-function-updates.tex. Alternatively for alpha=1/2,
choose h=log(2)/H for a power-of-two integer H of order log(1/rho).
Then t_j=a*2^(j/H) and t_j^(alpha+1) need only repeated positive square
roots, multiplication and division. Approximate fixed constants
log(2),1/pi by convergent scalar series to the budgeted accuracy.
All scalar work is polynomial in log(1/rho), not in a spectral ratio.

The optimized Gaussian rule in the existing sharp-update paper can
replace the uniform grid. Its node locations satisfy |log(t_j/a)|=O(L)
and coefficient mass poly(L), L=log(1/rho). Equation (5) then requires
only eta=exp(-O_alpha(L))/sqrt(2k). Thus the optimized node leading
coefficient transfers, but a claim of SHARP graph-specific compression
requires a graph-specific lower bound; the arbitrary-PSD lower theorem
alone does not supply one.

6. OUTPUT FACTORS AND COMPLETE COST
==================================
Concatenate the q blocks

  P_j=sqrt(w_j) Vhat_j J, Q_j=sqrt(w_j) What_j.

Then P Q^T=sum_j w_j Dhat_j, width at most kq, and forming every factor
entry costs O(Nkq). The operator error bound applies although this
approximation is not exactly symmetric. If exact symmetry is required,
replace each node by its symmetric part. The identity

  (V J W^T+W J V^T)/2
   =[V+W,V-W] diag(J/4,-J/4) [V+W,V-W]^T               (8)

gives an explicit real signed factor at width 2kq, in O(Nkq) arithmetic.
Symmetrization cannot increase error to the symmetric target because
|| (Z+Z^T)/2 ||<=||Z||. This symmetric approximation is generally signed;
it is not asserted to be the Laplacian of a graph or a PSD correction.

There are 2kq positive-definite SDD solves, each with tolerance (7).
The sparsity of either shifted endpoint is O(M+N). Therefore standard
nearly-linear solvers give expected total

  ~O_alpha((M+N) k q log(1/eta))
   =~O_alpha((M+N)k),

including factor materialization and sparse normalization. Dense output
adds O(N^2 kq), with O(N^2) storage if kept. For fixed k and inverse-
polynomial epsilon, the full dense CORRECTION takes ~O_alpha(N^2).
This does not compute arbitrary matrix products in quadratic time.

7. COMMON VERTEX MASS EXTENSION AND ITS EXTRA PARAMETER
=====================================================
For a common positive diagonal mass matrix H, one may instead consider
A=H^-1/2 L H^-1/2 and A'=H^-1/2 L' H^-1/2. Shifted actions are
implemented by

  (A+tI)^-1=H^1/2 (L+tH)^-1 H^1/2.

The inner matrix is SDD, and the energy error transports isometrically
under this change of coordinates. Replace U_e by
sqrt(|d_e|)H^-1/2 b_e, and compute a=||A'-A||_F sparsely. Then all
preceding formulas hold with b=||U||_F^2. Define

  chi=max(1,b/a).

Use eta<=c_alpha rho^(1/alpha)/chi. The work acquires logarithmic
dependence on chi. It is WRONG to use b/a<=sqrt(2k) for arbitrary
signed mass-weighted changes: diagonal cancellations and extreme masses
can violate that bound. For changes of one sign, b=|tr(A'-A)|<=sqrt(k)a,
so the extra mass-dependent parameter disappears. A different mass at
each endpoint, as in degree-normalizing each updated graph separately,
is not covered by this common-mass argument.

8. PRIMARY SOLVER SOURCE AND CONFIDENCE SCOPE
===========================================
Ioannis Koutis, Gary L. Miller, Richard Peng,
"A nearly-m log n time solver for SDD linear systems," FOCS 2011.
Primary complete PDF inspected:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.4842

Theorem 4.6 (page 11 in the PDF) states relative energy-norm solution
accuracy in expected ~O(m log n log(1/eta)) time. Lemma 4.3 (page 9)
explicitly covers A=L_G+D for nonnegative diagonal D; hence L+tI and
L+tH are in scope. Lemma 4.5 (page 10) gives a good BuildChain(G,p)
with probability >=1-p; its proof on page 11 records logarithmic
dependence on 1/p. However, an earlier sampling theorem in that PDF
restricts its failure parameter to Omega(1/n). For a fully explicit
arbitrary-delta statement, it is enough to use constant-failure solvers
and the following elementary amplification, rather than extrapolate
that intermediate theorem's parameter range.

For one right-hand side run an independent constant-success solver
s=O(log(2kq/delta)) times, with requested energy accuracy eta/3.
Choose s odd. For each candidate compute its median A-energy distance
to all candidates, including itself; select a candidate minimizing
this median. If a strict majority of candidates satisfy the requested
guarantee, each good candidate has median distance at most 2r,
r=(eta/3)||A^-1u||_A. The selected candidate therefore lies within
distance 2r of a strict majority. That set intersects the good majority,
so its distance to the exact answer is at most 3r=eta||A^-1u||_A.
An ordinary binomial tail makes the majority event hold with failure
<=delta/(2kq). All distances are computable: their squares are
(x_i-x_j)^T A(x_i-x_j), using sparse A multiplication and comparisons.
Pairwise distance work is O((M+N)s^2) per column, which is absorbed
by the displayed soft-O bound. This is still expected arithmetic
because the source solver has expected work. For the mass extension,
perform the energy-distance selection in the equivalent L+tH system.
A union bound now proves all solve accuracies simultaneously.

One can reuse a good chain for several right-hand sides and the
diagonal shifts permitted by Lemma 4.3, but reuse is unnecessary for
the displayed asymptotic work.

The EXPECTED qualifier in the stated work follows the published
Theorem 4.6. Merely union-bounding successful solve events does not
upgrade an expected runtime bound to a deterministic cap. A separately
proved high-probability runtime cap, or explicitly charged truncation,
would be needed for that stronger statement. The current theorem does
not claim it. No dependence on ||L||/||E||, lambda_2(L), or endpoint
weight ratio appears in the exact-arithmetic energy-error transfer.

9. BOUNDED PRIORITY COMPARISON
=============================
Beckermann, Kressner, Schweitzer, "Low-rank updates of matrix functions"
(2018), primary arXiv record and author-hosted complete PDF:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.03045
https://sma.epfl.ch/~anchpcommon/publications/rank_one.pdf
Their general task is already f(A+D)-f(A), with small-rank D, via
tensorized Krylov spaces; graph centrality/communicability applications
are explicit. Thus low-rank graph-function updating itself is classical.

Beckermann, Cortinovis, Kressner, Schweitzer, "Low-rank updates of matrix
functions II: Rational Krylov methods" (2021):
https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11501
Already supplies rational-update exactness and error mechanisms.
The resolvent product identity (1) is classical and is not a novelty
claim. The cancellation-normalized graph implementation and its
uniform graph-solver cost are the specific deductions being checked.

Benzi, Simunec, "Rational Krylov methods for fractional diffusion
problems on graphs" (2021):
https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08389
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10543-021-00881-0
Treats fractional graph diffusion and rational shifted solves, including
handling the zero eigenvalue by shifts or projections. Its target is
f(L)b rather than an intrinsically normalized full low-rank correction
under signed edge changes. This difference narrows the comparison; it
does not establish that our synthesis is globally new.

The 2023 Charalambides--Hero graph-AMM connection is also antecedent:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08581
It treats randomized matrix multiplication for graph sparsification;
the present deduction concerns edge-update corrections to fractional
powers. "Combining matrix multiplication and spectral graphs" alone
cannot be used as a novelty claim.

Queries included combinations of "fractional graph Laplacian",
"low-rank updates", "SDD", "edge updates", and "fractional power".
No primary theorem with the exact combined signed-update, intrinsic
epsilon||E||^alpha, condition-independent graph construction guarantee
was located. This is a bounded search, not an exhaustive priority
certificate. Routine-composition priority risk remains substantial.

10. AUDIT CONCLUSION
====================
The two-endpoint proof avoids assumptions about linear/symmetric
approximate inverse maps, rank-k small-core conditioning, endpoint
connectivity, supplied base fractional powers, and represented-factor
cancellation. Its graph-specific cancellation bound is essential to
uniform polylogarithmic precision. The theorem gives a concrete
near-linear thin correction and near-quadratic full correction for
fixed update size. General AMM, external novelty, numerical stability,
and sharp compression lower bounds restricted to ordinary graphs
remain separate questions.
