EXCESS-CORANK LAW FOR NONBIPARTITE REGULAR RANDOM LIFTS
=====================================================
Independent derivation, 2026-11-15. graph_breakthrough.
Only this new file is written. The earlier moment proof remains frozen.

THEOREM
-------
Let B be the adjacency matrix of a fixed connected NONBIPARTITE simple
d-regular base graph, d>=3. Let A_N be the adjacency matrix of its random
N-lift, with an independent uniform perfect matching over each base edge.
Fix an ODD prime p and lambda in F_p, and put

 t=dim_Fp ker(B-lambda I),
 K_N=dim_Fp ker(A_N-lambda I)-t.

Every positive lift size N is allowed. Then K_N is a nonnegative integer,
and its distribution converges in total variation to the probability law

 pi_p(k)=c_p / product_(j=1,...,k)(p^j-1),  k=0,1,2,...,             (1)

where empty products are one and

 c_p=product_(j>=1, j odd)(1-p^(-j))
    =1/product_(j>=1)(1+p^(-j)).                                   (2)

Equivalently,

 pi_p(k)=c_p p^(-k(k+1)/2)/product_(j=1,...,k)(1-p^(-j)).            (3)

Thus the finite-field excess corank has the classical limiting law of a
uniform symmetric matrix over F_p. In particular, the probability that
the full lambda-eigenspace is exactly the fiber-constant old eigenspace
tends to c_p. If t=0, this is the limiting nonsingularity probability.

No distributional convergence rate in N, simultaneous-prime statement,
cokernel/group law, characteristic-two assertion, bipartite-base
extension, or historical-priority certificate is asserted.

1. SHARP ENOUGH MOMENT GROWTH AND TAILS
--------------------------------------
Define the finite positive constant

 Z_p=product_(j>=1)(1+p^(-j)),   C_p=2Z_p.

Its convergence follows from sum_j p^(-j)<infinity. For r>=1,

 F_r=2 p^(r(r-1)/2) product_(j=1,...,r-1)(1+p^(-j))
     <=C_p p^(r(r-1)/2).                                          (5)

Also F_0=1. If K is ANY nonnegative integer random variable satisfying
E p^(rK)=F_r for all r>=0, Markov at r=k gives, for k>=1,

 P(K>=k)<=p^(-k^2)F_k
          <=C_p p^(-k(k+1)/2).                                  (6)

The same upper bound with C_p is valid at k=0. This quadratic tail
decay will force uniqueness of the measure on the discrete support.
For a sequence with moments converging as in (4), equation (6) is a
bound on any subsequential limiting law; no uniform-in-growing-k or
uniform-in-growing-r use of the source theorem is required.

2. DISCRETE p-MOMENT DETERMINACY BY AN ENTIRE-FUNCTION ARGUMENT
-------------------------------------------------------------
Lemma. There is at most one probability law on the nonnegative integers
whose p-power moments are all F_r.

Proof. Suppose rho and rho' are two such laws, and put

 a_k=rho({k})-rho'({k}),
 f(z)=sum_(k>=0) a_k z^k.

By (6),

 |a_k|<=2C_p p^(-k(k+1)/2).                                      (7)

Thus the series defines an entire function, and all moment evaluations
are absolutely convergent. Equality of moments gives

 f(p^r)=0 for every integer r>=0.                                (8)

Assume f is not identically zero. Let m be its finite order of vanishing
at zero, so a_m!=0. For n>=0 choose

 R_n=p^(n+1/2),   M_f(R)=max_(|z|=R)|f(z)|.

By (7) and completing the square,

 M_f(R_n)
 <=2C_p sum_(k>=0) p^(-k(k+1)/2+(n+1/2)k)
 =2C_p p^(n^2/2) sum_(k>=0) p^(-(k-n)^2/2)
 <=C'_p p^(n^2/2),                                               (9)

where C'_p=2C_p sum_(j in Z)p^(-j^2/2) is independent of n.

On the other hand, apply Jensen's formula to f(z)/z^m, whose value at
zero is a_m. The n+1 known nonzero zeros 1,p,...,p^n lie strictly inside
the circle of radius R_n. Other interior zeros only increase the lower
bound. Since the circular average of log|f| is at most log M_f,

 log M_f(R_n)
 >=log|a_m|+m log R_n+sum_(r=0,...,n)log(R_n/p^r).

The zero sum is exactly ((n+1)^2/2)log p. Therefore

 M_f(R_n)>=|a_m| p^(m(n+1/2)+(n+1)^2/2).                         (10)

The usual limiting form of Jensen's formula also handles any unrelated
zeros on the chosen circle; the specified zeros are strictly interior.
Comparing (9) and (10) yields

 C'_p>=|a_m| p^((m+1)n+(m+1)/2),

which is impossible as n tends to infinity. Thus f is identically zero,
all a_k vanish, and rho=rho'. This proves the lemma.

The linear gap between the exponents in (9) and (10) is essential.
One cannot replace this argument by an unverified general statement
that arbitrary p-power moments determine a measure.

3. EXPLICIT CONSTRUCTION AND NORMALIZATION OF THE LIMIT LAW
----------------------------------------------------------
Set q=p^(-1) in (0,1), and define

 b_0=1,
 b_k=q^(k(k+1)/2)/product_(j=1,...,k)(1-q^j),
 A(z)=sum_(k>=0)b_k z^k.

The coefficient ratio b_k/b_(k-1)=q^k/(1-q^k) makes A entire. It also
gives b_k(1-q^k)=q^k b_(k-1), hence the exact functional identity

 A(z)=(1+qz)A(qz).                                                (11)

Iterating and using A(q^M z)->A(0)=1 uniformly on compact sets gives

 A(z)=product_(j>=1)(1+q^j z).                                   (12)

This is a direct proof of the relevant Euler identity; the infinite
product converges uniformly on compact sets. In particular A(1)=Z_p.
The probabilities pi_p(k)=b_k/A(1) are nonnegative and sum to one.

For fixed r>=0,

 sum_(k>=0)pi_p(k)p^(rk)=A(p^r)/A(1)
 =product_(j>=1)(1+p^(r-j))/product_(j>=1)(1+p^(-j))
 =product_(h=0,...,r-1)(1+p^h)=F_r.                              (13)

The cancellation of the two convergent product tails is valid for each
fixed r; at r=0 the ratio is one. Thus the constructed law has precisely
the required moments, and Section 2 proves it is their unique law.

Finally,

 1/A(1)=product_(j>=1)(1-q^j)/product_(j>=1)(1-q^(2j))
       =product_(j>=1, j odd)(1-q^j)=c_p,

and b_k=1/product_(j=1,...,k)(p^j-1). This proves (1)--(3).
An equivalent probability-generating function is

 E z^K=product_(j>=1)(1+p^(-j)z)/(1+p^(-j)).                       (14)

For example, this is the law of a sum of independent Bernoulli variables
with respective success probabilities 1/(p^j+1), j>=1. That sum is finite
almost surely because the success probabilities are summable.

4. FROM CONVERGING MOMENTS TO TOTAL-VARIATION CONVERGENCE
--------------------------------------------------------
The moment r=1 in (4) bounds E p^(K_N) for all sufficiently large N.
Markov therefore makes the laws of K_N tight on the nonnegative integers.
Every sequence of lift sizes has a subsequence along which the point
probabilities converge to some probability law rho.

For each fixed r>=0 the NEXT moment in (4) implies uniform integrability:

 E[p^(r K_N) 1_(K_N>L)]
 <=p^(-L) E p^((r+1)K_N) -> 0 uniformly in sufficiently large N
 as L tends to infinity.                                        (15)

For r=0 the same argument controls tail mass. By first taking a finite
sum over k<=L and then sending L to infinity, the subsequential law rho
has E_rho p^(rK)=F_r for every fixed r. This does not interchange a
growing moment order with the limit in N. Determinacy identifies rho
with pi_p. Every subsequential limit is therefore pi_p, so the full
sequence of point probabilities converges to (1).

On a countable discrete space, pointwise convergence to a probability
law implies total-variation convergence: for any L, the sum of absolute
probability differences on k<=L tends to zero, and the two tails can be
made arbitrarily small using tightness and the limiting law's tail.
Thus

 (1/2)sum_(k>=0)|P(K_N=k)-pi_p(k)| -> 0.                          (16)

This completes the theorem.

5. INDEPENDENT SYMMETRIC-MATRIX COMPARISON
-----------------------------------------
For clarity, the identification with the uniform symmetric ensemble also
follows from an elementary exact moment count, without the published
finite-size distribution formula. Let S_M be uniformly symmetric over
F_p and let L_M=dim ker S_M. For a fixed M-by-r matrix X of rank h,
the equation S_M X=0 imposes

 Mh-h(h-1)/2

independent linear conditions on the symmetric bilinear form, so it has
probability p^(-Mh+h(h-1)/2). The number of rank-h such X is

 [r choose h]_p product_(j=0,...,h-1)(p^M-p^j).

Consequently

 E p^(rL_M)
 =sum_(h<=min(r,M))[r choose h]_p p^(h(h-1)/2)
                       product_(j=0,...,h-1)(1-p^(j-M))
 ->sum_(h=0,...,r)[r choose h]_p p^(h(h-1)/2)=F_r.                 (17)

The last equality follows from the Gaussian-binomial recurrence, as
derived in the frozen moment note. Applying Sections 1--4 to L_M proves
its total-variation limit is the same pi_p. If instead a symmetric form
is constrained to annihilate a fixed t-dimensional subspace, it descends
to a uniform form on the quotient; its corank is t+L_(M-t). This comparison
does not require that the fixed subspace be nondegenerate for any chosen
dot product. It is a comparison of corank laws, not an assertion of a
matrix-level distributional identity for the graph lift.

6. SCOPE, DEPENDENCIES, AND REVIEW
----------------------------------
The entire graph-specific analytic burden remains precisely the frozen
all-r theorem (4). Its uniform-phase rank, phase separation, exact scalar
section, and leakage arguments are neither enlarged nor silently replaced
here. This new deduction only needs each fixed moment separately and
uses r+1 to pass moment r to a subsequential limit.

The assumptions NONBIPARTITE, simple connected fixed base, d>=3, odd
fixed prime, and scalar target are inherited unchanged. In particular,
possible bipartite or characteristic-two variants need separate moment
theorems. Arbitrary nonscalar intertwiner targets do not inherit this
corank distribution. No real-eigenspace recovery claim follows by
identifying a finite-field distribution with an analytic spectral law.
