A mandatory relative outlier and the bounded-degree obstruction
==============================================================
Date: November 15, 2026. Authors of derivation: root and spectral_audit.
Status: complete proof; separate independent review still desirable.
The matrix lemma was sharpened independently through the same scalar
resolvent Jensen step. No historical-novelty claim is made.

1. General matrix statement
----------------------------
Let 0<alpha<1, A positive definite, u nonzero, B=A+uu^T. Define

 eta=||u||^2,      a=(u^T A u)/eta,
 tau=u^T A^-1 u,
 C=A^(-alpha/2)(B^alpha-A^alpha)A^(-alpha/2).

Then

 lambda_max(C) >= (1+eta/a)^alpha-1.                  (1)

The same result holds for PSD endpoints with common kernel when u is
orthogonal to that kernel: simply restrict to the common positive support.
This involves no global condition number or upper spectral bound on A.

Proof. Set z(t)=u^T(A+tI)^-1u. By the spectral theorem and scalar Jensen
for x->1/(x+t), z(t)>=eta/(a+t). The fractional difference identity is

 D=B^alpha-A^alpha
   =c_alpha integral_0^infty t^alpha (A+tI)^-1uu^T(A+tI)^-1
                                       /(1+z(t)) dt,
 c_alpha=sin(pi alpha)/pi.

Therefore

 u^T D u=c_alpha integral_0^infty t^alpha z(t)^2/(1+z(t)) dt.

The scalar function z^2/(1+z) is increasing for z>=0. Applying Jensen's
lower bound inside this positive integral gives

 u^T D u >= c_alpha eta^2 integral_0^infty
                       t^alpha/[(a+t)(a+eta+t)] dt
          = eta[(a+eta)^alpha-a^alpha].

The last equality is the scalar divided-difference identity for x^alpha.
All integrals converge since 0<alpha<1.

On the other hand scalar concavity of x^alpha in the spectral measure of
u gives u^T A^alpha u<=eta a^alpha. The maximum relative eigenvalue has
the generalized Rayleigh characterization

 lambda_max(C)=sup_(x!=0) x^T D x/(x^T A^alpha x).

Testing x=u proves (1). Equivalently one may test the ordinary Rayleigh
quotient of C at A^(alpha/2)u. Both bounds become equalities whenever
u is an eigenvector of A, so the general matrix inequality is sharp.

2. Lost logarithmic budget
---------------------------
Every correction eigenvalue is nonnegative and

 sum_j log(1+lambda_j(C))=S:=alpha log(1+tau).

Suppose lambda_max(C)>=d0>0 and put S0=log(1+d0). For every
0<epsilon<d0, if m eigenvalues exceed epsilon, then

 m <= 1+(S-S0)/log(1+epsilon).                         (2)

Indeed one of them contributes at least S0 to the logarithmic budget,
and every other counted eigenvalue contributes strictly more than
log(1+epsilon). The weak bound (2) includes the m=1,S=S0 endpoint.
Consequently, for any class with fixed S and a uniform positive d0,

 limsup_(epsilon->0) epsilon * sup N_epsilon(C) <= S-S0. (3)

Jensen for x^-1 also gives tau>=eta/a, so S>=S0 for the d0 in (1);
there is no negative-budget inconsistency in the matrix statement.

3. A unit edge insertion at fixed resistance one
-------------------------------------------------
Let L be the ordinary combinatorial Laplacian of a finite simple connected
unweighted graph, let p,q be nonadjacent vertices, and insert the unit edge
pq. Set u=e_p-e_q, so eta=2, and assume its resistance in the HOST graph
is exactly one, hence tau=1. On the common support 1-perp,

 a=[deg(p)+deg(q)]/2.

If the host maximum degree is at most Delta, then a<=Delta, and (1) yields

 lambda_max(C_alpha) >= (1+2/Delta)^alpha-1.           (4)

The total logarithmic budget is alpha log2. Equations (2)--(3) imply

 limsup_(epsilon->0) epsilon *
   sup_(hosts max degree<=Delta, insertion resistance=1)
           N_epsilon(C_alpha)
 <= alpha log[2Delta/(Delta+2)].                     (5)

For the relevant nonempty classes Delta>=2. In particular Delta=3 gives
upper coefficient alpha log(6/5), strictly below the unrestricted-degree
coefficient alpha log2 from the unweighted bipartite universality theorem.
The supremum may be restricted further to bipartite hosts and updates;
the same upper bound continues to apply. No matching bounded-degree lower
construction is asserted here.

4. Doubling an existing unit bridge
-------------------------------------
If pq is an existing unit-conductance edge, and that conductance is doubled,
then u=e_p-e_q, eta=2, and

 a=[deg(p)+deg(q)+2]/2<=Delta+1.

If the edge is a bridge, its initial resistance and leverage are exactly one.
Thus

 lambda_max(C_alpha)>=(1+2/(Delta+1))^alpha-1,

and its worst strict-count leading coefficient is at most

 alpha log[2(Delta+1)/(Delta+3)].                    (6)

For a nonbridge, leverage is tau=R_eff(p,q)<=1; the sharper applicable
budget is alpha log(1+tau). Subtracting the same outlier budget gives
an upper coefficient no larger than (6), whenever the family is nonempty.
For fixed Delta=3, the bridge-doubling coefficient is at most
alpha log(4/3), again strictly below alpha log2.

5. Consequence for the universality theorem
--------------------------------------------
The unweighted bipartite closure theorem allows unbounded degree. Equations
(4)--(5) show that this is not merely an artifact of its complete-bipartite
cell proof. At fixed maximum degree, every response retains one outlier of
uniform positive size, so one cannot approximate arbitrary long lists whose
product is 2^alpha and whose entries are all arbitrarily close to one.
Their omitted logarithmic mass cannot disappear into arbitrarily many tiny
relative corrections without paying the nonzero outlier budget.

The natural remaining problem is the sharp bounded-degree leading count
coefficient. The bounds (5)--(6) need not be sharp for graph edge vectors:
Jensen equality requires u to be an exact Laplacian eigenvector, which may
be incompatible with the insertion geometry at Delta>=3. Additional local
walk constraints could force a larger outlier or multiple mandatory modes.
This note supplies a rigorous obstruction, not that full characterization,
a new algorithm, a finite-precision theorem, or a novelty certificate.
