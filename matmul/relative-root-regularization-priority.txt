2. SHORT EXACT REGULARIZATION DERIVATION
========================================
Let A,B>=0, E=B-A, rank(E)<=r, and max(||A||,||B||)<=Lambda.
Put Delta=sqrt(B)-sqrt(A), d=||Delta||, and assume a known d0 with
0<d0<=d. The zero-update case is removed first as usual. Let
0<eps<1 and define

  gamma=(eps*d0/16)^2,
  A_gamma=A+gamma I, B_gamma=B+gamma I,
  Delta_gamma=sqrt(B_gamma)-sqrt(A_gamma).               (2.1)

Both regularized endpoints are SPD and their difference is STILL E,
so its rank is unchanged. For any PSD S, spectral calculus gives

  ||sqrt(S+gamma I)-sqrt(S)||<=sqrt(gamma).

Therefore

  ||Delta_gamma-Delta||<=2sqrt(gamma)=eps*d0/8,
  ||Delta_gamma||<=(1+eps/8)d.                           (2.2)

Because 0<=sqrt(A),sqrt(B)<=sqrt(Lambda) I, we have d<=sqrt(Lambda),
and hence gamma<=Lambda/256. Also -Lambda I<=E<=Lambda I implies
||E||<=Lambda. The SDA parameter for A_gamma,B_gamma satisfies

  kappahat_gamma
   <=1+2sqrt(2Lambda+gamma)/sqrt(gamma)
   <=1+3sqrt(Lambda)/sqrt(gamma)
   <=49sqrt(Lambda)/(eps*d0).                           (2.3)

Apply (1.1) with j=1 and choose

  l=ceil[log(8/eps)*log(4*kappahat_gamma)/pi^2].          (2.4)

By best low-rank approximation there is F_gamma of rank at most r*l
(or the full dimension, if smaller) with

  ||F_gamma-Delta_gamma||<=(eps/2)||Delta_gamma||.

Combining with (2.2) gives

  ||F_gamma-Delta||
    <=eps*[1/2+eps/16+1/8]*d
    <=(11/16)eps*d < eps*d.                             (2.5)

Equations (2.3),(2.4) show that the rank is

  O(r log(C/eps)[log(C/eps)+log(sqrt(Lambda)/d0)]).       (2.6)

Thus the precise singular-endpoint rank ORDER is already a short
corollary of the published result. With the computable intrinsic scale
a=||E||_F, d0=a/(2sqrt(r Lambda)), rho=4r Lambda/a, one has
sqrt(Lambda)/d0=rho/(2sqrt(r)), so (2.6) is exactly

  O(r L[L+logrho]),   L=log(C/eps).                     (2.7)

For a one-sign perturbation, Delta_gamma has that same sign by operator
monotonicity. Its best spectral truncation can preserve the sign.
Thus even one-sign approximate-factor EXISTENCE at this rank order
does not become novel merely by allowing singular original endpoints.

This argument asserts neither a new best constant nor a newly printed
theorem in SDA. It establishes that our stated rank order is obtained
by a short application of old theorems, defeating a novelty inference
based only on SDA's literal SPD hypothesis.

3. REGULARIZED CLASSICAL ADI ALSO HAS THE SAME RAW BACKEND
=========================================================
The conclusion is not limited to a nonconstructive truncated-SVD rank
corollary. Let P_gamma=sqrt(A_gamma), Q_gamma=sqrt(B_gamma). Then

  P_gamma Delta_gamma+Delta_gamma Q_gamma=E,             (3.1)

and both positive root spectra lie in
[sqrt(gamma),sqrt(Lambda+gamma)]. Classical low-rank Sylvester ADI
applies directly to this separated interval. An entirely elementary
version uses the dyadic pole grid

  tau_i=2^i sqrt(gamma),
  i=0,...,h, 2^h sqrt(gamma)>=sqrt(Lambda+gamma),

repeated p times with 3^(-p)<=eps/8. Its product R has norm at most
eps/8 on BOTH root spectra. The familiar exact ADI identity gives

  Delta_gamma-F_k=R(P_gamma)Delta_gamma R(Q_gamma),
  rank(F_k)<=kr,
  ||Delta_gamma-F_k||<=(eps/8)^2||Delta_gamma||.           (3.2)

There are k=O(L[L+log(sqrt(Lambda)/d0)]) poles. This supplies the
same rank order without any low-low argument. It is classical ADI on
regularized endpoints; dyadic poles are simply a coarse explicit
choice rather than optimal Zolotarev poles.

No regularized root needs to be given as numerical input. The already
proved Stieltjes identity becomes

 (sqrt(S+gamma I)+tau I)^(-1)
  =(1/pi) integral_0^infinity
      sqrt(t)/(t+tau^2) * (S+(gamma+t)I)^(-1) dt.         (3.3)

For S=A, every physical solve is the existing raw shifted A solve at
u=gamma+t. For S=B it is the existing A solve plus the same small
Woodbury core at u. The scalar identity-shift never requires an
N-column raw factor or a fresh dense Gram. One OSE of X continues to
control every shift and adaptive RHS on its same event.

The physical shift lower bound is now even simpler:

  u/a >= gamma/a = eps^2/(256rho).                      (3.4)

The upper pole is at most 2sqrt(Lambda+gamma), and internal Stieltjes
nodes have |log(t/tau^2)|=O(log(C/eta)); thus all precisions still
involve only logarithms of eps,rho and the node tolerance. The common
B inverse is requested literally by

  nu_A<=nu_B/[100(1+a/u)^4].                            (3.5)

The same common-operator/ordered-product proof from the frozen note
propagates inexact actions. For example keep
eta=eps^2/[10^6 rho(k+1)^2]. The minimum ADI pole is
sqrt(gamma)=eps*d0/16, so the previous relaxed sum bound gives

  ||Fhat_k-F_k||
    <=72a(k+1)^2 eta/sqrt(gamma)
    =(1152/10^6)eps*d0.                                (3.6)

Equations (2.2),(3.2),(3.6) leave ample room below eps*d. The complete
cost remains, for H=O(L+logrho+logr+1),

  tilde O(Nmr*H^5+m^3*H^4),                            (3.7)

with dimension/confidence logarithms as before. Symmetrization gives
at most2kr signed columns with no error increase. Under the existing
diagonal-Gram sandwich the same shifted coarse inverse D+uI is
diagonal, so the deterministic no-m^3 backend also remains valid.

This does not prove that the exact fully charged raw-factor theorem
was stated previously. It does prove that its singular-endpoint part
and rank order can be combined from regularization, classical ADI and
the already established raw shifted-solve machinery. The novelty
burden cannot be met by pointing to the SPD premise alone.
