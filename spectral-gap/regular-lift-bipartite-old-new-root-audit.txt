1. MODEL, OLD DIMENSION, AND WHAT THE COMPANION COUNTS

Write B=[0 T;T^T 0]. A connected regular bipartite base has equally many
vertices on its two sides: m each. Hence T is square, and the lift has
A_N=[0 X;X^T 0] with X square of order mN. This equality is substantive.

Fix an odd prime p and monic irreducible g of degree r over Fp, with
companion R. Set Z_g(A)=#{Y:AY=YR}=|ker g(A)| and
h=dim_Fp ker g(B). The companion identity follows from the cyclic module
description Hom(Fp[t]/g,V_A). It is valid when A modulo p has Jordan
blocks; it does not count generalized g-primary dimensions. In particular
h in the moment theorem must be geometric. At good base primes only,
semisimplicity identifies it with r times the old g multiplicity.

Fiber-constant pullback J is injective over every field, and A_N J=JB.
Its injectivity uses no averaging and no division by N. Therefore all
moments have the deterministic baseline p^h even when p divides N.

2. FULL-UNIFORM CONGRUENCES AND THE EXTRA FORM SPACE

For compatible tables, the congruences have vector coordinates
C_i(a)=sum_(j~i,b) b m_ij(a,b)-(aR)n_i(a).
The 2-by-2 edge-interior transfers impose on a dual dependency h_i(a)
the rectangle identity

 (b-b')[h_i(a)-h_i(a')]+(a-a')[h_j(b)-h_j(b')]=0.

Setting a'=b'=0 and testing coordinate vectors gives
h_i(a)=K_i a^T+k_i and K_j=-K_i^T. Connected bipartiteness therefore
leaves one arbitrary matrix M: K_i=M on the left and -M^T on the right.
There is no odd-cycle relation forcing M itself to be skew.

The remaining root-margin coefficient at a vertex is linear in a plus
the quadratic term -aR K_i a^T. Evaluate at a and -a. Since p is odd,
the two parts vanish separately. The linear part is the old adjoint
intertwiner equation, whose dimension equals h. The quadratic part says
RM and RM^T are skew. Thus the additional dependency dimension is

 b(R)=dim_Fp{M:RM and RM^T are skew}.

These conditions are sufficient as well as necessary. The compatible
integer lattice really is freely parameterized by zero-total integer
root margins and the edge-interior integer entries, with the last row
and column filled from the margins. Consequently reduction modulo p is
surjective; a necessary congruence rank is not silently substituted for
the rank on a smaller unsaturated lattice.

The maximal legal-star lattice has the prescribed vector congruences
and no others. Its character proof uses d>=3 to make the slot functions
affine with one common additive character. The legal and independent
neighbor laws have the same positive covariance. The exact independent
weighted normalizer is one. Combining their full-lattice index with
the compatible finite-index fraction gives the full-uniform coefficient
p^(h+b(R)), not merely a bound on its exponential scale.

All-zero legal compatible stars give an integer affine origin for every
N. A fixed-index lattice has a point a bounded distance from the uniform
mean in its real span; the scaled offset tends to zero. Thus neither
p|N nor integrality of N/p^r is required. The inherited relative local
limit estimate and positive weighted precision justify the factorial-
weighted sum and the extension from the logarithmic Gaussian window
to a fixed sufficiently small uniform neighborhood.

3. INVERTIBLE TARGETS, R^2, AND THE EVEN-POLYNOMIAL DICHOTOMY

For invertible R put L=RM. Then L^T=-L. Since M=R^(-1)L,
RM^T=-R L R^(-T). Its being skew is equivalent to

 R^2 L=L(R^T)^2.

I checked both transposes and the minus sign directly. Hence b(R) counts
alternating forms for which right multiplication by R^2 is self-adjoint.

For irreducible nonzero g, identify the companion module with
E=Fp[lambda] and put K=Fp[lambda^2]. Since lambda solves
z^2-lambda^2 over K, [E:K]<=2. If the index is two, the nontrivial
automorphism sends lambda to -lambda. Thus -lambda is a conjugate and
g(-t)=(-1)^r g(t). Its nonzero constant term forces r even, so g is even.
Conversely even irreducible g permits the nontrivial automorphism
lambda->-lambda fixing K. Therefore index two is equivalent to even g.

If g is not even then K=E, R^2 is cyclic, and every self-adjoint
alternating form vanishes. For a cyclic vector v, balance gives
beta(f(R^2)v,h(R^2)v)=beta(v,f(R^2)h(R^2)v), which is symmetric in f,h;
alternation and odd characteristic give zero. Thus b(R)=0.

If deg g=2s and g is even then |K|=p^s and dim_K E=2. The forms above
are the K-balanced alternating Fp-bilinear forms. They correspond
bijectively to alternating K-bilinear forms Omega via beta=Tr_(K/Fp)Omega.
This assertion does NOT require Tr(1)!=0. The trace polynomial is a
nonzero polynomial of degree less than |K|, so trace is nonzero and its
pairing is nondegenerate. Define Omega(x,y) uniquely by

 Tr(c Omega(x,y))=beta(cx,y) for every c in K.

Uniqueness supplies additivity and K-linearity in the first variable;
balance supplies it in the second. Skew-symmetry follows by testing all
c, and odd characteristic gives alternation. This yields dimension
s*n(n-1)/2 over Fp on a K-space of dimension n. At n=2 the dimension is
s. Therefore the even case has b(R)=s, including p dividing s.

At g=t, R is the scalar zero and scalar M is unconstrained; b(0)=1.

4. NON-EVEN TARGETS HAVE ONLY TWO PHASE TYPES

The zero-phase conditions imply lambda H_L subset H_R and
lambda H_R subset H_L. Invertibility forces equal dimensions, so
H_R=lambda H_L and lambda^2 H_L=H_L. If K=E, irreducibility leaves only
H_L=0 and H_L=E. The old point maps give p^h in total; full uniformity
gives p^h. The sparse and compact-complement estimates give o(1), hence

 E Z_g(A_N)=2p^h+o(1)  (g nonzero and not even).

5. EVEN TARGETS: ALL MIXED PHASES AND EXACT QUOTIENT SECTIONS

If E has K-dimension two, every proper nonzero H_L is a K-line uK,
and H_R=lambda uK. There are p^s+1 line pairs. In these coordinates
the two directional target maps are I and C, where C is multiplication
by c=lambda^2 on K and has irreducible cyclic minimal polynomial of
degree s over Fp.

Choose any other K-line u'K complementing uK, and use lambda u'K on
the opposite side. Multiplication by lambda carries each left line
to its corresponding right line, and carries that right line back
to c times the same left line. Thus BOTH directional maps preserve
the splitting. For the base and every lift, the whole equation
operator is a direct sum of two identical restricted (I,C) operators.
If the restricted old kernel dimension is h_H, then h=2h_H; the
quotient is isomorphic to the restriction.

This proves more than the existence of quotient point maps: the chosen
complement gives an EXACT linear section. Every quotient old point map
lifts to an exact old solution, hence to a fiber-constant solution in
every lift. Translation removes its affine offset without changing the
equations. This rules out hidden residue or solvability conditions.

The restricted uniform congruences have no extra form dimension.
The left identity map makes M skew; the right condition becomes
CM=MC^T. The cyclic alternating-form argument gives M=0. The constant
dependencies have dimension h_H, so its uniform coefficient is p^h_H.
There are p^h_H quotient point maps. Each fixed line pair contributes
p^h_H*p^h_H=p^h.

The in-coset contribution must be its UNIFORM phase, not its entire
restricted moment. The latter would contain point phases already counted
elsewhere. The source explicitly uses disjoint sufficiently small edge-
table neighborhoods. Product edge laws determine the cosets, so the
finitely many centers really are distinct. Positive leakage is bounded
by O(N^(-(d-2))) times a bounded restricted homogeneous moment, using
the matching-forcing rank coupling rather than an independence claim.

As a further independent check on the analytic prerequisite in this
particular restriction: phase inclusions for (I,C) force H_L=H_R and
C-invariance; irreducibility of C leaves only point and full phases.
The usual point sparse estimate and the uniform coefficient above
already give restricted moment 2p^h_H+o(1). Thus the required bounded
restricted moment can also be obtained without a general quiver theorem.

Adding the point, full and p^s+1 mixed families gives

 p^h+p^(h+s)+(p^s+1)p^h=2(p^s+1)p^h.

6. ZERO TARGET: SINGLETON-SIDE LATTICE AND THE TWO MIXED FAMILIES

Let s0=nullity T=nullity T^T, so h=2s0. The scalar subspace pairs are
(0,0),(Fp,Fp),(Fp,0),(0,Fp), with no others.

For the left-uniform/right-point family, quotient point maps are
a_R in ker T, numbering p^s0. Their exact sections are (0,J a_R).
The homogeneous restriction is X^T z_L=0. Thus again no affine
extension obstruction remains.

Before compatibility, the maximal legal lattice has one neighbor-sum
congruence at each old right type, giving index p^m. With left alphabet
Fp and right alphabet a singleton, compatible integer directions are
freely chosen left root margins n_i(a), sum_a n_i(a)=0. Edge tables
are those margins or their transposes, with no interaction variables.
The first moments S_i=sum_a a n_i(a) range independently over Fp:
the transfer n_i(1)=1,n_i(0)=-1 realizes one unit. The compatible
congruence is T^T S, of rank m-s0. Thus its index is p^(m-s0),
and the exact uniform coefficient is p^m/p^(m-s0)=p^s0.

The unequal-alphabet covariance calculation is nondegenerate on its
actual span. The ambient and compatible dimensions are respectively
m(d+1)(p-1) and m(p-1); their difference is dm(p-1), exactly twice
the matching Stirling power. The weighted Gaussian exponent is
-(p/2)sum_i ||r_i||^2, strictly negative on nonzero compatible directions.
The all-zero star supplies the affine lattice origin for EVERY N.
The independent-neighbor weighted normalizer is exactly one; under
that normalized weighting the left labels are iid uniform. Therefore
the relative local limit and compatible Gaussian fraction give the
coefficient just computed, with no omitted normalization or residue.

Multiplying p^s0 quotient maps by coefficient p^s0 gives p^h for this
mixed family. The other mixed family is its transpose. Their leakage
uses the bounded one-sided moment exactly as in the even case. This
one-sided moment can itself be checked from its only point/full phase
types if desired; its central coefficient is p^s0.

Together with point contribution p^h and full coefficient p^(h+1):

 E|ker A_N|=(p+3)p^h+o(1).

7. SHIFTED MARKOV, INCLUDING THE NECESSARY TWO-STEP ZERO THRESHOLD

For a nonzero irreducible g, dim ker g(A_N) is a multiple of r,
including in the presence of modular Jordan blocks. Subtracting p^h
before Markov gives respectively

 not even:  1/(p^r-1),
 even r=2s: (2p^s+1)/(p^(2s)-1)

as upper bounds on the limsup probability of excess geometric kernel.

For zero, square X gives dim ker A_N=2(mN-rank X), even over every
field. The old h is even too. Any excess is therefore at least two,
and shifted Markov gives (p+2)/(p^2-1). A one-step threshold at zero
would fail to tend to zero; the manuscript uses the correct threshold.

All three bounds are at most 2/(p-1) for p>=3. For the even case put
x=p^s>=p: (2x+1)/(x^2-1) decreases for x>1 and at x=p is strictly
below 2/(p-1). No control on factor degrees as p varies is required.

8. COMPLETE REAL SPECTRAL TRANSFER

Let F be the squarefree minimal polynomial of the real symmetric
integral base B. It is monic integral with nonzero discriminant.
Fix an odd prime not dividing that discriminant. Then F modulo p is
squarefree, the base modulo p is semisimple, and distinct rational old
factors have disjoint reduced factors.

For an old irreducible integer factor f, degree ell and old multiplicity
t, write its reduction as product_j g_j, sum_j deg g_j=ell. Each old
g_j kernel has dimension (deg g_j)t. If a NEW real eigenvalue equals
ANY root of f, rationality makes f divide the new rational characteristic
polynomial. Real symmetry makes the rational dimension of ker f(A_N)
at least ell(t+1). Reduction of the integer matrix f(A_N) cannot
increase rank, so the same lower bound holds modulo p.

Polynomial CRT gives ker fbar(A_N)=direct-sum_j ker g_j(A_N), without
assuming the lift modulo p is semisimple. The deterministic old baseline
sum is ell*t, so some factor has excess geometric kernel. Apply the
appropriate shifted bound (the two-step one if g_j=t) and take a union
bound. Its limsup is at most 2ell/(p-1). Summing the fixed old factors
gives 2deg(F)/(p-1).

Arbitrarily large good primes exist, and the order of quantifiers is
essential: FIX p, take limsup_N, THEN let good p tend to infinity.
There is no growing-prime local-limit assertion, no splitting-prime
assumption, no division by N, and no modular old/new direct-sum premise.
This proves complete old/new disjointness in the bipartite case and
combines with the separately audited nonbipartite case as stated.

9. SCOPE BOUNDARY: UNEQUAL BIPARTITE DEGREE FRAMES FAIL

The simple regular-base assertion must not be replaced by an assertion
for arbitrary reversible regular degree frames. If the old bipartite
type counts are k_L!=k_R while the physical vertex counts on both sides
are equal, write the normalized old cross block S with rank rho. Its
old left and right zero dimensions are z_L=k_L-rho and z_R=k_R-rho.
The full square cross block X has equal left/right zero dimensions
z>=max(z_L,z_R), since old vectors inject on both sides. Hence the NEW
zero dimension is at least

 (z-z_L)+(z-z_R)>=|k_L-k_R|.

The root counterexample's cubic frame
Q=[[0,3,0],[1,0,2],[0,3,0]], with class sizes (N,3N,2N), has old
eigenvalues 3,-3,0 and at least one NEW zero in every realization.
This does not apply to simple d-regular base lifts: their two base
parts have the same number m of types, exactly the square setting used
above. The current assembly states this boundary correctly.
