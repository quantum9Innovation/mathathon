A. Complete fixed-dimensional spectral closure
----------------------------------------------
Checked necessity: the positive Stieltjes difference integral gives Q>=I,
and the determinant lemma gives product eigenvalues=(1+tau)^alpha.
The admissible set is closed. This needs and states 0<alpha<1.
At alpha=1 the asserted full closure would be false for n>1; that endpoint
is explicitly excluded. At tau=0 SPD implies u=0, exactly as stated.

Independently derived the proposed lower construction. For ai=delta^(n-i),
zi=(ri-1)product_(j>i)rj, ui=sqrt(ai zi), relative entries equal

 c_alpha sqrt(zi zj)(ai aj)^((1-alpha)/2)
 integral_0^infty t^alpha /[(ai+t)(aj+t)
                    (1+sum_k ak zk/(ak+t))] dt.

For i=j, t=ai s gives exactly manuscript eq. (diag). For each s>0 the
contributions from k<i tend to zero, k=i remains zi/(1+s), and k>i
tend to zk. The fixed integrable majorant is s^alpha/(1+s)^2.
Its limit is the scalar difference ri^alpha-1 because
1+sum_(k>i)zk=product_(k>i)rk and zi divided by that quantity is ri-1.
The case zi=0 is an exact zero row/column and causes no division issue.

For i<j, dropping the denominator (which is >=1) bounds the off-diagonal
by sqrt(zi zj) x^((1-alpha)/2)(1-x^alpha)/(1-x), x=ai/aj->0.
This tends to zero. The divided-difference identity follows directly
from the scalar Stieltjes formula, so no eigenvector asymptotic is hidden.
Fixed dimension then gives operator-norm convergence. Telescoping zi
preserves leverage exactly at every finite delta. All requested q_i
are obtained by ri=q_i^(1/alpha), with ordering restored afterwards.
No restriction beyond positivity/product remains in the closure.

B. Exact strict-count law
-------------------------
If m correction eigenvalues are >epsilon, their log increments sum to
strictly more than m log(1+epsilon). Since all the remaining increments
are nonnegative, m<R with R=alpha log(1+tau)/log(1+epsilon).
This gives max(0,ceil(R)-1), with min(n,...) in fixed dimension.
For every allowed m>0, the equal target q=(1+tau)^(alpha/m) is strictly
above 1+epsilon, so the closure theorem supplies an actual finite witness
retaining all m strict inequalities. This is an attained integer count,
not merely a limiting count. At integral R, exactly R is correctly
excluded. The O(1) remainder in the 1/epsilon asymptotic is valid.

C. Positive-mass path transfer
------------------------------
Reconstructed the Lanczos/Jacobi and grounding algebra. For cyclic SPD
T with negative off-diagonals, d=T^-1 e1 is strictly positive; DTD has
row sum d1 e1. Ground-edge conductance d1 and vertex masses di^2 give
exactly T as grounded normalized operator. Adding ground mass R^2 yields
H_R with top-left d1/R^2, off-diagonal -e1/R. The update vector is
(-d1/R,e1), multiplied by sqrt(kappa). Its physical edge increment is
kappa d1^2, so increment/base ratio is kappa d1=tau exactly.
Both endpoints have kernel span(R,d). As R grows they tend to 0 direct
sum T and its update; there remains exactly one zero eigenvalue and a
uniform positive gap for all other eigenvalues. Continuous functional
calculus therefore applies to positive and pseudoinverse negative powers.
No singular inverse is taken across a vanishing positive eigenvalue.

The noncyclic approximation is valid: simple SPD matrices are dense,
cyclic vectors for each are dense, and the rescaling factor enforcing
exact tau tends to one. Relative matrices are continuous on the SPD
cone. This justifies all desired zero correction eigenvalues and preserves
the original dimension before adding the ground vertex.

D. Unit-mass ordinary weighted path transfer
--------------------------------------------
The block construction preserves the graph being a path. All edges have
positive conductance. For fixed integer block sizes, S has kernel spanned
by block constants. Compressing external edges to normalized block
indicators yields H_s exactly. The changed edge remains an interblock
bridge, and base/increment are both multiplied by s, so tau is preserved
independently of internal conductance T.

Verified the diverging fast modes, a potentially delicate point. The
resolvent converges by finite-dimensional Schur complement. The difference
integral is uniformly dominated by
 t^alpha min(2/t,||E||/t^2),
which is integrable because 0<alpha<1. Therefore fractional DIFFERENCES
converge in operator norm despite both endpoint powers diverging on fast
modes. For negative pseudoinverse powers, the low nonzero eigenvalues
converge to those of the connected quotient and remain separated from
zero, the common constant kernel stays exact, and the fast eigenvalues
diverge linearly. Their negative powers tend to zero. Multiplication of
these convergent factors gives the stated relative convergence.

The final s->infinity step is finite-dimensional continuity with constant
rank (quotient size minus one), and common support dimension; kernel
vectors may rotate continuously without obstructing pseudoinverse
continuity. The two limits are correctly sequential, so no uniform-in-s
fast-mode claim is required. Unrestricted enlargement of path dimension
and conductance ratios is explicit and essential.

E. Consequences and limits
---------------------------
The updated-energy reciprocal-spectrum assertion is correct even when
A,B do not commute: XX^T and X^TX have the same positive spectra with
X=A^(-alpha/2)B^(alpha/2), and inverse generalized pairs give reciprocal
lists. The strict-count threshold 1/(1-epsilon) is therefore correct.
The multi-rank determinant identity is correct. The residual inequality
for a truncated relative correction follows by congruence with L^(alpha/2).

This is a structural spectral-closure and exact worst-case rank theorem.
It supplies no algorithm for computing the relative truncation, no bit
complexity or stability bound, no result for unweighted paths, and no
fixed-size or bounded-conductance-ratio lower construction. Those limits
are accurately stated. Historical novelty remains to be investigated.
