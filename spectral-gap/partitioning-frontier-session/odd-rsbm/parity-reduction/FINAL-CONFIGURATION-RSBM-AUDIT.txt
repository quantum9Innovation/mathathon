-----------------------------------------

Shrinking eta preserves the nearconstant bound. The chosen eta makes
nearconstant and central classes disjoint for all sufficiently large n.
Their union with the remainder covers every nonconstant input; boundary
equalities cause no gap because the nearconstant class is inclusive.

3. Two-block affine and kernel moments
-------------------------------------
For p not dividing d2, summing Q_x(delta x+b) gives at most one total
unit of mass from nonconstant x (one nonzero scalar sum constraint)
and at most one from constants (b=d2 a1 fixes a). This proves the
uniform affine bound and its 2+e_N probability analogue.

Block independence yields the exact displayed expected kernel sum.
The first replacement error is <=(2+e_N)e_N and the second <=2e_N;
both use the affine bounds without an uncontrolled factor p^N.

Both nonconstant input vectors satisfy the SAME sum constraint
s(x)+s(y)=0, so this reference contribution is <=p. Both constant
vectors require a+b=0 and contribute exactly p. In each mixed case,
the fixed constant input prescribes By; at most p^(N−rank B) solutions
and a factor p^(−N+1), summed over p constants, give p^(2−rank B).
No determinant nonsingularity of B is assumed.

The greedy row-support argument gives rank B>=N/[1+d2(d2−1)] over
EVERY field: each selected nonzero row excludes at most that many rows,
and disjoint nonempty supports imply linear independence. It includes
d2=1 and is uniform over every deterministic admissible B. Thus mixed
terms vanish for each fixed prime, and the expected kernel limsup is
at most2p uniformly over B.

4. Limits, real rank, conditioning and recovery
---------------------------------------------
Real nullity>=2 forces every integer minor of order2N−1 to vanish;
reduction modulo any prime therefore has nullity>=2 and kernel size
at least p^2. Markov gives limsup sup_B failure<=2/p in the pairing
model. There are arbitrarily large fixed odd primes avoiding d2.
The proof first takes N to infinity at each such fixed prime, THEN
takes the infimum of these limsup bounds. No growing-prime estimate
is used or required.

Configuration simplicity has a positive limiting probability for fixed
d1 along all admissible sizes. Conditioning the two independent
blocks on simplicity costs a bounded factor only AFTER configuration
failure has been shown o(1). The conditional graph law is exactly the
uniform labelled simple law. Conditioning does not involve B, so the
uniformity survives; integrating over an independent cross graph is
also valid. No even-block contiguity theorem or surgery law is needed
in this final configuration proof.

On the one-dimensional real-kernel event, a rational basis vector is a
multiple of the planted sign vector and has nonzero first coordinate.
Its normalization recovers the signs. For fixed degrees, Hadamard
bounds all minors by numbers with O(N) bits; fraction-free elimination
therefore has polynomial bit complexity.
