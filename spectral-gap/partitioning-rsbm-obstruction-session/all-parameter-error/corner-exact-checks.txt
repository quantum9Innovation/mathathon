TWO EXACT FIRST-MOMENT CHECKS OF THE NEW ENDPOINT CONSTANTS
2026-11-15. Independent finite-N identities, not statistical experiments.

(1) a=1,b=2: E X=2N/(N-1)^2 exactly.

Fix ANY simple two-regular bipartite cross graph on N+N vertices, and
independently sample the two internal uniform perfect matchings (N even).
For a cross edge u in V_+, v in V_-, let u' be v's other cross neighbor
and v' be u's other cross neighbor. They are distinct from u,v because
the cross graph is simple. A leading minimum flip with support {u,v}
exists precisely when u is internally matched to u' and v to v'.
Its boundary then is the two exterior vertices {u',v'}.

Each matching event has probability 1/(N-1), and the two factors are
independent. The cross graph has exactly 2N edges. Linearity therefore
gives E X=2N/(N-1)^2, with no assumption of independence among different
support events and no averaging over the cross factor needed.
Consequently E X~2/N, confirming C_(1,2)=2 independently of the general
stub/simplicity-ratio computation. For N>2, each leading flip SET has
its unique least-size global orientation as in the main proof.

(2) a=0,b>=3: the isolated-K_(b,b) count is exact by deletion.

Let s_M be the probability that the b-regular bipartite configuration
on M+M vertices is simple. If X counts isolated complete b-by-b
components in the uniform-simple graph on N+N vertices, then

 E X=binom(N,b)^2 (b!)^(2b)/(bN)_(b^2) * s_(N-b)/s_N.

For fixed support sets the complete bipartite core saturates all their
stubs; its physical assignments number (b!)^(2b). The residual factor
is EXACTLY the b-regular configuration on N-b vertices per side, and
there are no forbidden residual edges to the saturated support. Thus
the displayed ratio is exact, not merely a completion lower bound.

Since s_M tends to exp[-(b-1)^2/2]>0,

 E X~ [(b!)^(2b-2)/b^(b^2)] N^(-b(b-2)).

For b=3 this coefficient is 16/243. There is no internal simplicity
factor at a=0. These identities confirm the two delicate endpoint
normalizations; they do not replace the overlap proof converting E X
to the ambiguity probability.
