1. The scalar input was independently checked against the full primary
   Stahl1993 PDF, Theorem1 and equations2.1--2.2:
     https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9301217
   It gives the real rational type(q,q) rate exp(-2pi sqrt(alpha*q))
   with fixed positive prefactor for 0<alpha<1. Scaling the compact
   interval only changes that prefactor. The resulting count upper is
   r L^2/(4pi^2 alpha)+O(L+1), uniformly over the matrix class.

2. The noncommuting rational rank proof is valid. The divided polynomial
   (P(x)Q(y)-Q(x)P(y))/(x-y) has degrees at most q-1 separately. Left
   multiplication by B and right multiplication by A commute as maps on
   matrices, giving the displayed Bezout identity. Its column range has
   dimension at most q*rank(B-A). Invertible denominator factors preserve
   rank. No pole location, rational-coefficient oracle, or simultaneous
   diagonalization is silently needed. Functional calculus bounds the
   difference error by twice the scalar approximation error.

3. The lower has genuinely bounded endpoint norms. Its probability
   density lives only on (-infinity,log h], with h<(R-1)/4. On the finite
   interval the update norm is t_T=1-exp(-eta*T). Normalizing both endpoints
   by t_T, with t_T>=1/2, gives endpoint norms at most 1+2h<R and exact
   update norm one. This strict margin survives finite-matrix transfer.
   The finite step-function models preserve the update norm exactly.

4. The same Woodbury/Cauchy domination used in the companion is applicable
   on this finite interval. The normalized weight has only a negative
   infinite tail, asymptotic to a positive constant times exp(alpha*x).
   Thus s*integral(q(x)^s) tends to 1/alpha. Together with
   s*J_(1/2,s)->1/pi this gives moment coefficient 1/(2pi^2 alpha).
   Finite transfer is made before either infinite-interval or small-s
   limits, so the proof invokes no functional calculus of an unbounded
   operator. Direct sums multiply the coefficient by r.

5. All corrections have norm at most one. Loewner--Heinz applied to
   A<=B+I, followed by the scalar inequality (x+1)^alpha<=x^alpha+1,
   proves this even for noncommuting endpoints. The uniform quadratic
   upper and layer-cake limit therefore meet the companion's deficit
   lemma hypotheses. That lemma proves the supremal strict-count limit,
   rather than assuming that a moment asymptotic for one operator implies
   a pointwise count asymptotic.

6. The exact R=1 boundary is correctly distinct. A unit eigenvalue of
   E forces Aw=0,Bw=w (or the reverse for eigenvalue -1). The corresponding
   subspace reduces both endpoints, and its correction is exactly +/-I.
   At least one such channel exists. Removing all t saturated directions
   leaves perturbation rank at most r-t, to which the rational upper still
   applies without a unit-norm residual assumption. This yields r-1.
   One scalar saturated block plus r-1 half-scaled R=2 lower witnesses
   proves the matching coefficient. For r=1 the correction is exactly
   rank one with its only singular value equal to one. R<1 is empty.

7. The R>1 path transfer protects the endpoint bound. Replacing A by
   lambda*A and B by lambda*A+ww^T preserves the unit positive update
   and creates strict endpoint slack for every lambda<1. Strict counts
   survive as lambda increases to one. The already audited finite-ground
   path construction then preserves both the count and the norm bound.
   Weak unchanged bridges give the rank-r connected-path lower. At R=1,
   a positive update on a connected host is impossible in this normalized
   class: its saturated vector would be in the host kernel and also
   orthogonal to the same mass-weighted constant vector. The note does
   not incorrectly extend its connected-path lower to that boundary.
