\begin{abstract}
The frozen strict-weight recovery theorem requires a positive omission
weight on every active factor. This separate addendum permits zero
weights while retaining the strict upper bound of one half. A weighted
variance comparison gives an explicit one-hot entropy gap without a
positive lower bound on the omission weights. The polynomial moment,
mixed-degree support argument and complete unknown-frame algorithm
therefore extend. A connected twelve-type example has a factor on which
every certificate must vanish. No constant modular moment, quadratic
runtime or finite-field phase classification is asserted here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and the separate extension}
Use the independent simple-factor frame model of the frozen source
dated November 15, 2026,
\begin{center}\texttt{strict-q-blind-frame-recovery.tex},\end{center}
whose SHA256 is
\begin{center}\small\ttfamily
0042ca4067451330f955c9621bc741694369f4743ee4823c70f16f6a87d4c634.
\end{center}
For clarity, fix supplied bounds $K,D$. The unknown nonnegative integer
frame $Q=(d_{ij})_{i,j\in[k]}$ has $k\le K$, connected off-diagonal
support (with the one-type case allowed), and total degrees
$d_i=\sum_jd_{ij}\le D$. Its positive class sizes satisfy
$n_id_{ij}=n_jd_{ji}$ and internal parity, and $\alpha_i=n_i/n$,
$n=\sum_i n_i$. Independently sample a uniform simple internal
$d_{ii}$-regular graph for each active internal factor and a uniform
simple $(d_{ij},d_{ji})$-biregular graph for every active cross factor.
The planted indicator $J$ obeys $AJ=JQ$. Sizes tend to infinity along
any admissible fixed-proportion sequence on which these factors exist.

The new hypothesis is the existence, on every active factor, of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
 q_{ij}=q_{ji},\qquad 0\le q_{ij}<\tfrac12,
 \qquad \sum_jd_{ij}q_{ij}=1\quad\text{for every }i.
\end{equation}
An internal weight is used once per physical slot. Define
\[
 c=\min_{d_{ij}>0}(1-2q_{ij})>0.
\]
Necessarily $d_i\ge3$: at most two weights strictly below one half
cannot sum to one. No positive lower bound on the $q_{ij}$ is assumed.
The certificate is a hypothesis on the true frame and is not supplied
to the algorithm.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:extension}
Under \eqref{eq:certificate}, with high probability every equitable
partition with at most $K$ nonempty classes is a compatible coarsening
of $J$. Given only $A,K,D$, the frozen source's complete binary
intertwiner enumeration recovers $J$, its unknown number $k$ of classes
and $Q$, up to class names, with high probability. Its unconditional
expected bit work is polynomial and its bit space is $O_K(n^2)$.
The same deterministic polynomial cap with explicit abstention gives
worst-case polynomial work and high-probability recovery.
\end{theorem}

The statement extends the frozen theorem; it does not alter that
theorem's hypotheses. The proof below supplies the new gap and checks
every input needed to compose the existing support and enumeration
arguments. All constants concern fixed $K,D$ and a fixed true frame.

\section{Entropy and the modular polynomial moment}
A legal ordered star at old type $i$ has root $X_i$ and $d_i$ ordered
physical neighbor labels $Z_{is}$. Its equation determines any one
neighbor from the root and the other $d_i-1$ neighbors. For binary
vector labels the equation is $\sum_s Z_{is}=X_iR$; for one-hot labels
it is the original integer equation. Let $P_i$ be its law. Symmetrize
only physical slots with the same old neighbor type, writing
$\overline P_i$. This preserves legality, root laws and pooled pair
laws, and increases star entropy.

Let $\rho_i$ be the root law, $\nu_{ij}$ the pooled pair law and
\[
 w_{ii}=\alpha_id_{ii}/2,\quad
 w_{ij}=\alpha_id_{ij}\ (i<j),\quad
 \Phi(P)=\sum_i\alpha_i\HH(P_i)
              -\sum_{i\le j}w_{ij}\HH(\nu_{ij}).
\]
Compatible pair laws reverse across cross factors and are symmetric
internally. Put $I_{ij}=\II(\nu_{ij})$. For omission of physical slot
$t$ let
\[
 J_{it}=\sum_{s\ne t}\HH(Z_{is}\mid X_i)
              -\HH((Z_{is})_{s\ne t}\mid X_i)\ge0.
\]
The frozen entropy calculation remains valid with zero weights:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy}
 \Phi(P)\le\Phi(\overline P)
 =-\sum_{i\le j}w_{ij}(1-2q_{ij})I_{ij}
   -\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_tq_{it}J_{it}\le0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, lossless omission and $\sum_tq_{it}=1$ give
\[
 \HH(\overline P_i)=\HH(\rho_i)
   +\sum_s(1-q_{is})\HH(Z_{is}\mid X_i)-\sum_tq_{it}J_{it}.
\]
Substitute pair entropy minus root entropy. Balance and symmetry
cancel the root coefficient
$\alpha_i(1-\sum_jd_{ij}q_{ji})=0$, and leave precisely the pair
coefficients in \eqref{eq:entropy}, including the internal half factor.
This uses no division by an omission weight.

Here and below entropies use natural logarithms. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B}
 a_*=2^K,\qquad F=K+\frac{K(K+3)}2a_*^2,
 \qquad B=Ka_*^D+F,
\end{equation}
the frozen exact configuration type count and uniform factorial
remainder give, for every fixed candidate $r\le K$ and every binary
$r$-by-$r$ matrix $R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
 \E_{\mathrm{config}}|\{Y:AY=YR\}|
 \le (3D)^F(n+1)^B,\qquad
 \E_{\mathrm{simple}}2^{h_R}\le C(K,D)(n+1)^B.
\end{equation}
To check precisely what is inherited, the exact type is specified by
its legal-star counts, with at most $(n+1)^{Ka_*^D}$ possibilities.
Its expectation is at most
$\exp(n\Phi+F[\log(Dn+1)+1])$.
These counts and remainders do not involve $q$; their only entropy
input is $\Phi\le0$, now proved in \eqref{eq:entropy}.
Conditioning the finitely many fixed-degree configuration factors on
simplicity costs a bounded factor. The resulting laws are exactly the
stated independent simple factors. Thus \eqref{eq:moment} is
unconditional, including on graphs that fail structural recovery,
and it requires no feasibility of the candidate $R$.

\section{The new one-hot gap}
Here labels are ordinary unit vectors in $\mathbb R^r$, and every
occupied star satisfies
\[
 \sum_{s=1}^{d_i}e_{Z_{is}}=R_{X_i,*}
\]
over the integers, with a nonnegative integer candidate matrix $R$.
Set
\[
 \delta(P)=\sum_i\alpha_i(1-\max_a\rho_i(a)).
\]
This is the normalized distance to an arbitrary old-type point map.
Unused labels and non-surjective maps are permitted.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gap}
Every compatible legal one-hot collection satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 \Phi(P)\le-\gamma\delta(P)^2,
 \qquad \gamma=\frac{9c^3}{4D^4}>0.
\end{equation}
In particular zero entropy forces every old root law to be a point
mass, even when some omission weights vanish.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Root laws are unchanged by symmetrization, so it suffices to prove the
bound after symmetrizing. Write $L=-\Phi\ge0$ and
$J_q=\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_tq_{it}J_{it}$.
For each $(i,t)$ let $\mathsf Q_{it}$ keep the actual root law and
independently draw the retained neighbor labels from their old-type
marginals, independently also of the root. The divergence of the actual
retained law from this reference law is
\[
 \kappa_{it}=J_{it}+\sum_{s\ne t}\II(X_i;Z_{is}).
\]
It is finite, including on boundary distributions, because a product
of marginals is positive on each tuple having positive actual mass.
Define the omission-weighted divergence
\begin{align}\label{eq:kappa}
 A_q&=\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_tq_{it}\kappa_{it}\nonumber\\
 &=J_q+\sum_{i\le j}2w_{ij}(1-q_{ij})I_{ij}
 \le \frac{2}{c}L.
\end{align}
For the equality, each physical slot $s$ is retained with total weight
$1-q_{is}$, and reversibility combines its two orientations. The
internal half factor gives the same expression. For the inequality
use $2(1-q_{ij})\le2$, $1-2q_{ij}\ge c$,
$0<c\le1$, and \eqref{eq:entropy}. No $q_{ij}^{-1}$ occurs.

Write
\[
 \mu_{is}=\E e_{Z_{is}},\quad
 V_{is}=1-\|\mu_{is}\|_2^2,\quad S_i=\sum_sV_{is},
 \qquad B_i=\E\bigl\|R_{X_i,*}-\sum_s\mu_{is}\bigr\|_2^2\ge0.
\]
For the retained data define
\[
 f_{it}=\left\|R_{X_i,*}-\sum_{s\ne t}e_{Z_{is}}-\mu_{it}\right\|_2^2.
\]
Its actual expectation is $V_{it}$, since the legal equation makes
the vector inside the norm equal to $e_{Z_{it}}-\mu_{it}$.
Independence under $\mathsf Q_{it}$ gives
$\E_{\mathsf Q_{it}}f_{it}=S_i-V_{it}+B_i$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Delta}
 \Delta_{it}:=\E_{\mathsf Q_{it}}f_{it}-\E f_{it}
                 =S_i-2V_{it}+B_i.
\end{equation}
Every occupied row $R_{X_i,*}$ has total mass $d_i$; so does
$\sum_{s\ne t}e_{Z_{is}}+\mu_{it}$. Both are nonnegative under both
laws. Hence $0\le f_{it}\le2D^2$. Total variation and Pinsker give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pinsker}
 |\Delta_{it}|\le \sqrt2D^2\sqrt{\kappa_{it}}.
\end{equation}
The decisive change from the frozen argument is to average
\eqref{eq:Delta} with the omission weights themselves:
\[
 \sum_tq_{it}\Delta_{it}
 =S_i-2\sum_tq_{it}V_{it}+B_i
 \ge c S_i.
\]
This holds since $\sum_tq_{it}=1$, $B_i\ge0$ and
$q_{it}\le(1-c)/2$. Individual $\Delta_{it}$ need not be positive.
After summing over old types, \eqref{eq:pinsker}, weighted
Cauchy--Schwarz and \eqref{eq:kappa} imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
 c\sum_i\alpha_iS_i
 \le \sqrt2D^2\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_tq_{it}\sqrt{\kappa_{it}}
 \le \sqrt2D^2\sqrt{A_q}
 \le 2D^2\sqrt{L/c}.
\end{equation}
The total Cauchy--Schwarz weight is exactly
$\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_tq_{it}=1$.

For the reverse bound, reversibility of the actual frame gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_i\alpha_iS_i
 &=\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_jd_{ij}(1-\|\rho_j\|_2^2)\\
 &=\sum_j\alpha_jd_j(1-\|\rho_j\|_2^2)
 \ge3\sum_j\alpha_j(1-\max_a\rho_j(a))=3\delta(P).
\end{align*}
Here $\|\rho_j\|_2^2\le\max_a\rho_j(a)$ and $d_j\ge3$.
Combine this with \eqref{eq:upper} and square to obtain
$9c^2\delta(P)^2\le4D^4L/c$, which is exactly
\eqref{eq:gap}. The initial symmetrization then proves it for the
original laws as well.
\end{proof}

For each fixed $\eta>0$, the same exact one-hot type count and
factorial remainder therefore give, uniformly over the finite bounded
integer candidate family,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:macro}
 \E_{\mathrm{simple}}\#\{Y:\ AY=YR,\ Y\text{ one-hot},\
             \delta(Y)\ge\eta\}
 \le C(K,D)(n+1)^B e^{-\gamma\eta^2 n}.
\end{equation}
There is no lower bound on candidate class masses. If $\delta=0$,
the root laws and hence all compatible stars are deterministic; their
integer legality is precisely $QT=TR$ for the corresponding point
map $T$. Conversely each compatible point map has entropy zero.

\section{Support closure and the complete algorithm}
We record why no remaining recovery step needs positive omission
weights. Balance and connectedness imply
$\alpha_{\min}\ge\rho:=1/(KD^{K-1})$ by following paths of length at
most $K-1$. Thus every active stub pool has at least $\rho n$ stubs.
The mixed-degree support lemma in the frozen source uses only these
pool bounds and $3\le d_i\le D$. It gives a constant
$\eta=\eta(K,D)>0$, which may also be chosen less than
$\rho/[2(D+1)]$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support}
 \Prob_{\mathrm{simple}}(\exists\varnothing\ne S,\ |S|\le\eta n:
      \text{no outside vertex has exactly one neighbor in }S)
       =O_{K,D}(n^{-(d_{\min}-2)}).
\end{equation}
Specifically, if all stubs at $S$ produce $a$ internal edges and $b$
boundary edges reaching $w$ exterior vertices, then
$2a+b\ge d_{\min}|S|$ and $b\ge2w$.
Writing $e=a+b$ gives
$e-|S|-w\ge(d_{\min}-2)|S|/2$.
All witness probabilities and counts in that lemma concern uniform
pairings or bijections and therefore contain no omission weight.
Its summable bound is $C^s(s/n)^{\lceil(d_{\min}-2)s/2\rceil}$;
the simple model excludes singleton bad sets. This is precisely the
proof of \eqref{eq:support}. The same event excludes every nonzero
row-sparse matrix solution of $AV=VR$, over any field and for any $R$:
an exterior vertex with one neighbor in the support has a nonzero
row in $AV$ and a zero row in $VR$.

There are finitely many candidate integer matrices $R$ with $r\le K$
and row sums at most $D$. Apply \eqref{eq:macro} to all of them and
intersect with the good support event. Any accepted equitable indicator
$Y$ then differs from an old-type point map $JT$ on fewer than $\eta n$
vertices. At most $(D+1)\eta n$ vertices are changed or adjacent to
a change. Each old class contains a vertex whose entire closed
neighborhood is unchanged. Evaluating the integer equations there
proves $QT=TR$, including for non-surjective $T$. Hence
$V=Y-JT$ obeys $AV=VR$ and has small row support, so the support event
forces $V=0$. This proves the structural assertion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:extension}.

The complete algorithm needs only $A,K,D$. For every candidate $r,R$
compute a binary basis of
\[
 \ker_{\F}(I_r\otimes A-R^T\otimes I_n),
\]
enumerate all basis combinations, and retain exactly the one-hot
matrices with nonempty classes satisfying the original integer
identity $AY=YR$. After completing all enumeration, output an accepted
partition of maximum class count. The true $(J,Q)$ is included.
On the structural event every accepted partition is a compatible
coarsening; its maximum class count is $k$ and equality makes its map
$T$ a permutation. This recovers the partition and quotient.

Binary elimination costs $O_K(n^3)$ bit operations, and each enumerated
matrix costs at most $O_{K,D}(n^2)$ bit operations, including the
integer verification. The unconditional moment \eqref{eq:moment}
therefore gives the frozen bounds
\[
 \E[\text{base bit work}]\le C(K,D)n^{B+2},\qquad
 \E[\text{total bit work}]\le C(K,D)n^{B+3},
\]
with $O_K(n^2)$ bit space. These bounds include rare large kernels.
Instrumenting the base operations with a binary clock and stopping at
$n^{B+4}$ operations gives $O_{K,D}(n^{B+5})$ deterministic total
bit work; the clock has $O_{K,D}(\log n)$ bits. Return explicit
\texttt{FAILURE/ABSTENTION} if interrupted, never an unfinished maximum.
Markov bounds interruption by $O_{K,D}(n^{-2})$. Overall failure is
bounded by
\[
 O_{K,D}(n^{-(d_{\min}-2)}+n^{-2})
       +C(K,D)(n+1)^B e^{-\gamma\eta^2 n}.
\]
Thus both versions have the claimed recovery guarantee, and the capped
version has worst-case polynomial work.

For fixed $K,D$ only finitely many connected integer frames occur.
Choose one certificate \eqref{eq:certificate} for each qualifying
frame. Each chosen $c$ is positive, and their finite minimum is positive.
Consequently the entropy rates and eventual constants may be made
uniform over this qualifying bounded family without exposing the
certificate to the algorithm. This completes the proof.

\section{A strict enlargement of the positive-weight hypothesis}
Take two disjoint copies of $K_{3,3}$ and add one bridge from a left
vertex of the first copy to a right vertex of the second. Regard the
twelve vertices as twelve types, with $d_{ij}=1$ on base edges and
equal type sizes $N$. All total degrees are three or four, and the
base is connected. Give each edge within either copy weight $1/3$
and the bridge weight zero. These weights satisfy
\eqref{eq:certificate}, with $c=1/3$.

Every symmetric row-sum-one certificate must give the bridge weight
zero. Sum its row equations over the three left vertices of the first
copy and subtract those over the three right vertices of that copy.
All edges within the copy cancel and only the bridge remains, so its
weight is $3-3=0$. This example therefore has no all-positive
certificate and is not covered by the frozen positive-weight theorem.
Its independent matching lifts are covered by this addendum.

The proof does not allow $q_{ij}=1/2$: its constant $c$ would vanish.
It proves a polynomial modular moment through entropy and exact type
counting. It does not prove a bounded modular moment for frames that
require zero omission weights, nor an improved runtime from such a
moment. Finite-field zero phases need not be those of the all-positive
case; they are not classified or used here. Historical novelty and
practical efficiency are not claimed.
