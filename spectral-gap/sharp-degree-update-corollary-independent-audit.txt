1. True-eigenvalue threshold in the near-sharp algorithm
---------------------------------------------------------
The raw relative factor error and total Gram error are each <=rho*eps/4.
The retained diagonalized Gram threshold is (1-rho/2)*eps. Two successive
Weyl comparisons therefore force the same number of TRUE C eigenvalues
strictly above
 (1-rho/2)*eps-rho*eps/4-rho*eps/4=(1-rho)*eps.
This argument concerns every retained value with strict threshold, including
repeated eigenvalues, and is on the SAME simultaneous success event as the
error bound. Thus r<=N_C((1-rho)*eps), not merely the elementary determinant
bound. The discarded relative residual plus raw error totals <=eps.

2. Vanishing rho and preservation of charged work
---------------------------------------------------
Let rho(eps)=1/log(e^2/eps). For 0<eps<1/2 it lies in (0,1), tends to
zero, and log(1/rho)=O(log log(1/eps)). Writing t=(1-rho)*eps and using
 t W_(d,alpha)(t)->A_(d,alpha),
we get on success
 eps*r <= [A_(d,alpha)+o(1)]/(1-rho)=A_(d,alpha)+o(1).
The o(1) is uniform over the entire graph class because it comes from the
single supremum function W. It need not have an effective rate, and none
is claimed. The algorithm's proof allocates raw/Gram errors proportional
to rho and increases only accuracy/cutoff logarithms by O(log(1/rho)).
Consequently this variable choice preserves the stated expected nearly-
linear arithmetic work and joint confidence, including the unconditional
failure guards and certified resistance preprocessing.

If an implementation in the arithmetic-and-square-root special case wants
to avoid evaluating an exact variable logarithm, take instead rho=1/k,
where k=ceil(log_2(4/eps)) is computed by repeated doubling and comparison.
It has the same required asymptotic properties and overhead. This is an
implementation-equivalent parameter choice, not a change to the proof.

3. The worst-case lower rank conclusion
-----------------------------------------
For any matrix F of rank <=r, its restriction/congruence
A^-alpha/2 F A^-alpha/2 on the positive support has rank <=r. The spectral
best-rank-r approximation bound for the PSD matrix C implies error >=
lambda_(r+1)(C). Therefore relative error <=eps forces r>=N_C(eps).
This remains true for indefinite or nonsymmetric F. The sharp bridge
examples, also in the bipartite subclass, force worst-case necessary rank
[A_(d,alpha)-o(1)]/eps. Combined with Section 2 this proves asymptotic
optimality of the charged algorithm's uniform leading rank constant.
This is a rank statement, not a lower bound on arithmetic runtime.

4. Independent verification of the sharp lower input
------------------------------------------------------
To avoid treating an unverified lower theorem as a premise, I read and
reconstructed the actual lower proof in the identified TeX source.
The finite-dimensional separated-mass lemma is correct. The elementary
symmetric asymptotics and P_k<=e_k<=binom(m,k)P_k first give adjacent
spectral ratios O(delta), after which every other symmetric product is
negligible. Successive products then identify lambda_i~kappa_i delta^(m-i).
The scaled resolvent limit identifies leverage weights z_i->tau_i-tau_(i+1).
In the fractional rank-one integral, diagonal dominated convergence gives
[(1+tau_i)/(1+tau_(i+1))]^alpha-1, while the off-diagonal bound
 sqrt(z_i z_j)x^((1-alpha)/2)(1-x^alpha)/(1-x)
vanishes as the eigenvalue ratio x->0. The proof remains valid along
convergent SPD pair families; compactness gives the required uniformity.

The reverse-pivot target formula is correct. With c=1/R_d,c'=1/R'_d,
f(g)=(c'+g)/(c+g), the prescribed decreasing f(g_i) gives increasing g_i
and positive q_i. Sherman--Morrison yields
 det K'_(T_i)/det K_(T_i)=f(g_(i-1))/f(g_m),
so successive reverse pivots have exactly log ratio s/m. This produces m
equal target correction values exp(alpha*s/m)-1.

For the general-degree circuit, each depth-h (d-1)-ary top tree has energy
J^2(1-1/Q)/(d-2), Q=(d-1)^h. Copy symmetry and uniqueness apply to arbitrary
signed terminal currents, so each bottom copy carries j_i/Q. The resulting
impedance matrix is exactly the displayed diagonal neck term plus
[r_b+2(1-1/Q)/(d-2)]11^T+R_bottom/Q. Its old/new limits are R_d and R'_d,
whose ratio is 2d/(d+2). A binary bottom tree with arbitrary m leaves keeps
all degrees <=d and avoids a geometric restriction on the final count.

The multiport contact bound is correct in MEAN coordinates. Uniform source
j_i/n_i in a reservoir and port currents a_i leave zero-sum internal demand
P_i a_i-(sum a_i)1/n_i, whose minimum internal energy is a_i^T H_i a_i.
Uniform reservoir gap bounds H_i between zero and R I. Dropping contact
energies gives the ideal impedance lower bound; the symmetric ideal flow
costs at most (R/Q)(||j||^2+J^2) in contacts and proves the uniform upper
bound. Doubling changes only the fixed bridge current term, hence the
terminal stiffness change remains positive rank one. The contact family
is compact and its stiffness pairs stay uniformly SPD near the chosen
finite target, justifying choosing mass separation before reservoir volumes.

The fixed-core reservoir realization is also sound. Its block estimates
A_a=O(1/a),B_a=O(a^-1/2),F_a>=xi I follow from internal gaps and a fixed
core Dirichlet estimate. The scaled compressed resolvent tends to the
static mass pair; its Schur correction is O(t/a). The compressed first
moment is uniformly bounded. Therefore the fractional integral is dominated
by t^(alpha-1)min(1,C/t), an integrable fixed-dimensional envelope. This
proves positive fractional compression convergence without asserting high
response disappears. Generalized Ritz min--max captures all m desired

A compact contact-subsequence argument supplies the stated uniformity.

The bipartite reservoir modification is valid. Two-sided cubic expanders
exist for every sufficiently large even size by the actual Bordenave
Theorem 1 in the repository source (the theorem was freshly read here).
Their bipartite double covers have the needed positive Laplacian gap.
Replacing selected edges by length-three paths creates two opposite-color
internal vertices per edge; choosing the appropriate one as port preserves
all exterior color constraints. Assigning each new vertex to its adjacent
old endpoint gives the stated uniform gap estimate. Sizes in the resulting
fixed residue class approximate any prescribed positive masses after a
common volume scaling. Degrees remain <=3<=d and the edited bridge remains
a bridge throughout.

Finally the choices Q, mass separation, anchor mass and volume are all
