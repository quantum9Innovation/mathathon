1. The graph normalization and initial clipping check.

After scaling A=L/d, the normalized update is uu^T with u=e_p-e_q,
and tau=u^T A^dagger u. The mean-zero dipole potential v=A^dagger u
has range exactly tau, hence ||v||^2<=n tau^2. No eigenvalue endpoint
is needed. The interpolation bound u^T A^-alpha u<=tau^alpha 2^(1-alpha)
has the correct exponents.

The clipping lemma does not assume that the exact solution lies in
the approximate clipping interval. This is legitimate: clipping fixes
the two approximate endpoint coordinates, so it preserves u^T x,
decreases x^T A x, and therefore decreases ||x-v||_A^2. The energy
error controls the approximate endpoint difference to relative eta,
giving correct endpoint order on the success event. Centering then
gives the stated Euclidean bound independent of lambda_2(A).

2. The feasible-flow mechanism survives the small-eigenvalue test.

For the augmented graph, a feasible error satisfies
 e=D^T(f-f_*)=-(g-g_*), with e orthogonal to constants.
Its excess energy E bounds both ||e||_(A^dagger)^2<=E and
||e||^2<=tE. Spectral Holder therefore gives
 ||A^-alpha/2 e||^2<=t^(1-alpha)E.
Together with E<=zeta^2 ||vhat||^2/t this gives precisely the displayed
zeta ||vhat|| t^-alpha/2 bound. The initial-potential bias uses
sup lambda^(1-alpha)/(lambda+t)^2=K_alpha t^(-1-alpha), so the eta
term has the correct t exponent. These estimates remain valid with
arbitrarily small positive eigenvalues; ordinary shifted-potential
energy error alone would not suffice.

The physical columns D^T f are exactly mean zero because the added
ground demand is zero. This holds even for poor feasible flows.
The two flow moment bounds follow from total energy and feasibility,
with no spectral norm of A entering.

3. The factor error allocation checks algebraically.

With quadrature weight of size h t^(alpha+1), summing squared eta
biases gives O_alpha(eta^2 tau J). Summing squared flow errors gives
O_alpha(zeta^2 n tau^2 J t_+). Relative square-root weight errors
give O_alpha(kappa sqrt(tau J)) in factor Frobenius norm. These are
the three terms in the stated sufficient allocation.

Since the exact relative factor has Frobenius norm at most
K_alpha sqrt(tau J), the denominator 1+sqrt(tau J) in that allocation
does absorb the amplification in TT^T-SS^T. Its sum with the epsilon/32
quadrature error can be made at most epsilon/8. Tolerances may always
be capped above by a fixed constant when tau is tiny, so their logs
need not introduce log(1/tau).
