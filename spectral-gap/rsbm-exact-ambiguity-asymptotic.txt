EXACT FIRST-ORDER AMBIGUITY AND BAYES ERROR IN THE TWO-CLASS RSBM
================================================================

THEOREM
-------
Fix integers a>b>0. Set

 D=a+b, delta=a-b, t=delta+1, nu=(D-2)t,

 c_(a,b)= ((bt)!)^2 (a!)^(2t)
             / [(t!)^2 (b!)^(2t) a^(t*delta)].           (1)

On two fixed planted classes V_+,V_- of size N, independently sample
uniform simple a-regular internal graphs and a uniform simple
b-regular bipartite cross graph. All limits are through admissible N,
in particular aN even. Let K(G) be the number of equitable (a,b)
unordered bisections of the observed labeled graph. Always K(G)>=1.
Then, as N tends to infinity,

 Pr[K(G)>=2] ~ c_(a,b) N^(-nu),                          (2)
 Pr[K(G)>=3] = O_(a,b)(N^(-nu-1)).                       (3)

If the planted balanced unordered partition is hidden uniformly and
only the labeled graph and a,b are observed, the optimal exact-recovery
Bayes error satisfies

 R_N ~ (c_(a,b)/2) N^(-nu).                             (4)

The asymptotic is expressed in BLOCK size N, not total size n=2N.
For (a,b)=(2,1), t=2, nu=2 and c_(2,1)=4, so ambiguity is ~4/N^2
and Bayes error ~2/N^2. An equivalent expression in total graph size
would multiply these leading constants by 2^nu.

The proof counts ALL leading boundary patterns, rather than only the
single paired motif used for the earlier order-of-magnitude lower
bound. It distinguishes a flip SET from different labeled drawings of
the same motif, which is essential for the exact coefficient.

1. ORIENTATION, MINIMUM FLIP SIZE, AND NOTATION
----------------------------------------------
Let sigma be +1 on V_+ and -1 on V_-. An equitable sign vector tau
satisfies A tau=delta tau. It is automatically balanced: A1=D1 and
symmetry give (D-delta)1^T tau=0, with D-delta=2b>0.
Thus its flip counts on V_+,V_- agree, say k. Replacing tau by -tau
permits k<=N/2. Put

 S_+= {v in V_+:tau_v!=sigma_v},
 S_-= {v in V_-:tau_v!=sigma_v},
 w=(sigma-tau)/2=1_(S_+)-1_(S_-).

Then Aw=delta w. At a vertex in S_+, its equation is

 delta=|N(v) intersect S_+|-|N(v) intersect S_-|.

Since the graph is simple, this forces k-1>=delta, hence k>=t.
The same holds on S_-. For all sufficiently large N, N>2t.
An alternative unordered partition with k=t then has a UNIQUE such
orientation: its opposite orientation has N-t>t flips per old side.

The total support is 2k. The underlying graph has degree D, including
in all admissible parity classes. No fixed-even-N restriction is used.

2. NONMINIMAL FLIPS ARE LOWER ORDER
----------------------------------
We record the upper estimate needed at first-order precision. For any
fixed set S of size h<=epsilon N, the necessary condition for support
of a nonzero vector with Aw=delta w is that no outside vertex has
exactly ONE incident edge into S. In the colored configuration model,
expose all edges incident to S, skipping previously paired stubs.
At every selected step, the probability of hitting S or an already
hit outside vertex is <=C_(a,b) h/N, for sufficiently small fixed
epsilon. The relevant typed pools still have Theta(N) remaining stubs.

If e edges lie inside S and B leave it, there are e+B actual exposures.
If T is the outside neighbor set, the necessary condition gives
|T|<=B/2. At least e+B-|T|>=e+B/2=Dh/2 exposures are collisions.
Padding with noncollisions to Dh steps, conditional multiplication
and an index-set union bound give

 Pr_configuration[S has this property]
     <=2^(Dh)(C h/N)^(Dh/2).

Union bounding over at most (2e_Euler N/h)^h supports yields

 Pr_configuration[there is such a support of size h]
     <=[A(h/N)^eta]^h, eta=(D-2)/2.                      (5)

Its consecutive ratio is <=A exp(eta)((h+1)/N)^eta, so epsilon can
be fixed small enough that the sum is geometrically decreasing.
Conditioning all three factors simple costs a fixed positive factor
and gives the exact requested uniform-simple model. The minimum
support statements themselves are invoked only AFTER simplicity.

Nonminimal alternatives have k>=t+1 and hence h>=2t+2. Summing (5)
from 2t+2 to epsilon N gives

 O(N^[-(D-2)(t+1)])=O(N^[-nu-(D-2)]).                   (6)

For the remaining support sizes, flip fraction r=k/N lies in
[epsilon/2,1/2]. The exact integer-Boolean configuration count has
per-N entropy exponent

 Phi=H(star_+)+H(star_-)
       -(a/2)[H(internal_pair_+)+H(internal_pair_-)]
       -b H(cross_pair).

Here the standard exact count assigns ordered stars to labeled
vertices, with two internal factorial pairing weights and one cross
factorial bijection weight. It includes the root-label multinomials.
Symmetrize within internal/cross slots, delete one internal sign, and
let J_+,J_- be the conditional total correlations of retained slots.
The exact entropy loss is

 L=-Phi=((a-2)/2)(I_++I_-)+b I_cross+J_++J_-.

Keep the actual internal conditional one-slot laws, but independently
sample cross neighbors from the opposite root marginal. The resulting
comparison retained-star law Q has KL(P||Q)=J_++b I_cross<=L.
For W the inferred omitted internal sign and m(X) its original
conditional mean, the actual E(W-m(X))^2 is the conditional variance.
Under Q its increase is at least b Var(opposite_root), since a>=2.
The squared function lies in [0,4a^2] under both laws. Pinsker gives

 Phi<=-(2b^2/a^4)r^2(1-r)^2.                            (7)

This remains valid at (2,1). Uniform zero-safe factorial bounds cost
only a polynomial prefactor; finitely many fixed-degree star labels
give polynomially many empirical types. Thus the macroscopic range
has exponentially small probability. This reconstructs the upper
input without a sharp saddle or real-kernel-simplicity assumption.

Combining with (6), and using D>=3, the event E_large that an
alternative has least-oriented flip count k>=t+1 satisfies

 Pr(E_large)=O(N^(-nu-1)).                              (8)

3. COMPLETE CHARACTERIZATION OF MINIMUM FLIPS
---------------------------------------------
Suppose k=t. At every S_+ vertex,

 delta=internal_support_neighbors-cross_support_neighbors,
 internal_support_neighbors<=t-1=delta.

Therefore ALL delta other S_+ vertices are neighbors and there are
NO neighbors in S_-. The identical statement holds on S_-.
Consequently S_+ and S_- induce their K_t cliques and no cross edges
join the two support sets. Each S vertex still has exactly b internal
edges and b cross edges to the outside; all its stubs are prescribed
by its clique and these boundary edges.

For an outside vertex in V_+, the zero coordinate equation for w
requires equal numbers of neighbors in S_+ and S_-. Call this number
m_v. These are respectively internal and cross edges. The same
statement holds in V_-, with S_- internal and S_+ cross. Let W_+,W_-
be the outside vertices with m_v>0. On EACH side,

 sum_(v in W_+)m_v=bt,   sum_(v in W_-)m_v=bt.           (9)

In particular |W_+|,|W_-|<=bt. Call the flip set LEADING if equality
holds on both sides, equivalently every m_v=1. Its complete incident
motif then has

 v_0=2t+2bt, e_0=t*delta+4bt, e_0-v_0=nu.               (10)

Every S vertex has motif degree D, every W vertex motif degree two.
Both support cliques are connected and every W joins them, so every
such motif is connected and has minimum degree at least two. These
statements hold for EVERY allowed assignment of its boundary edges,
not just the earlier motif pairing vertices with the same index.

4. BOUNDARY COLLISIONS LOSE AT LEAST ONE POWER OF N
--------------------------------------------------
A nonleading minimum flip has m_v>=2 somewhere, hence

 |W_+|+|W_-|<=2bt-1.

All its incident edges still number EXACTLY e_0: the two support
cliques contribute t*delta edges, and the remaining 4bt edges are
the b internal and b cross boundary edges at each of the 2t support
vertices. No other edge touches S. In particular collision changes
the number of outside vertices, not this edge count.

There are finitely many resulting simple typed incidence patterns
because a,b,t are fixed; invalid patterns can be discarded. For any
one pattern with w_+,w_- outside vertices, its possible placements
number O(N^(2t+w_++w_-)). At specified vertices, the finite prescribed
stub-assignment upper bound gives occurrence probability O(N^(-e_0))
in the configuration model; division by positive simplicity probability
gives the same upper order in the simple model. No conditional lower
completion estimate is required for this bound.

Thus the expected count of nonleading minimum flip sets, and therefore
the probability E_collision of ANY such flip set, are

 O(N^(2t+2bt-1-e_0))=O(N^(-nu-1)).                     (11)

This argument includes all multiplicity profiles in (9). At b=1,
m_v cannot exceed one at all, so E_collision is empty. Repeated graph
edges are not being admitted: the patterns in this simple-model event
use distinct support neighbors at each W; impossible incidence patterns
have probability zero. Every possible valid minimal collision is
covered by the finite pattern enumeration.

5. COUNT EACH LEADING FLIP SET EXACTLY ONCE
------------------------------------------
Let X(G) count leading flip SETS (S_+,S_-), not embeddings of an
abstract labeled drawing. Equivalently enumerate the following data:

 * S_+ subset V_+, S_- subset V_-, both of size t;
 * W_+ subset V_+\S_+, W_- subset V_-\S_-, both of size bt;
 * four functions specifying W_+ -> S_+ internal,
   W_+ -> S_- cross, W_- -> S_- internal, and W_- -> S_+ cross,
   EACH with exactly b preimages at every support vertex.

The cliques on S_+,S_- and these four functions specify the complete
motif. Each function has (bt)!/(b!)^t choices on its fixed labeled
domain and target SET. Hence the number of data choices is

 A_N=binom(N,t)^2 binom(N-t,bt)^2
          * [ (bt)!/(b!)^t ]^4.                        (12)

A leading valid flip set uniquely determines ALL this data: every
outside support neighbor is in its W set, and each W has exactly one
internal and one cross support neighbor. Conversely occurrence of
any prescribed motif from these data makes the flip valid, because
support stubs are saturated and each W exchanges one internal/cross
edge. Extra edges among/outside W are irrelevant. Thus (12) has no
extra t!, (bt)!, automorphism, or orientation factor. It counts each
leading flip set exactly once whenever it occurs.

This distinction from the earlier fixed-motif embedding variable is
the reason the first moment below has the correct exact coefficient.
For N>2t, a counted flip set is also one unique alternative unordered
partition; its opposite orientation is not counted again.

6. EXACT SIMPLE-MODEL PROBABILITY OF ONE LEADING PATTERN
-------------------------------------------------------
For any fixed data in (12), each support vertex has prescribed internal
degree a and cross degree b. Each W vertex has one internal and one
cross prescribed edge. The number of physical stub assignments is

 c_H=(a! b!)^(2t) (ab)^(2bt).                           (13)

Each internal block has e_int=t*delta/2+bt prescribed edges, and the
cross factor has e_cross=2bt. A particular stub assignment has exact
configuration probability

 P_N^*=product_(j=0)^(e_int-1)(aN-2j-1)^(-2)
                 *[(bN)_(2bt)]^(-1).                  (14)

Different assignments for the same data are disjoint. Every prescribed
edge touches a saturated S vertex, so no configuration containing all
required distinct neighbors can contain an extra parallel copy of any
required edge; all physical stubs on these edges are determined.
Thus the unconditioned pattern probability is EXACTLY c_H P_N^*.

Let s_N be the full configuration simplicity probability. After one
assignment is prescribed, remaining pools are uniform. S has residual
degrees zero, W residual internal degrees a-1 and cross degrees b-1,
and all other vertices retain a,b. No prescribed edge has two residual-
positive endpoints, so full simplicity is EQUIVALENT to residual-factor
simplicity, without any extra forbidden-pair event.

Call that residual probability s_N^*. Its residual degree sequences
are the SAME, up to labels, for every data choice and stub assignment.
They change the original regular degrees at only O_(a,b)(1) vertices.
Consequently loop and double-edge factorial moments have the same
limits as the original factors, and

 s_N^* -> s_infty, s_N -> s_infty>0,
 s_infty=exp[-(a^2-1)/2-(b-1)^2/2].

The fixed-degree Poisson zero-event proof, or the bounded-degree
simplicity theorem, applies to these finitely modified sequences.
The conditioning ratio is therefore s_N^*/s_N=1+o(1), UNIFORMLY
over all data choices. This ratio is stronger than merely bounded
conditional simplicity and is required for the exact constant.

All residual pools are admissible. The internal residual total is
aN-tD, with tD=delta(delta+1)+2b(delta+1) even, and the cross residual
total is bN-2bt on either side. Thus no extra parity restriction has
entered. Internal graph degrees remain bounded with a regular bulk,
and the residual cross factor at b=1 is automatically simple.

Using (13)--(14), the probability in the uniform-simple model is

 c_H P_N^* (s_N^*/s_N)
  =(1+o(1)) (a! b!)^(2t) a^(-t*delta) N^(-e_0).         (15)

Indeed internal denominators contribute a^[-t*delta-2bt], cross
denominators b^(-2bt), and the W stub factor (ab)^(2bt) cancels
both boundary powers. The b! factors at saturated support vertices
remain and are canceled only partly by the boundary-function count.

7. THE EXACT FIRST MOMENT
-------------------------
Multiply (12) by (15). Since t,b are fixed,

 A_N ~ N^(v_0) ((bt)!)^2 /[(t!)^2(b!)^(4t)].

Therefore

 EX=(1+o(1)) N^(-nu)
       * ((bt)!)^2 (a! b!)^(2t)
           /[(t!)^2(b!)^(4t)a^(t*delta)]
   ~ c_(a,b) N^(-nu),                                 (16)

with c_(a,b) exactly as in (1). For (2,1), this gives 4. As another
algebra check, (3,1) gives 64 and (3,2) gives 1296.

8. FACTORIAL SECOND MOMENT: DISTINCT FLIP SETS ARE LOWER ORDER
-------------------------------------------------------------
We need a factorial, not merely ordinary, second-moment bound.

Elementary lemma: if H is connected and has minimum degree >=2,
every proper subgraph F with nonempty vertex set satisfies

 e(F)-v(F)<=e(H)-v(H)-1.                                (17)

For a spanning proper subgraph, at least one edge is removed. Otherwise
remove r>0 vertices. Connectedness gives B>0 edges to the retained set;
minimum degree gives 2E_removed+B>=2r. The number of removed edges
is E_removed+B>=r+B/2>r, and so is at least r+1. Removing more edges
only decreases excess. Common isolated vertices must be INCLUDED in
v(F), and are included in this argument.

All patterns in (12) have the same v_0,e_0 and excess nu, are connected,
and have minimum degree two. Their motif-degree-D vertices are EXACTLY
S_+ union S_-, since D>=3; their W vertices have motif degree two.
Consequently, two DISTINCT flip sets cannot have identical motif
vertex-and-edge images. This statement refers to motif degrees in
the prescribed edge image, not the full degree-D ambient graph.
The intersection of any two distinct counted data choices is therefore
a proper subgraph of one of their motifs whenever nonempty.

For any fixed overlap identification type, let F include every common
vertex and common edge. Its union J has

 e(J)-v(J)=2nu-[e(F)-v(F)].

There are finitely many boundary-pattern and overlap types. At given
vertices a prescribed simple typed union has probability O(N^(-e(J)))
by its finite stub-assignment count and bounded simplicity conditioning;
incompatible unions have probability zero. It has O(N^(v(J))) possible
placements. By (17), each proper nonempty overlap therefore contributes
O(N^(-nu-1)). Disjoint vertex sets contribute O(N^(-2nu)), also
O(N^(-nu-1)) since nu>=2.

Pairs with the SAME flip set do not appear in X(X-1). They also cannot
give multiple data for that flip set, by Section 5. There is thus NO
diagonal O(N^(-nu)) term left, and

 E[X(X-1)]=O(N^(-nu-1)).                               (18)

For every nonnegative integer X,

 0<=EX-Pr(X>=1)<=E[X(X-1)]/2,
 Pr(X>=2)<=E[X(X-1)]/2.

Hence

 Pr(X>=1)=EX+O(N^(-nu-1)) ~ c_(a,b)N^(-nu),
 Pr(X>=2)=O(N^(-nu-1)).                                (19)

No independence of disjoint occurrences is assumed; the typed union
count handles their global configuration correlations directly.

9. COMPLETE AMBIGUITY PROBABILITY AND TWO-STATE LEADING EVENT
-------------------------------------------------------------
Let E_bad=E_large union E_collision. Equations (8),(11) give
Pr(E_bad)=O(N^(-nu-1)). Outside E_bad, EVERY alternative unordered
partition is a unique leading minimum flip set, so K(G)=1+X(G).
Therefore

 |Pr[K>=2]-Pr[X>=1]|<=Pr(E_bad),
 Pr[K>=3]<=Pr(E_bad)+Pr[X>=2]=O(N^(-nu-1)).

Combining with (16),(19) proves (2)--(3). In particular, conditionally
on ambiguity, there are exactly TWO valid unordered partitions with
probability 1-O(1/N). This statement is about the Boolean partition
list; no real-nullity upper bound is inferred from it.

10. EXACT POSTERIOR AND THE BAYES CONSTANT
-----------------------------------------
Hide a balanced unordered partition uniformly; equivalently choose an
ordered balanced sign vector uniformly and identify its global negative.
For every fixed balanced sigma, any labeled graph G has likelihood

 Pr(G|sigma)=1/[G_a(N)^2 B_b(N)]

if sigma is equitable (a,b), and zero otherwise. Given sigma the two
internal graphs and cross graph are uniquely determined by G. Their
numbers depend only on N,a,b, not on the chosen vertex labels.
Thus the posterior on valid ordered signs is uniform, and each
unordered partition has exactly its two opposite signs. The posterior
on the K(G) valid unordered partitions is EXACTLY uniform.

Every exact single-partition estimator, including randomized ones,
has conditional success at most 1/K(G). Choosing ANY verified valid
partition attains it, because all posterior masses are equal. Hence

 R_N=E[1-1/K(G)].

The event K>=2 is invariant under relabeling. Its probability under
the hidden model is the same as under any fixed planted balanced
partition, so (2)--(3) apply without an extra multiplicity factor.
The fact that a leading ambiguous graph admits two possible plants
is already accounted for by the marginal graph law, not a reason to
divide (2) by two.

Finally,

 0<=R_N-(1/2)Pr[K>=2]<=(1/2)Pr[K>=3]
                                  =O(N^(-nu-1)).

Equation (4) follows. The sole factor one-half in the Bayes leading
constant comes from the two equal posterior possibilities on the
leading ambiguous event. Without a hidden prior, a deterministic
known planted vertex set would permit a trivial constant estimator,
so this qualification is essential.

11. OPTIONAL COMPUTATIONAL CONSEQUENCE AND SCOPE
------------------------------------------------
The previously proved aggregate-fiber moment gives
E|ker_F3(A-delta I)|=O_(a,b)(N^50) in this actual simple model, at all
admissible sizes. Exact kernel enumeration, integer sign verification,
and a cutoff N^(50+nu+2) therefore have cap probability O(N^(-nu-2)).
Use an incremental base-three odometer for O(N) amortized work per
field vector. On a completed run, select any verified valid partition,
rather than reporting ambiguity as automatic error. This is exactly
Bayes-optimal on that observed graph. On a cap event choose an
arbitrary output. Its contribution is o(N^(-nu)), so this deterministic
polynomial worst-case estimator attains the Bayes leading constant
c_(a,b)/2 under the uniform hidden prior. Its exponent is a theoretical
fixed-degree bound, not a practical speed prediction.

This uses the actual uniform-simple random-cross distribution and
fixed a,b. The leading motif probability does not extend unchanged
to an arbitrary prescribed/adversarial cross matrix. The result is
not a noisy-data or growing-degree theorem, and says nothing here
about an analytic eigengap or the sharp probability of arbitrary
extra real eigenvectors.
