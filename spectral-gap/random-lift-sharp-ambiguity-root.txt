SHARP AMBIGUITY RATE FOR LIFTS OF A COMPLETE REGULAR BASE
15 November 2026. Independent derivation; no historical priority assertion.

RESULT

Fix d>=3, put k=d+1, and let G be the random N-lift of H=K_k. Its old
fibers V_1,...,V_k have size N, and each base edge receives an independent
uniform perfect matching. Fix K>=k, independent of N. Let A_K be the event
that G has an equitable partition into at most K nonempty classes which
does not coarsen the planted partition P={V_1,...,V_k}.

Then, with a_d=binom(d+1,2) and beta=d-2,

  P(A_K)=a_d N^(-beta)+o(N^(-beta)).                    (1)

In particular, the failure probability of uniqueness of the finest bounded-
class equitable partition has the sharp power N^(-(d-2)); it cannot generally
be replaced by o(N^(-(d-2))). More precisely the proof below gives an error
O_(d,K)(N^(-3 beta/2)). This is an asymptotic assertion for fixed d,K.

If the base K_k is KNOWN and the equal-size planted partition is hidden by
a uniform vertex relabeling, let R_N^* be the minimum average error probability
of any estimator of the unlabeled planted partition, given G. Then

  R_N^*=(a_d/2) N^(-beta)+o(N^(-beta)).                (2)

The same leading asymptotics hold upon conditioning the total lift G to be
connected. No noise or growing-base assertion is made.

The upper bound uses the all-slot entropy and small-support mechanism, here
specialized and derived below. Its existing general-frame source chain is:

1. THE TRUE-TWIN MOTIF IS POSSIBLE AND PRESERVES THE COVER PARTITION

For each unordered color pair {a,b} and u in V_a, v in V_b, let I_uv be the
event that u,v are adjacent and have the SAME neighbor in V_c for every
c not in {a,b}. Since the matching between V_a,V_b is independent of all
remaining factors,

  P(I_uv)=N^(-1) * product_(c not in {a,b}) N^(-1)=N^(-d).

For each c the two independent matching images are uniform in V_c; these
coincidences use different independent base-edge matchings as c varies.
Thus this motif really is possible, including d=3 (a K_4 with one possibly
missing edge between its two noncentral vertices).

On I_uv, the closed neighborhoods N_G[u] and N_G[v] are identical. Hence
the transposition of u and v, fixing all other vertices, is an automorphism
of G. Applying it to the planted partition gives

  V_a'=(V_a minus {u}) union {v},
  V_b'=(V_b minus {v}) union {u},
  V_c'=V_c otherwise.

This is another balanced K_k-cover partition. For N>=2 it is a different
unlabeled partition, not merely a global renaming of classes. It therefore
witnesses A_K for every fixed K>=k.

Let X be the number of such unordered cross-fiber true-twin pairs. Exactly,

  E X=binom(k,2) N^2 N^(-d)=a_d N^(-beta).            (3)

This is an expectation of twin PAIRS, not an assumption that their events
are disjoint. We next control all overlaps.

2. FACTORIAL SECOND MOMENT AND THE LEADING MOTIF CONSTANT

Let F be the graph with two distinguished centers, their joining edge, and
d-1 other vertices each adjacent to both centers. Thus

  v(F)=d+1=k,  e(F)=2d-1,  e(F)-v(F)=d-2=beta.

A twin pair corresponds to exactly one colored copy of F with one vertex
in each old fiber: its other vertices are its uniquely determined neighbors.
Conversely every such copy with its specified center pair gives a twin pair,
since the centers already have all their d incident edges prescribed.

Expand X as the sum of these colored-copy indicators, including the finite
choices of center colors. For two copies, let J be their common graph,
including all shared vertices even if isolated in J. Write m=v(J), h=e(J).
There are O_d(N^(2k-m)) assignments for a fixed overlap pattern. If the union
of prescribed edges is compatible with the base-edge matchings, its probability
is a product of 1/(N)_r, one factor per base edge, with bounded r (indeed r<=2).
Consequently it is O_d(N^(-(2(2d-1)-h))). Incompatible prescriptions have
probability zero and may be discarded. The contribution of this pattern is

  O_d(N^(-2 beta+h-m)).                               (4)

For every pair of DISTINCT feasible motifs one has h<=m. Here is a short
graph proof covering every overlap pattern. If the two center sets coincide,
matching injectivity forces all their other neighbors to coincide, so these
are the identical motif, excluded from X(X-1). Otherwise at least one center
of the first copy is not a center of the second. It is absent there or has
degree at most two there. Removing that center from the first F leaves a
star, hence a forest. Therefore its common graph J is a forest with at most
one extra cycle, and e(J)<=v(J). Isolated common vertices only strengthen
this inequality. There are finitely many colored overlap patterns for fixed d.

It follows from (4) that

  E[X(X-1)]=O_d(N^(-2 beta)).                          (5)

The elementary inequalities
  E X - (1/2)E[X(X-1)] <= P(X>=1) <= E X
now give

  P(X>=1)=a_d N^(-beta)+O_d(N^(-2 beta)),
  P(X>=2)=O_d(N^(-2 beta)).                           (6)

No independence between different twin-pair events was assumed.

3. MACROSCOPIC ALTERNATIVES HAVE EXPONENTIALLY SMALL PROBABILITY

This section records enough of the entropy argument to specify the exact
upper-bound premise and its constants without a spectral assumption.
Let n=kN, let J be the planted n-by-k one-hot matrix, and let Y be an
equitable partition into r<=K classes with integer quotient R. Every row
of R is nonnegative and has sum d. There are finitely many possible R.
For each old type i, record the empirical law P_i of the ordered star

  (X_i,(Y_ij)_(j != i)),

where X_i is the candidate label at an old-type-i vertex and Y_ij is the
candidate label of its unique neighbor in V_j. Its root law is pi_i.
The local integer constraint is

  sum_(j != i) e_(Y_ij) = R_(X_i).                    (7)

Let mu_ij be the joint law of (X_i,Y_ij). Matching consistency says
mu_ji is its transpose; its marginals are pi_i,pi_j. For an empirical
star-count table T_i=N P_i and matching table m_ij=N mu_ij, its exact
expected contribution is

  product_i [N!/product_s T_i(s)!]
   * product_(i<j) [product_(x,y) m_ij(x,y)!/N!].     (8)

The first factor already assigns root labels. The second is the probability
that each independent uniform matching joins the prescribed directed
label-pair groups. There are polynomially many tables for fixed d,K.
Uniform Stirling estimates, including zero entries, give the exponential
factor exp(n Phi) times a polynomial, where

  Phi=(1/k)sum_i H(P_i)-(1/k)sum_(i<j) H(mu_ij).

Deleting ANY one neighbor slot t is lossless by (7). Let J_it be the
conditional total correlation of the d-1 retained slots given X_i, and
let I_ij be the mutual information in mu_ij. Averaging all deletions and
using every row sum d gives exactly

  L:=-Phi=(d-2)/(dk) sum_(i<j) I_ij
             +1/(dk) sum_i sum_t J_it >=0.           (9)

There are no repeated slots of a neighbor type in this simple-base case,
so no within-type symmetrization is needed here.

For completeness, a quantitative equality argument supplies a fixed gap.
Set delta=(1/k)sum_i(1-max_x pi_i(x)); this is the minimum fraction of
changed rows between Y and ANY old-block point map JT. For omission t,
compare the retained law to the law with the same root but with retained
neighbors independent of one another and the root. Its relative entropy is
  kappa_it=J_it+sum_(s != t) I_is.
For fixed i, equation (9) gives
  (1/k)sum_t kappa_it <=2d(d-1)/(d-2) L.

Write mu_s=E e_(Y_is), V_s=1-||mu_s||^2 and use the bounded function
  f_t=||R_(X_i)-sum_(s != t)e_(Y_is)-mu_t||^2,
whose range under both laws is [0,2d^2]. Under the actual law E f_t=V_t.
Under the independent law it is sum_(s != t)V_s+B_i with the SAME B_i>=0
for all t. The sum of expectation differences is therefore
  (d-2)sum_s V_s+d B_i >=(d-2)sum_s V_s.
Pinsker and Cauchy-Schwarz imply
  L >= (d-2)^3/[4k d^6(d-1)] (sum_s V_s)^2.
Some root law pi_j has variance at least delta, because
1-||pi_j||^2>=1-max pi_j and the average of the latter defects is delta.
Taking any old type adjacent to j gives a slot of that variance. Hence

  Phi <=-c_d delta^2,
  c_d=(d-2)^3/[4k d^6(d-1)]>0.                      (10)

For any fixed eta>0, equations (8)-(10), a polynomial table count, and
a finite union over r<=K,R yield

  P(M_eta) <= polynomial_(d,K)(N) exp(-k c_d eta^2 N), (11)

where M_eta is the event of an equitable candidate at distance at least eta*n
from every old-block point map. This concerns actual integer one-hot
labels; no finite-field equality classification or eigenspace simplicity
is used in (11).

4. SMALL SUPPORTS: REMOVE SIZE TWO TO IMPROVE THE REMAINDER

Call S bad if no vertex outside S has exactly one edge into S. Let s=|S|,
let W be its outside neighbor set of size w, let a count edges within S,
b count S-W edges, and e=a+b. Then
  2a+b=d s,  b>=2w,  e-s-w >=(d-2)s/2.
Put c=beta/2. A typed matching witness specifies S,W, all stubs at S,
selected stubs at W, and e prescribed pairs. Each base-edge matching has
pool size N. For s<=N/(4d), the probability of the prescription is at most
(2/N)^e; impossible prescriptions have probability zero.

An upper count of witnesses with fixed s,w,e is
  binom(n,s) binom(n,w) 2^(d w) (2d s)^e.
As in the source support proof, substitute the binomial bounds, use
w<=ds/2, e<=ds and (s/w)^w<=exp(s/euler), and sum the O_d(s^2) possibilities
for e,w. For some C_d>=1 this gives uniformly

  P(exists bad S with |S|=s)
       <=C_d^s (s/n)^ceil(c s).                     (12)

Choose fixed eta>0 small enough that (12) applies through eta*n and
C_d exp(c) eta^c<=1/2. The real-power upper bounds
u_s=C_d^s(s/n)^(c s) then have consecutive ratio at most one-half.
In particular, starting the sum at THREE rather than two gives

  P(B_>=3):=P(exists bad S, 3<=|S|<=eta*n)
       =O_d(N^(-3 beta/2))=o(N^(-beta)).              (13)

Singletons cannot be bad in a simple positive-degree graph. In the K_k-lift,
the bad sets of size TWO are EXACTLY the true-twin pairs counted by X:

* Two vertices in the same old fiber cannot share a neighbor, by injectivity
  of each base-edge perfect matching, so their pair is not bad.
* For u in V_a,v in V_b with a!=b, if they are not adjacent, the neighbor
  of u in V_b is outside {u,v}. It has just one edge into {u,v}, since it
  has no same-fiber edge to v. Thus this pair is not bad.
* If u,v are adjacent, badness means each of the other d-1 neighbors of u
  is also adjacent to v, and conversely. These are precisely I_uv.

This elementary classification is the reason the leading constant can
be read from the motif rather than only the order of a support bound.

5. EVERY OTHER PARTITION FORCES A TWIN OR A LARGER BAD SUPPORT

Shrink eta further so (d+1)eta*n<N, for example eta<1/[2k(d+1)]. Outside
M_eta, choose a closest old-block point map JT for any equitable Y.
Let S be its row-disagreement set, |S|<eta*n. In each old fiber there is
a vertex whose closed neighborhood avoids S, since S and all its neighbors
occupy at most (d+1)|S|<N vertices globally. Evaluating AY=YR there gives

  QT=TR over the integers, where Q=A(K_k).

Consequently V=Y-JT satisfies AV=VR. If V is nonzero, its row support S
must be bad: an outside vertex with exactly one neighbor in S would have
one nonzero row on the left and zero on the right. This does not depend
on the candidate class proportions, surjectivity of T, or spectrum.

Thus, outside M_eta and B_>=3, any Y not equal to a point map has support
of size two, and the graph has X>=1. Conversely X>=1 always supplies a
noncoarsening balanced k-class partition by Section 1. Therefore

  {X>=1} subset A_K subset {X>=1} union M_eta union B_>=3.

Combining (6), (11), and (13) proves (1), including the stated error term.
This is the matching sharp ambiguity rate; the original unrefined upper
bound O(N^(-beta)) alone would not identify its leading coefficient.

6. KNOWN BASE, HIDDEN FIBERS: EXACT POSTERIOR AND LEADING BAYES RISK

For the statistical statement, take a fixed vertex set of size kN. Choose
the ordered balanced fibers uniformly, then choose the independent matchings.
Equivalently start with named fibers and apply a uniform random vertex
permutation before revealing the graph. Without such hiding, the canonical
names (i,t) themselves reveal the fibers and no statistical impossibility
claim would be meaningful.

Let Z(G) be the number of UNLABELED balanced K_k-cover partitions compatible
with the observed labeled graph. Every ordered balanced partition has the
same prior probability, and its likelihood for any compatible G is exactly
(N!)^(-binom(k,2)). Each unlabeled partition has k! orderings. It follows
that the posterior is exactly uniform over the Z(G) compatible partitions.
The optimal conditional error is therefore 1-1/Z(G), and

  R_N^*=E[1-1/Z(G)].                                (14)

The twin event alone implies Z>=2, so (6) immediately gives a lower bound
with leading constant at least a_d/2. To show this constant is exact, work
outside M_eta and B_>=3 and suppose X<=1. Every compatible balanced k-class
partition is within eta*n<N rows of a point map JT. Such a T MUST be onto:
otherwise an omitted candidate label, whose class has N vertices, would
contribute at least N disagreements. Since T maps k types to k labels,
it is a permutation.

After renaming labels, T is the identity. If the partition differs, its
support is exactly a true-twin pair {u,v}. Both rows change. Preservation
of all k class sizes forces the two distinct original labels to be swapped;
there is exactly one resulting unlabeled alternative for this physical pair.
If X=0, Z=1. If X=1, Z=2. In particular,

  P(Z>=3) <=P(X>=2)+P(M_eta)+P(B_>=3)=o(N^(-beta)),
  P(Z>=2)=a_d N^(-beta)+o(N^(-beta)).

On Z=2 the conditional error in (14) is exactly one-half; the event Z>=3
has smaller order. This proves (2), with the same O(N^(-3 beta/2)) remainder.
The argument handles global permutations of quotient names explicitly;
the two partitions counted when X=1 are different sets of fiber subsets.

7. UNKNOWN BASE AND COMPUTATIONAL CAP: WHAT DOES AND DOES NOT FOLLOW

Equation (1) concerns blind bounded-frame identifiability and holds even
when candidate quotients differ from K_k. On the no-macro/no-large-support
event, no alternative can have more than k classes. Indeed if its support
is two, its two nonzero difference rows must cancel at each common outside
neighbor. A true-twin pair has d-1 such neighbors, so V_u+V_v=0. For one-hot
rows this says either no change or a swap of two DISTINCT point labels;
it introduces no new label. Hence all occupied candidate labels already
occur in T and r<=k. The balanced original and swapped partitions are the
two maximal k-class possibilities when X=1; their coarsenings may also occur.

A Bayes posterior when the base is itself random depends on the prior on
bases, so (2) is deliberately stated for KNOWN K_k. Giving the estimator
the true base is extra information. Thus its lower bound also rules out
o(N^(-beta)) recovery error uniformly over any unknown-base model class
which includes this hidden K_k-lift ensemble. One must not assign an
unspecified 'unknown-base posterior' the uniform law without a prior.

For polynomial algorithms, structural ambiguity and artificial work-cap
failure are different events. The existing generic cap gives interruption
O(n^-2), which need not preserve a sharp N^(-(d-2)) total failure rate when
d>=5. If the expected work is O(n^(B+3)), a cap n^(B+d+2) makes interruption
O(n^(-(d-1)))=o(N^(-beta)); its exponent is still constant for fixed d,K.
Enumerating compatible known-base partitions and choosing uniformly (for
example by reservoir sampling) then attains the leading Bayes constant,
up to this smaller cap contribution. No practical exponent is claimed.

8. CONDITIONING ON A CONNECTED TOTAL LIFT

For completeness, disconnectedness is of smaller order here. Every connected
component of a cover of the connected base meets every fiber in the same
number m of vertices. If G is disconnected, one component has 1<=m<=N/2.
For given m-subsets of all k fibers, the probability that each base matching
preserves them is binom(N,m)^(-binom(k,2)). A union bound gives

  P(G disconnected)
    <=sum_(m=1)^floor(N/2) binom(N,m)^(-r_0),
  r_0=binom(k,2)-k=k(d-2)/2.

Here r_0>=2 and r_0>beta. The m=1 term is N^(-r_0); all m>=2 terms are
O(N^(1-2r_0)) by binom(N,m)>=binom(N,2). Thus
  P(G disconnected)=O_d(N^(-r_0))=o(N^(-beta)).
Conditioning on connectedness therefore preserves (1), its leading constant,
and the Bayes asymptotic (2). In the hidden-partition experiment, conditioning
on the observed graph being connected does not change the uniform posterior
among its compatible partitions.

CHECKED SCOPE

The motif lower bound, its full factorial-second-moment overlap proof, the
size-two bad-support classification, and the posterior calculation are new
derivations in this note. The complete-base specialization of the macro and
support upper bounds was reconstructed here with its exact count and positive
entropy gap. No unpublished general-frame claim is needed beyond the displayed
specialized argument. No claim is made that every base has the same lower rate:
bases without the requisite adjacent true-twin type pattern may have fewer
ambiguities. The order N^(-(d-2)) is sharp universally because K_(d+1) attains it.
