Possible expected nearly-quadratic recovery beyond the corank-one promise
=======================================================================
Root proposal, November15. Requires actual black-box linear algebra audit.
This is not an implementation/performance claim or an audited theorem yet.

Input moment: the derived ALL-PARITY configuration mixing proof for a>=3,
and the separately root-audited cubic a2,b1 moment proof, appear to give
for one FIXED odd prime p0>b:
  E |ker_Fp0(A-(a-b)I)| <= C(a,b), uniformly N.
This is a raw moment for the configuration model transferred by conditioning
on simplicity, whose probability is a fixed positive constant. Unlike the
older matching-model contiguity proof, this DOES transfer a bounded raw
moment. The all-parity mixing proof still needs its root external audit.

If available, every polynomial moment of the base-field corank r is bounded,
and enumerating p0^r field vectors costs constant expectation. Exact integer
checks retain only planted-parameter balanced sign vectors. Failure/abstention
can then be restricted to genuine statistical ambiguity, whose sharp order
is being investigated in rsbm-sharp-ambiguity-root-proof.txt.

Potential black-box basis construction:
1. Work for computational preconditioning in an extension F_(p0^s) of size
   at least n^6; s=O(log n). Rank is unchanged from Fp0. Sparse multiplication
   remains O(n) extension-field operations for fixed degree.
2. For M=A-(a-b)I, choose invertible random diagonal left preconditioner D,
   H=DM. Need a PRIMARY theorem that with probability 1-O(n^2/q), zero is
   semisimple and all nonzero invariant factors are cyclic. For symmetric M
   in odd characteristic, some rank-sized principal minor is nonzero; this
   makes the generic zero semisimplicity plausible. Do not assume the
   nonsingular-only diagonal theorem automatically proves the full claim.
   If necessary, use a verified general black-box rank preconditioner with
   O(n polylog n) matrix-vector cost, retaining its inverse transformations.
3. Generate 2n terms of the SHIFTED scalar sequence u^T H^(j+1)v and apply
   Berlekamp--Massey. Its finite complexity ell is ALWAYS <=rank(H), since
   the entire sequence factors through im(H). Thus it is a deterministic
   lower-rank certificate; it cannot overstate rank.
4. On a good preconditioner/probe, ell=rank(H) and the returned polynomial
   g annihilates its nonzero part, g(0)!=0. If zero is semisimple, g(H)
   maps a random vector uniformly (up to a scalar) into ker(H).
5. Apply g(H) to random vectors, VERIFY Hx=0 for every returned candidate,
   and keep independent kernel vectors. Stop only after n-ell independent
   verified vectors exist. Then rank>=ell and rank<=ell, so the nullspace
   basis is certified even if random preconditioning/probing had failed.
6. Cap each trial at n+1 probes. With q>=n^6, bad trials have sufficiently
   small probability to absorb their O(n^3) cost. Good trials need expected
   O(r+1) probes; each polynomial action is O(n) sparse matrix-vector calls,
   hence O(n^2) field work. A valid proof should yield O~(n^2(r+1)) expected
   work for every fixed M, then average over the bounded-corank moment.
7. Descend the extension-field basis to Fp0, rather than enumerating the
   enormous extension-field kernel. Either take coefficient components
   of extension vectors and select a base-field basis, or normalize a
   basis to identity on r pivot rows; the canonical solution basis has
   base-field entries because M does. Verify the descent explicitly.
8. Enumerate the base-field kernel, normalize signs and verify integer
   degrees/balance. Bounded E p0^r gives bounded expected candidate work.

Need to charge irreducible-polynomial/extension construction, randomness,
all polynomial actions, probes, basis storage, descent, and certification.
The main risk is the exact rank-deficient preconditioner theorem in step2;
the existing corank-one acceptor does not establish it by itself.

If the pipeline checks, this gives expected near-quadratic BIT work for
full-model recovery/listing, with no eigengap, no real-corank-one promise,
and statistical error limited by ambiguity. It is a derived application
of classical black-box methods, not a claimed new linear algebra primitive.
