\paragraph{Scope.}
This is an exact counting counterexample to general nonregular
fiber rigidity, with no unproved second-moment, entropy-maximization,
or eigenvalue assertion. It concerns the actual fine fiber partitions,
not merely a failure of a proposed entropy inequality. Historical
novelty is not asserted. The counting construction was proposed by the
root agent and reconstructed independently here.

\section{Observation model, including a helpful genie}
Fix integers $a,b\ge3$ and a positive integer $N$. The base is the
connected simple graph $K_{a,b}$, with minimum degree at least three.
There are $a$ labelled left fibers $L_1,\ldots,L_a$, each of size $N$,
and a right vertex set $V_R$ of size $bN$. A genie reveals all left
fibers, with their order, and the set $V_R$. It does not reveal the
right fiber partition.

Choose an ordered partition $(R_1,\ldots,R_b)$ of $V_R$, each part
having size $N$, uniformly. Independently for each pair $(i,j)$,
choose a uniform perfect matching between $L_i$ and $R_j$.
Observe the uncoloured union graph $G$ and the genie information.
Conditional on the hidden partition this is exactly the usual random
$N$-lift of $K_{a,b}$: every base edge is replaced by an independent
uniform matching. The graph is simple automatically. Every left
vertex has degree $b$, and every right vertex has degree $a$.

A compatible right partition is an unordered partition of $V_R$ into
$b$ sets of size $N$ such that every left vertex has exactly one
neighbour in each right set. A right vertex already has one neighbour
in each of the revealed left fibers. Thus every compatible right
partition, together with the fixed left fibers, is a full fine fiber
partition with quotient exactly $K_{a,b}$. Write $W(G)$ for the number
of such unordered partitions.

\begin{theorem}[Exact recovery bound]\label{thm:count}
For any estimator, including a randomized estimator using the genie,
the probability of recovering the hidden right partition up to
permutation of its $b$ labels is at most
\[
 S_{a,b,N}:=
 b!\frac{[(bN)!]^{a-1}}
 {(b!)^{aN}(N!)^{(a-1)b}}.
\]
Also $\E[1/W(G)]\le S_{a,b,N}$.
Put
\[
 \Gamma_{a,b}=a\log(b!)-(a-1)b\log b.
\]
For fixed $a,b$,
\[
 S_{a,b,N}=
 \Theta_{a,b}\!\left(
 N^{-(a-1)(b-1)/2}\exp\{-\Gamma_{a,b}N\}\right).
\]
Whenever $\Gamma_{a,b}>0$, for every fixed $0<c<\Gamma_{a,b}$,
\[
 \Prb\bigl(W(G)\le e^{cN}\bigr)
 \le e^{cN}S_{a,b,N}=e^{-(\Gamma_{a,b}-c)N+O_{a,b}(\log N)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The number of ordered right partitions is $(bN)!/(N!)^b$.
For each such partition the $ab$ matchings have $(N!)^{ab}$
possibilities. Each tuple of those matchings determines a unique graph;
conversely, the graph and ordered partition determine all its matchings.
Consequently the sample space is uniform on exactly
\[
 T=\frac{(bN)!}{(N!)^b}(N!)^{ab}
   =(bN)!(N!)^{(a-1)b}
\]
marked pairs consisting of a graph and a compatible ordered partition.

An observed graph is specified by a map from $V_R$ to $L_i$ for each
$i$, where every vertex of $L_i$ has exactly $b$ preimages. Each such
map has $(bN)!/(b!)^N$ possibilities. The maps for the $a$ left fibers
are independent combinatorial choices; their images lie in different
left fibers, so they always define a simple graph. Hence the total
number of graphs with those degrees and the revealed-neighbour property
is exactly
\[
 U=\left[\frac{(bN)!}{(b!)^N}\right]^a.
\]
Some of those graphs might have no compatible right partition; let
$\mathcal G$ be the support of the lift observation law. Only the
upper bound $|\mathcal G|\le U$ is needed.

Every compatible unordered partition has exactly $b!$ orderings,
since its nonempty parts are distinct. Uniformity on marked pairs gives
\[
 \Prb(G)=\frac{b!W(G)}T,
\]
and the posterior conditional on $G$ is uniform on its $W(G)$
unordered compatible partitions. No estimator can have conditional
success probability greater than $1/W(G)$; randomization cannot
improve that maximum atom. Averaging gives
\[
 \E\frac1{W(G)}
 =\sum_{G\in\mathcal G}\frac{b!W(G)}T\frac1{W(G)}
 =\frac{b!|\mathcal G|}T
 \le\frac{b!U}T=S_{a,b,N}.
\]
Stirling's formula, with $a,b$ fixed, gives
\[
 \log S_{a,b,N}=-\Gamma_{a,b}N
       -\frac{(a-1)(b-1)}2\log N+O_{a,b}(1).
\]
Finally, on $W(G)\le e^{cN}$ one has $1/W(G)\ge e^{-cN}$.
Markov's inequality applied to $1/W(G)$ proves the asserted probability
bound. This uses no estimate of $\E W(G)$ or of its second moment.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A fixed base of minimum degree three]
For $a=3,b=12$,
\[
 \Gamma_{3,12}=3\log(12!)-24\log12
 =\log\frac{7133328125}{5159780352}>0
 \quad(\text{approximately }0.3238838921).
\]
Thus a random $N$-lift of the fixed base $K_{3,12}$ has exponentially
many compatible fine fifteen-class fiber partitions with probability
tending to one, even when all three left fibers are revealed. Any
Bayesian exact-recovery estimator has success probability at most
$O(N^{-11}e^{-\Gamma_{3,12}N})$.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Fine partition rigidity really fails.}
All the $W(G)$ partitions have exactly $a+b$ nonempty classes and the
same class sizes. Two different such partitions cannot be proper
coarsenings of one another: a coarsening preserving the number of
nonempty parts is the identical partition. In particular the additional
partitions do not merely exhibit several quotients coarser than one
recoverable finest base-fiber partition. They are distinct partitions
at precisely the planted resolution and with the very same base.
This refutes a general assertion that every equitable partition into
at most $a+b$ classes must coarsen the planted fibers.

\paragraph{Fixed partitions and equivariance.}
The theorem explicitly uses the uniform hidden-partition prior, as is
appropriate when the fibers are blind. An arbitrary estimator could
memorize one fixed labelling, so no meaningful impossibility assertion
can quantify over arbitrary estimators at that one publicly fixed
labelling alone. The Bayesian bound implies the impossibility of a
uniform guarantee over hidden labellings. For a right-vertex
permutation-equivariant estimator its success probability is identical
at every fixed planted partition, so the same upper bound applies there.

The structural claim about $W(G)$ needs no estimator equivariance:
$W(G)$ is invariant under right-vertex relabelling, and the conditional
lift laws at different fixed partitions are transported to one another
by such permutations. Its distribution, and therefore the exponential
ambiguity conclusion, is the same for every fixed planted partition.

\paragraph{A genuine modular-moment obstruction as well.}
Take the candidate quotient $R=A_{K_{a,b}}$ and encode all compatible
ordered fiber partitions by one-hot matrices $Y$. They satisfy the
integer equation $AY=YR$ and hence its reduction modulo two.
The vector-space kernel of this intertwiner equation therefore has
at least $b!W(G)$ elements, even after keeping the left labels fixed.
If $\Gamma_{a,b}>0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:count} gives a linear-in-$N$
lower bound on its binary nullity with probability tending to one.
Consequently an unrestricted polynomial moment bound for that kernel
cannot hold for all fixed nonregular frames of minimum degree three.

The unbalanced bipartite adjacency matrix also has a deterministic zero
eigenspace of dimension at least $|b-a|N$, by its rectangular block
form. That spectral fact alone would not establish fiber ambiguity;
the exact partition count above is what establishes it.

\paragraph{The threshold within this counting family.}
For $a=3$, $b=12$ is the first positive-$\Gamma$ integer $b\ge3$.
For $b\ge7$,
\[
 \Gamma_{3,b+1}-\Gamma_{3,b}
 =\log(b+1)-2b\log(1+1/b)>\log(b+1)-2>0.
\]
The remaining finite values are checked directly with integer
factorials; at $b=11$ the exponential ratio is
$47784725839872000000/61159090448414546291<1$.
For each fixed $b$, $\Gamma_{a,b}$ decreases as $a$ increases, since
$\log(b!)<b\log b$. Thus, with $3\le a\le b$, $K_{3,12}$ has the
fewest base vertices among examples detected by this particular bound.
This is not a claim of a smallest counterexample among all bases.
