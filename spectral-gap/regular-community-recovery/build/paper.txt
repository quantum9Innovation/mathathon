              Exact algebraic recovery in regular block models
                        without spectral separation
                                         Research manuscript

                                          November 15, 2026


                                                Abstract
         For every fixed pair of integers a > b > 0, we prove polynomial-time exact recovery in the
     two-class regular stochastic block model at every admissible size, with probability 1 − O(N −2 ).
     We use algebraic constraints rather than separation of the planted eigenvalue from the remaining
     spectrum. Two complementary arguments are developed. A configuration-model count bounds
     a fixed finite-field kernel moment by a polynomial; exact enumeration then reduces recovery
     to uniqueness of sign eigenvectors. A separate argument applies strong finite-field mixing to
     independent internal graphs and excludes exact collisions with every fixed quotient eigenvalue,
     uniformly over prescribed cross graphs. It yields exact rational recovery for general fixed sym-
     metric degree frames, including repeated and irrational quotient eigenvalues. The statements
     distinguish the random-cross model from the prescribed-cross model, and sign uniqueness from
     real eigenspace simplicity. All algorithms include their algebraic and bit costs. Aggregate-fiber
     entropy gives an O(N 24 ) binary kernel moment for every fixed degree pair, yielding an O(n28 )
     bounded-work recovery algorithm. The finite-field mixing and contiguity inputs are classical;
     no claim of historical priority is made.

1    Models, statements, and proof routes
Let a > b > 0 be fixed integers, D = a + b, and δ = a − b. The two-class regular stochastic
block model has two planted classes V+ , V− of size N . Each class contains an independent uniform
simple a-regular graph. Independently, the cross edges form a uniform simple b-regular bipartite
graph. All limits are through admissible sizes, in particular aN is even. A uniformly hidden planted
partition can be obtained by a final independent relabeling; every algorithm below receives only
the relabeled adjacency matrix and the fixed degree parameters. Set n = 2N and σ = (1N , −1N )
before relabeling. For every realization,

                                     A1n = D1n ,       Aσ = δσ.                                      (1)

A sign vector τ ∈ {−1, 1}n satisfies Aτ = δτ if and only if each vertex has a neighbors with its own
sign and b with the opposite sign. Such a vector is automatically balanced: D1T τ = 1T Aτ = δ1T τ
and D − δ = 2b > 0. The unavoidable ambiguity is the exchange of the two signs.

Theorem 1.1 (Recovery in the random-cross model). For every fixed pair of integers a > b >
0, there is a deterministic algorithm with polynomial worst-case bit complexity which, given the
adjacency matrix of the two-class model and a, b, returns the planted partition up to exchange with
probability 1 − Oa,b (N −2 ). This holds at every admissible block size and requires no inequality
placing δ outside the remaining spectrum. The algorithm uses OD (n28 ) elementary work and O(n2 )
storage; its untruncated expected work is Oa,b (n25 ).
    An untruncated version lists all sign vectors satisfying Aτ = δτ , up to global sign, with expected
polynomial work. In particular, the planted partition is the unique such partition with probability
1 − Oa,b (N −2 ).

   The proof of Theorem 1.1 has two independent parts. First, a finite-field moment makes the
candidate list polynomial with high probability. Second, Boolean rigidity makes the verified list
unique. Neither step asserts that kerR (A − δI) is a line.

Relation to earlier work. Brito, Dumitriu, Ganguly, Hoffman and Tran introduced this regular
model and proved rigidity, spectral recovery in a parameter regime, and bootstrapping results [1].
Barucca studied equitable graphs and proposed full recovery beyond the usual adjacency outlier
regime, supported by numerical evidence [2]. The algebraic mixing input in our prescribed-cross
theorem is Mészáros’s strong fixed-group distribution theorem [4]; its use of finite-field moments
and fixed primes follows an established method. The model transfer is the contiguity theory of
Greenhill–Janson–Kim–Wormald and its predecessors [5, 6, 7].
    Zhu and Zhu’s Lemma 9.1 states simplicity of the two planted eigenvalues for the random-
cross model [3]. Thus a simplicity statement for that model already appears in print. Its cited
argument uses a random-lift spectral bound; such a bound by itself does not exclude an exact
collision with a planted eigenvalue lying in the bulk. We give the complete finite-field argument
and state the stronger uniform prescribed-cross quantifiers explicitly. The present paper does not
certify historical novelty. Section 8 records the precise scope of each conclusion and the distinction
between recovery and eigenspace simplicity.

2.5   Conditioning gives exactly the uniform-simple model
Every simple labeled internal a-regular graph has (a!)N distinguished-stub realizations. Every
simple labeled b-regular bipartite graph has (b!)2N cross-bijection realizations. Conditioning the
three independent factors on simplicity therefore gives exactly the three independent uniform-
simple laws in our model. At fixed degrees their joint simplicity probability is bounded below by
a positive constant along all admissible sizes. This follows, for example, from the ordinary and
bipartite configuration criteria in Janson [8, Theorems 1.1 and 5.1]; the latter theorem credits the
bipartite criterion to Blanchet and Stauffer. For a nonnegative random variable Z and the joint
simplicity event S, E[Z | S] ≤ EZ/P(S). Applying this to (18) proves Theorem 1.2. This transfer
uses bounded-probability conditioning, rather than contiguity between different graph models.

5.4   Event transfer and its uniform quantifiers
For each fixed d ≥ 3 and even N , the union of d independent uniform perfect matchings is mutually
contiguous with the regular configuration model conditioned to have no loops. The all-degree-one
specialization is included in the loopless exception of Greenhill–Janson–Kim–Wormald [5, Theorem
1.3]. Their Corollary 1.1 supplies the corresponding simple-graph contiguity. Earlier factorization
results and a direct account of the transfer chain appear in [6, 7].
    Here is why finite products of these model changes are legitimate. If νN is contiguous with
respect to µN and 0 ≤ gN ≤ 1, then

                                  EµN gN → 0      =⇒      EνN gN → 0.                             (40)

Indeed Markov transfers {gN > η} as a vanishing event for each η > 0, and Eν gN ≤ η + ν(gN > η);
then let η ↓ 0. Apply this to sections of the failure event while changing the independent internal
block laws one at a time. Conditioning loopless configurations on simplicity costs a bounded
factor and gives uniform simple internal graphs. Equivalently one can condition matching sums on
simplicity first and use the simple-graph contiguity directly. Neither route asserts that a simple
matching sum is itself uniform.
    For every deterministic sequence of permitted cross matrices the failure probability therefore
tends to zero in the target model. If its supremum did not tend to zero, choose a violating de-
terministic sequence, contradicting that conclusion. This proves the uniformity in (5). Averaging
gives any joint random cross law independent of the internal factors. It does not give simultaneous
success for all cross matrices on the same internal realization, or permit selecting cross matrices
after inspecting the internals. The transfer concerns the bounded failure event; contiguity alone
does not transfer (38) as an expectation. This completes Theorem 1.4.

8      Scope, comparison, and remaining questions

restrictions on internal degree and block parity. The assumptions and output guarantees are col-
lected below. All high-probability statements are asymptotic with the degrees and number of classes

   The polynomial enumeration exponents are proof bounds, not claims of practical efficiency. In
particular the nearly quadratic certified bound is not the runtime proved for the all-parameter
theorem. The all-parameter theorem does not require uniqueness of all real eigenvectors, and its
proof does not establish that uniqueness.

Exact collision versus a numerical gap. The frame theorem excludes equality of a new eigen-
value with any fixed quotient target. It gives no quantitative distance to nearby new eigenvalues.
Exact rational computation and modular certificates therefore have a different justification from
floating-point eigenvector approximation. There is no claim of noise stability, robustness to edge
errors, or finite-precision rounding guarantees.

Uniformity and independence. Uniformity over prescribed cross matrices means the supre-
mum of the failure probability tends to zero. It does not mean one draw of the internal graphs
succeeds simultaneously against every possible cross matrix. An adaptive cross matrix chosen after
seeing the internal graphs is outside the model. Conversely, the full all-admissible-size recovery
theorem uses the randomness of its independent bipartite configuration factor in its exact count; it
does not establish that theorem for arbitrary prescribed cross graphs. A globally regular equitable
graph chosen adversarially need not identify a designated planted partition.

Classical inputs and prior statements. The moment-and-rigidity route uses configuration
counting, entropy subadditivity, mutual information, Pinsker’s inequality, and the usual small-
support exploration principle. The uniform-simple conditioning criterion is due to Janson in the
ordinary case and Blanchet–Stauffer in the bipartite case, as recorded in [8]. The fixed-target
route imports Mészáros’s strong distribution theorem and classical regular-model contiguity. The
algebraic root-prime reduction is the elementary Schur argument combined with Bézout and integer
minors. The fast algorithm uses Wiedemann recurrences, classical diagonal preconditioning, and a
prime-interval estimate. These are credited inputs rather than newly claimed primitives.
    Brito et al.’s explicit spectral recovery theorem assumes (a − b)2 > 4(a + b − 1) and even in-
ternal degree in the cited version [1, Theorem 2]; its rigidity theorem is stated for sufficiently large
fixed degrees. Barucca’s work proposes recovery throughout the assortative range and presents
numerical evidence [2]. Zhu–Zhu’s cited version both records the full-range efficient-recovery con-
jecture in its introduction and states unqualified planted-eigenvalue simplicity in Lemma 9.1 [3].
The latter statement overlaps a random-cross specialization of our fixed-target result and must be
acknowledged when discussing priority. The cited random-lift norm bound only separates a planted
eigenvalue when it lies outside the allowed new-spectrum interval; an additional argument is needed
for an exact bulk collision. Our prescribed-cross proof gives that argument under its stated parity
and degree hypotheses. We make no assertion that all the derived results here are historically first.

Unresolved extensions. The all-parameter recovery theorem concerns fixed known positive
integers a > b, equal class sizes, and the exact independent-factor model. The frame theorem
concerns fixed known symmetric integer frames with equal even classes. Growing degrees, growing
numbers of classes, unequal block sizes, unknown general frames, correlated factors, noisy observa-
tions, effective spectral-gap estimates, and optimal practical runtimes require further arguments.
In particular the present prescribed-cross eigenspace proof leaves odd block size at internal degree
four and the internal-degree-two spectral question outside its coverage. Those spectral limitations
do not remove any parameter pair from the random-cross recovery theorem.
