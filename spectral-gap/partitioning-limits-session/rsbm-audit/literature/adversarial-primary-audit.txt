DISPOSITION
-----------
No fatal mathematical applicability gap was identified in this source.
The stated even-block-size, d1>=3 theorem is a valid-looking elementary
two-block consequence of a very strong existing mixing theorem, followed
by established contiguity. The proof does not establish a new mixing
technique, a polynomial failure rate, odd-N coverage for even d1, or (2,1).

There is a material PRIOR-STATEMENT obstacle to novelty: Zhu--Zhu v3,
Lemma9.1, ALREADY STATES planted-eigenvalue multiplicity one without the
SNR qualification. Even if its cited justification is insufficient,
the random-cross conclusion cannot fairly be described as an entirely
new statement absent from the literature. The defensible contribution
is a complete derivation of an even-N subcase of that already asserted
claim, with additional uniformity over prescribed cross matrices. A
missing proof of a previously stated lemma is different from a wholly
unstated theorem. The source itself acknowledges the lemma's issue.

ADVERSARIAL PROOF CHECKS RELEVANT TO THE PRIOR-ART BOUNDARY
---------------------------------------------------------

* The q-affine estimate is correct even when p divides N or d1:
  p must avoid d2, and then d2*s(x)=s(b) has p^(N-1) solutions. The
  constant-input contribution is at most one. Thus the factor2 bound
  used when summing point errors does not hide an exponential loss.
* Independence of C,D and B being fixed is essential. The result is
  uniform over deterministic B sequences, not over B chosen adaptively
  after inspecting C and D. The manuscript's prescribed-B wording is
  consistent with that limitation.
* The deterministic rank bound for B is valid over every field: a row
  intersects at most d2(d2-1) other rows and disjoint nonzero supports
  give independent rows. No random-B nonsingularity is being assumed.
* Fixed-p-then-large-p limit order is sound. The O(p) kernel expectation
  gives an O(1/p) bound for real nullity>=2, and the real event implies
  modular nullity>=2 for every p via integer minors. No growing-p error
  estimate is needed or claimed.
* Finite-product contiguity plus the adversarial deterministic-sequence
  argument justifies uniformity after transfer. There is no illicit
  transfer of the kernel expectation itself.
* Once real nullity1 is established, rational elimination is polynomial
  in the bit model for fixed degrees. This recovery step is elementary;
  its simplicity is not a novelty advantage over prior algebraic methods.

A FURTHER SIMPLE CONSEQUENCE OF THE IMPORTED INPUT
-------------------------------------------------
The same mixing theorem immediately gives the following one-block
deformation observation (not a claimed new theorem): for any deterministic
matrix T over Fp with T1=T^T1=d1*1, uniformly over T,

  E|ker(C-T)| <= 2p+e_N.

Constants contribute exactlyp. For all nonconstant inputs the sum
constraint is automatic, each reference mass is p^(-N+1), and there
are at mostp^N inputs. Thus E<=p+p+e_N. For integer T with those row
and column sums, the fixed-prime argument gives only the forced constant
real kernel with high probability in the matching model, with usual
contiguity transfer. The stated RSBM theorem is not literally this
one-block statement, but this observation shows how much power is already
contained in the2018/2020 input. The two-block first moment is a useful
application, not a new finite-field universality framework.
