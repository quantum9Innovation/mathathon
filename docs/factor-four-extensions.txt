1. EXACT FINITE-DIMENSION CROSSOVER THEOREM

Fix 0<alpha<1 and beta=1-alpha. For each positive integer n, let
H_(s,n)^F be the optimal POWER constant

  sup tr|F(B)-F(A)|^s / tr|B-A|^(alpha s)

over the same input class as the manuscript, restricted to n-by-n
matrices. Zero padding identifies this with inputs of dimension <=n.
The maps F are:

  PSD power: positive A,B, F(A)=A^alpha;
  Hermitian even: F(A)=|A|^alpha;
  Hermitian odd: F(A)=sgn(A)|A|^alpha;
  square absolute right/left: F(X)=|X|^alpha or |X*|^alpha;
  square polar: F(X)=X|X|^(alpha-1), defined by singular values.

Put

  c_F = 1/(4 pi^2 alpha beta)      for the PSD power,
  c_F = 1/(pi^2 alpha beta)        for the other five maps,

so the checked dimension-free theorem is
s^2 H_s^F -> 2c_F.

THEOREM. Let s_j ->0 and let n_j be arbitrary positive integers with

  s_j^2 n_j -> t,   0<t<infinity.

Then

  s_j^2 H_(s_j,n_j)^F -> Phi_(c_F)(t),               (1)

  Phi_c(t)=2c[1-(1+sqrt(t/c)) exp(-sqrt(t/c))].        (2)

The two endpoint regimes have the sharper interpretations

  H_(s_j,n_j)^F / n_j ->1       if s_j^2 n_j ->0,     (3)
  s_j^2 H_(s_j,n_j)^F ->2c_F    if s_j^2 n_j ->infinity. (4)

In particular, for each fixed n, H_(s,n)^F ->n as s->0.

The statement is for square matrices. An arbitrary aspect-ratio
crossover is NOT automatically included: a rectangular absolute map
can have a larger output support than the smaller input dimension.

2. THE EXACT COMPRESSION IDENTITY

Start with a finite Hermitian rank-one pair

  A=A*, B=A+vv*, ||v||=1.

For a finite conjugate-closed set Z of nonreal poles let P be the
orthogonal projection onto

  K=span{(A-zI)^(-1)v : z in Z}.

For PSD A and negative real poles the same definition is used without
conjugate duplication. Work on K and set

  A'=PAP|K,  v'=Pv,  B'=A'+v'v'^*.

Do not renormalize v'. Its squared norm is at most one. A' is
Hermitian, and is PSD whenever A is PSD; the corresponding B' is
admissible as well.

For z in Z put u_z=(A-zI)^(-1)v in K. Then exactly

  (A'-zI)u_z=P(A-zI)u_z=Pv=v',
  (A'-zI)^(-1)v'=u_z,
  v'^*(A'-zI)^(-1)v'=v* u_z.                         (5)

The last scalar equality uses Pu_z=u_z. Woodbury gives

  (B-zI)^(-1)-(A-zI)^(-1)
   =-u_z u_(conj z)^*/[1+v*(A-zI)^(-1)v].           (6)

The compressed pair has exactly the same vectors and denominator in
(6). Since Z is conjugate closed, both range and domain vectors lie
in K. Therefore each resolvent DIFFERENCE is exactly reproduced as
an operator on the original space, extending the compressed operator
by zero on K-perp. For negative real poles the same statement holds
with u_z u_z*.

This proves exactness for any linear combination of these resolvent
differences, including every node in the manuscript's Gaussian
quadrature. It is an elementary rank-one rational Krylov exactness
identity; the general rational-Krylov principle is prior work.

3. SHARP DIMENSION FOR A COMPRESSED MATRIX-FUNCTION MODEL

Fix d inside the admissible strip and let L be sufficiently large.
The manuscript's positive Gaussian quadrature Q_L gives

  ||Delta-Q_L|| <= exp(-L),
  Delta=F(B)-F(A).

PSD: the nodes are negative real poles -t_j and their number obeys

  q_L <= c_d L^2+O_(alpha,d)(L^(7/4)),
  c_d=1/(4 pi d alpha beta), d<pi.

Hermitian even/odd: each real t_j uses poles +it_j and -it_j; thus
the total pole count, and hence dim K, obeys

  dim K <= c_d L^2+O_(alpha,d)(L^(7/4)),
  c_d=1/(2 pi d alpha beta), d<pi/2.

In either case c_d decreases to the relevant c_F as d approaches
the strip boundary. The node ranks and pole counts agree, which is
the decisive feature of this compression.

The compressed pair has ||B'-A'||=||v'||^2<=1. The same quadrature
and the same error bound apply to this pair without rescaling: the
uniform resolvent envelope for unit input norm also bounds every
input difference of norm <=1. This is immediate from the separate
resolvent bounds and the resolvent identity used in the manuscript.
The quadrature weights/nodes depend only on alpha,d,L and their
uniform envelope constant, not on the background spectrum.

By (5)--(6), its quadrature is exactly the SAME Q_L, as an operator
on the original space. Hence, with Delta'=F(B')-F(A') extended by zero,

  ||Delta-Delta'|| <=2 exp(-L),
  dim K <= c_d L^2+O_(alpha,d)(L^(7/4)).              (7)

This is stronger than approximation by an arbitrary low-rank matrix:
the approximant is itself the fractional correction of a compressed
rank-one input pair. It preserves the admissible PSD/Hermitian class.
If v'=0 the reproduced node differences vanish; otherwise the Holder
ratio of the compressed pair is at least tr|Delta'|^s because its
denominator ||v'||^(2 alpha s)<=1. No normalization loss occurs.

The construction uses shifted solves and an orthonormal basis. Their
arithmetic cost must be paid. Formula (7) by itself gives no near-input
runtime bound for arbitrary implicit or raw-factor matrices.

4. UPPER BOUND IN THE CROSSOVER THEOREM

The manuscript's rank-one reduction works within each fixed dimension,
so H_(s,n) is a supremum over unit-norm rank-one increments. All such
corrections obey a fixed operator bound M_alpha (M_alpha=1 for PSD)
and, for each fixed admissible d,

  N_Delta(L) <= min(n,c_d L^2+C_d L^(7/4)+K_d), L>=0. (8)

The constants are uniform in n and the background matrices. Values
above one contribute at most n(M_alpha^s-1) to the power trace.
After multiplication by s^2 this vanishes when n s^2 stays bounded.

For the remaining layer-cake integral put x=sL. If n s^2->t, then

  s^2 tr|Delta|^s
  <= o(1)+int_0^infinity e^(-x)
      min(s^2 n,c_d x^2+C_d s^(1/4)x^(7/4)+K_d s^2) dx.

The integrand is dominated by an integrable multiple of e^(-x),
because s^2 n is bounded. Dominated convergence therefore gives

  limsup s^2 H_(s,n)
       <= int_0^infinity e^(-x) min(t,c_d x^2) dx.

Now let d approach the strip boundary. Continuity under this integral
gives the same expression with c_d replaced by c=c_F. Writing
a=sqrt(t/c), direct integration yields

  int_0^infinity e^(-x) min(t,c x^2) dx
       =2c[1-(1+a)e^(-a)].                           (9)

This argument applies also to square right/left absolute and polar
maps, whose output singular-value count is at most n and whose sharp
uniform count bounds have the whole-line coefficient c_F.

5. LOWER BOUND: CAP THE EXTREMAL PROFILE WITHOUT LOSING DIMENSION

For the PSD or Hermitian map choose finite unit-norm rank-one pairs
with corrections Delta_s satisfying

  tr|Delta_s|^s >= H_s-1.

The proof of the manuscript's deficit lemma gives more than one
threshold: for EVERY fixed x>0,

  s^2 N_(Delta_s)(x/s) -> c x^2.                     (10)

The same conclusion holds when ||Delta_s||<=M_alpha>1: the uniform
rank-one count bound at ONE fixed positive logarithmic threshold also
bounds the count throughout the bounded negative logarithmic interval.
Its contribution is therefore o(1) after the s^2 scaling, even though
the original matrix dimensions may be arbitrarily large. This is a
uniform-over-examples extremal-profile statement;
it is not a fixed-operator Weyl law.

Fix d inside the strip and 0<eta<1. Put

  b=(1-eta) sqrt(t/c_d),  L_s=b/s.

Because c_d b^2=(1-eta)^2 t<t, (7) and n_s s^2->t imply
dim K_s<=n_s for all sufficiently small s. Let Delta'_s be the
compressed correction. By (7), its distance from Delta_s is at most
2 exp(-b/s).

Fix 0<x<b and choose 0<x'<x. For small s,

  exp(-x'/s)-2 exp(-b/s) > exp(-x/s).

The singular-value perturbation inequality therefore gives

  N_(Delta'_s)(x/s) >= N_(Delta_s)(x'/s).

Use (10) and let x' increase to x. For x>=b use monotonicity and
thresholds below b. Altogether

  liminf s^2 N_(Delta'_s)(x/s) >= c min(x^2,b^2)
       for every x>0.                               (11)

The compressed input increment has norm <=1, so its ratio is at least
its output power trace. Pad by zeros up to n_s, apply layer cake on
the positive logarithmic half-line, and then use Fatou:

  liminf s^2 H_(s,n_s)
   >=int_0^infinity e^(-x)c min(x^2,b^2) dx
    =2c[1-(1+b)e^(-b)].                             (12)

Finally let eta decrease to zero, then d approach the strip boundary.
The number b increases to sqrt(t/c), so (12) matches (9) exactly.
This proves (1)--(2) for PSD and the two Hermitian maps.

For the square right/left absolute maps use the Hermitian even
subclass in this lower argument. For the square polar map use the
Hermitian odd subclass. These subclasses attain the same c_F as
their respective global upper bounds, proving the same crossover.

6. ENDPOINT REGIMES

6.1 Fixed dimension

Choose a positive diagonal A with n distinct eigenvalues and a unit
vector v having every coordinate nonzero. For 0<alpha<1 the positive
update correction is strictly positive definite. In fact its resolvent
integral quadratic form on a vector z can vanish only if

  v*(A+tI)^(-1)z=0 for all t>0.

Distinct eigenvalues and nonzero coordinates of v force every residue,
and hence z, to vanish. Thus tr[(A+vv*)^alpha-A^alpha]^s ->n.
The universal operator bound gives H_(s,n)<=n M_alpha^s by rank-one
reduction, so H_(s,n)->n. The same positive pair is admissible for
all five other square maps, where their outputs equal the PSD power.

6.2 If n_s s^2 ->0, including n_s->infinity

For each delta>0, (10)--(11) applied with auxiliary exponent 1/sqrt(n)
and with fixed compression parameters provides, for every sufficiently
large n, an admissible rank-one pair in dimension <=n with at least
(1-delta)n singular values larger than exp(-C_delta sqrt(n)).
Here is an explicit parameter choice: choose admissible d and numbers
0<a<b<1/sqrt(c_d), with c a^2>1-delta; this is possible by taking d
near the boundary. Use (10) at x=a and compress at depth b sqrt(n).
Then dim K<=n and the count claim follows, after an arbitrarily small
extra allowance in delta for strict thresholds.

Its s-power trace is at least

  (1-delta)n exp(-C_delta s sqrt(n)).

When n_s s^2->0 the exponential tends to one. The preceding
upper n M_alpha^s and delta->0 prove (3). Bounded n_s are covered
by the fixed-dimension statement, uniformly over a finite set of n.

6.3 If n_s s^2 ->infinity

The global dimension-free bound gives limsup<=2c. For any fixed
T>0, pad examples of dimension floor(T/s^2) to n_s. The proved
finite-t theorem gives liminf>=Phi_c(T). Let T->infinity to get
liminf>=2c. This proves (4).

7. EXACT FINITE-DIMENSION COUNTING CROSSOVER

Let N_(F,r,n)(L) be the manuscript's worst-case singular-value count
at error exp(-L), with rank(B-A)<=r, ||B-A||=1, and square size <=n.
For each fixed positive integer r and any sequence n_L with
n_L/L^2 -> t in (0,infinity),

  N_(F,r,n_L)(L)/L^2 -> min(t,r c_F).                (13)

Upper: the manuscript's uniform rank-r bound and the cap n_L give
limsup<=min(t,r c_F).

For r=1, fix 0<a<b<1 and c_d b^2<t. Apply the original extremal
profile at auxiliary exponent 1/L and compress at log-depth bL.
The compression preserves at least (c a^2+o(1))L^2 singular values
larger than exp(-L), since their original threshold exp(-aL) is much
larger than the compression error 2exp(-bL). Taking a,b up to
min(1,sqrt(t/c_d)), then d to the strip boundary, yields the r=1
lower min(t,c_F). To impose the counting class's exact unit input
norm, ONLY AFTER proving this preservation, divide both compressed
endpoints by theta=||B'-A'||, if theta<1. The already nonzero
correction ensures theta>0; homogeneity multiplies all output singular
values by theta^(-alpha)>=1, so no count is lost. No claim of matching
the original quadrature after this final normalization is needed.
For r>1 take r direct sums in dimensions at most
floor(n_L/r); their input norm remains one and their input rank is r.
This gives min(t,r c_F). The square-map subclasses transfer as above.

When n_L=o(L^2), the sharper saturation N_(F,r,n_L)(L)/n_L->1
holds (with n_L>=r eventually if exact rank r is desired), by the
endpoint construction in Section 6.2. For bounded n_L the strictly
positive definite rank-one example already eventually has full count.

8. WHAT THE FACTOR FOUR NOW MEANS

The asymptotic power constant and the required approximation rank are
four times larger on the full line than for PSD powers. The theorem
above shows precisely when this difference becomes visible: the
governing dimension scale is n approximately c_F/s^2, and the
full-line scale is four times the PSD scale. At fixed finite dimension,
both power constants instead tend to the same number n.

With t=n s^2 fixed, the ratio of limiting full/PSD POWER constants is

  Phi_(4c)(t)/Phi_c(t),

which tends to 1 as t->0 and to 4 as t->infinity. Thus the factor
four is a sharp unbounded-dimension effect, with an explicit continuous
transition. It is not a factor-four ordinary-norm estimate:
K_(s,n)=H_(s,n)^(1/s).

For compression at error epsilon=e^(-L), (13) says the sharp leading
worst-case rank is the dimension cap n or r c_F log^2(1/epsilon),
whichever is smaller. The compressed-input construction (7) attains
the same leading state dimension. This supplies mathematical content
beyond merely repeating the leading constants.

10. AN EXACT MATRIX-DEGREE DICHOTOMY FOR SEMIFINITE ALGEBRAS

This is a structural extension, rather than another matrix corollary.
Its proof does not assume hyperfiniteness or approximation of a von
Neumann algebra by finite matrix algebras.

Let (M,tau) be a NONZERO von Neumann algebra with a normal semifinite
faithful trace. Define H_s^F(M) by replacing matrix trace with tau in
the original Holder ratio, taking BOUNDED inputs in M, and requiring

  0<tau(|B-A|^(alpha s))<infinity.

For the PSD and Hermitian maps impose the original positivity or
self-adjointness hypotheses. The square absolute and polar maps are
defined for arbitrary bounded elements of M by functional calculus
and polar decomposition. This statement does not purport to treat
all unbounded measurable inputs or type-III Haagerup spaces.

Let D(M) be the smallest integer D such that M is D-subhomogeneous,
or infinity if no such integer exists. Equivalently, for finite D,

  M = directsum_(k<=D) M_k(A_k),

with abelian von Neumann algebras A_k and a nonzero D summand; zero
summands are omitted. This is bounded MATRIX DEGREE, not vector-space
dimension of M: a diffuse abelian algebra has D=1.

THEOREM.

  If D(M)<infinity, then H_s^F(M)=H_(s,D(M))^F
  (in particular for 0<s<=1), and lim_(s->0) H_s^F(M)=D(M).

  If D(M)=infinity, then

     lim_(s->0) s^2 H_s^F(M)=2c_F.                  (14)

Thus boundedness of H_s^F(M) as s->0 is equivalent to subhomogeneity.
For a fixed algebra there is no intermediate divergence rate between
a finite degree and the universal s^-2 law. The PSD versus whole-line
coefficients in the latter case retain their exact factor four.

10.1 Upper bound for increments of finite trace support

For a bounded operator T in M define its trace rank by

  rank_tau(T)=tau(supp |T|).

Polar decomposition equates the trace of the left and right supports.
Bounded multiplication cannot increase this trace rank, and the
trace rank of a sum is at most the sum of trace ranks.

Consider first E=p with a projection p satisfying tau(p)<infinity.
The PSD and Hermitian resolvent identities give sample trace ranks
at most tau(p), respectively 2tau(p), at exactly the real nodes used
in the matrix proof. The same statements for the absolute/polar maps
hold for any partial-isometry increment E=W with finite support trace:
if p=W*W, each of the two terms in the manuscript's Gram-difference
or polar resolvent identity has trace rank at most tau(p).

The Banach-valued Gaussian quadrature applies in operator norm on M
without change. Therefore for norm-one increments of this form,

  tau(1_(exp(-L),infinity)(|Delta|))
   <=tau(p)[c_d L^2+C_d L^(7/4)+K_d].               (15)

The approximation-number argument behind (15) is the generalized
singular-number form of the ordinary matrix best-rank theorem. For
completeness, if ||Delta-Q||<=epsilon and rank_tau(Q)<=R, the support
projection of |Q| has trace <=R and Delta is bounded by epsilon on
its orthogonal complement; hence the generalized approximation
number at trace index above R is <=epsilon. This gives the displayed
strict-threshold count. The harmless equality-at-the-boundary issue
can also be handled by an arbitrarily small change in epsilon.

The operator bound M_alpha is unchanged. Layer cake for the normal
trace then yields, uniformly in M,A,p and in the partial isometry,

  tau(|Delta|^s)<=tau(p) U_(s,d),
  limsup_(s->0) s^2 U_(s,d)<=2c_d.                 (16)

The constant term in (15) also multiplies tau(p): it comes from the
same finite-node support bound at one fixed threshold. There is no
extra additive term independent of tau(p), which would destroy the
normalization on small trace projections.

10.2 Arbitrary bounded differences and trace Fatou

For a positive finite-spectrum increment E=sum_j t_j p_j with
orthogonal finite-trace projections p_j, telescope along the positive
path. Homogeneity, the trace s-power triangle for 0<s<=1, and (16)
give

  tau(|F(A+E)-F(A)|^s)
    <=U_(s,d) sum_j t_j^(alpha s) tau(p_j)
     =U_(s,d) tau(E^(alpha s)).                      (17)

For a general positive bounded E with tau(E^(alpha s)) finite, take
finite spectral step approximants E_j<=E converging in operator norm,
with tau(E_j^(alpha s))<=tau(E^(alpha s)). Such approximants have
finite trace support by the input integrability. Continuous functional
calculus gives norm convergence of the corrections, and normal-trace
Fatou gives (17) in the limit. The trace Fatou step follows directly
by testing on arbitrary finite-trace projections and taking their
supremum; it does not require finite-dimensional approximations of M.

For positive endpoints with a signed difference E=E_+-E_-, use
C=A+E_+=B+E_- and the two positive paths. Applying (17) separately
and the power triangle gives denominator
tau(E_+^(alpha s))+tau(E_-^(alpha s))=tau(|E|^(alpha s)).
All endpoints on these positive paths remain PSD.

For general Hermitian endpoints use signed spectral step increments.
For arbitrary bounded X,Y in the absolute/polar case write
E=U|E| and approximate |E| by finite spectral steps; its increments
are t_j U p_j. Each U p_j is a partial isometry with finite right
support p_j, so (16), telescoping and Fatou apply with the same
denominator. Functional calculus of Hermitian dilations supplies the
needed operator-norm continuity of these rectangular-style maps.

Thus every bounded admissible pair satisfies (17) with its appropriate
denominator and c_d. Let s->0 and then d approach the strip boundary:

  limsup_(s->0) s^2 H_s^F(M)<=2c_F.                (18)

10.3 Lower bound when matrix degree is unbounded

If M is not n-subhomogeneous, it contains a nonzero *-homomorphic
image of M_(n+1) entirely in M intersect L1(M,tau). This precise
finite-trace statement is recorded and proved in the primary source
Arhancet cited below. Since a nonzero homomorphism from a matrix
algebra is injective, its restricted trace is a positive finite scalar
multiple of ordinary matrix trace. That scalar cancels between
numerator and denominator. Also all the fractional maps vanish at
zero, so extension from the corner by zero gives precisely the
desired functional-calculus ratio in M.

Consequently, if D(M)=infinity,

  H_s^F(M)>=sup_n H_(s,n)^F=H_s^F.

The checked matrix theorem and (18) prove (14). This works equally
in nonhyperfinite type-II factors: only embedded matrix CORNERS are
used for the lower bound, and the upper was proved directly in M.

10.4 Bounded matrix degree

For M=directsum_(k<=D) M_k(A_k), perform functional calculus fiberwise
over the abelian centers. Integrating the finite k-by-k inequality
against the central trace measure gives

  H_s^F(M)<=max_(A_k nonzero) H_(s,k)^F=H_(s,D)^F.

Conversely the nonzero D summand has a finite-positive-trace central
piece, on which constant D-by-D matrices give the reverse inequality;
the trace weight cancels. Thus equality holds, and Section 6.1 gives
its limit D. In particular the limit itself recovers the matrix degree,
with infinity interpreted via (14).

The finite-dimension crossover also transfers immediately to any
family of such algebras whose finite degrees D_s satisfy s^2 D_s->t:
replace n by D_s in (1). This is a quantitative matrix-degree law,
not merely an assertion that commutative and noncommutative algebras
behave differently.

13. A 1:2:4 LAW FOR PSD, ONE COMPLEX BRANCH, AND REAL HOMOGENEOUS MAPS

There is a larger exact-constant theorem suggested by the pole geometry.
All coefficients below are fixed before taking s->0 or L->infinity.

13.1 Every nonzero real homogeneous scalar function on the full line

Let a,b be fixed REAL numbers, not both zero, and define

  f_(a,b)(x)=a (x_+)^alpha+b ((-x)_+)^alpha.

This is the entire class of continuous, real-valued, positively
degree-alpha homogeneous functions on R. Put e=(a+b)/2 and o=(a-b)/2,
so f=e f_e+o f_o in the manuscript's notation.

THEOREM. On Hermitian inputs every such nonzero map has the SAME
leading power and rank-count coefficients as the even/odd maps:

  s^2 H_s(f_(a,b)) ->2/(pi^2 alpha beta),
  N_(f_(a,b),r)(L)/L^2 ->r/(pi^2 alpha beta).        (19)

Its square finite-dimension crossover is also exactly (1)--(4) with
c=1/(pi^2 alpha beta). The semifinite-algebra dichotomy in Section 10
holds with this coefficient as well.

Upper proof: combine the even and odd integrands BEFORE taking sample
ranks. Each combined node is still a linear combination of just the
two conjugate rank-one resolvent differences. Its sample rank is <=2,
its strip is pi/2, and its two envelope slopes are alpha,beta.
The envelope prefactor depends on a,b, but disappears from the leading
coefficient. Applying the power triangle to the two FINAL functions
would instead lose an unnecessary factor two and is not the proof.

For the lower, use the checked centered construction, for which

  Delta_o=diag(S_+,-S_-),
  Delta_e=[[0,O],[O*,0]],  S_+,S_->=0.

If o!=0, then Delta_f=e Delta_e+o Delta_o is selfadjoint with
opposite-sign diagonal blocks o S_+ and -o S_-. Min--max applied
separately to its positive and negative eigenvalues gives

  tr|Delta_f|^s >=|o|^s(tr S_+^s+tr S_-^s).

This uses TWO SEPARATE eigenvalue min--max comparisons, for the
positive and negative spectra, whose contributions are disjoint and
can be added. It does not invoke contractivity of ordinary Schatten
pinching, which would be false in this quasi-Banach range.

Each channel has the single-channel lower coefficient
1/(pi^2 alpha beta). Since |o|^s->1, the two channels give (19).
If o=0, then e!=0 and this is a nonzero scalar multiple of the
already proved even map. The count theorem follows from the same
deficit lemma. The compressed model, finite-dimension proof and
trace-algebra extension use exactly the same two-pole ranks as before.

In particular the one-sided fractional ReLU f(x)=(x_+)^alpha has
the full factor-FOUR coefficient compared with PSD powers, despite
vanishing identically on the negative half-line. The factor four is
not caused by equal branch amplitudes of an even or odd function.
This asymptotic is not uniform as a,b approach zero or depend on s.

13.2 One analytic complex branch has exactly half the full-line cost

Let phi=pi alpha/2 and define, on Hermitian A by spectral calculus,

  g_+(x)=(-ix)^alpha
     =cos(phi)|x|^alpha-i sin(phi)sgn(x)|x|^alpha,
  g_-(x)=conj(g_+(x))=(ix)^alpha.                    (20)

These are the principal fractional powers of the rotated imaginary
axis. Up to a constant unit phase, g_+ is the boundary value taking
x^alpha for x>0 and exp(i pi alpha)|x|^alpha for x<0. Outputs are
usually nonselfadjoint; the singular-value power ratio is still used.

THEOREM.

  s^2 H_s(g_+)=s^2 H_s(g_-) ->1/(pi^2 alpha beta),
  N_(g_+,r)(L)/L^2=N_(g_-,r)(L)/L^2
      ->r/(2 pi^2 alpha beta).                       (21)

The equality notation for counts means the two count SUPREMA agree
by adjoints; no fixed-pair equality with a real map is asserted.

Here is the exact one-pole integral. For R_A(z)=(A-zI)^(-1),

  g_+(B)-g_+(A)
   =-i c_alpha int_0^infinity t^alpha
           [R_B(-it)-R_A(-it)] dt,
  c_alpha=sin(pi alpha)/pi.                          (22)

One can derive (22) by inserting (20) in the checked even/odd
integrals: the +it terms cancel exactly because
cos(phi)c_e=sin(phi)c_o=c_alpha. Alternatively apply the elementary
fractional-power integral to -iA. Thus no unproved accretive-operator
functional-calculus theorem is needed for these Hermitian inputs.

The logarithmic integrand has strip pi/2 and the SAME slopes
alpha,beta, but one node now has rank ONE. Therefore

  c_(branch,d)=1/(4 pi d alpha beta), d<pi/2,
  c_branch=1/(2 pi^2 alpha beta),

giving the upper in (21). Rank-one telescoping is valid for this
complex-valued continuous homogeneous function as well.

For the lower use the same centered endpoints. The top-left block
of the correction of g_+ is -i sin(phi)S_+. Singular values of an
orthogonal compression cannot exceed those of the full operator,
so

  tr|g_+(B)-g_+(A)|^s >=sin(phi)^s tr S_+^s.

The checked SINGLE-channel C_1 lower calculation gives
liminf s^2 tr S_+^s >=1/(pi^2 alpha beta), with the same bounded-
interval, finite-matrix transfer and delta->0 order as in the paper.
Since sin(phi)>0 is fixed, its s-th power tends to one. This matches
the one-pole upper exactly. Adjoint symmetry gives g_-. The same
moment-to-count deficit argument and direct sums prove (21).

The result exhibits coefficients in the ratio 1:2:4:

   PSD power                     1/(2 pi^2 alpha beta),
   one complex analytic branch   1/(  pi^2 alpha beta),
   nonzero real homogeneous map  2/(  pi^2 alpha beta).

These are POWER coefficients, with rank-count coefficients half as
large. The comparison fixes both alpha and the scalar function.

14. ISOSINGULAR-SPECTRUM COMPRESSION FOR ONE-SIDED COMPLEX POLES

A direct orthogonal compression for (22) would include both conjugate
resolvent spaces and apparently require 2q states for q rank-one
nodes. The following positive Gaussian-quadrature construction removes
that mismatch. It is what permits a SHARP finite-dimension crossover
for the intermediate coefficient, not just its dimension-free limit.

Let A=A*, ||v||=1, B=A+vv*, and let z_1,...,z_q lie strictly in
one open half-plane (in (22), z_j=-it_j). Aggregate duplicate nodes
if necessary. Let mu be the scalar spectral measure of (A,v), so

  m(z)=v*(A-zI)^(-1)v=int (lambda-z)^(-1) dmu(lambda),
  int dmu=1.

It is a positive finite measure of bounded support. Define

  w(lambda)=product_(j=1)^q (lambda-z_j)(lambda-conj(z_j))>0
       on R,
  dnu=dmu/w.

Take ordinary positive Gaussian quadrature for nu with at most q+1
nodes lambda_k and weights a_k, exact on all polynomials of degree
<=2q+1. If the original measure has fewer than q+1 support points,
take it exactly instead. The nodes lie in the convex hull of the
original support. Reweight by b_k=a_k w(lambda_k)>0 and set

  mu'=sum_k b_k delta_(lambda_k).

Gaussian exactness for w, of degree 2q, gives total mass one. Its
exactness for w/(lambda-z_j) and w/(lambda-conj(z_j)), of degree
2q-1, gives, writing m_new for the transform of mu',

  m_new(z_j)=m(z_j), m_new(conj z_j)=m(conj z_j).    (23)

Realize mu' by the Hermitian diagonal A'=diag(lambda_k) and unit
v'=(sqrt(b_k)); put B'=A'+v'v'^*. The input dimension is <=q+1
and its rank-one increment has norm EXACTLY one.

Write

  U_j=(A-z_j)^(-1)v, V_j=(A-conj z_j)^(-1)v.

The column Gram matrices satisfy

  (U*U)_ij=[m(conj z_i)-m(z_j)]/(conj z_i-z_j),
  (V*V)_ij=[m(z_i)-m(conj z_j)]/(z_i-conj z_j).     (24)

Denominators do not vanish because all z_j lie in one half-plane.
Thus (23) gives exactly the same two Gram matrices for the primed
vectors. Woodbury also gives the same node scalars 1/(1+m(z_j)).

Any quadrature Q=sum_j a_j[R_B(z_j)-R_A(z_j)] has form

  Q=U D V*, D diagonal.

The primed quadrature Q'=U' D V'^* therefore has IDENTICAL nonzero
singular values. Indeed these depend only on U*U, V*V and D, as
seen by polar decomposition of U,V. The left and right isometries
may differ; a common operator-norm embedding is not being asserted.

For the function correction and its primed counterpart, each is
within exp(-L) of its own quadrature, using the same uniform envelope.
The singular-value perturbation inequality and the exact matching
of quadrature singular values give, with zero padding,

  |sigma_j(Delta)-sigma_j(Delta')|<=2 exp(-L)
       for every j,
  dim A'<=q+1<=c_(branch,d)L^2+O(L^(7/4))+1.         (25)

This is enough for every lower-bound count-preservation step in
Sections 5--7. Consequently the FULL finite-dimension crossover
(1)--(4) and count law (13) hold for g_+,g_- with

  c=c_branch=1/(2 pi^2 alpha beta).

The semifinite-algebra dichotomy also transfers, since its upper only
uses one-pole trace ranks and its lower embeds these finite matrices.

Gaussian quadrature existence here is the classical elementary one:
for an r-node rule, divide a polynomial of degree <=2r-1 by the
degree-r orthogonal polynomial; orthogonality kills the quotient
term and interpolation handles the remainder. Real interior nodes
are the roots of that polynomial, and the weights are positive
because each equals the integral of the squared Lagrange basis
polynomial. No Gaussian-quadrature novelty is claimed.

For PSD real poles, matching the resolvent-column Gram matrix also
requires derivatives at each real pole. Taking the positive weight
product_j(lambda+t_j)^2 gives q+1 states by the same construction.
For a conjugate-closed real-map rule, derivatives/cross-Grams require
each conjugate pair twice, giving 2q+1 states. These agree to leading
order with the direct orthogonal-compression dimensions already used.

15. SCOPE AND PRIORITY OF THE 1:2:4 EXTENSION

The one-pole cancellation in (22), elementary min--max in (19),
positive rational Gaussian quadrature and resolvent Gram formulas
are standard tools. They are not separate claimed breakthroughs.
The proposed stronger content is the exact asymptotic universality
law, including the intermediate complex-branch coefficient and its
sharp joint dimension/accuracy crossover.

This does not classify ALL complex combinations a x_+^alpha+
b x_-^alpha. Real a,b and the two analytic branch directions are
the proved classes above. General complex coefficients may have
additional cancellation phenomena and are not silently included.

A fresh bounded primary-keyword screening for accretive/normal
fractional-power Holder constants and complex-branch Schatten
small-exponent constants located no matching exact formula. Existing
operator Holder theory and rational approximation remain relevant
priority hazards. A specialist comparison is still required before
claiming first discovery of any exact coefficient or crossover.

Primary source confirming that the positive modified-measure/reweight
construction is classical:

  W. Gautschi, The use of rational functions in numerical quadrature,
  J. Comput. Appl. Math. 133 (2001), 111--126, Theorem 3.1 and
  Section 6(ii) (simple conjugate complex poles):
  https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/wxg/selected_works/section_08/167.pdf
  https://doi.org/10.1016/S0377-0427(00)00637-3

The original author's PDF was inspected. It gives the ordinary-Gauss
rule for a measure divided by a prescribed pole polynomial and the
same reweighting of its nodes. Conjugate poles make the divisor
positive on R. Thus Section 14 is an application of rational Gaussian
quadrature, followed by the explicit Gram/singular-spectrum argument;
the quadrature construction itself is not new.

Additional independent algebra diagnostics: for random real diagonal
24-by-24 A, q=1,...,5 negative-imaginary nodes, the (q+1)-state
construction matched mass to 3e-15, both-half-plane m-values to
2e-15, and quadrature singular values to 2e-15. The branch identity

integration at alpha=1/2, for both positive and negative scalar

These are diagnostics, not substitutes for the exact proofs.
