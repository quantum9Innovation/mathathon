MAIN CONCLUSION
---------------
For every fixed 0<alpha<1, let W_2,alpha(epsilon) be the supremal strict
relative correction count for doubling an existing unit edge in finite
connected simple unweighted graphs of maximum degree two. Then

 W_2,alpha(epsilon) ~ [1/(2 pi^2)] log^2(1/epsilon).

The upper proof is uniform over all path lengths, edge positions and
cycle lengths. It gives the stronger finite comparison

 N_C(epsilon) <= A_alpha log(1/epsilon)+N_Talpha(b_alpha epsilon), (A)

where A_alpha,b_alpha>0 are fixed and Talpha is ONE fixed positive
weighted convolution operator, independent of the finite input graph.
Laptev's theorem gives its coefficient 1/(2 pi^2). The lower bound
uses the already proved centered infinite-path limit and finite strict-
threshold approximation, choosing a separate sufficiently long finite
path for each threshold. No monotonic finite approximation is required.

1. THE EXACT DERIVATIVE DOMINATOR AND FINITE SPECTRAL MEASURES
-----------------------------------------------------------
For A=L restricted to its positive support, B=A+uu^T with u an edge
dipole, the fractional relative correction C is positive. Its positive
rank-one integral is dominated by the integral with the denominator
1+u^T(A+tI)^-1u removed. Call that derivative dominator H. In an A
eigenbasis, after diagonal sign/phase changes, it has kernel

 H_ij=sqrt(w_i w_j) K_alpha(x_i-x_j),
 x_i=log lambda_i,
 w_i=|<u,phi_i>|^2/lambda_i,
 K_alpha(s)=sinh(alpha s/2)/sinh(s/2), K_alpha(0)=alpha.

Thus 0<=C<=H. The Fourier transform, with exp(-i xi s), is

 Khat_alpha(xi)=2 pi sin(pi alpha)/
                        [cosh(2 pi xi)+cos(pi alpha)]>0,

and Khat_alpha(xi)<=C_alpha exp(-2 pi |xi|). Total spectral-measure
mass sum w_i is the edge leverage, at most one for every such path
or cycle. These formulas are already derived in the frozen degree-
dichotomy manuscript.

On a path with n vertices and edge position p,

 lambda_k=4sin^2(k pi/(2n)),
 w_k=(2/n)sin^2(k pi p/n)<=2/n, 1<=k<n.

On a cycle, combine each repeated eigenspace into its single active
dipole direction. The distinct positive eigenvalues have the form
lambda_k=4sin^2(pi k/n), 1<=k<=floor(n/2), and weights at most 2/n.
The exceptional eigenvalue at k=n/2 for even n has weight 1/n.
All inactive directions contribute zero correction eigenvalues.

2. THE HIGH-ENERGY PART COSTS ONLY O(log(1/epsilon)) RANK
------------------------------------------------------
Split the spectral coordinates at lambda=1, equivalently x=0.
For the two corresponding orthogonal projections P,Q, positivity gives

 H<=2(PHP+QHQ).

Consequently its strict count at epsilon is bounded by the sum of the
two compression counts at epsilon/2. On the high block x_i in [0,log4],
K_alpha(x-y) is analytic in x on one fixed complex neighborhood of
that interval, uniformly for real y in that interval. Approximate it
there by a degree-r Chebyshev polynomial in x. The uniform error is
at most C_alpha exp(-c_alpha r). This separated kernel has rank r+1.
Its weighted finite-matrix operator error is at most the scalar error
times sum w_i<=1 (the Hilbert--Schmidt estimate suffices).
Therefore the high-block count is O_alpha(log(1/epsilon)), uniformly
over graph size and all the atom weights.

It remains to control the low block x_i<0 sharply.

3. DELETE O(log(1/epsilon)) LOW INDICES AND SMOOTH EVERY OTHER ATOM
----------------------------------------------------------------
Put L=log(1/epsilon). Choose

 Omega=max(1,(L+C_alpha)/2pi), h=1/Omega,

with C_alpha large enough that the Fourier-frequency tail |xi|>Omega
has total Khat mass at most epsilon/16. As sum w_i<=1, the corresponding
positive finite Gram matrix has norm at most epsilon/16: its trace
already has that bound.

Remove the low-grid coordinates with k<M=ceil(1/h). This deletes at
most M=O_alpha(L) coordinates. Principal-submatrix interlacing bounds
the original low-block count by M plus the remaining count. If no
coordinates remain, the desired O(L) bound is immediate.

For each remaining atom set

 psi_i(x)=h^-1 1_[x_i-h/2,x_i+h/2](x),
 rho_sm(x)=sum_i w_i psi_i(x).

The intervals may overlap; disjointness is neither assumed nor needed.
All intervals lie in x<1 because x_i<0 and h<=1.

CLAIM: rho_sm(x)<=C exp(x/2) 1_(x<1), uniformly in n,p,epsilon.

To verify it, the path/cycle low grid has lambda_k=4sin^2(sigma k/(2n))
with sigma=pi or 2pi. On x<=1/2, the inverse grid function has derivative
at most C n exp(x/2). Thus an x-interval of length h contains at most
C n exp(x/2)h+1 grid points. Using w_i<=2/n gives

 rho_sm(x)<=C exp(x/2)+2/(nh).

If x is in its support, some k>=M has |x-x_k|<=h/2. The elementary
sin bound gives exp(x_k/2)=sqrt(lambda_k)>=2k/n, and hence

 exp(x/2)>=2exp(-h/4)k/n>=c/(nh).

This absorbs the last term and proves the claim. The estimate remains
valid when some path weights vanish, because only their upper bound
was used. Endpoint behavior at lambda=4 is absent from this low block.

4. A BOUNDED FACTORIZATION OF THE SMOOTHED FINITE GRAM
----------------------------------------------------
Set rho(x)=exp(x/2) on (-infinity,1), and define the fixed positive
operator Talpha on L^2(-infinity,1) by kernel

 Talpha(x,y)=sqrt(rho(x)rho(y)) K_alpha(x-y).

It is positive trace class: its positive Fourier representation has
integrable feature trace alpha int rho<infinity.

Define F from the remaining finite coefficient space into this L^2
space by

 (Fv)(x)=rho(x)^(-1/2) sum_i sqrt(w_i) v_i psi_i(x).

Cauchy--Schwarz in the positive sum gives

 |sum_i sqrt(w_i)v_i psi_i(x)|^2
     <=rho_sm(x) sum_i |v_i|^2 psi_i(x).

Divide by rho, use rho_sm<=C rho, and integrate int psi_i=1. Therefore
||F||^2<=C uniformly, even with overlapping intervals and arbitrarily
many atoms.

The finite smoothed Gram H_h is exactly F^* Talpha F. Its Fourier
kernel representation is obtained from H by multiplying Khat_alpha(xi)
by sinc^2(xi h/2), where sinc(z)=sin(z)/z. For |xi|<=Omega and h=1/Omega,

 sinc^2(xi h/2)>=(sin(1/2)/(1/2))^2=:c0>0.

Hence the retained frequency Gram H_band satisfies the Loewner bound
H_band<=c0^-1 H_h, since all Fourier densities are nonnegative.
Adding the previously bounded high-frequency tail yields

 H_rest<=c0^-1 F^*Talpha F+(epsilon/16)I.

Min-max/singular-value composition gives

 lambda_j(F^*Talpha F)<=||F||^2 lambda_j(Talpha)<=C lambda_j(Talpha).

At the count threshold epsilon/2 inherited from pinching, this proves
N_Hrest(epsilon/2)<=N_Talpha(b_alpha epsilon) for a fixed b_alpha>0.
Together with the deleted O(L) indices and the analytic high-energy
rank bound, this proves finite uniform comparison (A).

5. THE FIXED CONTINUOUS MODEL HAS COEFFICIENT 1/(2pi^2)
------------------------------------------------------
The ratio Khat_alpha/Khat_(1/2) is bounded above and below by positive
constants depending only on fixed alpha. Positive Fourier comparison
therefore sandwiches Talpha by fixed positive multiples of T_(1/2).
Translation of x<1 to x<0 only multiplies this weighted convolution
operator by a fixed scalar, without changing threshold asymptotics.

For alpha=1/2, K_(1/2)(s)=1/[2cosh(s/4)]. The unitary substitution
u=exp(x/2), x<0, maps the weighted convolution kernel to

                   2sqrt(uv)/(u+v), 0<u,v<1.

Laptev, Spectral asymptotic behavior of a class of integral operators,
Mathematical Notes16(1974),1038--1043, Theorem(3),p1039, gives coefficient
1/(2pi^2 rho) for u^beta v^gamma/(u+v)^a with
rho=beta+gamma-a+1>0. Set a=1,beta=gamma=1/2, rho=1. The kernel is
positive, so its singular count is its eigenvalue count. Multiplication
by two and the fixed alpha-comparison constants do not change the
leading logarithmic-square coefficient.

This primary theorem and its exact coefficient were checked directly
in the author PDF and the rendered p1039 image:
  https://www.ma.ic.ac.uk/~alaptev/Papers/MatZam74.pdf
  https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?wshow=paper&jrnid=mzm&paperid=7513&option_lang=eng
The screenshot confirms (2 pi^2 rho)^-1; a search-engine PDF text rendering
mis-OCRs this formula and must not be used for the constant.

Thus N_Talpha(t)~[1/(2pi^2)]log^2(1/t). Comparison(A) proves the claimed
UNIFORM limsup for every moving finite path/cycle sequence.

6. MATCHING LOWER WITHOUT FINITE-N MONOTONICITY
----------------------------------------------
The frozen degree-dichotomy manuscript proves Hilbert--Schmidt convergence
of centered-path corrections, after isometric embedding, to a positive
compact operator C_infinity with count coefficient 1/(2pi^2).
For any fixed epsilon>0 and gamma>0, all finitely many eigenvalues of
C_infinity greater than (1+gamma)epsilon persist above epsilon in some
sufficiently long finite centered path. This follows from compact-operator
eigenvalue continuity, not monotonicity in path size. Therefore

 W_2,alpha(epsilon)>=N_Cinfinity((1+gamma)epsilon).

Its fixed-threshold rescaling leaves the logarithmic-square coefficient
unchanged. This matches the uniform limsup above and proves the result.

7. BOUNDARY CROSSOVER: EXPLORATORY FORMULA ONLY
---------------------------------------------
The uniform upper above is a theorem-level proof. The following more
refined moving-boundary calculation is NOT proved and is not needed for it.

Let the edge distance to the nearer path endpoint be p, and heuristically
write log p~kappa L, log n~sigma L, 0<=kappa<=sigma. On low energies,
the leverage density has envelope

 rho_p(x)~exp(x/2)min(1,p^2 exp(x)).

For x=-u L its logarithmic potential is
V(u)=u/2+(u-2kappa)_+. A semiclassical phase-volume prediction, with
the finite-grid cutoff u<=2sigma, is

 [L^2/(2pi^2)] int_0^(2sigma) [1-V(u)]_+ du.

If sigma is large enough not to cut off the active region, this predicts
coefficient

 (1+4kappa-2kappa^2)/(6pi^2), 0<=kappa<=1,
 1/(2pi^2),                              kappa>=1.

It interpolates fixed-endpoint 1/(6pi^2) and centered 1/(2pi^2), but a
uniform lower proof must handle rapidly oscillating sin^2(p theta),
finite-grid aliasing, and parameter-dependent continuous models. This
note does not claim that additional crossover law. In particular the
verified uniform coefficient above does not require any such heuristic
or an assumption that one finite centered path dominates all others.
