Primary comparison: hidden fibers in random regular graph covers
================================================================
15 November 2026. This report audits the literature scope, not the
correctness of the new all-slot entropy proof. It changes no earlier
packet. All new files from this review are confined to this priority/
directory.

VERDICT
-------
No inspected primary theorem directly gives the proposed complete result:
for a random N-lift of every fixed connected simple d-regular base H,
d>=3, its planted fibers are, with high probability, the unique finest
equitable partition among partitions with at most a supplied fixed K
classes; unknown H and its fibers are recovered by an expected-polynomial
exact algorithm without an old/new spectral gap.

This is a focused negative search result, NOT certification of historical
firstness. The new mathematical proof must pass separately. Several
nearby claims are already known and must not be presented as new:
noiseless lift detection by exact spectral features; the graph-cover
model and its connectivity; the intertwining characterization of an
equitable partition; coarsest equitable-partition extraction; and
spectral recovery of suitable separated/scaled frame models.

Fresh comparator: Isaev et al.2602.17567/ICALP2026 cover sufficiently large
constant degree in their actual Theorem1. Section7 records its scope.

1. The target, with terminology that prevents false comparisons
-------------------------------------------------------------
The object to recover is the vertex partition into base fibers, up to
names. For a simple base H with adjacency Q, its indicator J satisfies
AJ=JQ, with Q diagonal zero and entries zero or one. Every vertex has
exactly one neighbor in each neighboring fiber. A nonempty one-hot
solution of AY=YR for a simple adjacency target R describes a locally
bijective cover projection. General integer R describes an equitable
partition, a larger candidate family than simple covering targets.

The proposed theorem concerns the FINEST equitable partition within a
fixed bound K. Every graph also has the n-singleton equitable partition;
there is no claim of an unrestricted globally finest planted partition.
The output may have coarser quotients as well. If H itself covers a
smaller base, that yields compatible coarsenings and is not an obstruction
to selecting the maximum number of classes at most K.

The words 'regular graph cover' in much of the algorithmic literature
mean a cover produced by a semiregular group of deck transformations.
They do not mean an arbitrary cover of a d-regular graph. Independent
uniform edge permutations define general covers; no transitive deck group
is supplied. Conversely, recovering a canonical labeling or proving
asymmetry does not by itself identify a cover projection or its fibers.

2. Foundational random-cover source, read as a full primary paper
---------------------------------------------------------------
Alon Amit and Nathan Linial, Random Graph Coverings I: General Theory
and Graph Connectivity, Combinatorica22(2002),1-18.
Primary author PDF:
 https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~nati/PAPERS/rlg_connectivity.pdf
Publisher record:
 https://doi.org/10.1007/s004930200000

Coverage: all18 pages/Sections1-3, including model/labels, Theorem1 and
its thick/thin-set argument, conclusion, and variants. The model assigns
independent uniform permutations to base edges. Theorem1 states that a
fixed connected simple base of minimum degree delta>=3 has delta-connected
random covers with probability tending to one. No reconstruction
algorithm or unique hidden-fiber theorem is stated.

Important subtlety in Section1.3: their labeled/unlabeled equivalence
comes from relabeling WITHIN the given fibers. The resulting asymptotic
triviality of automorphisms is about cover objects in this setup. It is
not a proof that the same uncolored total graph has a unique projection
partition. Section3 also explicitly distinguishes a single random large
cover from a tower of smaller random covers: their laws differ.

3. Worst-case recognition and multicovers
----------------------------------------
Jiri Fiala, Note on the computational complexity of covering regular
graphs, primary author preprint,2pages:
 https://kam.mff.cuni.cz/~fiala/papers/2000F.pdf

Coverage: entire note, including Theorem3 and proof. It proves H-Cover
NP-complete for every fixed k-regular simple target H with k>=3. The proof
uses the bipartite double cover and prior hardness for edge-colorable
regular targets. This concerns worst-case input G and an existence
decision for a specified target. It neither forbids an average-case
random-lift recovery algorithm nor supplies one. The proposed theorem
must not be described as solving worst-case H-Cover in polynomial time.

Jan Kratochvil, Andrzej Proskurowski, Jan Arne Telle, Covering Regular
Graphs, JCTB71(1997),1-16:
 https://doi.org/10.1006/jctb.1996.1743
Publisher primary abstract:
 https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0095895696917438

Coverage limit: publisher abstract, plus explicit multicover definition
and existence lemma in the author's GROW2022 slides, pages8-10:
 https://kam.mff.cuni.cz/~honza/2022-09-21-GROW-Kratochvil.pdf
The original1997 proof was not fully retrieved in this pass. These
sources construct graphs with many covering projections onto a fixed
regular target. They show why unrestricted deterministic uniqueness is
false; they do not state a typical-random-cover uniqueness theorem.

4. Algorithmic RegularCover is a different probability-free problem
------------------------------------------------------------------
Jiri Fiala, Pavel Klavik, Jan Kratochvil, Roman Nedela, Algorithmic
Aspects of Regular Graph Covers, arXiv1609.03013v2,29January2017:
 https://arxiv.org/html/1609.03013v2

Coverage: abstract, Sections1.1-1.4, Theorem1, and covering/group
definitions in Section2; full text available, technical meta-algorithm
proof not re-audited. Its 'regular' projection is prescribed by a
semiregular automorphism subgroup. Theorem1 gives an FPT algorithm for
PLANAR source G, exponential in the target's edge count. Related
polynomial cases in that algorithm retain the structural assumptions
of its source graph class. This does not yield reconstruction of fibers
in unrestricted sparse random lifts of every regular base.

Bok, Fiala, Jedlickova, Kratochvil, Computational Complexity of Covering
Regular Trees, MFCS2025,26:1-26:15:
 https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2025.26
Full primary HTML:
 https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol345-mfcs2025/html/LIPIcs.MFCS.2025.26/LIPIcs.MFCS.2025.26.html
Coverage: introduction, definitions, main statements, and references.
This extends worst-case H-Cover hardness to trees regularized with
semi-edges and explicitly recalls hardness for every connected simple
regular target of valency above two. It is not a random-input theorem.

5. Cover order does not identify a particular random projection
-------------------------------------------------------------
Jan Kratochvil and Roman Nedela, Graph covers and semi-covers: Who is
stronger?, arXiv2504.17387v1,24April2025:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17387v1

Coverage: Sections1-4 definitions, observations, main results, and proof
constructions inspected; no independent proof certification. 'A stronger
than B' means every SIMPLE cover of A also covers B. Observation2 makes
this equivalent to A covering B whenever A is itself simple: choose A
as the source. Consequently the stronger-than conjectures in this paper
should not be advertised as new consequences solved for simple bases
by random-fiber recovery. Its nontrivial cases involve multigraphs,
loops, and semi-edges, with edge-map conditions in addition to vertices.
The source neither proves nor obstructs the proposed random-fiber claim.

Related deterministic context: finite regular graphs can have common
finite covers, classically Angluin-Gardiner/Leighton. A primary new proof
and exposition is Walter Neumann, On Leighton's graph covering theorem:
 https://www.math.columbia.edu/department/neumann/preprints/leighton1.3.pdf
Coverage: introductory regular-case proof and Theorem1.1. This is an
existence result; it gives no lower bound on the probability of ambiguous
projections under independent uniform lift permutations. Thus common
covers are not counterexamples to a high-probability theorem.

6. Detection and noise: important known results, different output
----------------------------------------------------------------
Dmitriy Kunisky and Xifan Yu, Computational hardness of detecting graph
lifts and certifying lift-monotone properties of random regular graphs,
arXiv2404.17012v1 / FOCS2024:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17012v1
 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.17012

Coverage: entire introduction/main statements and open questions,
Sections2.2-2.3 model/noise definitions, Section3.2 partition-matrix
formulation, plus text search of the full64-page numbered body for
recovery, reconstruction, kernel, and rigidity. Technical SDP lower-bound
proofs were not independently re-audited. The task is hypothesis testing
against uniform regular graphs. The introduction explicitly explains
that exact noiseless spectral features make the uncorrupted detection
problem easy. The hardness conjecture adds noise and concerns Ramanujan
bases; proved evidence is against local-statistics SDPs. No hidden-fiber
recovery theorem is stated. New brittle exact recovery would not refute
their noisy conjecture. Their paper also distinguishes its convenient
matching-loop convention from standard topological loops.

Xifan Yu, Analysis of Polynomial Threshold Functions on Random Regular
Graphs: Computational Complexity of Detecting Noisy Random Lifts,
arXiv2608.28539v2,4September2026:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28539v2
Coverage: introduction, model definitions, all main theorem statements,
open questions and full-text search for recovery. The paper analyzes
noisy lift detection, proves low-degree polynomial-threshold lower
bounds, and studies cycle counts. It does not assert reconstruction of
the planted fibers. Its introduction again emphasizes that adding noise
is substantive. 'Noiseless detection is new' would be an incorrect claim.

7. Fresh 2026 comparator for UNPLANTED equitable-partition rigidity
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Mikhail Isaev, Tamas Makai, Brendan McKay, Pawel Pralat, Jane Tan,
Maksim Zhukovskii, Canonical labelling of random regular graphs,
arXiv2602.17567v1,19February2026; ICALP2026,114:1-114:23:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17567v1
 https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.114

Coverage: all introduction, Theorems1,4,5, proof strategy, discussion of
equitable partitions, and sparse-case theorem statements. Technical
switching proofs not independently re-audited. The ACTUAL Theorem1
assumes d0<=d<=n/2 for a sufficiently large universal constant d0. From
any nontrivial initial bipartition, color refinement becomes discrete
with high probability. The paper explicitly derives absence of every
equitable partition except one class and n singletons. Its abstract's
d->infinity restriction is for the stated canonical-labeling corollary;
one must not incorrectly restrict Theorem1 to growing degrees.

This is a strong prior rigidity theorem for uniform UNPLANTED regular
graphs. It does not apply after conditioning on a prescribed nontrivial
fiber partition, an event excluded by its own conclusion. Its Remark2
acknowledges a barrier at d=3 and conjectures extending its statement to
all d>=3. The proposed bounded-K result would not settle that unrestricted
color-refinement conjecture. If the new theorem includes k=1, credit the
overlap for sufficiently large constant d.

8. Frame clustering, general equitable extraction, and local laws
----------------------------------------------------------------
Gerandy Brito, Ioana Dumitriu, Kameron Decker Harris, Spectral gap in
random bipartite biregular graphs and applications, arXiv1804.07808v6:
 https://arxiv.org/html/1804.07808
Coverage: Sections1.2 and6 in full, including Theorem19. The frame model
has prescribed reversible block degrees and includes lift-like examples.
Theorem19 permits scaling all degrees by a sufficiently large constant
to separate the frame eigenspace, assumes a nonzero least-magnitude
frame Markov eigenvalue, and recovers classes from leading eigenvectors.
This is substantial prior exact frame recovery, but not every fixed
degree-three lift, zero quotient mode, or unknown-frame bounded-finest
claim without separation. The adjacency intertwining relation is old.

Michael Scholkemper and Michael Schaub, Blind extraction of equitable
partitions from graph signals, arXiv2203.05407v1:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2203.05407v1
Coverage: Sections1-3 and algorithm/theorem statements. Its target is
the COARSEST equitable partition, from graph-filter observations. It
explicitly notes the trivial n-singleton finest partition, and states
AH=HA^pi as the usual algebraic characterization. It is not a theorem
identifying planted bounded-finest fibers from one sparse adjacency
matrix. In an uncolored regular graph, ordinary color refinement starts
and stays at one class, so that target is different.

Jiaoyang Huang and Ruizhe Xu, Local Law and Eigenvector Delocalization
for Random Lifts, arXiv2610.37463v1,27October2026:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37463v1
Coverage: introduction, Sections1.1-1.2, main local-law/delocalization
statements and discussion of old/new spaces. For fixed connected simple
bases of minimum degree at least three it gives local resolvent estimates
away from singular energies and delocalization. Such estimates neither
identify a hidden partition nor by themselves exclude exact collision
between a new eigenvalue and an old interior one. No all-base exact
fiber-recovery algorithm is stated. These spectral results should be
credited as background, not treated as a substitute for the proposed
finite-field count or one-hot rigidity.

9. Search coverage and remaining uncertainty
-------------------------------------------
Fresh searches used combinations of random lift/cover, fiber/fibre,
recover/reconstruction, unique/uniqueness/identifiability, recognition,
covering projection, equitable partition, finite field/nullity, and
graph isomorphism. References were followed from current primary
inference, graph-cover complexity, and local-law papers. Search engines
returned many unrelated 'cover' topics and automated summaries; those
were not used as evidence. In particular an automated summary of the
2026 canonical-labeling paper did not replace reading its Theorem1.

This pass does not certify exhaustive citation coverage, unpublished
knowledge, non-English theses, all graph-cover enumeration papers, or
the proofs of every technical comparator. Prior frame/RSBM comparisons
in ../../PRIORITY_REVIEW.txt remain relevant, especially
Karmakar-Podder2002.05577 and Brito-Dumitriu-Ganguly-Hoffman-Tran1507.00930;
they were not re-audited as full papers in THIS fresh cover-focused pass.

Recommended claim if the mathematical audit succeeds:
  'We prove exact recovery of the unknown bounded frame in the stated
   independent regular-frame model, including the fibers of random lifts
   of every fixed connected simple regular base of degree at least three,
   without a spectral separation assumption.'
State the fixed K bound, noiseless exact-degree model, supplied degree
bound, all randomness assumptions, expected complexity/cap semantics,
and bounded-finest quantifier explicitly. Avoid 'first', 'breakthrough',
'new detection threshold', 'solves H-Cover', or any noisy robustness claim
without a separate established comparison.

No direct source quotation is used in this report apart from titles and
mathematical notation. Every source summary is scoped to material
actually inspected, with coverage limits disclosed above.
