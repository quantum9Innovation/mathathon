\section{Bounded intertwiner moments for regular lifts}\label{sec:lift-moment-proof}

Let $B$ be a fixed connected simple $d$-regular graph, $d\ge3$, with
$k$ vertices and $e=kd/2$ edges. Its random $N$-lift $G_N$ is obtained
by independently choosing a uniform perfect matching above each base
edge. Write $A_N$ for its adjacency matrix. All expectations in this
section are under this lift law; no simplicity conditioning is used.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lift-bounded-moment}
For every fixed prime $p$ and integer $r\ge0$ there is a finite constant
$C(B,p,r)$ such that, for every $R\in\mathbb F_p^{r\times r}$,
\[
 \sup_{N\ge1}\mathbb E\,
 |\{Y\in\mathbb F_p^{kN\times r}:A_NY=YR\}|\le C(B,p,r).
\]
The base may be bipartite, and the target may be singular or
nonsemisimple. There is no restriction on $N$ or on $p$ dividing $d$.
\end{theorem}

For bipartite bases we prove a larger class of statements, which is
needed to close the induction. If the base has sides $L,R_0$, give
them spaces $V_L,V_{R_0}$ of dimensions $r_L,r_{R_0}$ over
$\mathbb F_p$. At every base vertex $i$ fix a linear map
$T_i:V_{\mathrm{side}(i)}\to V_{\mathrm{opposite}(i)}$, possibly
depending on $i$. A variable $Y_v$ belongs to its side's space, and
the equation at a vertex above $i$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-system}
 \sum_{w\sim v}Y_w=T_i(Y_v).
\end{equation}
We prove that its expected number of solutions is bounded uniformly
in $N$ and in these maps, for fixed base, prime, and dimensions.
The original equation is the case of equal spaces with $T_i(x)=xR$.
Zero-dimensional spaces are allowed. For a nonbipartite base we use
one space $V=\mathbb F_p^r$ and the common map $x\mapsto xR$.

In either class let $V_i$ be the root space at type $i$, of size $q_i$,
and let $q'_i$ be the common size of its neighbor spaces. Put
$c=q_iq'_i$: this is independent of $i$, equal to $q^2$ in the
single-space case and to $uv$ in the bipartite case, where
$u=|V_L|$, $v=|V_{R_0}|$. Regular bipartite bases have equally many
vertices on their two sides. The case in which all spaces are zero
has exactly one assignment and will be excluded from local limit
calculations below.

\subsection{Exact counting and every zero of the entropy exponent}
An ordered star at type $i$ has root $x\in V_i$ and one label
$z_j\in V_j$ for each neighbor $j$ of $i$, subject to
$\sum_{j\sim i}z_j=T_i(x)$. There are
$q_i(q'_i)^{d-1}$ legal stars. Let $t_i(s)$ count these stars at the
$N$ labeled vertices of type $i$, and let $m_{ij}(a,b)$ be the
directed root--neighbor table. Compatibility is
$m_{ji}=m_{ij}^{\mathsf T}$. The exact contribution of a compatible
collection of star counts to the expected number of solutions is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-exact}
 \prod_i\frac{N!}{\prod_s t_i(s)!}
 \prod_{\{i,j\}\in E(B)}\frac{\prod_{a,b}m_{ij}(a,b)!}{N!}.
\end{equation}
The multinomials assign the stars to labeled vertices, including their
root labels. For a fixed assignment each matching pairs a category
$(a,b)$ with its reverse, giving the displayed factorial. Conversely
a lift and a root assignment determine all ordered stars uniquely.

Let $P_i=t_i/N$, let $\mu_i$ be its root law, and put
$\nu_{ij}=m_{ij}/N$. Its two marginals are $\mu_i,\mu_j$. The exponent
per fiber size is
\[
 \Phi=\sum_i H(P_i)-\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(B)}H(\nu_{ij}).
\]
All logarithms are natural. Zero-safe factorial bounds give the
contribution \eqref{eq:lmp-exact} as
$\exp\{N\Phi+O(\log(N+1))\}$, uniformly over the types. There are
polynomially many types, since every alphabet and the base are fixed.

For each omitted slot $t$, let $J_{it}$ be the conditional total
correlation of the retained $d-1$ labels given the root. The omitted
label is determined with coefficient one, so averaging the $d$
lossless deletions gives
\[
 H(P_i)=H(\mu_i)+\frac{d-1}{d}\sum_{j\sim i}H(Z_{ij}\mid X_i)
                    -\frac1d\sum_tJ_{it}.
\]
Using edge reversal and regularity cancels the root entropies and yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-entropy}
 \Phi=-\frac{d-2}{d}\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(B)}I(\nu_{ij})
              -\frac1d\sum_i\sum_tJ_{it}\le0.
\end{equation}
This is an identity of real entropies, also for unequal side alphabets;
it requires no division in the finite field.

We classify its zeros directly. Suppose $Z_1,\ldots,Z_d$ in a finite
vector space have fixed sum, and every retained $d-1$ tuple is a
product with fixed one-coordinate marginals. Fix two coordinates and
condition the other $d-2$ to any support values. Each remaining
conditional marginal is still its original marginal: the retained
product laws make these conditioning events positive and preserve
both marginals. Their sum is
fixed. The two marginals are therefore reflections of one another,
and their support-difference spaces coincide. Doing this for every
pair gives one common difference space $H$. Varying one conditioned
coordinate, available because $d\ge3$, makes these reflection
identities imply invariance under every generator of $H$. Each
marginal is consequently uniform on a full coset of $H$.

At equality in \eqref{eq:lmp-entropy}, every edge mutual information
and every $J_{it}$ vanish. Conditional on a root value, apply the
preceding argument to its neighbors. Each root law is observed as a
neighbor law at another base vertex. Thus all root laws are uniform
on cosets $a_i+H_i$, and all neighbor subspaces at any one vertex
coincide. Even walks have one class in a connected nonbipartite base
and exactly two classes in a connected bipartite base. Hence the
complete possibilities are:

In the nonbipartite case there is one common subspace $H\le V$, with
$HR\subseteq H$ and $a_iR-\sum_{j\sim i}a_j\in H$.
In the bipartite case there are subspaces $H_L\le V_L$ and
$H_{R_0}\le V_{R_0}$, with
$T_i(H_{\mathrm{side}(i)})\subseteq H_{\mathrm{opposite}(i)}$ and
$T_i(a_i)-\sum_{j\sim i}a_j\in H_{\mathrm{opposite}(i)}$ for every $i$.

Conversely each such collection gives a zero: choose a uniform root
in its coset, any $d-1$ independent uniform neighbor labels in their
cosets, and infer the last label. This is the unique uniform law on
the legal affine star support, and has the prescribed product edge
laws. We call these finitely many laws the phases. There is one full
uniform phase; every other phase has a proper subspace on at least
one side. Representatives need not themselves solve the base equations.

\subsection{A bounded open neighborhood of the full uniform phase}
We give both the normalization and the finite lattice factors. Under
$\mathsf P$ choose independently at each lift vertex a uniform legal
star. Under $\mathsf Q$ choose its root and all its neighbor labels
independently uniformly in their respective spaces. Let $M$ be the
whole directed-table array, and set
\[
 W(m)=\prod_{\{i,j\}\in E(B)}\frac{\prod_{a,b}m_{ij}(a,b)!}{N!}
\]
on compatible arrays and zero otherwise. Exact counting gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-normalizer}
 \mathbb E Z=c^{eN}\mathbb E_{\mathsf P}W(M),\qquad
 c^{eN}\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q}W(M)=1.
\end{equation}
For the second equality, every lift and every unrestricted root
assignment determine the neighbor labels uniquely. Dividing this
count by the number of independent-star assignments gives precisely
$c^{-eN}$. The first equality uses
$\prod_i q_i(q'_i)^{d-1}=c^e$.

Use $N$ iid copies of a vector containing one independent star of
each old type. The local real table-direction space $\mathcal H_i$
consists of $d$ tables of total zero with identical root-row margins.
Its dimension is $(q_i-1)+dq_i(q'_i-1)$. On
$\mathcal H=\bigoplus_i\mathcal H_i$, both laws have the same mean
table $\mu_{ij}=\mathbf1/c$ and the same positive covariance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-covariance}
 \Sigma_i=c^{-1}[I+(d-1)\Pi_i],
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_i$ projects onto the common row-constant modes. To verify
this, conditional on the root any two distinct neighbor labels are
independent uniform under either law: a third slot absorbs the sum
constraint. Conditional variances give $c^{-1}I$ on row-centered
modes, while the common root gives eigenvalue $d/c$ on the
$q_i-1$ common-row modes. Thus no other real restriction exists.

Let $\Lambda_0$ be the full integer common-row lattice in
$\mathcal H$. The $\mathsf Q$ support differences generate it:
within-row transfers generate the neighbor directions and root
changes generate the common-row directions. The $\mathsf P$
difference lattice $\Lambda$ is exactly its sublattice given, for
every type $i$ and root label $a$, by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-congruences}
 \sum_{j\sim i}\sum_b b\,m_{ij}(a,b)-T_i(a)n_i(a)=0
          \quad\hbox{in }V_{\mathrm{opposite}(i)},
\end{equation}
with $n_i(a)$ the common row margin. These congruences are independent
on $\Lambda_0$: a within-row transfer in one slot changes one
congruence by any desired neighbor-space vector.

Here is maximality, beyond necessity of the congruences. A circle
character constant on the legal local stars has functions
$\theta_j(a,b)$. Fix $a$. Varying two neighbor entries while a third
completes their sum forces
$\theta_j(a,b)=\alpha_j(a)+\chi_a(b)$, where $\chi_a$ is a common
additive character of the neighbor space. Root variation forces
$\sum_j\alpha_j(a)+\chi_a(T_i(a))$ to be constant. Modulo characters
already constant on the independent-star support, these are exactly
the characters of \eqref{eq:lmp-congruences}. Thus duality of
finite-index integer lattices gives the exact index
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-index}
 [\Lambda_0:\Lambda]=I_*:=\prod_i(q'_i)^{q_i}
 =\begin{cases}
 q^{kq},&\text{one space},\\
 v^{|L|u}u^{|R_0|v},&\text{two spaces}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The affine lattice hulls for $N$ copies are translates of these
lattices; the common all-zero legal stars provide a compatible point
for every $N$. Finite supports need not equal their lattice hulls.

On the growing window $\|m-N\mu\|\le\sqrt N\log N$, a relative
lattice local limit estimate gives, on the legal affine lattice,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-relative}
 \mathbb P_{\mathsf P}(M=m)
       =I_*(1+o(1))\mathbb P_{\mathsf Q}(M=m),
\end{equation}
uniformly; off that lattice the left side is zero. We include its
justification to specify the relative error. The finite-support log
moment generating function has positive Hessian \eqref{eq:lmp-covariance}
on its real span, so its locally invertible mean map gives a tilt of
size $O(\log N/\sqrt N)$ matching $m/N$. At the tilted mean Fourier
inversion on the maximal-lattice torus has one modulus-one maximum.
The tilt tends to zero, so all support weights stay bounded away
from zero. Off a fixed neighborhood of that maximum compactness
gives characteristic-function modulus at most a fixed $\rho<1$,
uniformly in the tilt. Within a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood,
positive covariance gives modulus at most $e^{-c_3\|\xi\|^2}$, and
the centered logarithm is its covariance quadratic plus
$O(\|\xi\|^3)$, uniformly. Outside $\|\xi\|\le N^{-2/5}$ the local
integral is therefore $O(e^{-c_4N^{1/5}})$, whereas inside it the
exponent's cubic remainder is $O(N^{-1/5})$. Rescaling by $\sqrt N$
and Gaussian domination give the density with covolume factor.
Explicitly, for either law $\gamma$,
on its affine lattice and with $h=\dim\mathcal H$,
\[
 \mathbb P_\gamma(M=m)=(1+o(1))\,
 \frac{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_\gamma)}
 {(2\pi N)^{h/2}(\det_{\mathcal H}\Sigma)^{1/2}}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{\langle m-N\mu,\Sigma^{-1}(m-N\mu)\rangle}{2N}\right].
\]
Undoing the tilt has rate Taylor error
$O(\log^3N/\sqrt N)=o(1)$. The two means and covariances agree, so
their leading densities differ exactly by the lattice index. This
also proves positive probability at all legal central lattice points
for sufficiently large $N$; no semigroup saturation is assumed.
Multiply \eqref{eq:lmp-relative} by the same nonnegative weight
$c^{eN}W(m)$ and sum over compatible central arrays. The exact
normalizer \eqref{eq:lmp-normalizer} bounds this contribution by
$I_*+o(1)$.

We must enlarge this window to a fixed open neighborhood. Write
$m_{ij}=N\mathbf1/c+\sqrt N X_{ij}$. Stirling's formula on one edge is
\[
 \frac{\prod_{a,b}m_{ij}(a,b)!}{N!}
 =c^{-N}(2\pi N)^{(c-1)/2}c^{-c/2}
       \exp\{c\|X_{ij}\|^2/2\}(1+o(1))
\]
in the logarithmic window. On the compatible real subspace decompose
uniquely
\[
 X_{ij}(a,b)=\frac{r_i(a)}{q_j}+\frac{r_j(b)}{q_i}+D_{ij}(a,b),
\]
where the root margins have zero total and $D_{ij}$ has zero row and
column sums. With norms counting both directed orientations,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-precision}
 -\frac12\langle X,\Sigma^{-1}X\rangle+\frac c4\|X\|^2
 =-\frac12\sum_iq_i\|r_i\|^2
                   -\frac c2\sum_{\{i,j\}}\|D_{ij}\|^2.
\end{equation}
It is strictly negative in every nonzero compatible direction,
including when one side has size one. The ambient dimension $h$ and
compatible dimension $s$ satisfy
\[
 h=\sum_i[(q_i-1)+dq_i(q'_i-1)],\quad
 s=\sum_i(q_i-1)+\sum_{\{i,j\}}(q_i-1)(q_j-1),\quad
 h-s=e(c-1).
\]
In particular the factorial powers match the Gaussian dimensions.
More directly for the required tail bound, the finite-support
Cram\'er rate is analytic near the mean with Hessian $\Sigma^{-1}$,
and the factorial rate is analytic while table frequencies stay
positive. Equation~\eqref{eq:lmp-precision} lets us choose a small
fixed neighborhood in which their combined loss dominates
$c_0\|m/N-\mu\|^2$. Chernoff's inequality and zero-safe factorial
bounds therefore bound each weighted summand by
$C_0N^{C_1}\exp[-c_0\|m-N\mu\|^2/N]$ there. There are polynomially
many arrays. The part outside $\sqrt N\log N$ is consequently $o(1)$.
We have proved a bounded contribution from an open full-uniform
edge-table neighborhood, for every lift size sequence.

\subsection{Proper phases, including leakage outside their supports}
Fix a proper phase with cosets $a_i+H_i$ and let $M_H(N)$ be the
expected number of solutions to its homogeneous restricted system.
For a solution $Y$ put
$D_a(Y)=|\{v:Y_v\notin a_{\mathrm{type}(v)}+H_{\mathrm{type}(v)}\}|$.
We prove, for a sufficiently small fixed $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-boundary}
 \mathbb E\#\{Y:D_a(Y)\le\varepsilon N\}
       \le[1+O(N^{-(d-2)})]M_H(N).
\end{equation}
Quotient by the subspaces and subtract the coset representatives.
The resulting quotient assignment solves a homogeneous system,
because the coset equations hold on every lift. For any fixed
quotient assignment, its lifts form an affine linear system whose
nonempty fiber has exactly the restricted homogeneous kernel size
$Z_H(G_N)$. This remains true when the representatives are not exact
base solutions. Zero defects therefore contribute at most $M_H(N)$.

For $s>0$ quotient defects, an outside-zero vertex cannot have exactly
one nonzero quotient neighbor. Take all edges incident to their
support $S$ as a certificate. If $a$ edges lie in $S$, $b=ds-2a$
leave it, and $w$ exterior vertices are touched, then
\[
 w\le b/2,\qquad E=ds-a\le ds,\qquad
 v=s+w,\qquad E-v\ge(d-2)s/2.
\]
For $s\le N/(2d)$, a valid prescribed certificate has probability
at most $(2/N)^E$, as every base-edge matching contributes
$1/(N)_{E_{ij}}$.

We also need a bound weighted by the restricted kernel, without an
independence assumption. Force the certificate in independent
uniform permutations. To force $u\mapsto v$, swap the images of $u$
and the present preimage of $v$. Earlier prescribed pairs remain
fixed. If $t$ pairs were already forced, every output satisfying the
new pair has exactly $N-t$ preimages before this operation, one per
unused possible former preimage of $v$. Hence the output is exactly
uniform conditional on the certificate. At most $E$ swaps are used.
Each changes at most four lifted vertex equations. If $m$ denotes
the original ambient dimension in the one-space case, or the sum of
side dimensions in the two-space case, the restricted operator's
rank changes by at most $4mE$. Its domain is unchanged, so
the coupled graphs satisfy
$Z_H(G_N^{\mathcal C})\le p^{4mE}Z_H(G_N)$ pointwise. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-conditioned-kernel}
 \mathbb E[Z_H(G_N)\mathbf1_{\mathcal C}]
       \le(2/N)^E p^{4mE}M_H(N).
\end{equation}
This applies also to rectangular two-space operators; the on-site
maps are unchanged by a matching switch.

Choose the support and its $w$ exterior vertices in at most
$(kN)^{s+w}/(s!w!)$ ways, quotient labels in at most $p^{ms}$ ways,
and incident slots and pairs in at most
$2^{d(1+d/2)s}(2ds)^E$ ways. Invalid prescriptions only overcount.
Combining with \eqref{eq:lmp-conditioned-kernel}, using
$E-v\ge\alpha s$, $\alpha=(d-2)/2$, and summing $w$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmp-defects}
 \mathbb E\#\{Y:D_a(Y)=s\}
       \le M_H(N)\,ds\,L^s(s/N)^{\alpha s}
\end{equation}
for a constant $L$ depending only on the fixed data. Indeed the only
remaining size factors are bounded by
\[
 \frac{(4ds)^E N^{v-E}}{s!w!}
 \le(4d)^{ds}(s/N)^{\alpha s}\frac{s^s}{s!}\frac{s^w}{w!};
\]
use $s^s/s!\le e^s$, $\sum_w s^w/w!\le e^s$, and at most $ds$
choices of $E$. The factor $p^{4mE}$ is exponential in $s$, not a
power of $N$, and is absorbed in $L^s$.
For sufficiently small fixed $\varepsilon$, the right-hand terms
of \eqref{eq:lmp-defects} have consecutive ratio at most one-half
for $2\le s\le\varepsilon N$. Singleton support is impossible in
a lift of a simple base. The sum starts at $s=2$ and is
$O(N^{-(d-2)})M_H(N)$, proving \eqref{eq:lmp-boundary}.

\subsection{Closed induction and completion of the proof}
For a nonbipartite base induct on $r$. Each proper phase has a common
proper invariant $H$, and its homogeneous restricted problem is the
same problem in dimension $\dim H<r$. For a bipartite base induct on
$r_L+r_{R_0}$. Its restricted spaces $H_L,H_{R_0}$ and restricted
maps belong to the enlarged class, with strictly smaller total
dimension at every proper phase. This includes a zero-dimensional
side; total dimension zero has one assignment. There are finitely
many maps at each fixed dimension, so induction bounds can be uniform
over them.

Each proper phase has an open neighborhood in the feasible star-law
space on which root mass outside its cosets is less than
$\varepsilon$ in total. Its complete expected count is bounded by
\eqref{eq:lmp-boundary} and the induction hypothesis. The finitely
many such neighborhoods, together with the bounded open full-uniform
edge-table neighborhood, contain every entropy zero. Their closed
complement is compact. Entropy is continuous even at zero entries,
so $\Phi\le-c_2<0$ there. Equation~\eqref{eq:lmp-exact}, its uniform
polynomial factorial remainder, and polynomially many types bound
the complementary contribution by a polynomial times $e^{-c_2N}$.
Thus the full expected number has finite limsup. Finitely many
smaller lift sizes have finite moments and are absorbed into the
constant. Finitely many target matrices give uniformity in $R$.
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:lift-bounded-moment}.

The proof uses an upper comparison with an exact independent-star
normalizer; it needs no exact scalar phase coefficient. All bases,
fields and dimensions are fixed. No growing-parameter, degree-two,
nonregular-frame, deterministic-lift or numerical-stability assertion
is made here.
