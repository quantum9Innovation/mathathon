\paragraph{Scope and verdict.}
This note independently proves the counting and entropy part of
\texttt{regular-frame-no-internal-root.txt}. The all-slot identity is
correct after within-type slot symmetrization. Internal degrees zero and
one cause no obstruction. This note does not prove the separate real
one-hot rigidity, small-support exclusion, recovery theorem, or historical
novelty. All logarithms are natural.

\paragraph{Model and conventions.}
Let $V_1,\ldots,V_k$ be labeled classes of sizes $n_i>0$, with
$N=\sum_i n_i$. Fix a nonnegative integer frame $Q=(d_{ij})$ such that
\[
 \sum_jd_{ij}=d\geq3\quad\text{for every }i,\qquad
 n_i d_{ij}=n_jd_{ji},\qquad n_i d_{ii}\text{ is even}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Put $a_i=d_{ii}$; there is \emph{no} lower bound on $a_i$.
At each vertex of old type $i$, its $d_{ij}$ stubs toward old type $j$
are individually labeled. Uniformly pair each internal stub pool, and
uniformly match each active cross-factor's two equal pools, independently.
Write $M_{ii}=n_i a_i$ and $M_{ij}=n_i d_{ij}=n_jd_{ji}$ for $i<j$.
Zero pools are omitted. Loops and parallel edges are retained until
simplicity conditioning; a loop contributes two to the adjacency diagonal
and to the degree. Thus the adjacency equation uses exactly the stub sum.

Fix a prime $p$, an integer $r\geq1$, an alphabet $\mathcal A=\F_p^r$
of size $q=p^r$, and any $r\times r$ matrix $R$ over $\F_p$. Set
\[
 Z_R(A)=|\{Y\in\F_p^{N\times r}:AY=YR\}|.
\]
The matrix $R$ need not be invertible, diagonalizable, or a degree frame.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit polynomial first moment]
Assume $k,r\leq K$, and define
\[
 q_*:=p^K,\qquad
 F_*:=K+\frac{K(K+3)}2q_*^2,\qquad
 B_*:=Kq_*^d+F_*.
 \tag{2}
\]
For every admissible size in the configuration model,
\[
 \E Z_R(A)\leq(3d)^{F_*}(N+1)^{B_*}.                 \tag{3}
\]
The bound is uniform in $R$ and the positive class proportions. If the
fixed old frame is connected and all factors are conditioned simple,
then, eventually along every admissible size sequence,
\[
 \E_{\rm simple} Z_R(A)\leq
 2e^{K^2d^2}(3d)^{F_*}(N+1)^{B_*}.                 \tag{4}
\]
The eventual threshold can be chosen uniformly over the finite family of
connected integer frames with $k\leq K$ and row sum $d$. In particular
$p=2$ is allowed and then $B_*=K2^{Kd}+K+K(K+3)2^{2K}/2$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Legal ordered stars.}
Order each type's $d$ physical stub slots, retaining their old neighbor
types. An ordered star is $(x,z_1,\ldots,z_d)\in\mathcal A^{d+1}$
with local constraint
\[
 \sum_{t=1}^d z_t=xR.                              \tag{5}
\]
For \emph{any} omitted physical slot $t$, the root and other $d-1$
labels determine $z_t$ with coefficient one. Thus the legal-star set
$\mathcal S_i$ has exactly $q^d$ elements, even when $a_i=0$.
Let $T_i(s)$ be its star counts, summing to $n_i$, and let $t_i(x)$ be
the root count. The directed pooled stub table is
\[
 m_{ij}(x,y)=\sum_sT_i(s)
   \sum_{t:\text{slot }t\text{ has old type }j}
        \boldsymbol1\{\text{root}(s)=x,z_t=y\}.     \tag{6}
\]
Compatibility requires
\[
 m_{ii}(x,y)=m_{ii}(y,x),\quad m_{ii}(x,x)\text{ even},\qquad
 m_{ij}(x,y)=m_{ji}(y,x).                           \tag{7}
\]
For $a_i=0$ the internal table is absent. For $a_i=1$, (7) still holds
and expresses the ordinary perfect-matching categories.
For even $u\geq0$ write $P(u)=(u-1)!!$, with $P(0)=1$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact factorial contribution]
The exact contribution of a compatible collection $T=(T_i)$ to
$\E Z_R$ is
\begin{align}
 W(T)={}&\prod_i\frac{n_i!}{\prod_{s\in\mathcal S_i}T_i(s)!}
 \prod_{i:a_i>0}
 \frac{\prod_{x<y}m_{ii}(x,y)!\prod_xP(m_{ii}(x,x))}{P(M_{ii})}
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad\times\prod_{i<j:M_{ij}>0}
       \frac{\prod_{x,y}m_{ij}(x,y)!}{M_{ij}!}.
 \tag{8}
\end{align}
The moment is exactly the sum of (8) over compatible types.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first multinomial assigns ordered stars to labeled vertices, already
choosing every root label. There is no additional root-label multinomial.
For an internal factor, category $(x,y)$ must be paired with $(y,x)$.
For $x<y$ their bijections number $m_{ii}(x,y)!$; for $x=y$ their
pairings number $P(m_{ii}(x,x))$. The denominator counts all pairings.
For a cross factor, category bijections number $\prod_{x,y}m_{ij}(x,y)!$
out of $M_{ij}!$. These arguments apply without change to degrees one
and zero (the latter is an empty factor). Independence gives (8).
Conversely a configuration and a root labeling determine every ordered
star uniquely, including at loops. Hence nothing is missed or counted
twice. The count concerns uniform labeled-stub configurations, not
uniform multigraphs.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Entropy laws and uniform remainder.}
Write $P_i=T_i/n_i$ for the star law and $\mu_i=t_i/n_i$ for its root
law. For every active factor put $\nu_{ij}=m_{ij}/M_{ij}$. By (6)--(7),
internal pair laws are symmetric with both marginals $\mu_i$, and cross
pair laws have marginals $\mu_i,\mu_j$ and reverse on exchanging $i,j$.
Define the \emph{unnormalized} exponent
\[
 \Psi(P)=\sum_i n_i\HH(P_i)
 -\sum_{i:a_i>0}\frac{n_i a_i}{2}\HH(\nu_{ii})
 -\sum_{i<j:M_{ij}>0}M_{ij}\HH(\nu_{ij}).          \tag{9}
\]
Thus a normalized exponent would be $\Phi=\Psi/N$.

Set $f(u)=u\log u-u$, $f(0)=0$. For integers $u\geq0$,
\[
 0\leq\log(u!)-f(u)\leq\log(u+1)+1.
\]
Using $P(u)=u!/[2^{u/2}(u/2)!]$ for even $u$ gives
$|\log P(u)-f(u)/2|\leq2[\log(u+1)+1]$. All counts are at most $dN$.
The root multinomial has upper error at most $\log(dN+1)+1$ after
dropping the nonnegative denominator errors. An active cross factor has
upper error at most $q^2[\log(dN+1)+1]$. Symmetry of the internal table
gives its main term $-M_{ii}\HH(\nu_{ii})/2$ and upper error at most
\[
 \left[\frac{q(q-1)}2+2q+2\right][\log(dN+1)+1]
 \leq2q^2[\log(dN+1)+1].
\]
The last inequality uses $q\geq2$. Zero factors need no estimate. Summing
over at most $K$ roots, $K$ internal and $K(K-1)/2$ cross factors yields
\[
 W(T)\leq\exp\{\Psi(P)+F_*[\log(dN+1)+1]\}.       \tag{10}
\]
All these estimates are uniform at zero entries and on boundary types.

\begin{lemma}[All-slot entropy identity]
Symmetrize each $P_i$ over permutations of slots within each old neighbor
type, and write $\overline P_i$ for the resulting law. Let $X_i$ be its
root and $Z_{i,1},\ldots,Z_{i,d}$ its physical neighbor labels. For every
slot $t$ let
\[
 J_{it}=\sum_{s\ne t}\HH(Z_{i,s}\mid X_i)
           -\HH((Z_{i,s})_{s\ne t}\mid X_i)\geq0.
\]
Let $I_{ij}$ be the mutual information of the pooled pair law $\nu_{ij}$,
and put $E_{ii}=n_i a_i/2$, $E_{ij}=M_{ij}$ for $i<j$. Omitting zero
factors, the exact identity is
\[
 \Psi(\overline P)=
 -\frac{d-2}{d}\sum_{i\leq j}E_{ij}I_{ij}
 -\sum_i\frac{n_i}{d}\sum_{t=1}^dJ_{it}\leq0.
 \tag{11}
\]
Moreover $\Psi(P)\leq\Psi(\overline P)$, so every original compatible
type has a nonpositive exponent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Within-type slot permutations preserve the local sum constraint, root
laws and pooled pair tables. Entropy concavity increases star entropy;
the negative pair-entropy terms stay fixed. The averaged law need not be
an integer empirical type; it is used only in this entropy inequality.

For every physical slot $t$, equation (5) determines its value from the
root and retained slots. Hence
\[
 \HH(\overline P_i)=\HH(\mu_i)+
        \HH((Z_{i,s})_{s\ne t}\mid X_i).
\]
Average these $d$ exact identities and insert $J_{it}$:
\begin{align}
 \HH(\overline P_i)
 &=\HH(\mu_i)+\frac{d-1}{d}
           \sum_{s=1}^d\HH(Z_{i,s}\mid X_i)
          -\frac1d\sum_tJ_{it} \nonumber\\
 &=(2-d)\HH(\mu_i)+\frac{d-1}{d}
           \sum_jd_{ij}\HH(\nu_{ij})
          -\frac1d\sum_tJ_{it}.                  \tag{12}
\end{align}
The second line uses only exchangeability \emph{within} each neighbor
type. Different old neighbor types are never permuted or identified.
By reversibility,
\[
 \sum_i n_i\sum_jd_{ij}\HH(\nu_{ij})
      =2\sum_{i\leq j}E_{ij}\HH(\nu_{ij}).
 \tag{13}
\]
This includes internal factors: $n_i a_i=2E_{ii}$. Substitution into
(9) leaves coefficient $2(d-1)/d-1=(d-2)/d$ on each pair entropy.
Use
\[
 \HH(\nu_{ij})=\HH(\mu_i)+\HH(\mu_j)-I_{ij},
\]
with two copies of $\HH(\mu_i)$ when $i=j$. The root-entropy identity
\[
 \sum_{i\leq j}E_{ij}[\HH(\mu_i)+\HH(\mu_j)]
      =d\sum_i n_i\HH(\mu_i)
\]
cancels the $(2-d)$ term in (12) exactly and gives (11).
No internal slot was singled out, and no internal degree or prime
characteristic was divided by. Averaging by $d$ is an operation on real
entropies, so $p$ dividing $d$ is immaterial.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of (3)]
There are at most $\prod_i(n_i+1)^{q^d}\leq(N+1)^{Kq_*^d}$ count
types. Apply (10)--(11) to each. Since $dN+1\leq d(N+1)$ and $e<3$,
their sum is at most $(3d)^{F_*}(N+1)^{Kq_*^d+F_*}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Simplicity, including zero and one degrees.}
An internal degree-zero factor is absent and contributes simplicity
probability one. An internal degree-one factor is a perfect matching
on an even number of vertices and is always simple. For an internal
degree $a\geq2$, elementary bad patterns are loops and unordered pairs
of parallel edges, with limiting mean $(a-1)/2$ and $(a-1)^2/4$.
For an active cross $(b,c)$-biregular factor, the double-edge mean is
$(b-1)(c-1)/2$; it is zero if either degree is one. A $(1,1)$ factor is
exactly a perfect matching and is always simple.

For completeness, these means follow by choosing the one or two relevant
vertices and their stubs and dividing by the full pairing or matching
count. For every fixed factorial moment order, disjoint bad patterns give
the product limits. Consistent overlapping distinct bad patterns have
union edge excess at least one and contribute $O(1/\min_i n_i)$;
inconsistent prescriptions contribute zero. The edge-excess bound follows
also for overlapping double pairs sharing an edge: their union has at
least three parallel edges on two vertices. Fixed degrees and positive
proportions give $O(N^v)$ vertex choices and $O(N^{-e})$ prescribed-pair
probability for a union with $v$ vertices and $e$ edges. Finite Bonferroni
bounds for the event of no bad pattern, followed by growing truncation
order, therefore give a Poisson zero-event limit.

By factor independence, the joint simplicity probability tends to
$e^{-\Lambda}$, where
\[
 \Lambda=
 \sum_{i:a_i\geq1}\frac{a_i^2-1}{4}
 +\sum_{i<j:M_{ij}>0}\frac{(d_{ij}-1)(d_{ji}-1)}2
 \leq K^2d^2.                                    \tag{14}
\]
The restriction $a_i\geq1$ is essential in this notation: substituting
$a_i=0$ into $(a_i^2-1)/4$ would incorrectly give a negative contribution.
The contribution at $a_i=0$ is zero, and at $a_i=1$ the formula gives zero.
The limiting probability is positive, and eventually at least
$e^{-K^2d^2}/2$. Conditioning an expectation of a nonnegative random
variable costs at most its reciprocal, proving (4).

Every simple internal factor has exactly $(a_i!)^{n_i}$ labeled-stub
realizations, including $a_i=0,1$. Every simple cross factor has exactly
$(d_{ij}!)^{n_i}(d_{ji}!)^{n_j}$ realizations. Thus conditioning gives
precisely independent uniform simple factors. There is no extra even-size
condition: only $n_i a_i$ even and edge balance are used. In particular,
all cross perfect matchings between equal odd-sized classes are allowed.

For connected fixed frames, balance along paths gives
\[
 \min_i n_i\geq \frac{N}{K d^{K-1}}.
\]
There are finitely many integer frames
under fixed $K,d$, and each consistent connected frame fixes its size
ratios. Thus the eventual simplicity threshold is uniform over that
finite family and its admissible size sequences.

\paragraph{Consequences and limits.}
The finite-alphabet count also applies to any subset of a fixed additive
alphabet with an exact integer sum equation: legal stars then number at
most $q^d$. Thus the same entropy exponent and polynomial remainder are
available for real one-hot candidates, separately from the modular moment.
The stronger zero-exponent rigidity of such integer candidates still
requires its own proof. The moment theorem alone does not assert a
minimum kernel dimension, absence of new eigenvalues, or exact recovery.

For random lifts of a fixed connected simple $d$-regular base, take all
$a_i=0$, equal class sizes, and $d_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$. Every active factor
is a uniform independent perfect matching and is automatically simple;
so (3) holds directly in the intended lift model without conditioning.
At $d=2$, (11) remains nonpositive but its mutual-information coefficient
vanishes; neither one-hot rigidity nor recovery follows from this note.
No degree-independent polynomial exponent is claimed: (2) deliberately
retains $q_*^d$. Historical novelty and more refined counting are outside
the present audit.
