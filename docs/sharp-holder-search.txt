1. EXACT RANK-ONE REDUCTION
--------------------------
For 0<s<=1, the full PSD constant is exactly

 R_(s,alpha)=sup Tr[((H+vv*)^alpha-H^alpha)^s], H>=0, ||v||=1,

with the supremum over finite dimensions. One direction is immediate.
For the other, spectrally decompose E=B-A=E_+-E_- and telescope the two
increasing PSD paths

          A -> A+E_+=B+E_- <- B.

Split each E_+ and E_- into its positive orthogonal rank-one summands.
Homogeneity bounds an increment of magnitude t by R*t^(alpha*s).
The exact Schatten s-power triangle inequality gives the denominator
Tr|E|^(alpha*s), with no factor from the number of increments.

Thus it suffices to prove (E) for normalized positive rank-one updates.
Their correction Delta is positive and has ||Delta||<=1: indeed vv*<=I,
H+vv*<=H+I, and operator monotonicity gives

  0<=Delta<=(H+I)^alpha-H^alpha<=I.

2. TAPERED ANALYTIC QUADRATURE LEMMA
----------------------------------
The Gaussian-window implementation below is the independently derived
simplification supplied by nongalactic_wildcard; it replaces this file's
first super-Gaussian construction.

Let F(z) take values in a Banach space, be analytic on |Im z|<d0, and
for each fixed d<d0 satisfy

 ||F(x+i eta)|| <= M_d exp[-g(x)], |eta|<=d,
 g(x)=a*(-x)_+ + b*x_+,      a,b>0.                           (Q1)

Suppose every real F(x) has rank at most r when the values are operators.
Then, for every fixed d<d0 and large T, a finite REAL-node quadrature Q_T
satisfies

 || integral_R F(x)dx - Q_T || <= M_d exp(-T),

 number of nodes <= [T^2/(4*pi*d)]*(1/a+1/b)+O(T^(7/4)).       (Q2)

The implicit constant may depend on a,b,d, but is uniform over all F
obeying (Q1) with the same M_d. All quadrature weights are positive, so
positive real F gives a positive quadrature. The rank of Q_T is at most
r times the number of nodes.

2.1 Entire Gaussian windows

Put ell=T^(3/4), kappa=max(a,b), I_j=[j*ell,(j+1)*ell], j in Z, and

 phi_j(z)=(1/sqrt(pi))*integral_(I_j) exp[-(z-u)^2]du.

The phi_j are entire, nonnegative on R, and sum_(j in Z) phi_j(z)=1 on
any fixed horizontal strip (by Gaussian integration and analytic
continuation). Moreover

 |phi_j(x+i eta)| <= exp(d^2)*phi_j(x), |eta|<=d.

Writing m_j=min_(u in I_j)g(u), the Lipschitz bound
|g(x)-g(u)|<=kappa*|x-u| and the shifted Gaussian integral imply

 integral_R exp[-g(x)] phi_j(x)dx
 <= (1/sqrt(pi))*integral_(I_j) exp[-g(u)]du
                       *integral_R exp[-r^2+kappa*|r|]dr
 <= 2*exp(kappa^2/4)*ell*exp(-m_j).                         (Q3)

Consequently the sum of the two boundary L1 norms of F phi_j is at most
4*M_d*exp(d^2+kappa^2/4)*ell*exp(-m_j).

Retain just intervals I_j meeting

        [-(T+ell)/a, (T+ell)/b].

There are J=O(T/ell) retained intervals, whose union U is an interval
containing the displayed one. By the same convolution estimate, the
partition remainder satisfies

 ||integral F(x)[1-sum_(retained j)phi_j(x)]dx||
 <=2*M_d*exp(kappa^2/4)*integral_(R minus U) exp[-g(u)]du
 <=2*M_d*exp(kappa^2/4)*(1/a+1/b)*exp[-T-ell].               (Q4)

2.2 Windowwise sinc quadrature and truncation

For each retained j put

 Q_j=max(ell, T-m_j+3*ell),       h_j=2*pi*d/Q_j.

Apply the standard strip trapezoid bound separately to F phi_j. From
(Q3) and m_j+Q_j>=T+3*ell, its error is at most

         C_(a,b,d)*M_d*ell*exp[-T-3*ell].

Summing over retained j gives C*M_d*T*exp[-T-3*ell].

Truncate each real lattice to nodes in I_j+[-2*sqrt(T),2*sqrt(T)]. For
x outside I_j,

 phi_j(x)<=ell/sqrt(pi)*exp[-dist(x,I_j)^2].

Since h_j<=2*pi*d/ell, the omitted discrete Gaussian tail, including
its factor h_j, is at most

 C*M_d*ell*(h_j+T^(-1/2))*exp(-4*T).

Its sum over j is polynomially bounded times exp(-4*T). Combining this
with (Q4) proves the approximation error in (Q2) for sufficiently large
T. Every retained summand is h_j*phi_j(k*h_j)*F(k*h_j), with a positive
scalar weight.

2.3 Sharp leading node count

Window j needs at most (ell+4*sqrt(T))/h_j+2 nodes. Also

 Q_j <= (T-m_j)_+ + 3*ell.

The leading Riemann sum has error O(T*ell):

 sum_(retained j) ell*(T-m_j)_+
    =integral_R (T-g(x))_+ dx+O(T*ell)
    =(T^2/2)*(1/a+1/b)+O(T*ell).

The 3*ell contribution is O(T*ell). The expanded-window overhead is
O(sqrt(T)*J*(T+ell))=O(T^(5/2)/ell). With ell=T^(3/4), all errors are
O(T^(7/4))=o(T^2). This proves (Q2) with its sharp coefficient.

3. PSD UPPER CONSTANT
--------------------
Write c_alpha=sin(pi*alpha)/pi and beta=1-alpha. For Delta=(H+vv*)^alpha
-H^alpha, ||v||=1, the usual resolvent integral gives

 Delta=integral_R F(y)dy,
 F(y)=c_alpha*t^(alpha+1)*[(H+t)^(-1)-(H+vv*+t)^(-1)], t=exp(y).

For real y, F(y) is positive and rank one. For every d<pi, it is analytic
on |Im y|<pi and satisfies

 ||F(x+i eta)|| <= C_(alpha,d)
             min(exp(alpha*x), exp(-beta*x)), |eta|<=d.       (U1)

Indeed for x0>=0 and |arg t|<=d<pi,
|x0+t|>=cos(d/2)*(x0+|t|). This bounds t times each resolvent by a
d-dependent constant; the resolvent identity gives the alternative
constant/|t| bound because ||vv*||=1. Multiplying by t^alpha proves U1.

Apply (Q2) with a=alpha, b=beta and r=1. If N_Delta(lambda) denotes the
number of eigenvalues exceeding lambda, then uniformly in dimension,
H and v,

 limsup_(T->infinity) N_Delta(exp(-T))/T^2
                         <=1/(4*pi*d*alpha*beta).

The multiplicative U1 constant only shifts T by O_(alpha,d)(1). Letting
d increase to pi gives the uniform bound

 limsup_(T->infinity) sup_(H,v) N_Delta(exp(-T))/T^2
                         <=1/(4*pi^2*alpha*beta).           (U2)

Since ||Delta||<=1, layer cake gives

 Tr Delta^s=s*integral_0^infinity exp(-s*T) N_Delta(exp(-T))dT.

The uniform form of U2, and integral_0^infinity exp(-s*T)T^2dT=2/s^3,
prove

 limsup_(s->0) s^2 R_(s,alpha) <=1/(2*pi^2*alpha*beta).       (U3)

4. LOWER VIA WEIGHTED CAUCHY FACTORIZATION
---------------------------------------
Choose 0<epsilon<1 and delta>0, and let

 rho(x)=c0*exp(epsilon*x), x<=0;
 rho(x)=c0*exp(-delta*x),  x>=0;
 c0=(1/epsilon+1/delta)^(-1).

This is a probability density for log-spectrum x. Formally on L2(R),
H is multiplication by exp(x), v=sqrt(rho), and

 m(t)=<v,(H+t)^(-1)v>=integral rho(x)/(exp(x)+t)dx.

The rigorous finite-interval/finite-matrix passage is in Section 4.3.
Woodbury factors the correction as Delta=T T*, where T:L2(dy)->L2(dx)
has kernel

 T(x,y)=sqrt(c_alpha)*sqrt(rho(x))*exp((alpha+1)y/2)
                    /[(exp(x)+exp(y))*sqrt(1+m(exp(y)))].

Put

 C(x-y)=1/[2*cosh((x-y)/2)],
 a(x)=sqrt(c_alpha)*sqrt(rho(x))*exp(-x/2),
 b(x)=exp(alpha*x/2)/sqrt(1+m(exp(x))),
 q(x)=[a(x)b(x)]^2
     =c_alpha*rho(x)*exp(-beta*x)/(1+m(exp(x))).

Then T=M_a C M_b. With Fourier convention
hat C(xi)=integral C(x)exp(-i*xi*x)dx, one has exactly

                    hat C(xi)=pi*sech(pi*xi).                (L1)

4.1 A general lower bound for this nonsymmetric factor

Let I be a finite interval, P_I its multiplication projection, and
C_I=P_I C P_I on L2(I). Compress both variables of T to I, obtaining
T_I=M_a C_I M_b. Since a,b are positive and bounded above/below on I,

 T_I is similar to S_I=M_sqrt(ab) C_I M_sqrt(ab)

by multiplication with sqrt(a/b). S_I is positive. The standard
eigenvalue/singular-value inequality for compact operators gives, for
p>0,

       Tr|T_I|^p >= Tr S_I^p.

For 0<p<=1, reversed Araki--Lieb--Thirring gives

 Tr S_I^p >= Tr [M_(ab)^p C_I^p].

Compression Jensen for the operator-concave function t^p gives

       C_I^p >= P_I C^p P_I.

The Fourier multiplier C^p has diagonal kernel
(1/(2*pi))*integral [pi*sech(pi*xi)]^p dxi. Taking p=2s (s<=1/2)
therefore yields

 Tr Delta^s >= (1/(2*pi))
       * integral_I q(x)^s dx
       * integral_R [pi*sech(pi*xi)]^(2s) dxi.                (L2)

For a globally defined compact factor T, the double compression lowers
each SINGULAR VALUE: s_j(P_I T P_I)<=s_j(T). One must NOT assert the
generally false Loewner inequality TT*>=(P_I T P_I)(P_I T P_I)*. The
preferred bounded-interval proof in Section 4.3 instead establishes its
direct Loewner comparison Delta_R>=T_R T_R* from the positive integral,
before applying the similarity argument.

4.2 Evaluation of the two factors

At t down to zero, substitution r=exp(x) in m(t) gives

 m(t) ~ c0*[pi/sin(pi*epsilon)]*t^(epsilon-1).

At infinity m(t)<=1/t ->0. Hence

 q(x) ~ [sin(pi*alpha)*sin(pi*epsilon)/pi^2]*exp(alpha*x)
                                      as x->-infinity,
 q(x) ~ c_alpha*c0*exp[-(beta+delta)*x]
                                      as x->+infinity.

Elementary exponential-tail integration gives

 lim_(s->0) s*integral_R q(x)^s dx
                      =1/alpha + 1/(beta+delta).

Also, exactly,

 integral_R [pi*sech(pi*xi)]^(2s) dxi
       =pi^(2s-1)*sqrt(pi)*Gamma(s)/Gamma(s+1/2),

so s times this integral tends to 1/pi. Consequently (L2) gives

 liminf_(s->0) s^2 R_(s,alpha)
             >=(1/(2*pi^2))*[1/alpha+1/(beta+delta)].         (L3)

Let delta decrease to zero. The right side increases to
1/[2*pi^2*alpha*beta], matching U3 and proving (E), subject to audit.

4.3 Passage using only finite matrices

The clean restriction below was independently suggested by root. No
theorem for general unbounded-operator functional calculus is needed.
Fix I_R=[-R,R]. On L2(I_R), let H_R=exp(x) and v_R=sqrt(rho(x)), WITHOUT
renormalizing. Then t_R=||v_R||^2<=1 and the Woodbury denominator uses

 m_R(t)=integral_(I_R) rho(x)/(exp(x)+t)dx <=m(t).

Therefore the true positive correction Delta_R dominates the integral
factor obtained by replacing m_R by the larger GLOBAL m. Restricting
also the log-integration variable y to I_R shows

 Delta_R >= T_R T_R*,
 T_R=M_a C_(I_R) M_b,

where a,b,q are exactly the global functions in Sections 4--4.2. The
similarity/ALT/Jensen argument thus gives

 Tr Delta_R^s >= (J_s/(2*pi))*integral_(I_R) q(x)^s dx.

For fixed R, approximate exp(x) uniformly by positive step functions
with finitely many values, keeping E_R=v_R v_R* fixed. Since H_R is
bounded and bounded below, the resulting corrections converge in
operator norm by the fractional resolvent integral (or norm-continuity
of bounded positive functional calculus). Each step-function correction
acts only in the finite-dimensional span of the vectors
1_(step interval)*v_R, and is therefore exactly a finite-matrix rank-one
example. On the orthogonal complement the correction is zero.

For each such finite matrix, its input rank-one norm is t_R<=1, so

 R_(s,alpha) >= Tr Delta_(R,n)^s / t_R^(alpha*s)
             >= Tr Delta_(R,n)^s.

Fixed singular-value partial sums converge under norm convergence.
Fatou over these partial sums therefore gives

 R_(s,alpha) >= Tr Delta_R^s
             >= (J_s/(2*pi))*integral_(I_R) q(x)^s dx.

Let R increase to infinity. Monotone convergence of the last ordinary
scalar integral proves the finite-matrix form of L2 used in L3. This
avoids renormalization and any infinite-dimensional Holder theorem.

6.2 A central update with a positive scalar denominator

Fix delta>0 and take the positive-log-spectrum probability density

 rho(x)=c0*exp(x),       x<=0;
 rho(x)=c0*exp(-delta*x), x>=0;
 c0=(1+1/delta)^(-1).

Let D be multiplication by exp(x) on L2(R), w=sqrt(rho), and formally
set on L2(R) plus L2(R)

 H=diag(D,-D),    v=(w,w)/sqrt(2),    E=vv*,
 A=H-E/2,        B=H+E/2.

Then B-A=E has norm one. The rigorous finite-interval transfer below
again avoids any unbounded functional-calculus assumption.

Let P swap the two summands. Since PHP=-H and PEP=E, one has PAP=-B.
Under the fixed Hadamard unitary which diagonalizes P,

 H -> [0 D; D 0],      v -> (w,0),      E -> [ww* 0; 0 0].

For R_H(z)=(H-z)^(-1) and m(z)=v*R_H(z)v, the exact central Woodbury
identity is

 R_B(z)-R_A(z)=-R_H(z) E R_H(z)/[1-m(z)^2/4].                (W2)

Indeed the two rank-one resolvent denominators are 1+m/2 and 1-m/2;
subtracting their +/-E/2 formulas gives W2. At z=it,

 m(it)=i*M(t),
 M(t)=t*integral_R rho(x)/(exp(2*x)+t^2)dx >=0,

Thus the same REAL POSITIVE weight omega occurs for both poles +/-it.
This is the reason to center the endpoints at H rather than use H,H+E.

Put R(t)=(D^2+t^2)^(-1) and

 c_o=2*cos(pi*alpha/2)/pi,
 c_e=2*sin(pi*alpha/2)/pi.

6.3 Odd map: two exact positive channels

In the P-eigenbasis, direct substitution in the odd fractional-power
resolvent integral gives exactly

 f_odd(B)-f_odd(A)=diag(S_+,-S_-),

 S_+=c_o*integral_0^infinity t^(alpha+2)*omega(t)
                         R(t) w w* R(t)dt,
 S_-=c_o*integral_0^infinity t^alpha*omega(t)
                         D R(t) w w* R(t) D dt.             (W4)

For verification, in that basis

 R_H(it)=[it R(t), D R(t); D R(t), it R(t)],

and the real part of R_H(it) E R_H(it) is

 diag(-t^2 R(t)ww*R(t), D R(t)ww*R(t)D).

The odd integral multiplies this by -c_o*t^alpha*omega(t), proving W4.
Both S_+ and S_- are positive.

Now use the convolution

 C_1(x-y)=1/[2*cosh(x-y)],
 hat C_1(xi)=(pi/2)*sech(pi*xi/2).                          (W5)

After t=exp(y), the two positive operators factor as T_+ T_+* and
T_- T_-*, with

 T_+=M_(sqrt(c_o)*sqrt(rho)*exp(-x))
                         C_1 M_(exp((alpha+1)*x/2)*sqrt(omega(exp(x)))),
 T_-=M_(sqrt(c_o)*sqrt(rho))

The product of the two multiplier weights has the SAME square in both

       q_o(x)=c_o*rho(x)*exp(-beta*x)*omega(exp(x)).          (W6)

Each channel therefore admits exactly the similarity/ALT/compression

6.4 Even map: a second similarity gives the same two channels' coefficient

For the even map, the same resolvent computation gives exactly

 f_even(B)-f_even(A)=[0 O; O* 0],

Hence every singular value of O appears TWICE in the full correction.

 O=c_e*M_(sqrt(rho)*exp(-x)) C_1

On a fixed finite interval I, O is similar (by multiplication exp(-x/2))

 S=c_e*M_(sqrt(rho)*exp(-x/2)) C_1

Thus Weyl's singular-value/eigenvalue inequality gives

                  Tr|O|^s>=Tr S^s=Tr|U|^(2s).

6.5 Tail constants and exact lower coefficient

For the selected rho, substitution a=exp(x) gives

 M(t)=c0*t*integral_0^1 1/(a^2+t^2)da
               +c0*t*integral_1^infinity a^(-delta-1)/(a^2+t^2)da.

The first term is c0*arctan(1/t), while the second tends to zero as

 M(t)->c0*pi/2 as t->0,       M(t)<=1/t->0 as t->infinity.

Consequently omega has a strictly positive finite limit at each end,
and, for either q_o or q_e,

 q(x)~constant*exp(alpha*x)          as x->-infinity,
 q(x)~constant*exp[-(beta+delta)*x]  as x->+infinity.

Thus

 lim_(s->0) s*integral q(x)^s dx=1/alpha+1/(beta+delta).

For the narrower Cauchy kernel W5,

 J_(1,s)=integral [(pi/2)*sech(pi*xi/2)]^(2s) dxi

so lim s*J_(1,s)=2/pi. A SINGLE positive channel, or the singular-value

6.6 Finite-matrix passage and restriction monotonicity

Fix I=[-R,R] and restrict rho without renormalizing, as in Section 4.3.
Use symmetric H_I=diag(D_I,-D_I) and v_I=(w_I,w_I)/sqrt(2), so the
rank-one input E_I has norm t_I=integral_I rho<=1. Its central denominator
uses M_I(t)<=M(t); therefore omega_I(t)>=omega(t) pointwise.

For the odd map, both positive channels in W4 dominate the factors
using the GLOBAL omega and log-integration variable y restricted to I.
Their multiplier products are the global q_o on I, so the lower
similarity/ALT/Jensen estimates are unchanged.

For the even map, first apply the bounded similarity exp(-x/2) on I to
its actual O_I, obtaining the positive S_I with actual omega_I. Then
replace omega_I by smaller global omega and restrict y to I. These
operations lower S_I in Loewner order. The remaining factor U_I has
product q_e on I and admits the second similarity/ALT/Jensen bound.
This justifies both uses of positivity in Section 6.4.

Finally approximate D_I=exp(x) by finite step functions, keeping w_I
fixed, just as in Section 4.3. The full endpoints A_I,B_I are bounded
self-adjoint and their functional calculi converge in operator norm:
continuous functions on a bounded common real spectral interval can
be uniformly approximated by polynomials. Each correction is supported
on the two finite cell-vector spans and is a genuine finite-matrix
example. Its denominator is t_I^(alpha*s)<=1, so the optimal full
constant dominates its output power sum. Fatou of fixed singular-value
partial sums, then monotone convergence of integral_I q^s, prove W9
using ONLY finite-matrix examples.

7. RECTANGULAR ABSOLUTE-VALUE POWERS: NO DILATION LOSS
-----------------------------------------------------
The same exact leading coefficient as (W) also holds for the map

             X -> |X|^alpha=(X*X)^(alpha/2)

on arbitrary finite rectangular matrices. The analogous assertion for
X->|X*|^alpha follows by taking adjoints. This strengthens the naive
factor-two upper bound from retaining just one block of the full even
dilation inequality.

Indeed first suppose E=X-Y has rank one and ||E||=1. The even functional
calculus of the self-adjoint dilation gives the operator-valued integral
for |X|^alpha-|Y|^alpha with log-integrand

 F(y)=c_e*t^alpha*D_t,      t=exp(y),
 D_t=X*X*(X*X+t^2)^(-1)-Y*Y*(Y*Y+t^2)^(-1).

The already audited even-dilation resolvent bounds imply

 ||F(x+i eta)||<=C_(alpha,d)*min(exp(alpha*x),exp(-beta*x)),
                  |eta|<=d<pi/2.

However its real-node rank is only TWO, independently of the rank-four
bound for the entire dilation node:

 rank(D_t)<=rank(X*X-Y*Y)<=2,

because

 X*X-Y*Y=X*(X-Y)+(X*-Y*)Y=X*E+E*Y.

Thus Section 2 gives exactly the whole-line upper W1 for rank-one
rectangular updates. Telescope an arbitrary rectangular difference by
its finite SVD rank-one expansion; degree-alpha homogeneity and the
Schatten s-power triangle inequality give precisely Tr|E|^(alpha*s)
as the denominator, with no number-of-increments loss.

Hermitian matrices and the central rank-one examples of Section 6 are
a subclass of the rectangular domain. Therefore W9 supplies the matching
lower bound, and the exact coefficient is

                   2/[pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)].

This conclusion does not assert equality of the fixed-s best constants
between the rectangular absolute-value map and the Hermitian even map;
only their exact s->0 leading coefficients have been identified. For
the polar Mazur map, the fixed-s equality with the Hermitian odd map
follows from the exact self-adjoint dilation cancellation already audited.
