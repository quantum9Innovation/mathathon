The completed graph theorem gives degree-three worst-case relative rank
Theta_alpha(1/epsilon) for an existing unit edge doubled. The exact leading
coefficient is unknown. The new compulsory Rayleigh outlier only gives
limsup epsilon W_Delta <= alpha log[2(Delta+1)/(Delta+3)].

A stronger possible mechanism uses an entire compulsory LOCAL response,
rather than just its largest eigenvalue. For a sequence of rooted bridge
edits on bounded-degree hosts, take a rooted local graph limit. Each half
of the limiting bridge has root degree at most Delta-1. Nash--Williams
cutsets suggest that its wired resistance to infinity is at least
1/(Delta-2) when Delta>=3, with equality on the regular tree half.
Consequently the wired edge resistance is at least 2/Delta. If one half
is finite, the wired bridge resistance is one instead.

If the limiting fractional relative response necessarily consumes
alpha log(1+tau_wired) of the logarithmic determinant budget, then a
finite host's globally spread vanishing modes would have at most

 alpha log 2 - alpha log(1+2/Delta)
   = alpha log[2Delta/(Delta+2)]

available for its leading inverse-epsilon count. At Delta=3 this is
alpha log(6/5), stronger than the current alpha log(4/3) bound for
doubling a bridge. This is a CONJECTURED strengthening, not established
by the existing finite-dimensional Rayleigh proof.

Required proof obligations:
1. Local convergence of the generalized fractional pair on finite-support
   test vectors, taking care of the finite graph constant kernel.
2. Infinite-dimensional trace-class relative response and exact Fredholm
   determinant identity logdet(I+C)=alpha log(1+tau_wired), even if the
   infinite Laplacian is not bounded below.
3. A spectral lower/interlacing argument that forces the whole local budget
   into the finite graph, then leaves only the remainder for many small modes.
4. The wired-resistance lower bound for all rooted max-degree-Delta bridge
   halves, including finite/infinite and nonregular local limits.
5. Separate the BRIDGE class from arbitrary existing-edge doubling. A local
   nonbridge may have smaller resistance because of shared neighbors; the
   tree formula is not automatically valid for that larger class.

Even if this upper bound works, sharpness is a new obstacle. In a binary
tree with many grounded leaves, the last leaf can have open potential
of order1/m, so its maximum admittance increment is of order1/m^2.
Thus a naive equal-log-budget terminal ordering need not realize a flat
spectrum and does not prove coefficient sharpness. Neck lengths, terminal
geometry, reservoir attachment order, and active eigenvalue locations
need an actual construction. The existing lower bound only requires a
small fixed portion of the budget and does not settle this issue.

The point is a possible geometric characterization of spectral budget
that stays local versus budget that can escape into many global modes.
It connects the new fractional-update theory to local weak limits and
universal-cover/electrical arguments in the problem-21 sources.
