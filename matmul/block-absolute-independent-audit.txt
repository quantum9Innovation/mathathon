1. Coordinate and inverse-mass checks
------------------------------------
Use product-support coordinates M=Z Bbar, with Z=AW, Bbar=L B_orig,
G=Z^T Z and Q=Bbar Bbar^T. The support dimension k is rank(M) on the
common OSE event. The previous independent rank-support audit applies:
L need not be W^dagger after compression, and A=AWL need not hold.
Only M=Z Bbar and positive, constant-conditioned G are needed here.

Assume 0<theta<=h<=1/64 and

  I/(1+h)<=G<=I/(1-h), (1-h)Q<=Q0<=(1+h)Q.

Fix delta_mass=theta/10^6 and construct ONCE, before any threshold search,

  Bmass=sum_{i=0}^l(I-G)^i,
  [h/(1-h)]^(l+1)<=delta_mass.

Then Bmass G=I-(I-G)^(l+1), so

  (1-delta_mass)G^(-1)<=Bmass<=(1+delta_mass)G^(-1),
  (1-2h)I<=Bmass<=(1+2h)I.

The fixed degree and operator are essential: if a different approximate
inverse were selected at each threshold, the pencil would not have one
fixed generalized spectrum for bisection. Here every action is a fixed
symmetric polynomial in G, costing O(log(1/theta)) true G block actions.

For K_t=tBmass-Q define Hhat=Bmass^(-1/2)Q Bmass^(-1/2) analytically.
Its jth eigenvalue lies between lambda_j(MM^T)/(1+delta_mass) and
lambda_j(MM^T)/(1-delta_mass). This follows by comparing Bmass^(-1)
to G in Q^(1/2) coordinates and using min--max. No root is formed.
The negative inertia count of K_t is exactly #{lambda_j(Hhat)>t}.

2. Smooth absolute preconditioner and its finite construction
------------------------------------------------------------
For every t>0 let

  D=tI+Q0, S=(tI-Q0)D^(-1).

They commute, D is SPD and spectrum(S) lies in [-1,1]. Normalization
gives D^(-1/2)K_tD^(-1/2)=S+E, with ||E||<=2h. Indeed
t(Bmass-I) lies between +-2htI, and Q-Q0 between
+-hQ0/(1-h). The normalized bound is independent of lambda_1/t.

Take a=8, Y=S^2+a^2h^2I, and X0=2I. Newton's exact iteration

  X_{v+1}=(X_v+Y X_v^(-1))/2

has commuting symmetric iterates. For a scalar root rho and x>=rho,
x_next-rho=(x-rho)^2/(2x)<=(x-rho)/2. Thus a predetermined
O(log(1/h)) iterations give

  sqrt(Y)<=X<=sqrt(Y)+ahI.

Set F=D(X+ahI)=JJ^T. All matrices are explicit small cores; inversion,
multiplication and Cholesky cost ~O(k^(omega+zeta)) per threshold.
The actual finite F, not an ideal absolute value, satisfies

  8hD<=F<=2D, -F<K_t<F,
  0<=Edef:=F-K_tF^(-1)K_t<=36hD.                         (A)

Independent constant check: in D-normalized coordinates, start with
f0=sqrt(s^2+a^2h^2)+ah. Exactly f0-s^2/f0=2ah. The derivative of
f-s^2/f is <=2 for f>=|s|; Newton overestimate <=ah adds <=2ah.
The E cross terms cost <=4h, and the negative E Fbar^(-1)E term can
be dropped. Hence 4a h+4h=36h. The lower bound follows from the
two-sided domination by congruence. The condition h<=1/64 also safely
gives F<=2D; h<=1/16 alone would not justify that displayed constant.

Let C=J^(-1)K_tJ^(-T), T=I-C. Then C has spectrum in (-1,1) and
T is PSD with norm <=2. Since F-K_t>=6hD and F<=2D, also T>=3hI.
No root or Cholesky of a true input Gram is used.

3. Initial spectral quantile without a condition-number logarithm
---------------------------------------------------------------
For a PSD V with descending positive eigenvalues v_i, its elementary
symmetric coefficients obey

  v_j/j<=e_j(V)/e_{j-1}(V)<=(k-j+1)v_j.                 (B)

Lower bound: j e_j=sum_{|I|=j-1}v_I sum_{i notin I}v_i, and every
I misses at least one of the top j indices. Upper bound: delete the
largest-index element from each size-j subset. It is <=v_j; each
remaining subset has at most k-j+1 preimages. Both arguments check.

Apply (B) to Q0^p with p a power of two >=log_2 k. The pth root of
the coefficient ratio lies between v_j/2 and 2v_j. Compute pth roots
by repeated positive square roots. Form Q0^p by O(log log k) squarings
and obtain its characteristic coefficients through deterministic fast
Frobenius form. The primary field-arithmetic subroutine is:

  Neiger--Pernet--Villard (2024), Corollary 5.2,
  https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07345v2

For j=r+1 and r<k, the resulting rho gives [rho/4,4rho] containing
lambda_j(Hhat), using the mass and Q0 Loewner bounds. Its ratio is 16,
independent of v_1/v_j. For r>=k, use the exact support-recovery branch
before dividing coefficients. Thus zero optimal tails cause no precision
limit or singular-value-ratio dependence.

This is explicitly an arithmetic argument. Large powers and characteristic
coefficients may require arbitrarily large word lengths on some inputs.

4. Quantile estimate: what the block theorem actually supplies
-------------------------------------------------------------
Order the C eigenvalues increasingly, mu_1<=...<=mu_k. The T eigenvalues
are tau_j=1-mu_j in descending order. Run a target-(r+1) block-Krylov
call for T, form an exact orthonormal Krylov basis V, and form

  A_small=(T V)^T(T V).

Its j=r+1 Ritz eigenvalue beta_j is at most tau_j^2 deterministically.
Musco--Musco's PERVECTOR guarantee, not just its spectral-LRA guarantee,
gives beta_j>=tau_j^2-epsilon*tau_{j+1}^2>=(1-epsilon)tau_j^2 at
q=~O(epsilon^(-1/2)) block steps.

Primary source checked directly: Musco--Musco (2015), Algorithm 2,
Theorem 12, Lemma 9 Property 2, and Appendix A Lemma 4.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.05477
Appendix A explicitly says 99/100 confidence was chosen for simplicity;
higher confidence changes a Gaussian initial-factor constant only inside
logarithms. Alternatively independent calls can be concatenated: Ritz
eigenvalues are monotone under enlargement of the FULL space, so any
successful call supplies the lower bound. This eigenvalue monotonicity
does not assert monotonicity of a truncated rank-r Ritz projection.

No Ritz eigenproblem need be solved. For d=dim(V), start p=1,z=d and
repeatedly double p and square-root z until z<=1+alpha/100. Apply (B)
to A_small^p and compute

  rho_small=(e_j/e_{j-1})^(1/p), beta_lower=rho_small/z.

Then exactly beta_j/z^2<=beta_lower<=beta_j. With epsilon=alpha/100,

  (1-alpha/20)tau_j^2<=beta_lower<=tau_j^2.

The definition qvalue=1-sqrt(beta_lower) therefore obeys

  mu_j<=qvalue<=mu_j+alpha.                              (C)

This bound holds even for tiny tau_j: its squared error is RELATIVE,
not an absolute squared error that would require sqrt(alpha) conversion.
The coefficient power is O(log d/alpha), but computing it costs only
O(log(log d/alpha)) matrix squarings and scalar roots. Exact rank checks
handle d<j or rank(A_small)<j before division; those cases cannot occur
on the good event because T>=3hI and the Gaussian initial block has
rank at least j. Cost is ~O(d^(omega+zeta)), with no eigenvalue oracle.

5. Separate filtered-Gaussian projection: independently verified
---------------------------------------------------------------
One must NOT infer a good truncated rank-r projection merely because
the quantile Krylov space was enlarged. Instead, when tau_{r+1}<=B0
is known, use a fresh Gaussian block Omega of width r and the polynomial

  p_v(x)=Cheb_v(2x/B0-1)/Cheb_v(2L0/B0-1), L0=B0+alpha.

Let H index all tau>B0, with j=|H|<=r, and let Sstrong index tau>=L0.
Tail filter values have magnitude at most 1/denominator; strong values
are >=1. A Gaussian head has full row rank and polynomially bounded
pseudoinverse with high probability. One elementary proof takes the
first j square columns, uses conditional Gaussian column distances and
a union bound, then bounds the inverse Frobenius norm. A Markov bound
controls the tail Frobenius norm. The generous deterministic bound

  ||Omega_tail Omega_H^dagger||<=(100k/beta)^3

holds with allocated failure beta.

For Yout=p_v(T)Omega, analytically multiply by

  Omega_H^dagger E_Sstrong diag(p_v(tau_Sstrong))^(-1).

Its head is EXACTLY E_Sstrong, including zero coordinates in the weak
head. Only strong inverse filter values, all <=1, are used in the norm
bound. Thus its tail error is <=R/denominator. A denominator >=2R/alpha
gives ||P_range(Yout)^perp U_strong||<=alpha/2. All remaining eigenvalues
are below L0 and ||T||<=2, so

  ||P_range(Yout)^perp T||<=L0+alpha.                    (D)

For B0=1+alpha this is <=1+3alpha. Take ANY exact rank-revealing QR
basis U of Yout, of width <=r. No SVD, spectral gap, or approximate-
eigenvector perturbation theorem is needed. If j=0, the inverse is not
needed and the same norm bound is immediate.

The denominator grows exponentially in v sqrt(alpha), so
v=O(alpha^(-1/2)log(k/(alpha beta))). Actual computation uses only
the three-term Chebyshev recurrence. Its vectors may be huge; this
does not imply numerical stability, but its arithmetic count is bounded.

Let lambda*=lambda_{r+1}(Hhat). For a current positive threshold t,

  qvalue<0 => mu_j<0 => t<lambda*;

  c<=[alpha/(1-alpha^2)](1-c^2).

For c<=0 this is immediate; for c>0, c/(1-c^2) is increasing.

7. Quadratic-defect transfer and the physical dual lift
------------------------------------------------------
For the returned U, let b0=(1+3alpha)^2-1<=7alpha<1. The strong action
norm certificate implies, for w perpendicular to U,

  w^T(C^2-2C)w<=b0||w||^2.

Put w=J^T y. Write Edef=F-K_tF^(-1)K_t. The necessary algebra is

  2y^T K_t y
     >=y^T K_tF^(-1)K_t y-b0 y^T F y
     =(1-b0)y^T K_tF^(-1)K_t y-b0 y^T Edef y
     >=-36b0 h y^T D y.

It is ESSENTIAL to retain the b0 multiplying Edef. Dropping it would
lose the theta/h-to-theta improvement. Hence K_t>=-126alpha h D on
this dual complement.

The correct physical subspace is range(Z J U), not a lift involving
J^(-T) or an extra true G inverse. For x perpendicular to that space,
y=Z^T x has J^T y perpendicular to U. Moreover

  y^T G^(-1)y<=||x||^2, ||y||^2<=2||x||^2,
  y^T Q0 y<=2x^T M M^T x.

Combining the dual lower bound and Bmass's relative mass error gives

  x^T M M^T x
     <=[(1+252alpha h+delta_mass)/(1-252alpha h)]t||x||^2. (F)

10.1 Coefficient signs and singular inputs

Write det(xI-V^p)=x^d+c_{d-1}x^(d-1)+...+c_0. Then

  e_j(V^p)=(-1)^j c_{d-j}, e_0=1.

This is a known sign flip, not a numerical root-location test. For PSD V,
e_j>0 exactly when j<=rank(V); powers p>=1 preserve rank and all positive
eigenvalue ordering. If j>d or e_j=0, return the zero jth quantile BEFORE
dividing. Otherwise e_{j-1}>0, so the ratio and every repeated-square-root
radicand are strictly positive. A separate exact rank check is also valid,
but not necessary just to protect the coefficient ratio. Repeated positive
eigenvalues require no special branch and no eigengap.

Coarse Q0 is a sketched Gram, and the quantile core is (TV)^T(TV).
They are PSD on ALL random outcomes, even when the approximation event
fails. Thus these coefficient signs and division guards are structural, not
only successful-event properties.

10.2 Uniform powers and square roots, with actual counts

To obtain a constant-relative quantile, initialize p=1,z=d and repeatedly
replace p by2p and z bysqrt(z) until z<=2. To obtain the final certified
quantile, instead stop when z<=1+alpha/100. In either loop z=d^(1/p)
exactly. The latter p is O(1+log(d)/alpha), but the number s=log2(p) of
iterations is only O(1+loglog(d)+log(1/alpha)). These loop conditions use
the allowed exact order comparisons; their inputs are supplied dimensions
and accuracy, not unknown eigenvalue ratios.

Form V^p with s matrix squarings, compute its characteristic coefficients
once, take the positive pth root of their ratio with s scalar square roots,
divide by z, and take the final square root. There are O(s) scalar operations
and comparisons in addition to the characteristic algorithm. The arithmetic
cost is O(d^omega(1+log p)) up to the source's logarithms. The 1+ is needed:
p=1 still incurs nonzero characteristic-polynomial work.

Crucially V^p is a dense matrix of REAL VALUES, not a symbolic polynomial
of formal degree p passed to the algebraic subroutine. Its characteristic
matrix xI-V^p still has polynomial degree ONE in x. Thus no factor p is
hidden in that subroutine's input degree. Binary exponentiation is not p
matrix multiplications. It can, however, produce coefficients with degree,
height, word length or precision requirements growing with p and with the
input. Those costs are excluded by the stated model; there is no implied
Turing/bit-polynomial or stable numerical algorithm.

13. Sign-count threshold queries and polynomial-ratio initialization
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Independent audit of the parent's proposed simplification: PASSES. This
supersedes the need for root-squaring in the algorithm audited in Sections
10--11. Those earlier sections remain valid historical checks of a more
complicated routine. The finite Newton preconditioner and scalar square
roots used by Cholesky are unchanged. No matrix powers, scalar pth roots,
eigenvalue approximations, or spectral-decomposition oracle are needed
for threshold queries or relative-theorem initialization after this change.

13.1 Exact Descartes count, including zeros and multiplicities

For any nonzero real-rooted polynomial p of degree d, let z be its zero-root
multiplicity and P,N its strictly positive and strictly negative root counts,
each including algebraic multiplicity. Delete zero coefficients when counting
sign variations V(p). Descartes gives P<=V(p) and N<=V(p(-x)). To see that
these inequalities are equalities here, list the degrees of consecutive
nonzero coefficients of p. If their gap g is odd, that pair contributes
exactly one variation in total to p and p(-x). If g is even, it contributes
either zero or two, hence at most g. Summing the gaps gives

  V(p)+V(p(-x)) <= d-z = P+N.

Both Descartes inequalities must therefore be equalities. This proof covers
vanishing intermediate coefficients, repeated roots, and zero roots. The
standard Descartes inequality with multisets and missing coefficients is
stated and proved in Albouy--Fu (2014), Proposition1 and Section2:
https://arxiv.org/html/1307.6842v2 . The equality refinement above follows
directly from real-rootedness and is not a new root-counting principle.

For symmetric A_s, p_s(x)=det(xI-(A_s-sI)) is monic and real-rooted.
Its V(p_s) is EXACTLY the number of eigenvalues of A_s strictly greater
than s. Thus beta_j(A_s)>s iff V(p_s)>=j; beta_j(A_s)<=s iff V(p_s)<j.
An eigenvalue exactly equal to s creates a zero root and is correctly
excluded from the strict count, with its entire multiplicity. If d<j,
take beta_j=0 before calling the count (the thresholds below are positive).
If d=0, the constant polynomial has no sign changes.

One deterministic fast characteristic-polynomial call on the shifted dense
matrix and O(d) exact coefficient sign/zero comparisons cost ~d^omega.
No division by a possibly zero characteristic coefficient is involved in
this query routine. The previously audited deterministic arbitrary-field
characteristic-polynomial algorithms apply to singular and repeated-spectrum
shifts without a genericity hypothesis. Two thresholds require two calls,
still ~d^omega. Forming a shift requires only changing the diagonal; no
polynomial Taylor-shift algorithm is being hidden.

13.2 Correct three-way query and confidence amplification

Let eps_q=alpha/100 and s0=1-2 eps_q>0. For each independent per-vector
block-Krylov run let beta_i denote the jth actual compressed eigenvalue,
or zero in the d<j branch. Conditional on the common sketch event,

  beta_i <= tau_j(T)^2                         on every run;
  beta_i >= (1-eps_q)tau_j(T)^2                on a good run.

Each run is good with probability at least .99. Make sufficiently many
independent runs that at least one is good except with probability beta.
Use exact Descartes comparisons to implement these branches, in this order:

  (L) If ANY beta_i>1, set the lower endpoint to t.
  (U) Else, if ALL beta_i<=s0, set the upper endpoint to t.
  (?) Otherwise stop with an uncertain threshold t.

For (L), tau_j>1 and mu_j(C_t)=1-tau_j<0 deterministically, so inertia
makes t a safe lower endpoint even if every per-vector run failed.
For (U), one good run implies

  tau_j^2 <= s0/(1-eps_q) < 1,

so mu_j>0 and t is a safe upper endpoint. In the remaining case all
beta_i<=1 and at least one beta_i>s0. One good run gives the upper
bound and the deterministic inequality gives the lower bound in

  1-2 eps_q < tau_j^2 <= 1/(1-eps_q).

Consequently |1-tau_j|<2 eps_q<alpha. This supplies exactly the former
uncertain-threshold condition, including tau_j<=1+alpha for filtering.
Boundary beta_i=1 never triggers (L); beta_i=s0 belongs to (U). Both
equalities are handled safely. The errors are one-sided in the correct
direction; no eigenvalue validator is required for amplification.

After conditioning on the entire previous search history and the common
sketch event, fresh Gaussian starts retain the .99 per-run theorem. The
chance all repetitions fail is at most .01^R. Union-bound this over the
capped number of adaptive thresholds and reserve a separate fresh-filter
budget. The raw width is per run, not the width of concatenated repeats.
Range algebra, block actions, and every construction exponent are unchanged.

13.3 No initial powers are needed either

For j=r+1<=k, take just rho=e_j(Q0)/e_(j-1)(Q0)>0. The already proved
coefficient-ratio inequality gives

  rho/k <= lambda_j(Q0) <= k rho.

The coarse mass and covariance comparisons imply
lambda_j(Q0)/2<=lambda_j(Hhat)<=2lambda_j(Q0). Hence

  [ell0,u0]=[rho/(4k),4k rho]

is a valid positive bracket with ratio 16k^2. The factor four has slack;
no power or scalar root is used. Rank/support branches occur first, so
e_(j-1)>0 and this sole initial ratio cannot divide by zero.

After B bisections the absolute width is at most
2^(-B)(16k^2-1)ell0. Current ell>=ell0, so

  B=ceil(log2(16*10^6*m^2/theta))+1

is a safe cap for reaching u-ell<=alpha*h*ell. It can be computed by
successive scalar halvings, requiring only field operations and comparisons.
The extra dimension logarithm is already within the stated tildes. No
input singular-value ratio, factor norm, or condition number enters it.

14. Fixed-threshold absolute-error algorithm with rank doubling
--------------------------------------------------------------
Independent audit of the optional extension: PASSES with a high-probability
instance-dependent runtime, and with the raw-width condition stated for
the largest Krylov width actually processed. The exact-rank theorem does
not need to change. The old polynomial-power routine is not needed here.

Given absolute error eps_abs>0, set

  t=eps_abs^2/(1+theta/4),
  u=theta/10^6=alpha*h=eps_m,
  r_eps=min{r>=0: sigma_(r+1)(M)<=eps_abs/(1+theta)}.

Allow rank zero. On the common sketch event r_eps<=rank M<=m. Compute
the same sketches, support, fixed B_m, and a SINGLE preconditioner at t.
Try target ranks 0,1,2,4,..., capped at m. At each stage, the computed
support dimension <=r triggers the exact-support branch before any query.
Otherwise j=r+1<=k and perform the independent per-vector runs above,
testing ONLY whether ANY beta_i>1. If so, continue to the next rank. If
none exceeds one, accept this rank and use the fresh filtered projection
and physical lift. At r=0 the output subspace is simply {0}; no Gaussian
filter is needed. Allocate query failures over at most ceil(log2 m)+2
stages and reserve a separate final-filter budget.

14.1 Accepted rank gives the requested absolute error

If some query run is good and none has beta_i>1, then

  tau_(r+1)(T)^2 <= 1/(1-eps_q),
  tau_(r+1)(T) <= 1+alpha.

This is the sole hypothesis needed by the fresh-filter lemma. The existing
weighted strong-action transfer therefore gives

  ||M-C||_2^2 <= [(1+253u)/(1-252u)]t < eps_abs^2.

Indeed the bracketed factor minus one equals 505u/(1-252u), which is
at most .000506*theta for theta<=h<=1/64, much smaller than theta/4.
This covers r=0: T's full norm is at most 1+alpha, so the empty projection
already has the strong certificate, and the same transfer proof applies.
The exact-support branch is exact on the sketch event and also succeeds.

Conditioned on acceptance history, the final Gaussian filter is fresh.
The common sketches, at-least-one-good-run event at every stage, and final
filter can be allocated total failure delta. Thus the algorithm's returned
absolute-error guarantee has probability at least 1-delta for every fixed
pair of factors. No relative-tail test or initial bracket is needed.

14.2 Rank bound and deterministic stopping on the sketch event

On the sketch event, for every r>=r_eps,

  lambda_(r+1)(Hhat)
  <= eps_abs^2 / [(1+theta)^2(1-eps_m)]
  < eps_abs^2/(1+theta/4)=t.

The strict scalar inequality holds over 0<theta<=1/64, eps_m=theta/10^6.
Thus inertia gives tau_(r+1)(T)<1 and EVERY beta_i<=tau_(r+1)^2<1,
regardless of per-vector success. Such a stage cannot be rejected. Doubling
therefore accepts by the first tried rank at least r_eps, or reaches the
earlier exact-support branch. If R denotes the final target/output-width
bound, then

  R=0 when r_eps=0;
  R<2 r_eps (hence R<=2 r_eps) when r_eps>=1,

with the trivial cap R<=m. Exact-support output may have even smaller width.
The rank bound and stopping statement require only the common sketch event,
not good per-vector runs. Query failures may accept TOO EARLY, but cannot
force rejection once r>=r_eps. The joint success event establishes both
the norm bound and the rank bound.

14.3 Cost, raw width, and failed-outcome qualifications

Choose one global sufficient degree q=~O(sqrt(h/theta)) in advance; its
dimension and confidence logarithms cover all stages. Successive stage
sizes increase geometrically, so monotonicity and the logarithmic number
of stages give, on the common sketch event,

  ~O(I+G_h+m^omega+q Ac(2 r_eps+1)
                         +np+T_MM(n,2 r_eps,p)),

provided every raw Krylov matrix actually processed has width at most m.
A simple sufficient parameter condition is (2q+1)(2r_eps+1)<=m; the
actual checked condition may be weaker if acceptance or exact support
occurs earlier. For r_eps=0, Ac(1) is the rank-zero query cost, the output
is explicitly written zero, and T_MM(n,0,p) is interpreted as zero.
The final physical QR and projection fit Ac(R)+m^omega as before. All
initial sketches and the fixed-threshold preconditioner are reused.

This is deliberately a HIGH-PROBABILITY, instance-dependent runtime bound.
On failed sketch outcomes, the true r_eps does not control the computed
stopping rank. Cap the rank sequence at m and use the already proved
general raw-width fallback whenever the checked width exceeds m. This
gives a finite deterministic worst-case arithmetic bound in n,m,p,q,
including failures, but not the r_eps-sensitive bound on EVERY outcome.
One must not advertise the latter without an additional argument.
If the final stage is an exact-support branch it has no new Krylov raw
matrix, so its unprocessed hypothetical width need not satisfy the raw-width
condition. The parameter condition above is sufficient rather than necessary.
