THE SECOND COLLISION PHASE OF A BALANCED UNSIGNED SKETCH
======================================================

This note proves a sharp success transition for the particular centered
balanced sketch already defined in balanced-permutation-amm.txt. It
connects exact permutation assignments to the spectra of small colored
collision components. The factorial-moment/Poisson method is classical;
no novelty certificate, general embedding lower bound, or AMM runtime
improvement over signed sketches is claimed.

1. THE ASYMPTOTIC STATEMENT
==========================
Fix an integer d>=2 and a tolerance 0<epsilon<1 with

  1<d epsilon<sqrt(2).                                  (1)

Let t tend to infinity. For each t, let m=sq, s>=2, q>=2 be integers,
with

  s/t^(3/2)->c in (0,infinity),
  q->Q in {2,3,...} union {infinity}.                    (2)

If Q is finite, q is eventually the integer Q. Choose a deterministic
set I of t coordinates of [m]. The assumptions imply t<s<=m for all
sufficiently large t, so such sets exist. Independently for each of
d colors, partition [m] uniformly into s labeled buckets of size q.
For its incidence H_a put

  p=(q-1)/(m-1),
  W_a=(H_a^T H_a-p11^T)/(1-p),
  Wbar=d^-1 sum_a W_a.

The sketch has Gram matrix Wbar. Let

  V_I={x in R^m: supp(x) subset I, sum_i x_i=0},

of dimension r=t-1. Success means simultaneous squared-norm preservation:

  |x^T(Wbar-I)x|<=epsilon||x||^2 for EVERY x in V_I.     (3)

Use 1/Q=0 when Q=infinity, and define

  kappa_2=1-1/Q,
  kappa_3=(1-1/Q)(1-2/Q),
  lambda_W=d(d-1)kappa_2^2/(2c^2),
  lambda_T=d kappa_3/(6c^2).                            (4)

Then the exact limiting success probability is

  Pr(success on V_I)->exp(-lambda_W-lambda_T).          (5)

Thus for a fixed limiting failure target delta in (0,1), the critical
constant is

  c_delta^2=[d(d-1)kappa_2^2/2+d kappa_3/6]
                         /[-log(1-delta)].             (6)

Choosing c>c_delta gives a limiting success probability greater than
1-delta, and choosing c<c_delta gives one less than 1-delta. The total
shared-constant factor width is ell=1+d(s-1)~dc r^(3/2).
This is the explicit stacked width; rank Wbar<=min(m,ell). If Q>d,
including Q=infinity, it is genuinely compressed eventually. When
Q<=d the redundant stack may have width at least m. The theorem still
describes the stated Wbar, but an implementation taking an exact
identity fallback instead would have a different success law.

The strict window (1) matters. It allows isolated pair collisions while
rejecting every three-vertex path or triangle. No claim is made here
at d epsilon=1 or sqrt(2), or in other tolerance windows. In particular
the theorem does not supply a complete phase diagram.

2. EXACT SMALL PATTERN PROBABILITIES
===================================
For one balanced partition, two specified coordinates collide with
probability

  p=(q-1)/(m-1).

Three specified coordinates lie in one bucket with probability

  p_3=(q-1)(q-2)/[(m-1)(m-2)].                         (7)

Represent the partition by a uniform permutation into m slots, with
q slots in each of s buckets. More generally, suppose disjoint specified
coordinate groups of sizes v_1,...,v_h must each lie within a bucket,
and additionally require different groups to use different buckets.
For a=sum_j v_j the EXACT probability is

  (s)_h product_j(q)_(v_j)/(m)_a.                       (8)

This follows by assigning distinct buckets to the groups and distinct
slots to their labeled coordinates. All (m)_a assignments of distinct
slots to the specified coordinates are equiprobable. Here (z)_a is a
falling factorial. Formula (8), rather than independent coordinate
hashing, retains the finite population correction in (4).

For later use fix a finite colored constraint pattern on a bounded
number of vertices. For each color, let its nontrivial connected
components have sizes v_j. Requiring its indicated collision edges
means that every such component occupies a single bucket. Define
the total colored constraint rank

  R=sum_colors sum_components(v_j-1).                   (9)

Uniformly for q>=2 as s tends to infinity, its probability is O(s^-R),
where the constant can depend on the fixed pattern and d. To see this,
in each color expose one coordinate per component freely and then
the other coordinates in that component. At a constrained exposure
there are at most q allowable slots and at least m-a unused slots.
For a fixed pattern, q/(m-a)<=2/s eventually. There are exactly
sum_j(v_j-1) constrained exposures. Independence multiplies this bound
across colors. If q is smaller than a required component, the event
is impossible and the same upper bound is valid.

When every required component has size two or three, formula (8) also
gives its leading probability if different components of a color use
different buckets. Assignments merging at least two of those groups
use at least one fewer bucket; summing their finite possible mergers
and using (m)_a~m^a shows that they contribute O(s^(-R-1)). Thus for
disjoint vertex sets the leading factors are

  size two: s^-1*(q)_2/q^2=s^-1*(1-1/q),
  size three: s^-2*(q)_3/q^3
                         =s^-2*(1-1/q)(1-2/q).          (10)

All approximation errors here are uniform over q>=2 for each fixed
pattern, as absolute O(s^(-R-1)+s^-R/m) errors. A forbidden size-three
component at q=2 has probability zero exactly. No relative division
by its vanishing leading constant is used.

3. THE TWO RARE MOTIFS AND THEIR MEANS
=====================================
On I draw an edge of color a whenever two coordinates collide in
partition a. A color alone is a disjoint union of cliques. Different
colors can initially give multiple edges on the same pair.

Define W to count the following wedge objects: a center v, an unordered
pair {u,w} of other vertices, and two DISTINCT colors assigned to
the two indicated edges vu and vw. Each such color assignment is one
object; swapping the ordering used to describe the unordered endpoints
does not create another object with the same colored edges. Its event
is just the two prescribed collisions. There are

  d(d-1)t*binom(t-1,2)=d(d-1)(t)_3/2

possible wedge objects. Each has exact probability p^2, because its
two colors are independent. Consequently

  E W=d(d-1)(t)_3 p^2/2 ->lambda_W.                     (11)

Define T to count an unordered three-vertex set and a single color
in which all three vertices collide. Its graph is a monochromatic
triangle. There are d*binom(t,3) such objects, so

  E T=d(t)_3 p_3/6 ->lambda_T.                          (12)

These definitions impose no absence of other edges. The small-component
event in Section 5 will connect these raw motif counts to actual
collision components. In particular they need not be induced patterns
for the factorial-moment proof.

4. JOINT FACTORIAL MOMENTS AND INDEPENDENT POISSON LIMITS
=======================================================
For fixed nonnegative integers a,b, we prove

  E[(W)_a(T)_b]->lambda_W^a lambda_T^b.                 (13)

The zero-order case is one. Expand into ordered tuples of a DISTINCT
wedge objects and b DISTINCT triangle objects. Write h=a+b.
First retain tuples whose motifs have disjoint vertex sets. Their
exact number is

  (t)_(3h) [d(d-1)/2]^a[d/6]^b.                       (14)

They impose 2h colored constraints in the sense of (9). Applying
(8),(10) in each color and using independence across colors gives
their common leading event probability

  s^(-2h)(1-1/q)^(2a)[(1-1/q)(1-2/q)]^b,

with absolute error O(s^(-2h-1)+s^(-2h)/m). The grouping of components
among colors changes only this uniform error; it does not change the
leading factors. Combining with (14), t^3/s^2->c^-2, and q->Q gives
the right side of (13). At Q=2, any tuple containing a triangle is
impossible eventually, as required.

It remains to bound tuples with overlapping motif vertices. For each
such tuple let its underlying uncolored union graph have connected
components. For a component with v vertices, its colored constraint
rank R obeys

  R>=v-1.                                               (15)

Indeed, replace each connected component of each colored constraint
graph by an arbitrary spanning tree. The union of these trees remains
connected on the v vertices and has R edges, so must have at least
v-1 edges. No independence of edges within a color is asserted.

If v>=4, there are O(t^v) possible labels for each fixed abstract
pattern, and its probability is O(s^-R). Its total contribution is
therefore at most a constant times

  t^v/s^(v-1)=O(t^(3/2-v/2))=o(1).                    (16)

For v=3, a single motif has rank two and is the leading case already
counted. If a connected three-vertex component contains at least two
distinct motif objects, its rank is at least three. For if R=2, the
constraints can only consist of EITHER one connected monochromatic
three-vertex component (which defines exactly one triangle object),
OR two differently colored edges connecting the three vertices (which
define exactly one wedge object). No second distinct motif is possible
in either case. The contribution with v=3 and multiple motifs is
therefore O(t^3/s^3)=o(1).

Each overlapping tuple has at least one such negligible component.
All other components contribute O(1), with a lone motif contributing
O(t^3/s^2)=O(1). There are finitely many abstract constraint patterns
for fixed a,b,d. Multiplying the component labeling/probability bounds
and summing proves that all overlapping tuples contribute o(1).
This proves (13). Shared colors between vertex-disjoint components
do not affect this upper bound: (9) applies to their union and its
rank is the sum of their colored ranks.

For completeness, (13) implies the stated independent Poisson limit
without an unproved exchange of an infinite series. Independently
retain each wedge with probability 1-u and each triangle with probability
1-v, where 0<=u,v<=1. Let Z count retained motifs. The binomial identity
for falling factorials and (13) give, for every fixed j,

  E(Z)_j ->[(1-u)lambda_W+(1-v)lambda_T]^j.

Bonferroni's finite inclusion-exclusion bounds enclose Pr(Z=0) between
the odd and even truncations of sum_j(-1)^j E(Z)_j/j!. First let
t tend to infinity for a fixed truncation and then let its order tend
to infinity. The limiting exponential series converges, so

  E[u^W v^T]=Pr(Z=0)
    ->exp(lambda_W(u-1)+lambda_T(v-1)).                 (17)

First moments give tightness of (W,T). Any subsequential distributional
limit has the generating function (17), which uniquely identifies
independent Poisson variables of means lambda_W,lambda_T. Thus the
full joint distribution converges. Setting u=v=0 also directly proves

  Pr(W=T=0)->exp(-lambda_W-lambda_T).                   (18)

5. OTHER COLLISION COMPONENTS VANISH
===================================
With probability tending to one, the colored collision graph on I has
no repeated pair, no rainbow triangle, and no connected component with
four or more vertices. Here a rainbow triangle has three different
edge colors. These assertions follow from explicit first moment bounds:

  repeated pairs: O_d(t^2 p^2)=O_d(t^2/s^2)=o(1);
  rainbow triangles: O_d(t^3 p^3)=O_d(t^3/s^3)=o(1).

If a connected component has at least four vertices, it contains a tree
on four vertices. There are O_d(t^4) possible four-vertex trees with
three edge colors assigned. Each tree imposes colored rank exactly
three: each color subgraph of a tree is a forest, whose rank equals
its number of edges. Equation (9) bounds its event probability by
O_d(s^-3). Thus

  Pr(a component has at least four vertices)
      <=O_d(t^4/s^3)=o(1).                              (19)

Let G denote this joint high-probability event. Its simple components
are isolated vertices, isolated edges, three-vertex paths with different
edge colors, and monochromatic triangles. A path with two edges of one
color forces its third edge by equality of buckets, so is a triangle.
A triangle with exactly two colors forces a repeated pair by the same
transitivity, and has been excluded. Three differently colored edges
form the excluded rainbow case. On G, therefore, W counts precisely
the three-vertex path components, while T counts precisely the triangle
components. A triangle contributes one T and no differently colored
wedge; a path contributes one W and no T.

6. EMBEDDING SUCCESS IS CONTROLLED BY THEIR PERRON EIGENVALUES
============================================================
Let A_col be the t-by-t adjacency with entry K_ij counting pair
collisions over the d colors and zero diagonal. Let P_t=I_t-11^T/t.
For a vector supported on I with coordinate sum zero, the exact
restricted squared-norm error is represented by

  Z_t=(1-p)^-1 P_t(A_col/d+pI_t)P_t.                    (20)

This follows directly from H_a^T H_a=I_t+A_a on those coordinates;
the mean term p11^T vanishes on their contrast space. Success (3)
is equivalent to ||Z_t|_(1-perp)||<=epsilon.

On G and W=T=0, the collision graph is a matching plus isolated
vertices. Hence ||A_col||<=1, and

  ||Z_t||<=(1/d+p)/(1-p)<epsilon

for all sufficiently large t, because p->0 and d epsilon>1.

Conversely, on G and W+T>=1, select a three-vertex component. Its
local Perron eigenvalue is lambda=sqrt(2) for a path, or lambda=2
for a triangle. Let v be its unit nonnegative Perron vector, extended
by zero to all t coordinates. Then A_col v=lambda v. Put

  y=P_t v, z=(1^T v)^2/t<=3/t.

Now ||y||^2=1-z, and because A_col is entrywise nonnegative,

  y^T A_col y
    =lambda-2lambda z
       +(1^T v)^2 t^-2 (1^T A_col 1)
    >=lambda(1-2z).

For t>3 this supplies a nonzero contrast vector with Rayleigh quotient

  y^T A_col y/||y||^2
      >=lambda(1-2z)/(1-z)=lambda-O(1/t),              (21)

uniformly for the two component types. Equations (20),(21) show

  y^T Z_t y/||y||^2
    >=(lambda/d-O(1/t)+p)/(1-p)>epsilon

eventually, since lambda>=sqrt(2) and d epsilon<sqrt(2). Thus success
is equivalent to W=T=0 on G for all sufficiently large t. As Pr(G)->1,
equation (18) proves (5). Notice that simply taking the norm of a
rank-one projection perturbation is unnecessary; the local eigenvector
argument controls the mean-removal loss directly by O(1/t).

7. RELATION TO THE MATRIX AND PERMUTATION PROJECTS
================================================
Let U be any orthonormal basis matrix for V_I. Taking A=U^T, B=U
makes AB=I_(t-1), while A Wbar B-I is precisely the compressed Gram
error in (3). Therefore (5) is also an exact limiting spectral AMM
success law on this explicitly specified input family. Zero padding
can enlarge physical input/output dimensions without changing it.
It is not a guarantee for arbitrary factors or the relative-tail target.

The previous birthday phase in balanced-variance-and-birthday.txt,
where d epsilon<1 asymptotically, rejects even one pair collision and
has the t^2/s critical scale. Allowing isolated edges changes the first
rejected component to three vertices; its t^3/s^2 scale gives the new
t^(3/2) bucket threshold. The finite-q constants distinguish path
components across colors from same-color triangles. At Q=2 triangles
cannot occur and lambda_T=0, but lambda_W>0 for d>=2.

The exact assignment formula (8) is the compatible permutation matching
input used throughout spectral-gap/. The proof here organizes those
probabilities by collision graphs and weights them by the corresponding
small adjacency spectra. The sharp SG-11/14/16 mixed centered envelopes
remain available for broader weighted trace sums, but are not needed
to improve an inequality in this particular Poisson argument. A claim
that their new constants caused the phase law would overstate the
dependency. This is a new analysis within the worktree of the balanced
construction; external historical firstness remains unverified.

Classical sparse and signed embedding methods can have better row
tradeoffs than the restricted fixed-pass construction studied here.
No comparison to a global AMM runtime optimum follows from the phase
transition. It instead gives a proved sharp obstruction and matching
success law within a specific tolerance/pass window, beyond the
one-collision birthday bound.
