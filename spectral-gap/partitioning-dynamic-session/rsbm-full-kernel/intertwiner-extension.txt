NONSYMMETRIC INTERTWINERS: CERTIFIED KERNELS WITHOUT SQUARING THE MOMENT
15 November 2026
New supplement only. The frozen manuscript and full_kernel.py are unchanged.

Verdict
=======

YES: symmetric block linearization extends the per-input full-kernel
theorem to a square nonsymmetric intertwiner K. Computing the doubled
kernel costs only a polynomial factor in its doubled dimension/nullity.
Project to the RIGHT kernel before enumerating. Then a single bound
E[p^h]<=C, h=dim ker(K), suffices; E[p^(2h)] is NOT required.

The exact algebra and charging are proved below. A separate optional
random diagonal weighted-Gram construction is also valid when kernel
candidates are verified against K. Pure two-sided diagonal scaling and
the deterministic unweighted Gram matrix are not adequate substitutes.
These are derived uses of classical black-box tools, not new primitives.

1. Exact vectorization and sparse costs
=======================================

Let A=A^T be an n-by-n graph adjacency matrix, R a prescribed t-by-t
integer quotient matrix, and X an n-by-t indicator matrix. Stack COLUMNS:
  vec(X)=(X[:,1],...,X[:,t]).
Then
  vec(A X-X R) = K vec(X),
  K = I_t tensor A - R^T tensor I_n.                    (1)

Write D=tn for K's dimension and w=nnz(K). For arbitrary R, K need not
be symmetric. Its transpose acts on Y as
  vec(Y) -> vec(A Y-Y R^T).
In particular, using R instead of R^T in (1) silently solves the wrong
intertwining equation when R is nonsymmetric.

One product by K or K^T costs
  O(t*nnz(A)+n*nnz(R))
field operations. For fixed t and bounded graph/quotient degree, D=O(n),
w=O(n), and both products cost O(n). Explicit sparse construction also
has that cost. No dense D-by-D Kronecker matrix is required. Integer
entries of an equitable bounded-degree quotient lie in [0,d], so their
bit lengths are constant when d is fixed.

2. Symmetric block linearization: full proof
===========================================

For square K over the fixed odd base field F_p, put
  S = [ 0   K  ],   size2D,
      [ K^T 0  ]
and h=dim ker_Fp(K), k=D-h.

For vectors (u,v), S(u,v)=(K v,K^T u). Therefore
  ker(S) = ker(K^T) direct-sum ker(K),
  rank(S)=rank(K)+rank(K^T)=2k,
  corank(S)=2h.                                         (2)
These identities are valid over every field. There is no positive-
definite argument and no cancellation through a squared operator.

Apply the PER-INPUT certified full-kernel theorem to S, not its
moment-averaged corollary. A product by S calls K and K^T once each;
nnz(S)=2w. The expected bit work for a full BASE-field kernel basis is
  ~O_p((2D)(2w+2D)(2h+1))
       = ~O_p(D(w+D)(h+1)).                             (3)

If B is any complete basis of ker(S), its lower D-coordinate
projections span ker(K). Indeed, every v in ker(K) has (0,v) in ker(S),
and expressing this vector in B expresses v in those lower projections.
Select h independent projected columns and normalize on h pivot rows.
This is base-field arithmetic, since the S solver has already descended
its basis. Selection/canonicalization costs
  O(D*(2h)*h+h^3)=O(Dh^2+h^3),
which is <=O(D^2(h+1)). No extra exponential factor is present.
The resulting C has
  K C=0,  C[J,:]=I_h.
It is the complete RIGHT kernel basis of K.

The left kernel is computational intermediate data only. It is not
enumerated. The workspace is ~O_p(D(h+1)+w) bits, up to fixed factors
from retaining the doubled basis.

3. A smaller final certificate need not retain the doubled basis
===============================================================

Retain the S solver's field definition, diagonal preconditioner and
scalar probes, but omit its full kernel basis from the final certificate.
Include only C, its pivot rows J, and the reported shifted BM length ell.

A deterministic checker:
  * verifies the extension field and canonical encodings;
  * regenerates the shifted scalar sequence of the preconditioned S,
    recomputes its TRUE BM register length ell;
  * requires ell=2(D-h), where h is the number of supplied C columns;
  * verifies K C=0 and C[J,:]=I_h over F_p.

The unconditional shifted-sequence argument gives
  ell <=rank(S)=2rank(K).
The verified independent C columns give rank(K)<=D-h. Thus
  D-h=ell/2 <=rank(K)<=D-h,
so every accepted certificate proves the complete right kernel, with
ZERO verification error. The full S-kernel need not be trusted or sent.

Verification costs ~O_p(D(w+D)) bit work and the certificate has
  O_p(Dh+D log D)
bits. The verifier recomputes only O(D) black-box products and BM;
it does not perform a dense rank computation. An odd or otherwise
inconsistent claimed ell is rejected. The honest solver has even ell
by (2). The argument includes h=0 and h=D.

4. Why only ONE exponential moment is needed
============================================

Suppose the ACTUAL input law has, for one fixed odd p,
  E[p^h] <= C, h=dim ker_Fp(K).                          (4)
Then E(h+1)<=C. Average the PER-INPUT bound (3): it costs
  ~O_p,C(D(w+D))
in expectation. This step never invokes a raw moment for S.

Next enumerate only ker(K), using the projected h-column basis C.
The incremental base-p counter/Gray algorithm costs O_p(D p^h)
base operations overall, including maintaining each candidate vector.
For fixed t,d, check in O(n) operations per candidate:
  - reshape it into an n-by-t matrix;
  - entries must lift to integer0 or1;
  - every row must have exactly one1;
  - column sizes must match the specified positive fiber sizes;
  - A X=X R must hold over the INTEGERS.
This returns the complete list of valid labeled equitable partitions.
For fixed t, canonicalizing label permutations is a constant-factor
operation if unlabeled partitions are requested.

The expected enumeration/check cost is ~O_p,C(D). With D=tn and w=O(n),
the overall expected bit work is ~O_p,t,d,C(n^2), including all lists.
There is no algorithmic error, even if either kernel has large corank
on an exceptional input. The promise (4) is a new application hypothesis;
a scalar E|ker(A-lambda I)| bound alone does not automatically prove it
for a general quotient R.

If R is unknown but t,d are fixed, there are only finitely many bounded
nonnegative integer quotient candidates, at most (d+1)^(t^2). Running
the construction for each retains the expected near-quadratic bound
provided (4) is established for those candidates. Unknown R does not,
by itself, supply that moment hypothesis.

Enumerating ker(S) instead would cost p^(2h), which (4) does NOT control.
For example, let h_n=n with probability p^(-n) and0 otherwise. Then
  E[p^h_n]=2-p^(-n) <=2,
  E[p^(2h_n)]=1-p^(-n)+p^n ->infinity.
This illustrates the exact logical gap avoided by projecting first.

5. Important balance simplification for undirected graphs
=========================================================

For an INTEGER one-hot X with nonempty cell sizes N_1,...,N_t,
X^T X=diag(N_i). If A=A^T and A X=X R, then
  X^T A X=diag(N_i)R
is symmetric. Consequently
  N_i R_ij=N_j R_ji for every i,j.                      (5)

If every fiber has the SAME prescribed positive size, (5) forces
R=R^T. Therefore a genuinely nonsymmetric quotient candidate can be
rejected immediately in an equal-size undirected random-lift search,
without any kernel computation. This is an INTEGER condition and
remains true even when p divides the fiber size.

Nonsymmetric R is meaningful for unequal cell sizes. Example: the
triangle graph with cells {u},{v,w} has
  R=[[0,2],[1,1]],  (N_1,N_2)=(1,2).
Then R is nonsymmetric and satisfies (5). Linearization handles it
without introducing a weighted inner product or dividing by cell sizes.

6. Two tempting replacements that fail
======================================

(a) Nonsingular left/right DIAGONALS do not generically remove zero
nilpotent blocks of an arbitrary nonsymmetric K. For
  K=[[0,1],[0,0]],
every D_1 K D_2 remains a nonzero square-zero matrix. The nonsingular
diagonal cyclicity theorem does not justify applying the old symmetric
rank-deficient projection algorithm directly to this K.

(b) The deterministic Gram matrix K^T K can have a larger kernel in a
finite field, including odd characteristic. Over F5, take
  K=[[1,0],[2,0]].
It has rank1, but K^T K=0 because 1+2^2=0 modulo5. Thus the claimed
identity ker(K^T K)=ker(K) is false. Symmetric block linearization
avoids this isotropy problem entirely.

7. Optional independently audited weighted-Gram alternative
============================================================

This route is algebraically valid but is NOT needed for (2)--(4) or
implemented by the current intertwiner validation script. It can avoid
the doubled left kernel, including for rectangular matrices.

For arbitrary base-field K of size m-by-D and rank k, choose a random
invertible diagonal E on its m rows over the same large extension F_q,
and form only the BLACK-BOX operator
  G=K^T E K.
It is symmetric, rank(G)<=rank(K), and a product costs two sparse
products plus the row scaling; the dense Gram matrix is never formed.

Choose k independent columns J of K for the proof. Cauchy-Binet gives
  det(G[J,J])=sum_{|I|=k} det(K[I,J])^2 product_{i in I} e_i.
At least one coefficient is nonzero; the squarefree monomials are
distinct. This degree-k polynomial is not identically zero in ANY
characteristic. Hence
  P[rank(G)<k] <=k/(q-1).                               (6)
On rank preservation, ker(G)=ker(K).

Use a fresh nonzero diagonal D on G and the same shifted BM/projection
algorithm, BUT verify every candidate with Kx=0, not merely Gx=0.
For every unlucky choice, the deterministic lower certificate obeys
  ell<=rank(DG)=rank(G)<=rank(K).
If D-ell independent verified K-nullvectors are obtained, their upper
rank bound forces equality throughout. Acceptance is therefore sound,
including bad E choices; a rank-dropping E cannot pass.

On the good event in (6), the symmetric preconditioning results apply
to G over F_q even though G has extension-field entries. Its kernel
equals the base-defined ker(K), and fresh projections sample it
uniformly. The additional failure in (6) is O(D/q), absorbed by the
same capped-bad-trial analysis. For square K, expected work remains
  ~O_p(D(w+D)(h+1)).
For m-by-D K it is ~O_p(D(w+m+D)(h+1)), with h=D-rank(K), using both
K and K^T products and selecting q polynomial in max(m,D).

Descent must be justified using the ACCEPTED full basis of ker(K),
which is base-defined. One must NOT assert that ker(G) is Frobenius-
stable merely because the original K was base-field: G itself has
extension-field entries. Acceptance/rank equality or the good-event
ker(G)=ker(K) is the bridge that makes canonical descent valid.
The certificate can contain E,D,u,v and a canonical base kernel C;
the checker regenerates the implicit G sequence and uses the displayed
lower/upper rank chain. Only p^h vectors are enumerated afterward.
