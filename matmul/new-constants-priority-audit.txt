1. PRECISE NEW CLAIMS AND QUANTIFIERS
===================================

The endpoint manuscript is uniform-alpha-fractional-holder.tex, assigned
final source6b26021065625e6c6e2bd4062cfd7fbaa51f729d2c9d1eb5ef232872bc902a26.
Its H(alpha,s) is the supremum, over arbitrary finite positive matrices,
of Tr|B^alpha-A^alpha|^s / Tr|B-A|^(alpha*s). It asserts:

  (a) H(1-beta,s) ~ beta^(2s-1)/(2*pi^2*s^2) for EVERY joint
      s,beta->0, including superexponentially small beta;
  (b) at fixed0<s<1/2, H has order beta^(2s-1);
  (c) at s=1/2, H has order log(1/beta);
  (d) at fixed1/2<s<1, H is bounded but liminf is strictly above
      the exact linear-endpoint constant1.

It does not give an exact fixed-s endpoint constant. It optimizes over
finite dimensions and spectra which may depend on both parameters.

The localization manuscript is local-singularity-universality.tex,
assigned final source
  5bdf4f06e2de146e2b101a26946abfdecf1880a44a1f97adf038b83e31a11356.
For a fixed compact interval I and
f=h+sum F_j(. -c_j), with analytic h and same-alpha homogeneous
fractional F_j at distinct interior points, it gives exact small-s
power constants and leading rank counts by adding active analytic
channels. One positive rank-one perturbation attains the lower bound.
Its finite-s lower has mass factors p_j^(alpha*s); it does not assert
exact fixed-s additivity. A growing number M is allowed when s logM->0.

For f=sum exp(-b_j)|.-c_j|^alpha and
A(u)=#{j:b_j<=u}~C*u^nu, isolated c_j accumulating only at the boundary,
the claimed sharp laws are

 H_s^I(f) ~2*C*Gamma(nu+1)/(pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha))*s^(-nu-2),
 N^I_(f,r)(L) ~2*r*C/[pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)*(nu+1)*(nu+2)]*L^(nu+2).

The error scale is exp(-L)||B-A||^alpha and the perturbation norm may
shrink arbitrarily. That is essential: a fixed lower update norm or a
fixed endpoint/update ratio is a different extremal question. A scalar
uniform rational approximation on a compact interval does not by
itself imply this intrinsic-scale theorem.

2. RICARD2018: A DIRECT ENDPOINT-MECHANISM ANTECEDENT
===================================================

E. Ricard, Fractional powers on noncommutative Lp for p<1,
Adv. Math.333 (2018),194--211.
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1805.05677
Primary PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.05677

Fresh reading covered Corollaries2.8--2.14, Remark2.12, and the ENTIRE
Remark5.3, including its eigenvalue calculation. Remark5.3 takes
theta=1-epsilon, x=exp(t/epsilon), y=exp(u/epsilon). The divided
difference tends to exp(-max(t,u)). It reduces to exp(-|t-u|), computes
inverse-square eigenvalue decay, and obtains nonuniformity for p<=1/2;
the limiting kernel is bounded as a Schur multiplier for p>1/2.
Corollary2.11 instead supplies a coarse norm bound
C_p*(1-theta)^(-1/p), whose p-th POWER is O_p(beta^-1).

Neither the fixed-p beta^(2p-1) rate nor the critical logarithm is
stated in the inspected text. The limiting argument is qualitative,
and its constants do not give the exact all-joint s->0 asymptotic.
Those quantitative conclusions require additional estimates. The source
nevertheless directly anticipates the paper's endpoint witness and
threshold; citing only its coarser Corollary2.11 would understate overlap.

3. OUR INFERENCE: THE NONLINEAR QUALITATIVE LOWER IS A SHORT TRANSFER
==================================================================

This is an explicit inference, not a theorem quoted from Ricard.
For fixed finite positive u_i, take A_beta=diag(exp(u_i/beta)) and
a unit vector v. Let L_beta be the fractional-power Frechet derivative
at A_beta applied to vv*. The rank-one resolvent formula gives

 (1+lambda_min(A_beta)^(-1))^(-1)*L_beta
       <=(A_beta+vv*)^(1-beta)-A_beta^(1-beta)<=L_beta.

The inequalities are Loewner inequalities: the exact denominator
1+v*(A_beta+t)^(-1)v is between1 and1+lambda_min(A_beta)^(-1).
The two sides have the same finite-dimensional endpoint limit because
lambda_min(A_beta)->infinity. The limit matrix is

       K_ij=v_i conjugate(v_j) exp(-max(u_i,u_j)).

It is the Brownian covariance kernel after t_i=exp(-u_i). Arbitrarily
long finite spectral pieces of its continuum version give divergence
of the nonlinear supremum for fixed s<=1/2. Thus the new finite-coupling
formulation sharpens the presentation but does not require inventing
a new limiting model. Quantitative truncation at j of order1/beta and
a global matching upper are further work, not contained in the mere
limit statement. The exact all-joint constant is a stronger question.

For s>1/2, boundedness of a LIMITING kernel alone does not imply a
uniform bound on all finite-beta nonlinear corrections. One must still
control those corrections; the manuscript's positive Fourier domination
and low-block estimate do so. This distinction prevents both overstating
the prior and overlooking its direct relevance.

4. FINITE AND INFINITE SINGULARITIES: WHAT IS CLASSICAL
=====================================================

[PY] A. Pushnitski, D. Yafaev, Localization principle for compact Hankel
operators (2015 preprint, JFA270 (2016),3591--3621).
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1508.04279

Freshly inspected Introduction, Theorem1.1, Discussion, and Sections2.2--2.3.
Theorem1.1 gives additive limsup/liminf bounds for separated singular
supports, and an exact sum when the individual power-scale limits exist.
Its discussion states the equivalent singular-count formulation through
asymptotically orthogonal operators. This is direct precedent for the
idea of adding contributions from separate singularities. It concerns
fixed Hankel operators and power-scale asymptotics, not the new supremum
over finite nonlinear input pairs or an optimized update normalization.
It also does not by itself establish the stated infinite amplitude law.

[Sob] A. V. Sobolev, Functions of self-adjoint operators in ideals of
compact operators, JLMS95 (2017),157--176; preprint2015.
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1504.07261

Freshly inspected Introduction, Condition2.3, Theorem2.4, Corollary2.7
and the finite-singularity setup of Theorem4.4. Nonhomogeneous functions
with finitely many nonsmooth points are established functional-calculus
objects. Theorem2.4 uses sigma<gamma and a differentiability/ideal
condition; it does not cover the critical exponent by direct substitution.
Its constants do not give the new exact small-s limits, a one-rank-one
mass sandwich, or the claimed amplitude-counting asymptotics.

[HSZ] J. Huang, F. Sukochev, D. Zanin, Operator theta-Holder functions
with respect to ||.||p, JLMS105 (2022),2436--2477.
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2110.09708

Read the actual Introduction/Theorem1.2 and its stated comparison with
Sobolev. It gives all-p existence for a broad S_(d,theta) class, including
the critical exponent; it is not restricted to homogeneous powers.
Consequently neither nonhomogeneous extension nor existence for broad
function classes should be called a first breakthrough here. Its
parameter-dependent constants do not state the precise new extrema.

[MS] E. McDonald, F. Sukochev, Lipschitz estimates in quasi-Banach

Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2009.08069

Read Introduction, Theorem4.5.3 and the adjacent weak-type Theorem4.6.1.
Wavelet/Besov coefficient control of operator-Holder estimates and

5. HOW MUCH NEW MATHEMATICS THE LOCALIZATION PAPER IS CLAIMING
============================================================

The finite theorem rests on three steps: sharp single-cusp extrema,
ordinary power subadditivity for the upper, and a separated-block
first-order unitary expansion for the lower. The last step is a useful
way to preserve ONE rank-one global input while making local witnesses
independent after rescaling. The perturbation expansion is classical;
the proposed exact normalized consequence was not found in the sources
inspected. Its application here may be new without being a new general
perturbation principle.

Once the finite mass sandwich is established, the infinite moment law
is squeezed between two explicit Laplace sums of the amplitudes, and
the count law sums (L-b_j)_+^2 with controlled tails. The Gamma constants
and regular-variation calculation are standard analysis. They form a
stronger theorem family than a lone factor-four comparison, but are
quantitative consequences of the exact single-cusp theorem plus gluing.
They should not be advertised as an independent discovery that Hankel
or matrix-function compression can have decay outside root exponential.

I found no inspected primary theorem with BOTH this nonlinear intrinsic
normalization and the displayed exact infinite amplitude-counting
constants. The literature on general Hankel spectral asymptotics,
Peller/AAK approximation, and function-space multiplier localization is
too large for this bounded screen to certify that implication is absent.

6. ADDITIONAL CONCRETE ALGORITHM OVERLAP: REGULARIZE BEFORE SDA
============================================================

[SDA] S. Shmueli, P. Drineas, H. Avron, Low-rank updates of matrix
square roots, NLAA31 (2024),e2528; first online2023.
Primary preprint: https://arxiv.org/html/2201.13156v3

Read actual Problem2/Section2.3, Theorem3 and its full proof. Theorem3
allows arbitrary rank-k D with SPD endpoints and gives relative
singular-value decay through a Sylvester displacement bound. Its
parameter is bounded by a constant times sqrt(Lambda/gamma) after
adding gamma I to both endpoints. The same paper's numerical algorithms
use supplied root actions; that is separate from the rank theorem.

The following reduction was supplied by the coordinator and I checked
it against that actual theorem. Let Delta=sqrt(B)-sqrt(A), d=||Delta||,
and choose a computable d0<=d. For lambda=epsilon*d0/16 set gamma=lambda^2,
A_gamma=A+gamma I and B_gamma=B+gamma I. Scalar calculus gives

 ||[sqrt(B_gamma)-sqrt(A_gamma)]-Delta||<=2sqrt(gamma)
                                             <=epsilon*d0/8.

Applying SDA Theorem3 to these SPD endpoints at relative tolerance
of order epsilon gives rank

 O(r log(1/epsilon)
       [log(1/epsilon)+log(sqrt(Lambda)/d0)]).

The additive regularization error fits inside the original relative
budget because d0<=d. This establishes the same rank ORDER even for
singular endpoints. Thus the new low-low ADI cutoff is a direct proof
and can be algorithmically convenient, but the rank-order consequence
is already a short regularization corollary of the old theorem. It is
not necessary to pretend the old theorem literally included zero
eigenvalues to recognize this overlap.

The fully charged from-scratch raw-factor ADI/Stieltjes implementation
is not supplied just by this rank reduction. Its novelty remains a
separate synthesis/complexity question, as the earlier SOTA audit warns.
