1. THE HILBERT-SPACE QUADRATIC IDENTITY IS EXACT
----------------------------------------------
For positive definite self-adjoint P,Q and c, let z=(P+Q)^(-1)c.
For any x+y=c, write x=Pz+h and y=Qz-h. Expanding the two quadratic
forms gives

  <x,P^(-1)x>+<y,Q^(-1)y>
   =<c,(P+Q)^(-1)c>+<h,(P^(-1)+Q^(-1))h>.

The two cross terms cancel in their real parts, so the identity is
valid on a complex Hilbert space. The minimum is attained at x=Pz,
y=Qz and is unique. No commutativity of P,Q is required.

On matrix space, P=L_A and Q=R_B are positive self-adjoint for A,B>0.
The second quadratic form is

  <Y,R_B^(-1)Y>_HS=Tr[Y^*Y B^(-1)]
                       =Tr[Y B^(-1)Y^*].

This proves equation (2), including the essential order of the factors.
The inverse-of-sum identity is exactly the usual parallel-sum quadratic
variational identity; claiming that elementary identity as novel would
be unsupported. The source explicitly credits its classical status.

2. THE TWO SCHUR BLOCKS, DIMENSIONS AND AFFINENESS
-------------------------------------------------
For fixed A,B>0, the first block [A,X;X^*,U]>=0 is equivalent to
U>=X^*A^(-1)X. The second block [B,Y^*;Y,V]>=0 is equivalent to
V>=Y B^(-1)Y^*. Thus X+Y=C and tau>=TrU+TrV imply the epigraph
inequality, and the minimizing split constructs feasible U,V achieving
equality. The epigraph projection is exact rather than just a relaxation.

The optimal X=AZ and Y=ZB generally are not Hermitian, even for a
Hermitian C. The source correctly leaves them unrestricted complex.
The orientations of X, X^*, Y and Y^* in both displayed blocks are
correct. Replacing the second block by [B,Y;Y^*,V] would be wrong.

Every entry is affine in the stated matrices. There is no inverse,
Kronecker matrix, matrix product of two optimization variables, or
Sylvester solve in the LMI representation itself. The equality-certificate
Sylvester solve is used only in the proof and optional verification.

After eliminating Y, X has 2n^2 real coordinates and U,V each have
n^2, for exactly 4n^2 auxiliary real coordinates per node. The epigraph
variable tau and other problem variables are not counted as auxiliaries
of the node. Both blocks have order 2n over the complex Hermitian field.
For the rectangular identity, U has order q, V order p and X shape
p-by-q, giving two blocks of order p+q and (p+q)^2 real auxiliaries.

The alternative scaling in (6) is valid: use sqrt(t)X and
sqrt(1-t)Y as the original split. The two trace costs then have the
unscaled rho/sigma Schur forms. At endpoint t=0 or t=1 the remaining
single block is correct; all sharp interior quadrature nodes are
strictly inside (0,1), so no endpoint inverse ambiguity is hidden.

3. IT IS THE PETZ KERNEL, INCLUDING AT THE PSD BOUNDARY
-----------------------------------------------------
In left/right eigenbases the matrix element of Delta=rho-sigma is
(r_i-s_j)<u_i,v_j>. The Petz kernel weight is

  s_j*f_t(r_i/s_j)=(r_i-s_j)^2/[t*r_i+(1-t)*s_j].

This is precisely the Sylvester quadratic in (4). The result is not a
trace of the noncommutative maximal matrix perspective. All subsequent
scalar-to-quantum inequalities therefore use the intended divergence.

For singular A,B the generalized Schur complement adds the range
conditions range(X) subset range(A), range(Y^*) subset range(B), and
uses A^+,B^+. The Hilbert-space quadratic identity extends on those
ranges: if C is in range(L_A+R_B), choose Z=(L_A+R_B)^+C and take
X=AZ,Y=ZB. The same completed squares prove optimality. If C is
outside that range, no finite-cost split exists, since for positive
operators range(P+Q)=range(P)+range(Q) in finite dimensions.

For A=t*rho, B=(1-t)*sigma and C=Delta with 0<t<1, the range
condition always holds. A vector Z in the Sylvester kernel satisfies
rho Z=0 and Z sigma=0, hence both <Z,rho> and <Z,sigma> vanish.
Thus <Z,Delta>=0. This proves finiteness of every interior rational
kernel for all PSD rho,sigma, without a common-support assumption.

The regularization rho+epsilon I, sigma+epsilon I leaves Delta fixed
and adds epsilon I to the Sylvester operator. Since Delta is orthogonal
to its kernel, its inverse quadratic converges to the pseudoinverse
quadratic. This justifies the exact PSD extension stated in the note.

The entropy error/bracket theorem itself is correctly stated on positive
definite density matrices. If extended to singular states, a finite
entropy/chi-squared certificate requires support(rho) subset
support(sigma); the endpoint block with sigma enforces the corresponding
range condition. Otherwise the upper chi-squared term is infinite and
no finite approximation-gap claim follows. The note does not incorrectly
assert a finite uniform gap at such incompatible supports.

4. CONVEXITY, HOMOGENEITY AND DATA PROCESSING
-------------------------------------------
Projection of the affine LMI feasible set is convex. Scaling rho,sigma
and all split/slack matrix variables by the same positive scalar proves
positive homogeneity. Each kernel is a nonnegative inverse quadratic,
so every positive weighted sum is nonnegative and vanishes at equality.

For a CPTP map Phi, apply id_2 tensor Phi separately to the two block
constraints. Complete positivity preserves block positivity; star
preservation keeps their orientations; linearity preserves X+Y=Delta;
and trace preservation leaves the cost unchanged. The transformed
variables are feasible for the image problem with the same objective,
so minimization proves data processing. This proof works directly with
the PSD lift if a channel sends a positive state to a singular state.
No unproved continuity or maximal/Petz identification is used.

For m positive nodes these arguments give exactly 2m blocks and
4mn^2 auxiliary real coordinates. They apply also to the rescaled
lower approximation and its positive endpoint correction.

5. SCALAR ERROR TRANSFER AND THE GENERAL ONE-SIDED REFINEMENT
-------------------------------------------------------------
The eigenbasis weights s_j*|<u_i,v_j>|^2 are nonnegative, so the
absolute scalar relative error transfers termwise to the Petz sums.
For trace-one states, S_f1=D and
S_f2=(Tr[rho^2 sigma^(-1)]-1)/2. For unequal traces, the note uses
the correct generalized entropy, including -Tr(rho)+Tr(sigma), and
the quadratic correction Tr[Delta sigma^(-1)Delta]/2.

The sign of the actual positive construction is essential. Its exact
error has Omega(-x)=x(x-1) times a square, so f_a-r has sign x-1,
apart from interpolation zeros. The statement is correct for every
1<=a<b<=2 in this construction, including a=1,b=2.

For 0<x<=1, second derivatives give f_b<=f_a. To check the stronger
ratio bound including a=1, put y=-log x and compute

  a*f_a(x)=1-x-x*integral_0^y exp[-(a-1)t]dt.

The right side increases in a. Hence a*f_a<=b*f_b and
f_b<=f_a<=(b/a)f_b<=2f_b. For x>=1, second derivatives instead give
f_a<=f_b. These claims use only Taylor's integral formula and the
displayed elementary identity; the logarithm appears only in analysis.

If |f_a-r|<=e*f_b, on x<=1 the sign gives
f_a<=r<=f_a+e*f_b<=(1+e)f_a. Thus r/(1+e)<=f_a and its lower gap
is at most e*f_a/(1+e)<=2e*f_b/(1+e). On x>=1, r<=f_a and the
lower gap is [(f_a-r)+e*f_a]/(1+e), with the same upper bound.
The rescaled rational function remains nonnegative with strictly
positive weights, and has exactly the same nodes and rate.

The involution f_a(x)=x*f_(1-a)(1/x) sends a kernel node t to 1-t
without changing its weight. It therefore transports one-sidedness and
the relative error to the full negative parameter range. The statement
in the note is correctly scoped.

For b=2, adding eta*S_f2 to the rescaled lower Petz approximation
gives an upper convex SDP with one extra endpoint block. For a general
b<2, the pointwise upper obtained by adding eta*S_fb need not itself
have an exact single-block SDP; the note correctly restricts that
single-extra-block assertion to b=2.

6. ENTROPY BRACKETS, SHARP NODE COUNT AND FINITE CERTIFICATE
----------------------------------------------------------
Taking e=eta/2 gives a lower error coefficient
2e/(1+e)<=eta. Thus

  D_minus<=D<=D_plus=D_minus+eta*S_f2,
  D-D_minus<=eta*chi_P^2/2,
  D_plus-D<=eta*chi_P^2/2.

All functions used for the approximations are convex positive Petz
sums. The added endpoint block [sigma,Delta;Delta,W]>=0 contributes
exactly eta*TrW/2, whose minimum is eta*S_f2. Its orientation, factor
one-half and dimensions are correct.

The positive scalar result with e=eta/2 gives
m<=log^2(2C/eta)/(2pi^2)+3 for sufficiently small eta. The lower
function uses 2m order-2n blocks and the upper 2m+1. The change of
constant inside the logarithm does not alter the leading node
coefficient. No explicit numerical value of the unknown asymptotic
Ganelius constant is silently assumed.

The explicit comparison certificate (21) is correct. At a=1,b=2,
K_a=1, p=1/2, and the two prior comparison bounds become

  2pi*d_minus^2+4*d_minus*d_plus,
  4*d_minus*d_plus+2pi*d_plus^2.

Their maximum is exactly the displayed e_m. The product maxima use
s^(1/2)B(s)^2, so the earlier critical polynomial is used with r=1/4.
For identical n-node products, m=2n+1 and e_m=(2pi+4)d^2. A rigorous
upper bound e>=e_m is sufficient for the rescaling. Using
eta_m=2e/(1+e) gives a valid upper endpoint correction and the same
a posteriori bracket, without knowing the asymptotic constant C.
Root extraction and verified scalar evaluations are separately charged,
as the source explicitly says. This is not a finite-word theorem.

A finite positive sum of strictly interior kernels grows at most
linearly as x tends to infinity. It cannot globally upper bound the
x*log(x) entropy generator. The endpoint correction in the upper
approximation is therefore a substantive domain requirement, not an
unnecessary pole introduced by the proof.

7. OPTIMIZATION CERTIFICATES: VALID AND SLIGHTLY STRONGER FORM
-------------------------------------------------------------
Assume a nonempty feasible set of trace-one positive definite states,
with the stated affine/LMI restrictions. Pointwise bracketing gives
p_minus<=p<=p_plus for the three infima. Each infimum is finite since
the functions are nonnegative and a feasible positive pair has finite
value. The use of infima rather than attained optima is appropriate.

If chi_P^2<=R throughout the feasible set, the two individual errors
in (18) are valid by approximating sequences. In fact the pointwise
difference D_plus-D_minus=eta*chi_P^2/2 gives the stronger combined
bound

  p_plus-p_minus<=eta*R/2.

To prove it, evaluate D_plus along an approximating minimizing sequence
for D_minus. The note's eta*R combined bound is valid but loose. For a
desired combined width delta, eta=2delta/R already suffices (when the
small-eta count is used in its stated regime); its eta=delta/R choice
is conservative and valid.

Without a known R, an attained LOWER optimizer x_minus gives exactly

  D_plus(x_minus)-p_minus=eta*chi_P^2(x_minus)/2.

Therefore the proposed evaluated-gap bracket is valid without a supplied
global cap. For an approximately lower-optimal feasible x, add its
optimization suboptimality. For a certified dual lower bound ell,
the note's displayed bracket width is correct. A merely small residual
is not a certified feasible point or a valid lower bound, as the note
properly acknowledges.

There is an even more direct lift-level certificate: let x be feasible,
let q be ANY feasible objective value of its lower rational lift, and
let W satisfy the endpoint block. A valid dual bound ell for the lower
program gives the entirely algebraic interval

  ell <= p <= q+(eta/2)TrW.

This requires neither exact evaluation of D_minus(x), a Sylvester solve,
nor a matrix logarithm. Its width is directly checkable from primal
and dual certificates. At exact auxiliary minima it reduces to the
note's formula. No uniform chi-squared cap guarantees that this local
width is small or that the lower optimizer has a finite boundary gap;
those conclusions must come from the evaluated certificate itself.

If the domain is implemented by the PSD extension, finite candidate
certificates still require the support condition in section 3. Strict
positive definiteness can alternatively be kept as the stated domain,
or encoded by an additional inverse-epigraph block if a literal SDP
projection description is desired. These domain blocks and other
problem constraints are separate from the 2m kernel-block count.

The sufficient LMI rho<=kappa*sigma gives the claimed R=kappa-1:
with C=sigma^(-1/2)rho sigma^(-1/2), trace cyclicity gives
Tr[rho^2 sigma^(-1)]=Tr[sigma*C^2]<=kappa*Tr[sigma*C]=kappa.
At R=0 the states coincide and D=0. Noncommutativity does not invalidate
this trace calculation.

8. CONE COUNTS, BARRIER COMPARISON AND WHAT THEY DO NOT PROVE
-----------------------------------------------------------
A complex Hermitian cone of order d has d^2 real coordinates and
logdet barrier parameter d. Thus the old m order-(n^2+1) blocks have
m(n^2+1)^2 cone coordinates and parameter m(n^2+1), whereas the
new 2m order-2n blocks have exactly 8mn^2 cone coordinates and
parameter 4mn. The one final affine trace inequality adds one scalar
nonnegative cone. The upper approximation adds one order-2n cone
and n^2 auxiliary real coordinates. Other feasible-set cones add their
own parameters; the note states this explicitly.

The quoted square-root-barrier iteration comparison is appropriate
under the usual short-step assumptions, with initialization/gap/tolerance
and any extra cones accounted for. It says nothing by itself about the
size or cost of the Newton system. The note correctly excludes an
end-to-end n^2/n^3 solver bound and any bit/conditioning claim.

Realification doubles the complex block order and only changes these
scalings by constants. For real symmetric data, taking real parts of
a complex optimal split preserves feasibility and cannot increase the
quadratic objective, so real optimal variables suffice. The count
n^2+n(n+1)=2n^2+n real auxiliaries per node is correct; X remains
unrestricted real rather than symmetric.

I independently read the actual FF Theorem 5. It already states an
O(m)-block order-2n convex SDP approximation with a Petz-chi-squared

offers the result without proof and invokes forthcoming Fawzi work.

The Hirche--Tomamichel primary text explicitly defines a different
hockey-stick-integral divergence; its equation 1.7 gives the BKM-type
chi-squared integral rather than Tr[rho^2 sigma^(-1)]-1 in general.
The Kossmann--Schwonnek primary abstract states provable sublinear
discretization convergence and small SDP matrix dimensions. Their
useful scope is not negated by the note's separate fixed-hinge
obstruction.

That obstruction's elementary constant checks: f1''>=1/2 on [1,2].
On an affine piece of length h, the second midpoint difference is
at least h^2/8. Three value errors contribute at most 4E, so a
piecewise-linear approximant with at most m+1 pieces has
E>=1/[32(m+1)^2]. Since f2<=1/2 on this interval, a relative f2
error epsilon entails epsilon>=1/[16(m+1)^2]. This is a scoped
obstruction to fixed hinge quadrature, not a lower bound for all
algorithms or all quantum SDP representations.

I did not perform an exhaustive search for the unpublished Fawzi work
or certify the historical priority of every resulting corollary. The
source accurately states these limitations and does not infer a
general quadratic matrix algorithm from its small cones.

10.1 Interval arithmetic and the exact node definition

two adjacent alternating arctangent sums. Multiplying their intervals
by 16 and -4 with the correct endpoint choices encloses pi. Directed
division then encloses those exact fractions in 64-digit Decimal
endpoints. Integer/string Decimal constructors used here are exact.

Every basic interval addition, subtraction and multiplication is
outward-rounded; multiplication checks all four endpoint products.
The sqrt/ln enclosures follow monotonicity and one outward adjacent
number around each correctly rounded endpoint evaluation. For the
fixed n=12 run, the only default-context arithmetic, n0 minus 0.5,

10.2 Uniform cell bounds and coverage

For s=exp(u), the balanced product quantity is exactly

  d(exp(u))=exp(u/2)*prod_j tanh(|u-log(a_j)|/2)^2.

On a cell [lo,hi], interval subtraction bounds every u-log(a_j).
The maximum absolute endpoint bounds its absolute value. Since tanh
is increasing on nonnegative arguments, this supplies an upper bound
for each product factor. The implementation uses
tanh(distance/2)=(1-w)/(1+w), w=exp(-distance). It rounds w DOWN,
the numerator UP and the denominator DOWN before an UP division.
All quantities have the required nonnegative signs, so each resulting
factor is a valid upper bound. Multiplying their squares and the
upper bound exp(hi/2) therefore bounds d on the ENTIRE cell.

The coefficient 2pi+4 and final squares/products are also rounded UP.
For u<=-30, |B(exp(u))|<=1 yields d^2<=exp(-30), handled separately.
The initial pending cell covers [-30,0]. Every split uses a midpoint
strictly between its endpoints; exact midpoint evaluation is unnecessary
for coverage, though the actual shallow dyadic splits are exact here.
The two closed children cover their parent. Every terminal cell is
accepted only after its outward error bound is <=5e-7. Thus finite
termination certifies all u<=0, not just sampled grid points.

Run with assertions enabled, as its header requires. The depth/work
guards can fail the certificate but cannot turn an unresolved cell
into a success. No uncertified fallback is present.
