1. THE CONSTANT-ONE NUCLEAR TAIL IS A CLASSICAL COROLLARY
-------------------------------------------------------

Bourin and Uchiyama, "A matrix subadditivity inequality for f(A+B)
and f(A)+f(B)," arXiv:math/0702475, Theorem 1.1, PDF p1:
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0702475
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0702475

Their theorem says that nonnegative concave f on [0,infinity) satisfies
||f(X+Y)||<=||f(X)+f(Y)|| for PSD X,Y and every symmetric norm. They
explicitly identify its trace-norm case as classical and credit Rotfel'd.
Taking f(t)=t^alpha, Y=P=ww^T gives tr D<=1.

The following is our direct deduction, not an exact formulation located
in that paper. Loewner--Heinz gives B^alpha>=P; scalar Jensen gives
w^TA^alpha w<=a^alpha, where a=w^TAw<=h. Therefore

  tr(QDQ)=tr D-w^TDw
      <=1-[1-w^TA^alpha w]<=a^alpha<=h^alpha,
  sum_(j>=2)lambda_j(D)<=tr(QDQ).

The source's Schur certificate F=(Dw)(Dw)^T/(w^TDw) has D-F>=0 and
tr F>=w^TDw, hence ||D-F||_*<=h^alpha as well. Thus this entire
constant-one tail conclusion is an immediate established-inequality
corollary. It should not be described as a new ingredient. In particular,
the exact one-count region h^alpha<=epsilon follows classically too.

2. WHAT CLASSICAL RATIONAL APPROXIMATION GIVES DIRECTLY
------------------------------------------------------

Stahl, "Best uniform rational approximation of x^alpha on [0,1],"
Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 28 (1993), 116--122, Theorem 1, equations
(2.1)--(2.2), PDF p3:
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9301217
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9301217

The theorem gives the scalar type-(q,q) error
4^(1+alpha)*sin(pi*alpha)*exp(-2*pi*sqrt(alpha*q))*(1+o(1))
for 0<alpha<1. This is an established optimal scalar exponent.

Beckermann, Cortinovis, Kressner and Schweitzer, "Low-rank updates of
matrix functions II: Rational Krylov methods," SINUM 59 (2021),
1325--1347, Theorem 3.3 and Remark 3.4, PDF pp6--7:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11501
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.11501

These give rational-update exactness, including polynomial terms as
infinite poles. Their introduction explicitly describes approximating
f first by r and then using r(A+E)-r(A), crediting Bernstein--Van Loan.
Theorem 4.5 links Hermitian update convergence to a uniform rational
approximation problem; its analytic-domain assumptions must be respected
when a fractional-power spectrum reaches zero. The elementary direct
rational-difference rank argument does not need that analytic hypothesis.

Our deduction: using [0,2] scalar approximants and rank(E)=1 gives,
uniformly for h=epsilon^tau in [0,1],

  N(epsilon)<= [1/(4*pi^2*alpha)+o(1)]log^2(1/epsilon).

This direct bound has no tau dependence. Combining it merely with the
nuclear tail yields N(epsilon)<=1+h^alpha/epsilon, which establishes
the exact terminal one-count region but does not give the two positive
branches of the joint coefficient. These particular classical estimates
therefore do not by themselves prove the displayed joint formula.

3. RELATED LOW-RANK SINGULAR-VALUE RESULTS: DIFFERENT GUARANTEES
--------------------------------------------------------------

Shmueli, Drineas and Avron, "Low-Rank Updates of Matrix Square Roots,"
arXiv:2201.13156 (2023 version), Theorem 3, PDF p5:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13156
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13156

For SPD A,B=A+E and rank(E)=k, they prove geometric singular-value
decay for B^(1/2)-A^(1/2) with rate depending on a spectral condition
parameter involving lambda_min(A), ||A|| and ||E||. The proof uses a
Sylvester equation. The theorem is substantial square-root-update
antecedence. It is not a dimension-uniform joint epsilon/endpoint-gap
asymptotic for PSD endpoints, and it supplies no matching two-branch
supremal lower in the inspected statement.

Beckermann and Townsend, "On the singular values of matrices with
displacement structure," arXiv:1609.09494, Theorem 2.1, PDF p4:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09494
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.09494

If X has displacement rank nu under normal coefficient matrices with
spectra in E,F, the theorem bounds sigma_(j+nu*k)(X) by
Z_k(E,F)*sigma_j(X). This explains classical rational/Sylvester control
of numerical rank. It does not state the joint fractional rank-one
class, the endpoint-to-update saturation parameter h, or its exact
piecewise coefficient. Applying sharper parameter-dependent versions
may give other derivations; that possibility is not excluded here.

4. LIMITED CONCLUSION FOR THE JOINT FORMULA
------------------------------------------

The matching formula inspected locally is

 C_alpha(tau)=(1/alpha-tau^2)/(4*pi^2),              0<=tau<=1,
             =(1-alpha*tau)^2/(4*pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)),
                                                   1<=tau<=1/alpha,
             =0,                                   tau>=1/alpha.

No inspected primary theorem states this exact joint compression law
or its matching supremal lower bound. A small targeted search using
fractional powers, rank-one updates, compression, endpoint/boundary,
and singular-value asymptotics did not locate one. This negative result
is not evidence of exhaustive absence. Title similarities, unrelated
fractional PDE results, and condition-dependent square-root decay were
not counted as matching theorems.

The tail bound and terminal cutoff should be credited as classical
corollaries. The unresolved priority candidate is the precise positive
two-branch joint law, requiring uniform h-sensitive control plus matching
examples. In the local proof that work is carried by the projected
three-line envelope, moving Gaussian area estimate, varying small-mean
Cauchy density, and two-parameter count-deficit argument. Their use
here does not certify historical novelty. A more specialized classical
weighted-rational or weighted-Cauchy theorem could shorten that proof;
this limited audit has not established or excluded such a reduction.
