Matrix-free fine proxies and multilevel inverse-root preconditioning
2026-11-14

Status
------
A two-level construction survives the mathematical audit below. It strictly
improves the currently proved one-level cost tradeoff, including an explicit
quadratic-output regime at a smaller accuracy parameter. This is a theorem
derivation and primary-comparator audit, not a certified global novelty claim.
No numerical stability or bit-complexity result is asserted. The original
theta=1/m, N=m^(9/8), construction target is still not reached.

The setup, notation and arithmetic model are those in
docs/sketch-preconditioned-spectral.tex. This note does not modify that paper.
Its additional ingredient is to keep an accurate proxy in factored form and
apply its inverse square root through a coarse explicit preconditioner. Thus
the fine proxy need not be multiplied into a dense Gram matrix.

1. A proved two-level bound
--------------------------
Let M=A B, with A n x m, B m x p, m<=min(n,p), and I=m(n+p).
For distortion x, define sketch dimensions

  s_A(x)=min(n,~O(m/x^2)), s_B(x)=min(p,~O(m/x^2)),
  G_x=T_MM(m,s_A(x),m)+T_MM(m,s_B(x),m),
  J_x=m[s_A(x)+s_B(x)]+m^2.

For 0<theta<=h<=H/4 and H<=1/32, the two-level construction returns
an explicitly expanded rank-one C satisfying

  Prob{||M-C||_2 <= (1+theta) sigma_2(M)} >=1-delta

with total arithmetic cost, suppressing logarithms and exponent slack,

  I + G_H + m^omega + m^2/sqrt(H)
      + I sqrt(h/theta) + J_h sqrt(H/theta) + n p.             (1)

This statement holds for every fixed pair of real factors, including
cancellations, rank deficiency, zero product, zero tail, and repeated
singular values. The square roots and arithmetic used in the explicit
polynomial below are allowed in the original model. There is no hidden
trigonometric oracle or integral-coefficient computation.

The first, slightly weaker derivation had an extra I sqrt(H/h) term for
finding a fine eigenvalue bracket with true-factor actions. Section 4 removes
that term by finding the norm of the fine sketched product instead. That
operation uses fine-proxy actions and is absorbed in J_h sqrt(H/theta).

2. Coarse coordinates and a matrix-free fine proxy
------------------------------------------------
Form H-accuracy SRHT Grams P_H and Q_H, costing ~I+G_H, and use exactly
the support reduction and whitening from the main paper:

  W^T P_H W=I, L=W^dagger, Z=A W,
  G=Z^T Z, Q'=L B B^T L^T, Q_0=L Q_H L^T,
  K_t=tG-G Q' G.

All support algebra costs ~m^omega. If the support is zero, return zero.
The nonzero eigenvalues of M M^T agree with those of G^(1/2) Q' G^(1/2).
Write lambda=||M||_2^2. A true K_t action costs O(I), and

  I/(1+H) <= G <= I/(1-H).

The main paper constructs a coarse bracket [ell_0,b_0] for lambda and an
explicit preconditioner

  F_H(t)=(1+32H)t I-Q_0.

For every threshold in that bracket,

  22Ht I <= F_H-K_t <=42Ht I, F_H>=28Ht I,
  ||Q'||<=2t, (2/3)lambda<=t<=(3/2)lambda.                    (2)

The coarse norm estimate costs ~m^2/sqrt(H).

Independently compute h-accuracy sketch FACTORS

  A_h=S_h A, B_h=B T_h^T,

in ~I time, without forming their Grams. On their good OSE events,

  (1-h)A^T A <= P_h=A_h^T A_h <=(1+h)A^T A,
  (1-h)B B^T <= Q_h=B_h B_h^T <=(1+h)B B^T.

Use these operators implicitly:

  G_h=W^T P_h W, Q'_h=L Q_h L^T,
  K_h(t)=tG_h-G_h Q'_h G_h,
  F_h(t)=K_h(t)+32ht I.

Each application costs O(J_h). In particular, applying G_h consists of
W, A_h, A_h^T, W^T actions; no tall transformed factor is materialized.
Relative Loewner errors give ||G_h-G||<=h||G|| and
||Q'_h-Q'||<=h||Q'||. Expanding the two symmetric products, using
||G||<=16/15, ||G_h||<=17/15 and ||Q'||<=2t, gives

  ||K_h(t)-K_t||<=10ht,
  22ht I <= F_h-K_t <=42ht I.                               (3)

The mass metric in K_h is G_h, not I. Using tI-Q'_h here would retain
an O(Ht) error and lose the improvement. This distinction is essential.

3. Why accurate proxy factors are sufficient
-------------------------------------------
Suppose a fine bracket, obtained in Section 4, ensures

  (1-33h/8)lambda <= t
     <=(1+h)^2 lambda/[(1-h/8)(1-h)^2]

and is intersected with [ell_0,b_0]. Its precise upper endpoint is less
important than that intersection and the displayed lower endpoint.
Since h<=1/128, the negative part of K_t is bounded below by -5ht I:

  K_t=G^(1/2)[tI-G^(1/2)Q'G^(1/2)]G^(1/2) >=-5ht I.

Consequently F_h>=17ht I; use the conservative bound F_h>=16ht I.
Also ||F_h||<=6t by (2), (3), and ||G||<=16/15.

From (2), (3), and h<=H/4,

  0<=F_H-F_h<=42Ht I,
  F_H<=F_h+42Ht I<=(1+42H/(16h))F_h.

Factor only the COARSE matrix, F_H=J J^T, costing ~m^omega. Thus

  D=J^-1 F_h J^-T

is a symmetric implicit operator with

  [h/(3H)] I <= D <= I.                                   (4)

Indeed its lower bound is at least
h/[h+(42/16)H]>=h/(3H), since h/H<=1/4.
Applying D costs O(J_h), including the small triangular actions.

Put a=h/(3H), eta=h/1000, and choose a positive polynomial p on [a,1]
with relative inverse-root error

  |sqrt(x)p(x)-1|<=eta, x in [a,1].                         (5)

Section 5 constructs it with degree ~O(sqrt(H/h)), using arithmetic and
square roots only. Define R=J^-T p(D), apply R implicitly, and let

  E_t=R^T K_t R,
  Fbar=R^-T R^-1=J p(D)^(-2) J^T.

The last matrices are ANALYTICAL definitions; neither Fbar nor p(D)^(-1)
is formed. From (5),

  -4eta F_h <= Fbar-F_h <=4eta F_h,
  ||Fbar-F_h||<=24eta t.

Together with (3), this gives safe bounds

  21ht I <= Fbar-K_t <=43ht I, Fbar>=15ht I,
  spectrum(E_t) subset [-2,1].                             (6)

For the final line, write E_t=I-R^T(Fbar-K_t)R and use
43/15<3. This construction supplies a FIXED SYMMETRIC operator at each
threshold. It does not assume that adaptively stopped CG is a linear map,
and it does not pass inaccurate solves to Lanczos without error control.

A single E_t action costs

  ~O(I+J_h sqrt(H/h)).

The original actual-witness threshold argument applies using (6). If
q is a returned actual Rayleigh value with additive error tau and x=Ry,
then q<=tau implies

  x^T K_t x/(x^T G x) <=64 tau h t,

while q>=0 implies lambda<=t+46 tau h t. The minor change from 45 to
46 does not affect the original choice tau=e_*/(512h), e_*=theta/8:
(3/2)(64+46)/512<1. Negative and positive signs retain exact inertia
meaning because R is invertible. At most logarithmically many adaptive
tests return a vector z with z^T M M^T z>=(1-theta/8)lambda.

There are ~O(sqrt(h/theta)) E_t actions, so their cost is

  ~O(I sqrt(h/theta)+J_h sqrt(H/theta)+m^omega).

The relative-Nystrom lemma in the main paper converts the final Rayleigh
vector to a spectral rank-one approximation; final factor actions cost I,
and explicit expansion costs n p. This establishes (1), subject to the
bracket and polynomial constructions proved next.

4. A fine bracket without true-factor refinement
-----------------------------------------------
Let lambda_h=||A_h B_h||_2^2. The fine OSE event gives

  (1-h)^2 lambda <= lambda_h <=(1+h)^2 lambda.               (7)

The already formed P_H,Q_H are valid 2H-relative Gram proxies for the
FINE factors A_h,B_h. For example their Loewner ratios to P_h lie between
(1-H)/(1+h) and (1+H)/(1-h), both within [1-2H,1+2H]. The same holds
for Q_h. The main paper's coarse-proxy Rayleigh primitive can therefore
be applied to the fine factors, with distortion 2H<=1/16 and requested
Rayleigh parameter h. It uses the supplied cores; it need not resketch.
Its true actions now cost J_h, rather than I. Obtain an actual s_h with

  (1-h/8)lambda_h <= s_h <=lambda_h

in cost ~O(J_h sqrt(H/h)+m^omega+m^2/sqrt(H)). The exact squared
fine-product Rayleigh value is recomputed through A_h,B_h.
By (7), a bracket for lambda is

  [s_h/(1+h)^2,
   s_h/((1-h/8)(1-h)^2)].                                 (8)

Intersect it with [ell_0,b_0]. On all good events the intersection is
nonempty and still contains lambda. Its lower endpoint is at least

  (1-h/8)(1-h)^2/(1+h)^2 lambda
       >=(1-33h/8)lambda.

This proves the bracket property used in Section 3. The cost in this
section is absorbed by the J_h sqrt(H/theta) term because theta<=h.
The fine-proxy primitive uses a different inflation constant corresponding
to distortion 2H; this does not change F_H=(1+32H)tI-Q_0 used in the
final true-product threshold tests.

5. An explicit uniform inverse-root polynomial
----------------------------------------------
For D with spectrum in [a,1], a>0, set

  rho=(1-sqrt(a))/(1+sqrt(a)),
  c=(1+sqrt(a))^2/4,
  T=(1+a-2D)/(1-a).

Then spectrum(T) is in [-1,1] and

  D=c(I-2rho T+rho^2 I).

The Legendre generating function gives

  D^(-1/2)=c^(-1/2) sum_{j>=0} rho^j P_j(T),

where P_j are the ordinary Legendre polynomials and |P_j(t)|<=1 for
t in [-1,1]. The latter bound follows, for example, from the elementary
Laplace integral for P_j(cos(phi)): the integrand
(cos(phi)+i sin(phi)cos(psi))^j has modulus at most one. These integral
representations justify the identity; the algorithm evaluates no integral.

Truncate at degree k. Since c^(-1/2)/(1-rho)=1/sqrt(a), the operator
norm of the tail is at most rho^(k+1)/sqrt(a). As ||D^(1/2)||<=1,
this is also a relative error bound in (5). It suffices to choose k with

  rho^(k+1)<=eta sqrt(a),
  k=O(a^(-1/2) log(1/(eta sqrt(a)))).

The stopping index can be found by multiplying rho repeatedly; no
logarithm oracle is needed. Evaluate the polynomial on a vector using

  P_0(T)v=v, P_1(T)v=Tv,
  (j+1)P_{j+1}(T)v=(2j+1)T P_j(T)v-j P_{j-1}(T)v,

and accumulate the weighted sum. There are k D-actions and O(k) scalar
coefficient operations, plus O(km) vector work. All constants use arithmetic,
division and the allowed square root. This avoids coefficient-construction
cost and removes the need for a large-degree fallback. If a=1, use p=1.

The polynomial is a mathematically controlled replacement for an inverse
square root, not a claim that a raw polynomial recurrence is numerically
stable at arbitrary precision in standard floating point.
Primary formulas: NIST DLMF 18.12.11 (generating function), and 18.14.1
with Jacobi parameters alpha=beta=0 (the unit bound):
https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.12.E11
https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.14.E1

6. A strict improvement of the currently proved output regime
------------------------------------------------------------
Use m as the base and choose

  n=p=N=m^(59/50), theta=m^(-11/25),
  H=m^(-7/100), h=m^(-17/200).

All parameter inequalities hold for sufficiently large m. Fine sketch rows
have exponent 117/100<59/50, so J_h has exponent 217/100. Using the
same rectangular multiplication bound as the main paper, construction
exponents are

  coarse Gram: 3362/1425 ~=2.359298,
  true actions: 109/50+(11/25-17/200)/2=943/400=2.3575,
  fine-proxy work: 217/100+(11/25-7/100)/2=471/200=2.355,
  square core: 9/4, coarse norm estimate: 407/200,
  output: 59/25=2.36.

Hence construction is o(N^2) with a sufficiently small fixed exponent
slack, and total explicit-output time is O(N^2). Confidence can be fixed
or retained in logarithms. This strengthens the one-level quadratic-output
instance from theta exponent 25/59 ~=0.423729 to 11/25=0.44, for exactly
the same input dimensions and unrestricted general factors.

It is a strict improvement of the OPTIMIZED one-level tradeoff, not merely
of a poorly tuned one-level parameter. Output-optimal one-level true actions
would require a coarse distortion exponent at least

  11/25-2(59/50-1)=2/25=0.08.

But its Gram setup at that exponent is already

  2+4/25+1/[4(1+4/25)]=54/25+25/116>59/25.

Any smaller exponent makes the one-level true-action term exceed output;
any larger exponent makes its setup still larger. Clipping the sketch at
the original rows only restores the exact-Gram baseline. That baseline
and ordinary Krylov have respective bounds N^(7056/3481+o(1)) and
N^(120/59+o(1)) here, both above N^2.

7. Fixed-depth multilevel theorem
--------------------------------
The recursion closes if all levels use slightly larger, uniform inflation
constants. The independently valid two-level theorem (1) does not depend on
this extension. What follows is a full proof derivation, subject to separate
review of its constants; it makes no claim for growing recursion depth.

Fix an integer L>=1 INDEPENDENT of dimensions and accuracy. Suppose

  0<theta<=h_L, h_0<=1/1024, h_j<=h_{j-1}/8, j=1,...,L.

Compute explicit h_0-Grams and matrix-free h_j-sketch factors at every other
level, and whiten the original A-side with the h_0-Gram. Define K_j as in
Section 2 using level j's two factors; K_0=tI-Q_0. For j>=0 put

  F_j(t)=K_j(t)+128h_j t I.

On the joint OSE event, the algorithm below obtains the same spectral
rank-one guarantee with total cost

  ~O_L(I+G_{h_0}+m^omega+m^2/sqrt(h_0)
       +I sqrt(h_L/theta)
       +sum_{j=1}^L J_{h_j} sqrt(h_{j-1}/theta)+np).          (9)

The suppressed logarithm exponent and constants may depend on FIXED L.
Products of polynomial degrees, recurrence constants and logarithms are
not silently treated as constants for input-dependent growing L.

7.1 Uniform preconditioner induction

For the ANALYSIS, call a threshold level-j valid if

  |t-lambda|<=16h_j lambda.                                (10)

The algorithm does not need lambda to check this condition: the brackets
constructed below imply it for every threshold where it is used. Validity
at level j implies validity at all earlier levels.

On this window, t>=(63/64)lambda and the true metric has norm at most
1024/1023. Thus ||Q'||<=2t, ||K_j-K||<=10h_j t as before, and

  K>=-17h_j tI, ||F_j||<=4t,
  118h_j tI<=F_j-K<=138h_j tI, F_j>=100h_j tI.              (11)

For j=0 the same error bound follows from the coarse whitening formula.
Indeed the estimate -17 follows from
||G||*16h_j/(1-16h_j)<17h_j. The norm estimate uses
||K||<=||G||t+||G||^2||Q'|| and 138h_j<=138/1024.

Set R_0=J_0^-T, where F_0=J_0 J_0^T. Inductively suppose R_{j-1}
is an implicitly applicable invertible map and

  Fbar_{j-1}=R_{j-1}^-T R_{j-1}^-1,
  117h_{j-1}tI<=Fbar_{j-1}-K<=139h_{j-1}tI,
  Fbar_{j-1}>=99h_{j-1}tI.                               (12)

The base case satisfies these slightly relaxed bounds by (11). At a
level-j valid threshold, define

  D_j=R_{j-1}^T F_j R_{j-1}.

The differences in (11),(12), and h_j<=h_{j-1}/8, give

  0<=Fbar_{j-1}-F_j<=139h_{j-1}tI,
  Fbar_{j-1}<=F_j+[139h_{j-1}/(100h_j)]F_j.

Therefore

  [h_j/(2h_{j-1})]I<=D_j<=I.                             (13)

Apply the Section 5 polynomial with a=h_j/(2h_{j-1}) and eta=h_j/1000,
and define R_j=R_{j-1}p_j(D_j). Relative polynomial error yields

  ||Fbar_j-F_j||<=4eta||F_j||<=16h_j t/1000.

Consequently (12) holds with j in place of j-1, including Fbar_j>=99h_j tI.
This proves the induction, uniformly over each validity window. In particular

  spectrum(R_j^T K R_j) subset [-1,1],

since 1-139/99>-1. The maps are fixed symmetric-polynomial constructions
at each threshold; no nonlinear inner solve is passed to outer Lanczos.

7.2 Proxy-only bracket refinement at every level

The original h_0-Gram norm bracket [ell_0,b_0] contains lambda and, for
h_0<=1/1024, lies in [(1-6h_0)lambda,(1+6h_0)lambda].
Assume an available bracket [ell_{j-1},b_{j-1}] contains lambda and lies
within that same six-times-distortion window at level j-1. Let
lambda_j=||A_j B_j||_2^2. Its initial bracket is

  [(1-h_j)^2 ell_{j-1}, (1+h_j)^2 b_{j-1}].               (14)

All thresholds in (14) are within 8h_{j-1}lambda of lambda, and hence
are level-(j-1) valid. To estimate lambda_j, use actual-witness threshold
Lanczos on R_{j-1}^T K_j R_{j-1}, with TRUE mass G_j and TRUE product
being A_j B_j. There are no original-factor actions in this step.

Because ||K_j-K||<=10h_j t, its effective-preconditioner difference lies
between 115h_{j-1}tI and 141h_{j-1}tI. Bound (12) then implies that
the preconditioned operator has spectrum in [-1,1]. Also
I/2<=G_j<=2I, with the actual sharper bounds exceeding .99 below and
less than 1.01 above. For q<=tau the standard witness calculation gives

  x^T K_j x/(x^T G_j x)<=256tau h_{j-1}t;

for q>=0 it gives lambda_j<=t+256tau h_{j-1}t. Thus the same global
sign/uncertain-witness bisection argument works with tau=e/(2048h_{j-1}).
Take e=h_j/8. It returns an actual fine-product Rayleigh value s_j with

  (1-h_j/8)lambda_j<=s_j<=lambda_j

in ~sqrt(h_{j-1}/h_j) preconditioned actions. Unit physical fine-product
witnesses use A_j W x/sqrt(x^T G_j x). This matters: a Rayleigh quotient
using the original G would estimate the wrong product.
For completeness, the sharper calculations are below 160 for the witness
and below 143 for the uncertain upper bound, using G_j>=.99I; the relaxed
256 constants allow a common statement. All such thresholds have
t<=1.5lambda_j, so an uncertain witness loses at most
(3/2)(256+256)e/2048<e. At a final lower-bracket call of relative width
e/4, the deficit is at most (e/4+256e/2048)lambda_j<e lambda_j.

Translate back using (7):

  ell_j=s_j/(1+h_j)^2,
  b_j=s_j/[(1-h_j/8)(1-h_j)^2].                           (15)

This bracket contains lambda and lies within
[(1-5h_j)lambda,(1+5h_j)lambda]. It therefore satisfies the six-times
window needed for the next induction step. One may intersect it with prior
true-lambda brackets, but intersection is not needed for the window proof.
This establishes all required validity windows rather than assuming nested
brackets happen to be suitable for the next fine product.

After level L, apply the original true-product threshold witness primitive
using R_L on bracket (15), with requested Rayleigh error theta/8. Bounds
(12) give the same guarantees, with only absolute constant changes.
Finish through the main paper's relative-Nystrom lemma and explicit output.

7.3 Cost and failure accounting

Let c_j be the cost of one R_j or R_j^T application at a fixed threshold.
The explicit coarse inverse has c_0=O(m^2). The degree in (13) is
~sqrt(h_{j-1}/h_j). Each D_j action uses one F_j action and two previous
maps. Therefore, for fixed L,

  c_j=~O_L((J_{h_j}+c_{j-1})sqrt(h_{j-1}/h_j)),
  c_L=~O_L(m^2 sqrt(h_0/h_L)
           +sum_{j=1}^L J_{h_j}sqrt(h_{j-1}/h_L)).

Final true threshold tests cost ~sqrt(h_L/theta)(I+c_L). Proxy-only
bracket level j costs ~sqrt(h_{j-1}/h_j)(J_{h_j}+c_{j-1}), the same
order as c_j. Each c_j is absorbed in the final displayed sum because
theta<=h_L<=h_j; the fixed-L sum of refinements changes only constants
and logarithms. The propagated m^2 term is absorbed by the first J term,
as J_{h_1}>=m^2. Coarse factorizations are performed at logarithmically
many thresholds per level, costing ~O_L(m^omega) in total. This proves (9).

For any adaptive norm/threshold call, condition on the fixed initial OSE
events and all previous outcomes, and use a fresh random Lanczos start.
Allocate failure probability by a union bound over L+1 OSE pairs and all
bounded-in-advance primitive calls. Only logarithmic costs change. Zero
support/product exits and bounded budgets are handled as in the main paper.

7.4 Correct balancing and a stronger fixed-depth example

Let N=m^(1+d), theta=m^(-b), h_j=m^(-a_j), with all fine sketch widths
strictly below the original outer dimension. For a common construction
exponent E=2+b/2+u, the work terms in (9) impose

  a_j<=u/2+a_{j-1}/4, a_L>=2(d-u).

Balancing all propagated terms gives

  u=(6d-3*4^(-L)a_0)/(8-2*4^(-L)),

which tends to 3d/4 as fixed depth increases. Thus any fixed exponent
slack above the limiting construction envelope

  E_infty=2+3d/4+b/2

is attainable by choosing a sufficiently large but FIXED L, subject also
to coarse setup and output. This is an infimum over fixed-depth bounds,
not a claim that arbitrary growing depth preserves the suppressed factors.
Local balancing of only the newest level can leave an early implicit level
dominant; the global recurrence accounts for every propagated cost.

A concrete L=2 example needs no limiting argument:

  N=m^(59/50), theta=m^(-56/125),
  h_0=m^(-7/100), h_1=m^(-17/200), h_2=m^(-89/1000).

Construction exponents in m are

  coarse Gram: 3362/1425 ~=2.359298,
  level-1 propagated work: 2359/1000=2.359,
  level-2 propagated work: 4719/2000=2.3595,
  final true actions: 4719/2000=2.3595,
  explicit output: 59/25=2.36.

Thus construction is N^(2-1/2360+o(1)) and total time is O(N^2).
This improves the two-level theorem (1) at the same dimensions: making
its action and fine-work terms output bounded at b=56/125 would require
a_0>=5b-12d=2/25, whose Gram setup exceeds output as in Section 6.
All claims concern established UPPER bounds, not algorithmic lower bounds.

For the original high-accuracy parameters d=1/8,b=1, the limiting
construction exponent here is 83/32 in m, worse than exact Gram. Thus
even the multilevel extension does not solve that stronger target.

Diagnostic, separate from proof: a transient numpy sweep with seed2026111408
generated 100 random SPD factor-Gram pairs in dimensions 2 through 10 and
independent synthetic relative proxies at three distortion levels. It made
1000 effective-preconditioner checks, including expanded fine-product norm
bracket endpoints and recursive inverse-root polynomials. All displayed
spectral/Loewner/bracket assertions passed; the smallest margin inside the
normalized [117,139] difference bounds exceeded 9.07. The largest polynomial
relative error was 7.54e-10. This checks proof inequalities, not a full AMM
implementation, statistical SRHT performance, or numerical stability.

8. Primary-comparator and novelty scope
--------------------------------------
The current one-level paper supplies the OSE, support/whitening, actual-witness
threshold primitive and Nystrom finishing lemma. Its primary references are
retained there. This note introduces no new query lower-bound claim.

Derezinski--Musco--Yang, Faster Linear Systems and Matrix Norm Approximation
via Multi-level Sketched Preconditioning (SODA 2025), arXiv:2405.05865:
https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05865v2
https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10581694
Their Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 concern nonnegative-ridge systems based on explicit
rectangular inputs or explicit SPD kernels. Their norm theorem concerns
finite Schatten exponents below 2. The checked theorem does not directly
supply the negative-Gram shifted threshold, the present factored action costs,
or this spectral LRA output regime. Nonetheless recursive sketched inverse
application is established prior art; a novel-method claim would be wrong.

Classical polynomial inverse roots and congruence preconditioning are also
standard. The potentially new result is the specific spectral-product cost
bound (1), its fine-product bracket, and the resulting parameter regime.
No checked primary theorem currently subsumes that bound, but the global
literature audit remains open. Specialized covariance algorithms can remain
faster on B=A^T and are not universally dominated by this result.
