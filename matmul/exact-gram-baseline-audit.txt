Exact-Gram spectral baseline in the finite exact-real model
===========================================================
Independent audit, 2026-11-14. This verifies the parent's proposed
comparator, without an exact SVD, eigenvalue-root or eigenvector oracle.
Only this dedicated audit file was created. It is a baseline construction,
not a global novelty claim.

Result
------
For fixed arbitrary real A in R^(n by m), B in R^(m by p), target
1<=r<=m, and theta>0, there is a probability-one algorithm returning
width-at-most-r factors and explicitly forming C with

  ||AB-C||_2 <= (1+theta) sigma_(r+1)(AB).

In the exact field/comparison/positive-square-root/Gaussian model, its
uniform arithmetic cost is

  ~O(G_exact + m^(omega+zeta) + Acal(r)
                     + np + T_MM(n,r,p)),

where G_exact=T_MM(m,n,m)+T_MM(m,p,m). The tilde here hides dimension
and accuracy logarithms only. In particular the threshold search has
O(log(m/theta)) calls for 0<theta<=1, not O(log(m)/theta) calls.
There is no hidden input-condition or eigenvalue-ratio logarithm.
The finite caps apply even on exceptional zero-pivot outcomes, which
may return zero factors and have probability zero for each fixed input.

1. Exact support reduction and its cost
--------------------------------------
Form P=A^T A and Qraw=B B^T. Use exact rank revelation and core
Gram/Cholesky algebra, as in the reviewed manuscript's support reduction,
to obtain W in R^(m by k), Lc in R^(k by m), such that

  Z=A W, R=Lc B, M=AB=Z R, Z^T Z=I_k,
  H=R R^T=Lc Qraw Lc^T is positive definite, k=rank(M).

For example first whiten range(P) exactly using W0, L0=W0^dagger;
then restrict to the range of L0 Qraw L0^T. The equality A=Z Lc
is unnecessary; the product identity M=Z Lc B suffices. All algebra
after P,Qraw are formed has dimensions at most m and costs
~O(m^(omega+zeta)). Neither tall Z nor wide R must be materialized.

If k=0, write zero. If k<=r, form the factors Z,R using width k
factor products and return M exactly. Otherwise put j=r+1<=k and
lambda_*=lambda_j(H)>0. Nonzero eigenvalues of M M^T are those of H.

2. A condition-free positive upper threshold
-------------------------------------------
Let rho=e_j(H)/e_(j-1)(H), using exact characteristic coefficients.
Positivity of H ensures nonzero denominator. The elementary coefficient
ratio inequalities give rho/k<=lambda_*<=k*rho. Use this positive
bracket. At its midpoint s, the characteristic polynomial of H-sI
and exact Descartes sign variations count eigenvalues strictly above s.
If that count is at least j, set the lower endpoint to s; otherwise
set the upper endpoint to s. Equality at lambda_* is safely assigned
to the upper branch. Repeated eigenvalues cause no difficulty.

Stop once u-l<=(theta/100)l. Then

  lambda_*<=u<=(1+theta/100)lambda_*.

The initial ratio is at most k^2, so at most
ceil(log2(100*k^2/theta))+1 bisections suffice for theta<=1. Scalar
halving determines a safe cap with allowed field operations and comparisons.
Each query can recompute the shifted characteristic polynomial in
~O(k^(omega+zeta)); no root isolation or ordered eigenvalue is computed.
Alternatively polynomial shifting of previously obtained coefficients
costs O(k^2) per query. The simpler recomputation bound suffices.

Draw a fresh standard Gaussian scalar g and set

  t=u*(1+(theta/100)*g^2/(1+g^2)).

It satisfies u<=t<u*(1+theta/100). Its distribution has no atoms:
for each fixed t-value in its range there are at most two corresponding
g-values, with a possible singleton at g=0. Conditional on the already
computed u, the finitely many eigenvalues of H are therefore avoided
with probability one. Consequently K=tI-H is nonsingular almost surely.
Since t>=lambda_*, its negative index is at most r.

3. Gaussian congruence permits unpivoted fast LDL^T
--------------------------------------------------
Draw an independent k-by-k matrix S with independent standard Gaussian
entries. It is invertible almost surely. Conditional on any fixed
nonsingular symmetric K, every leading principal minor of

  C_S=S^T K S

is a polynomial in the entries of S which is not identically zero.
Indeed, take S to be an orthogonal eigenbasis of K for this existence
argument; then C_S is diagonal with all diagonal entries nonzero,
and all its leading principal minors are nonzero. A nonzero real
polynomial vanishes on a Lebesgue-null set. A finite union therefore
shows that all these leading minors are nonzero almost surely.

Thus the unpivoted symmetric elimination

  S^T K S = L D L^T

exists with L unit lower triangular and D diagonal with no zero entries.
This property is stronger than nonsingularity of the full matrix and
is exactly what the recursive no-pivot algorithm needs.

For a balanced block partition, recursively factor C11=L11 D1 L11^T,
compute X=C21 L11^(-T) D1^(-1), and recursively factor the Schur
complement C22-X D1 X^T. Then

  L = [[L11,0],[X,L22]], D=diag(D1,D2).

Every leading minor of that Schur complement is nonzero: the corresponding
leading minor of C_S is det(C11) times it. Hence the induction's required
prefix property propagates. Triangular inversions, diagonal scaling and
Schur products cost O(k^(omega+zeta)). The balanced recurrence

  T(k)<=2T(ceil(k/2))+O(k^(omega+zeta))

has that same asymptotic bound. The usual adjusted unequal subproblem
sizes handle odd k. Scalar pivots are checked before any division.
No positivity assumption is imposed on K or on D.

Computing S^(-T) has the same fast bound. Exact rank-revealing inversion
can be used; alternatively Gaussian S also has all its leading minors
nonzero almost surely, so recursive unpivoted LU/block inversion suffices.
The Gaussian draw and exact sign tests cost only O(k^2) and O(k).

4. The orientation and physical complement
------------------------------------------
Let E_- collect the standard-coordinate columns whose D entries are
negative, and define

  V=S^(-T) L E_-.

Its columns are independent and their number is at most r, by Sylvester
inertia. For any y with V^T y=0, put w=L^T S^(-1)y. Then E_-^T w=0
and

  y^T K y = w^T D w >= 0.

This proves the proposed orientation exactly. For a physical vector
x orthogonal to range(ZV), let y=Z^T x. Then V^T y=0 and
||y||<=||x|| because Z is an isometry. Therefore

  x^T M M^T x = y^T H y
              <= t ||y||^2 <= t ||x||^2.

It follows that, for U an orthonormal basis of range(ZV),

  ||(I-UU^T)M||_2^2 <= t
    < (1+theta/100)^2 lambda_*.

Thus the spectral residual is at most
(1+theta/100) sigma_(r+1)(M), which is stronger than required.
The method needs no ordered eigenvectors or subspace eigengap.

5. Final factors, complete costs, and exceptional outcomes
---------------------------------------------------------
First orthonormalize V in the k-dimensional core, obtaining V_o.
Since Z^T Z=I, U=Z V_o is then already physically orthonormal.
Compute it as A(W V_o), without materializing all of Z. This costs
O(Acal(r)+m^(omega+zeta)); computing R_p=M^T U through A,B has
the same block bound. Return C=U R_p^T and form every entry, charging
np+T_MM(n,r,p). Core inversion/congruence/LDL work stays within
~O(m^(omega+zeta)). The exact-Gram setup dominates reading the input.

For fixed A,B all undesired Gaussian events have probability zero.
Check zero pivots, rank and coefficient denominators before division;
an exceptional failed check returns zero factors within the same cap.
There are no retries, representation-length loops or input-dependent
precision searches. Almost-sure success implies success at least1-delta
for every supplied delta>0 without repeating the procedure.

The model still permits arbitrarily large or tiny exact real numbers.
Leading minors and D pivots can be arbitrarily close to zero; coefficient
signs may require arbitrarily high precision; S or the original factors
can be ill-conditioned. No floating-point, bit-complexity or stable
implementation statement follows. These are precisely the same exact-real
qualifications as the candidate theorem, so no SVD convention mismatch
is needed for the Gram comparison.

6. Consequence for the named endpoint comparison
-----------------------------------------------
For n=p=N=m^(1+d), r<=sqrt(m), theta=m^(-5d/2), current rectangular
multiplication gives

  G_exact=m^(2+d+1/[4(1+d)]+o(1)).

The core cost m^(9/4+o(1)), width-r factor products m^(2+d+o(1)),
and explicit output N^(2+o(1)) are all smaller for
1/8<d<(sqrt(2)-1)/2. Accuracy contributes only logarithms. Therefore
the complete exact-field Gram baseline has precisely the named exponent,
including at the optimized d_star. This removes the need to regard a
dense SVD or exact eigenroot as an uncharged primitive in that comparator.
