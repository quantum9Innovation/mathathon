\paragraph{Model.}
Fix $b\ge4$. Take a uniform independent $N$-lift of $K_{3,b}$.
Name the $b$ degree-three base vertices $L_1,\ldots,L_b$ and the
three degree-$b$ vertices $R_1,R_2,R_3$. Each old fiber has size $N$.
Between each pair $(L_i,R_j)$ place an independent uniform perfect
matching. There are no internal edges, and the resulting graph is simple
for every $N$. Suppose a genie reveals the three $R$ fibers, with their
names. We count alternative named $L$-fiber partitions, leaving all
revealed $R$ fibers fixed.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:count}
Along positive integers $N$ divisible by $b!$, the expected number $Z_N$
of alternative balanced lift projections described below is exactly
\[
 \E Z_N=
 \left[\frac{(N/b)!^{\,b}}{N!}\right]^{2b}
 \left[\frac{N!}{(N/b!)!^{\,b!}}\right]^3.
\]
In particular, with natural logarithms,
\[
 \log\E Z_N=N\gamma_b+O_b(\log N),\qquad
 \gamma_b=3\log(b!)-2b\log b.
\]
For every fixed $b\ge12$, $\gamma_b>0$, so this expectation is
exponential in $N$. Every counted projection respects the observable
degree colors, keeps all genie-revealed fibers fixed, and has exactly
$N$ vertices in every new fiber.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use candidate labels $1,\ldots,b$ on the degree-three side. In each
old $L_i$ fiber put exactly $N/b$ vertices of each candidate label.
There are
\[
 \left[\frac{N!}{(N/b)!^{\,b}}\right]^b
\]
ways to assign these root labels.
At every vertex of each revealed $R_j$ fiber, require that its ordered
neighbors in the old fibers $L_1,\ldots,L_b$ have all $b$ distinct
candidate labels. Thus its ordered neighbor-label list is a permutation
of $[b]$. In each $R_j$ fiber prescribe every one of these $b!$
permutations exactly $N/b!$ times. There are
\[
 \left[\frac{N!}{(N/b!)!^{\,b!}}\right]^3
\]
such assignments of ordered stars.

For one fixed old edge $(L_i,R_j)$, every candidate label is requested
by exactly $N/b$ vertices on its $R_j$ side and occurs at exactly $N/b$
vertices on its $L_i$ side. The probability its uniform matching obeys
all requests is $(N/b)!^b/N!$. All $3b$ matching factors are independent.
Multiplication gives the displayed exact expectation. There is no extra
star multiplicity: for a fixed graph and candidate root labeling, the
ordered neighbor-label list at every $R_j$ vertex is uniquely determined.

Every degree-three vertex still has one neighbor in each revealed
$R_j$ fiber. Every revealed $R_j$ vertex has one neighbor of each new
$L$ label. The new classes therefore give the exact same quotient
$K_{3,b}$. Each new $L$ class contains $N/b$ vertices from each of $b$
old fibers, hence has size $N$; it is not an old-fiber point map.
Stirling's formula gives the stated exponential rate.

Finally $(12!)^3>12^{24}$, so $\gamma_{12}>0$. For $b\ge11$,
\[
 \gamma_{b+1}-\gamma_b
 =\log(b+1)-2b\log(1+1/b)
 >\log(b+1)-2>0.
\]
Thus the rate is positive for all $b\ge12$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The corresponding legal entropy law.}
The same construction gives an exact positive phase in the usual
ordered-star entropy functional
\[
 \Psi=\sum_{\text{old vertices }i}N H(P_i)
          -\sum_{\text{old edges }ij}N H(\pi_{ij}).
\]
For each old $L_i$, take its candidate root label uniform on $[b]$;
its three neighbor labels are the deterministic names $R_1,R_2,R_3$.
Then $H(P_{L_i})=\log b$.
For each old $R_j$, the root label is its fixed revealed name and the
ordered $b$ neighbor labels are a uniform random permutation of $[b]$.
Then $H(P_{R_j})=\log(b!)$.
For every old edge, the pair law has one uniform and one deterministic
coordinate, so $H(\pi_{ij})=\log b$. These pair laws reverse correctly
across each matching, and every supported star obeys the exact integer
one-hot equation for the candidate quotient $K_{3,b}$. Hence
\[
 \Psi=N[b\log b+3\log(b!)-3b\log b]=N\gamma_b.
\]
There is no mixing of vertices with different degrees. Ordinary color
refinement sees exactly the two degree classes throughout every lift,
and all candidate labels refine those colors. Even keeping the three
genie-revealed fibers separate leaves this positive phase unchanged.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
For $b\ge12$, neither a universal nonpositive one-hot entropy bound nor
a polynomial expected count of all verified balanced projections can
hold for every fixed nonregular base of minimum degree at least three.
The theorem supplies an exact expectation lower bound, not a statement
that the number of alternatives concentrates or that recovery fails
with high probability. A posterior counting argument in the parent
session separately addresses information-theoretic recovery. Historical
priority has not been established.
