on theta. The nonlinear kernel pencil has K_theta=diag(1,-theta),
so ||K_theta||<=1 for EVERY |theta|<=1. The previously checked kernel
and product localization estimates therefore have constants uniform
in both the coefficient modulus and its phase.

With ell=sqrt(L), degree ceil(C1 L), and I_L extending to envelope
depth (K+1)L, the discarded rank is O_(alpha,delta,K)(L^(3/2)).
The norm error is O(exp(-(K+3)L)) after choosing C1 large enough.
The argument first bounds Wtilde=W-small_error, so a possibly huge
norm of the finite-rank approximation is never multiplied into this

On a periodic cell the two scalar singular channels retain amplitudes
1 and |theta| separately. The coefficient-uniform proof must NOT

2. UNIFORM PHASE VOLUME, INCLUDING ZERO EFFECTIVE DEPTH
------------------------------------------------------
At threshold 4 exp(-xL), the two effective depths are xL and
xL+log|theta|, with x in [0,K]. After taking positive parts, both
lie between zero and KL. Each is reduced by only C ell+C0 in the
cell lower bound. For any h in [0,KL], changing h by O(ell) changes
its squared envelope area by at most O_K(L ell). The Riemann error
has the same uniform order; the integer-mode error is O_K(L/ell).

Thus the estimate does not deteriorate when x approaches zero, when
the second depth crosses zero, or when log|theta| is arbitrarily
negative. For theta=0 the second positive part is zero; retaining its
possible -1 error only weakens the lower. The interval I_L contains
the triangular envelopes for all these depths simultaneously.

The resulting model count is

  b_delta L^2[x^2+(x-u_(theta,L))_+^2]-O(L^(3/2)),
  b_delta=(1/(2pi^2))[1/alpha+1/(beta+delta)],
  u_(theta,L)=-log|theta|/L.

The small norm error is below exp(-xL) uniformly on x in [0,K],
and the factor 4 supplies a strict margin. Rank perturbation costs
only O(L^(3/2)). This proves P in the bounded-interval model.

One finite transfer works for the entire coefficient disk and all

3. THE UNIFORM MOMENT LAW U
---------------------------
The Schatten power triangle gives, for 0<s<=1,

  H_s(kappa g_+ +lambda g_-)
     <=(|kappa|^s+|lambda|^s) H_s(g_+),

because the two branch corrections are adjoints. The one-branch limit
therefore gives a genuinely UNIFORM upper, with no coefficient-dependent
constant. Every nonzero coefficient pair can be reduced to
g_+ +theta g_-, |theta|<=1, by exact scalar scaling and, when necessary,
reflection of both Hermitian inputs. These operations preserve the
ratio in U. The denominator 1+|theta|^s is always at least one.

Apply P to the same finite pair at L=1/s. Its input power denominator
t_L^(alpha s)<=1 only helps the moment lower. Layer cake on positive
logarithmic thresholds yields (7). Singular values above one add a
nonnegative contribution and need not be controlled for this lower.

For every u in [0,infinity], including infinity,

  integral_0^infinity exp(-x)[x^2+(x-u)_+^2]dx
        =2(1+exp(-u)).

The omitted tail after K is bounded by 2 int_K^infinity exp(-x)x^2 dx,
uniformly in u. Thus division by 1+|theta|^s gives (9), uniformly over
the entire coefficient disk. First take s->0 at fixed delta,K, then
K->infinity, then delta->0. This proves U, including superexponentially
vanishing or identically zero smaller branches. No compactness argument
or supremum-integral interchange is required.

4. THE UNIFORM ACCURACY LAW A
-----------------------------
Separate one-branch quadratures at depths

  h_+=L+C_d,
  h_-=[L+log|theta|]_+ +C_d

give two weighted errors summing to at most exp(-L), after a fixed
choice of C_d. The same C_d passes the large-depth threshold even
when the second effective depth is zero. For theta=0 its quadrature
is omitted. For every other theta the weighted second error is at
most exp(-L-C_d), including theta much smaller than exp(-L).

The depth shifts add only O(L+1) to the sum of squares, and both
effective depths are <=L. Hence the source's rank bound (12), with
uniform O(L^(7/4)+1) remainder, is valid. It is a sum of ranks of
two quadratures, not an assumed conversion from a moment bound.

P with K=1 gives the uniform lower. Normalize BOTH finite endpoints
only after count preservation, so homogeneity increases singular values
and imposes input norm one. Direct sums give fixed rank r. The two
uniform bounds converge to A after the strip and delta limits in

The moment weight exp(-u) and the accuracy weight (1-tau)_+^2 are
different for the stated reason: they integrate or sample different
parts of the same shifted quadratic profile.

5. THE SOURCE'S MIXED-POLE G AND PERTURBATION ROUTE ARE VALID
------------------------------------------------------------
For a distinct nonreal pole set with no conjugate pair, the positive
weight w=prod(t-z_j)(t-conj z_j) has degree 2q. A q+1-node Gaussian
rule for mu/w preserves mass and all m-values, by exactness at degrees
2q and 2q-1. Both column Gram divided differences then have nonzero
denominators even across half-planes. Woodbury supplies the same
diagonal coefficients. The two independent span isometries give equal
quadrature singular values. A common congruence is not required.

The source's horizontal perturbation also works. Before perturbation
the two branch pole sets are purely imaginary; moving every positive
pole by the same nonzero real h removes every exact conjugate collision
without changing imaginary parts or same-half-plane distinctness.
The resolvent identity bounds the change by |h|/|Im z|^2 per
resolvent, uniformly for every Hermitian background. The finite weighted
sum therefore admits an arbitrarily small common error bound, for both
the original and the later compressed pair. This is not circular:
h is chosen using only the quadrature, before the compressed background.

Both original and compressed unit-input pairs obey the same unperturbed
branch quadrature bounds. The two approximation budgets plus exact
perturbed-quadrature singular matching prove (17). The very small
separation can be ill-conditioned, but no numerical stability or
arithmetic-cost theorem is being claimed for this realization.

6. VERIFIED STRONGER REPLACEMENT: EXACT q-STATE GAUSSIAN REALIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Here is a complete direct proof, found during this independent audit
and separately checked by the coordinator.

Let A=A*, ||v||=1, B=A+vv*, and let z_1,...,z_q be ANY distinct
nonreal poles, now permitting exact conjugate pairs. Define mu and w
as before. Take q-node positive ordinary Gaussian quadrature for
dnu=dmu/w, exact through degree 2q-1. If the measure has <=q support
points, take its support exactly. Reweight nodes t_k and weights a_k
by b_k=a_k w(t_k)>0 and define

  A'=diag(t_k), v'=(sqrt(b_k)), B'=A'+v'v'^*.

The m-values are preserved because w/(t-z_j) and w/(t-conj z_j)
are polynomials of degree 2q-1. To preserve the Grams, use their
integrands DIRECTLY, rather than divided differences:

  w(t)/[(t-conj z_i)(t-z_j)],
  w(t)/[(t-z_i)(t-conj z_j)].

These are polynomials of degree 2q-2 for EVERY i,j. At a conjugate
collision the needed double factor is already present in w, once
from each of the corresponding two poles. Polynomial exactness
therefore preserves both Grams without any derivative or limiting
divided-difference evaluation. The same Woodbury/scalar argument
gives EXACT equality of quadrature singular values in <=q states.

The reweighted mass need not equal one, but is positive and <=1.
Indeed let p_q be the real monic degree-q orthogonal polynomial
of nu in the nontrivial Gaussian case. Both w and p_q^2 are monic
degree-2q polynomials, so w-p_q^2 has degree <=2q-1. Exactness and
p_q(t_k)=0 imply

  1-sum_k b_k
    =integral w dnu-sum_k a_k w(t_k)
    =integral p_q(t)^2 dnu(t) >=0.

Thus ||v'||^2=sum b_k is in (0,1]. In the finite-support exact case
it is one. This smaller input norm is fully compatible with the
common envelope for ||B-A||<=1. Keep it unnormalized while comparing
the common quadrature; its power denominator only improves lower
ratios. For exact-unit counting, normalize only AFTER preservation,
which can only increase output singular values.

If exact unit mass is desired, q+1 Gaussian nodes instead preserve
