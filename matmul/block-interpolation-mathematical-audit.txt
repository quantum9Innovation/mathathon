1. Precise convention and fixed-input statement
-----------------------------------------------
Lambda_i are deterministic real diagonal b-by-b matrices, and spectra of
different blocks are disjoint. Within-block repetitions are allowed.
Omega_i are independent standard real Gaussian b-by-b matrices, and
B_i=Omega_i^-1 Lambda_i Omega_i. For a right-coefficient polynomial
P(z)=sum_l z^l C_l, its LEFT matrix evaluation is

  P(B)=sum_l B^l C_l.

Thus P(z)D evaluates as P(B)D for a constant right factor D. In contrast
one may NOT evaluate an arbitrary scalar-variable matrix-product
factorization by multiplying its factors at B. The proof never does so.

For fixed d,b and every fixed admissible collection Lambda_i, the desired
event is simultaneous over all roots k and all scalar z in a containing
interval. It is NOT a single Gaussian draw claimed to work for every
possible input spectrum. Every probability step respects this distinction.

2. Raw block Krylov invertibility and the leading coefficient
-------------------------------------------------------------
For any subset S of q blocks, let A_0=diag(Lambda_i:i in S) and
Gamma=stack(Omega_i:i in S). The determinant of

  K_q=[Gamma,A_0 Gamma,...,A_0^(q-1)Gamma]

is a nonzero polynomial in the Gaussian entries. Witness: Omega_i=I.
Then reorder rows/columns by coordinate inside each block to obtain a
direct sum of b scalar Vandermonde matrices, each on q distinct nodes.
Cross-block disjointness is exactly enough, even with repetitions within
a block. A finite union covers every subset of a fixed d-block family.

All block Krylov prefixes consequently have full column rank. Block
Gram--Schmidt gives orthonormal blocks V_0,...,V_(q-1) with

  V_j=sum_(l=0)^j A_0^l Gamma C_(l,j),

where C_(j,j) is invertible. If C_q=C_(q-1,q-1), orthogonality gives

  V_(q-1)^T A_0^(q-1) Gamma C_q=I.                         (A1)

Indeed left multiply the expansion of V_(q-1) by its transpose;
every lower-degree term vanishes. This identity has the correct order:
C_q is on the RIGHT. No inverse norm of K_q is assumed in proving it.

3. Terminal block Lanczos recurrence and polynomial annihilation
----------------------------------------------------------------
Normalize all scalar nodes into [0,1]. Since A_0 is symmetric, the
orthogonal-basis representation is block tridiagonal. At its last block,

  A_0 V_last=V_prev H+V_last T,

with the previous term absent for q=1, and ||H||,||T||<=1. To check the
absence of earlier blocks, use symmetry to move A_0 onto V_j, whose
image is in the Krylov prefix through degree j+1 and hence orthogonal
to V_last for j<=q-3. No residual outside the full qb-space remains.

Let P_j(z)=sum z^l C_(l,j). The i-th block row of V_j equals
Omega_i P_j(B_i), so

  R_q(z)=zP_last(z)-P_prev(z)H-P_last(z)T

has degree q and R_q(B_i)=0 for every selected original block. The
coefficient order matches the recurrence exactly. H/T are multiplied on
the right, and multiplication by scalar z becomes LEFT multiplication
by B_i at evaluation. The leading coefficient is the C_q in (A1).

4. Decisive end-to-end resolvent identity, including q=1
------------------------------------------------------
Write R_q(z)=sum_(l=0)^q z^l C_l. Annihilation is the rectangular
identity sum_l A_0^l Gamma C_l=0. For z outside spec(A_0), subtract
this zero from (zI-A_0)^-1 Gamma R_q(z). Since A_0 commutes with its
own resolvent, ordinary polynomial division gives

 (zI-A_0)^-1 Gamma R_q(z)
   =sum_(l=1)^q sum_(t=0)^(l-1)
                       z^(l-1-t) A_0^t Gamma C_l.

Left multiplication by V_last^T kills t<q-1. Only l=q,t=q-1 survives;
(A1) makes it I. Therefore, with

  W_q(z)=V_last^T(zI-A_0)^-1 Gamma,

one has W_q(z)R_q(z)=I. Both are square b-by-b, so R_q(z) is
invertible and its inverse IS W_q(z). In particular

  ||R_q(eta)^-1||<=||Gamma||/dist(eta,spec(A_0)).            (A2)

No updated nonnormal solvent or internal cross-gap occurs here.

For q=1 this reduces to V_0=Gamma C_0, A_0V_0=V_0T, and
R_1(z)=zC_0-C_0T. Directly,
W_1R_1=V_0^T(z-A_0)^-1(z-A_0)V_0=I. Thus the base terminal case
has no missing P_-1 term, indexing defect, or factor normalization.

5. Fresh original root, independent rows, and fundamental interpolants
----------------------------------------------------------------------
For root k, construct R_q using the OTHER q=d-1 blocks only. Set
N_k=Omega_k R_q(B_k). Row l is exactly

  omega_(k,l)^T R_q(eta_(k,l)),

where eta_(k,l) is the corresponding fixed diagonal root eigenvalue.
Conditional on all other blocks these rows are independent Gaussians.
By (A2), each covariance R_q(eta)^T R_q(eta) is at least
(delta_k/||Gamma||)^2 I, with delta_k the root-to-others gap. Therefore
the independent-row small-ball lemma applies CONDITIONALLY, even though
the covariance floor is a random function of the other blocks.

Define F_k(z)=R_q(z)N_k^-1 Omega_k. For other nodes its evaluation is
zero. At the root, left multiply by Omega_k to get

  Omega_k F_k(B_k)=N_k N_k^-1 Omega_k=Omega_k,

so F_k(B_k)=I. The order N_k^-1 Omega_k is essential and is correct
in the source. All d resulting polynomials give surjectivity of the
square interpolation map and hence uniqueness. Invertibility also
follows from the raw Krylov determinant argument, without circularity.

6. Inductive polynomial bound and the original recursive constant
-----------------------------------------------------------------
For q-node fundamentals G_i of the OTHER blocks, uniqueness yields

  P_j(z)=sum_i G_i(z)P_j(B_i),
  ||P_j(B_i)||=||Omega_i^-1 (V_j)_i||<=M,

where M=max_i||Omega_i^-1||. Thus sup||R_q||<=3q G_q M.
No multiplication order or q-versus-b dimension factor is missing.

The actual source's three event collections each have failure beta/3:
  (i) d inductive calls at beta/(3d);
 (ii) d Gaussian norm tails and d inverse tails, each beta/(6d);
(iii) d fresh-root N_k tails, each beta/(3d), obtained conditionally.
They need not be mutually independent; a union bound suffices.

With L_g=b sqrt(6d/beta), u=beta/(6d b^(3/2)),
v=beta/(3d b^(3/2)), the proof obtains

  3q^(3/2)L_g^2/(u v)=324q^(3/2)d^3 b^5 beta^-3.

This verifies recursion (15) exactly. Its log grows O(d^2 log(d+1)
+d log(b/beta)); there is no hidden dependence on any spectral gap.
Affine normalization restores (L_spec/Delta)^(d-1), because scalar
affine changes commute with left evaluation and preserve the fundamental
polynomial's sup over the corresponding containing interval.

7. Stronger fractional-moment/path bound supplied by the parent
----------------------------------------------------------------
This refinement is sound and improves log C from quadratic to linear
in d up to logarithms. Define every subset polynomial by a FIXED
measurable subset-only orthogonalization, so the nested conditioning
used below is legitimate. For S of size q+1>=2 and k in S put

  Z_(S,k)=delta_(S,k) ||N_(S,k)^-1||/||Gamma_(S\k)||.

Conditional on Omega_(S\k), the row lemma gives

  Pr{Z_(S,k)>z | Omega_(S\k)}<=min(1,b^(3/2)/z).

Integrating the tail of sqrt(Z) yields

  E[sqrt(Z_(S,k)) | Omega_(S\k)]<=2b^(3/4).                (A3)

Let E_0 be the global Gaussian event

  max_i||Omega_i||<=L_0=2b sqrt(d/beta),
  max_i||Omega_i^-1||<=U_0=4d b^(3/2)/beta.

The total norm-tail failure is beta/4 by Frobenius Markov, and the
inverse-tail failure is beta/4 by the row lemma. Thus Pr(E_0)>=1-beta/2.
On E_0, deterministic recursion for EVERY subset gives

 G(S)<=max_(k in S) [3q^(3/2)U_0 L_0^2/Delta]
                           Z_(S,k) G(S\k).                (A4)

Expand along each of the d! removal-order paths. For a FIXED path, later
Z factors depend only on the remaining Gaussian matrices. Repeated
conditioning in (A3) therefore gives

 E[product_path sqrt Z]<=(2b^(3/4))^(d-1).

IMPORTANT: do not condition this expectation on E_0, which would alter
the Gaussian laws. Obtain a separate Markov/path event unconditionally,
then union it with E_0. Markov and a union over d! paths show, with failure
at most beta/2,

 max_path product Z
   <=[2d! (2b^(3/4))^(d-1)/beta]^2.                         (A5)

Combining (A4),(A5), q=1,...,d-1, gives

 G_d<=4 beta^-2 (d!)^2 ((d-1)!)^(3/2)
                    [192 d^2 b^5/(beta^2 Delta)]^(d-1).   (A6)

The arithmetic factor checks: 3U_0L_0^2=48d^2 b^(7/2)beta^-2;
the squared moment term contributes (4b^(3/2)) per level, producing192.
An intentionally loose simple bound, valid for every d>=2, is

 G_d<=[768 d^8 b^5/(beta^4 Delta)]^(d-1).                  (A7)

Indeed d!<=d^(d-1), (d-1)!<=d^(d-2), 4<=4^(d-1), and beta<1.
The d=2 endpoint satisfies the same bound. Affine scaling replaces
Delta^-1 by L_spec/Delta. Thus the base in (A7) is polynomial in
d,b,1/beta, and log C=O(d log(db/beta)). There is no conditioning
on an intersection of random spectral or Gaussian events in the moment
calculation, and no union over a continuum of input spectra.

8. Graph-angle transfer and scope
---------------------------------
For a fixed symmetric n-by-n A and a db-dimensional target invariant
subspace, use a containing interval for the ENTIRE spectrum of A, so
tail eigenvalues also lie where the interpolation sup is controlled.
In eigen-coordinates the target-head raw Krylov matrix K_H is square;
the full K_d has graph matrix K_T K_H^-1. For target y=(y_i),

  p_y(z)=sum_i F_i(z)Omega_i^-1 y_i

has the exact head values. Cauchy--Schwarz over blocks then gives

  ||K_T K_H^-1||<=sqrt(d)||Omega_T||_F
                            max_i||Omega_i^-1|| G_d.

This equals the tangent of the largest principal angle. The source's
orientation is correct. A tail Frobenius Markov event and the joint
head events above yield the claimed spectrum-uniform relgap_b^(1-d)
dependence, allowing arbitrary within-block repeats. If n=db, the
tail graph is zero and the angle is zero almost surely.

This is an initial structural angle bound. An actual convergence theorem
to a target inside a larger spectrum still needs its stated EXTERNAL
separation. The result does not prove the disproved product-of-solvent
constants, construct raw powers faster, or automatically attain the
original factored-product N^2 goal. Its possible novelty is the direct
cluster-robust interpolation/angle theorem; primary-source comparison
and final manuscript copying require their own review.
