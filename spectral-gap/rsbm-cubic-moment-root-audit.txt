1. Model, gauge, and the exact ordered-profile identity
------------------------------------------------------
The original adjacency matrix, after relabeling the second block by
the independent cross perfect matching, is

  A=[C1 I; I C2],       sigma=(1_N,-1_N),       A sigma=sigma.

Each internal configuration pairs 2N distinguishable stubs. A loop
contributes TWO to the adjacency diagonal. This convention is essential:
it gives Cj 1=2 1 even for nonsimple configurations, hence preserves the
planted line throughout conditioning.

Write z=-y. The equation (A-I)(x,y)=0 is equivalent to

  C1 x=x+z,               C2 z=x+z.                        (A1)

At a supervertex, A=x_i and D=z_i, while B,C are the ordered first-block
neighbor labels and E,F the ordered second-block neighbor labels. Thus

  C=A+D-B,                F=A+D-E.

Choosing (A,D,B,E) in Fp^4 specifies every local label assignment exactly
once. Ordered stub slots matter even when their labels are equal.
For a full assignment the directed table m1 records the two first-block
stubs from label a to labels b,c, and m2 records the second-block stubs.
Pairing is possible precisely when each directed table is symmetric
and has even diagonal. Its number of consistent pairings is

  P(m)=product_{a<b}m_ab! product_a(m_aa-1)!!.

Off the diagonal this is the number of bijections between the two sets
of stubs. On the diagonal it is the number of pairings of m_aa stubs.
There is no additional factor from ordering the neighbor slots: those
slots were already distinguished in the full assignment.

For each fixed pairing and each kernel vector, every slot's neighbor
label is determined uniquely. Conversely a label assignment together
with a compatible pairing satisfies (A1). Therefore

  E_config |ker_Fp(A-I)|
    = [sum over N profiles of P(m1)P(m2)]/((2N-1)!!)^2
    = p^(4N) E[P(M1)P(M2)]/((2N-1)!!)^2.                 (A2)

This argument allows loops, double edges, odd N, and p|N. Its only
evenness requirement is that the number of internal STUBS be 2N.


2. Entropy enumeration and exhaustive equality cases
----------------------------------------------------
In ungauged coordinates let pi(a,b) be the empirical central-pair law,
and q1(a,b,u), q2(a,b,c) the ordered-star laws with common central
marginal pi. For integer counts n,n1,n2, the number of assignments is

  N! product_{a,b}n_ab! /
      [product_{a,b,u}n1_abu! product_{a,b,c}n2_abc!].

This follows either by assigning central labels first and then the two
ordered stars within each central class, or by summing the full-profile
multinomial over contingency tables with fixed two marginals. It agrees
with (A2); no independence of those empirical star tables is asserted.

For symmetric normalized edge laws rho1,rho2, the pairing entropy is
-N H(rhoj)+O_p(log(N+1)). Ordinary factorial bounds are uniform also
when some counts are zero. Consequently the logarithm of a positive
type summand is

  N F+O_p(log(N+1)),
  F=H(q1)+H(q2)-H(pi)-H(rho1)-H(rho2).                   (A3)

The admissible two-star state space is a compact polytope: probability
laws, common central marginal, and symmetric edge laws. The number
of integer table pairs is polynomial in N for fixed p. Diagonal parity
is a discrete feasibility condition; ignoring it when bounding this
continuous state space cannot introduce a missing entropy maximum.

Symmetrize q1 under u -> a-b-u and q2 under c -> b-a-c. Call the
symmetrizations s1,s2. Their central and averaged edge marginals are
unchanged. Since the symmetrized law is invariant under its involution,

  H(sj)-H(qj)=D(qj||sj).

The chain rule then gives exactly

  F= -I_pi(A;B)-I_s1(B;U|A)-I_s2(A;C|B)
     -D(q1||s1)-D(q2||s2) <=0.                           (A4)

Here is a direct verification of the equality classification.
If F=0, all five nonnegative losses vanish. Thus qj=sj,
pi=mu times nu, and

  q1(a,b,u)=mu(a)nu(b)K_a(u),
  q2(a,b,c)=mu(a)nu(b)L_b(c).

If nu has two distinct support values b,b', symmetry of the first
star reflects K_a about both a-b and a-b'. Their composition is
translation by the nonzero element b-b'. Since p is prime, this
translation generates Fp, so K_a is uniform for each supported a.
The symmetric first edge law is then rho1(a,u)=mu(a)/p. Symmetry
forces mu to be uniform, including at labels initially outside its
support. The same argument in the second block forces nu and all
L_b uniform. This is the unique full uniform phase.

If nu is a point mass b0, the symmetric second edge law, whose central
label is always b0, is concentrated at (b0,b0). Both second neighbors
equal b0. Their local equation gives 2b0=b0-A, so A=-b0. The first
block then also has its constant planted star. These are precisely
the p planted point phases. There are no remaining cases.

Thus F has exactly p+1 zeros. Every fixed closed region avoiding
neighborhoods of these zeros has F<=-eta_p<0 and contributes
polynomial(N) exp(-eta_p N)=o(1). This needs no estimate uniform in p.

The optional full-profile entropy Phi=F-I(U;C|A,B) is also correct,
but the global complementary-region bound already follows from F
and the exact two-star enumeration. Its use does not require proving
the optional uniform coercivity constants in the entropy note.


3. Sparse perturbations: cancellations, loops, and enumeration
---------------------------------------------------------------
After subtracting a planted vector, let k be the number of nonzero
individual coordinates, m the number of matching pairs they occupy,
and s the number of those pairs with exactly one nonzero coordinate.
Then m=(k+s)/2. Use all internal edges incident to the support as
the certificate, keeping loops, parallel edges, and block colors.
If e of its edges have both ends supported and B have one supported
end, stub counting gives

  2k=2e+B,          E=e+B=k+B/2<=2k.

Every singleton support pair has a zero matching partner. At that
partner the internal sum must cancel the NONZERO cross contribution,
so there is at least one boundary edge. Every touched zero coordinate
in a completely untouched matching pair has zero cross contribution.
It cannot have exactly one support-neighbor edge, irrespective of the
support value; it must receive both of its internal stubs from support.
If t counts those individual zero coordinates, then B>=s+2t.
If r counts new supervertices outside the m supported pairs, r<=t.
Therefore, with v=m+r,

  E-v >= k+(s+2t)/2-(k+s)/2-r >= k/2.                     (A5)

This argument explicitly allows sums of two support values to cancel.
A loop contributes two stubs at its own coordinate; it does not create
a counterexample to either zero-coordinate assertion. Parallel edges
are counted with multiplicity. No positivity of field values is used.

The certificate count is also quantitatively sound:

  * Supervertex locations: <=N^(m+r)/(m!r!).
  * Occupancy patterns and nonzero labels: <=3^m p^k.
  * All selected stubs: 4v<=8k. Partial pairings with E edges:
      <=2^(8k)(4k)^E.
  * For k<=N/4, a prescribed valid colored partial matching has
      probability <=N^(-E), because every conditional denominator
      2N-2h-1 is at least N.

Counting cross-color-invalid or equation-invalid certificates enlarges
the upper bound and is harmless. Every actual solution supplies one
enumerated valid certificate. Since E-v>=k/2,

  (4k)^E N^(v-E)/(m!r!)
    <=16^k (k/N)^(k/2) k^m/m! k^r/r!.

Summing m,r by exp(k)^2 and using at most 2k possible positive E
gives precisely the source bound

  E Z_k <=2k(Dp)^k(k/N)^(k/2),        D=12288 exp(2).        (A6)

The consecutive-term ratio is at most
2 sqrt(exp(1)) Dp sqrt((k+1)/N). The stated sufficiently small fixed
epsilon_p makes it <=1/2 eventually throughout k<epsilon_p N.
Support one is impossible: an identically zero whole block forces
the other block zero through the identity cross matrix. Thus summing
from k=2 gives O_p(N^-1), not merely o(1) after a polynomial loss.

The p translated neighborhoods are disjoint, since two distinct
planted vectors differ at all 2N coordinates. Their TOTAL contribution
is p+O_p(N^-1). If at most delta N central pairs differ from the
constant pair, at most 2delta N individual coordinates differ, exactly
as required to pass from a constant type neighborhood to (A6).


4. Joint covariance: independent derivation
------------------------------------------
Let H be all real p-by-p total-zero tables. Its dimension is
r0=p^2-1. Let R be row-constant orthogonal projection on H.
For total-zero test table f,

  Var[f(A,B)+f(A,A+D-B)]
    =(2/p^2)||f||^2+(2/p^3)sum_a(row_sum_a f)^2
    =(2/p^2)<f,(I+R)f>.

Indeed B and A+D-B are independent uniform given A. Every one of
(A,B),(A,C) is independent of every one of (D,E),(D,F): the four
corresponding linear forms are invertible coordinates in A,D,B,E.
Thus the two table blocks have zero cross-covariance. Their covariance
is Sigma direct-sum Sigma, with

  Sigma=(2/p^2)(I+R),
  det_H Sigma=(4/p^2)^(p-1)(2/p^2)^(p(p-1)).             (A7)

In particular covariance is strictly positive on H times H. This
also establishes that the support-difference lattice below has full
real rank; its finite index is not assumed without justification.


5. The full support lattice: complete character reconstruction
--------------------------------------------------------------
Let L0 be the integer total-zero lattice in H times H. Each one-block
root lattice has covolume p, so covol(L0)=p^2. Let L be generated
by all support differences W(a,d,b,e)-W(0,0,0,0).

Each block table has even row sums. On fixed-total tables this gives
p-1 independent parity restrictions per block, hence index 2^(2p-2).
Write the half row sums as n1_a,n2_d. Direct substitution of a local
profile gives the three source congruences

  L1=sum_ab b m1_ab -sum_a a n1_a-sum_d d n2_d=0,
  L2=sum_de e m2_de -sum_a a n1_a-sum_d d n2_d=0,
  Q =sum_ab ab m1_ab-sum_de de m2_de
       -sum_a a^2 n1_a+sum_d d^2 n2_d=0                  (mod p).

They are independent on the row-even lattice. In coefficient form,
the first-table coefficient of b in a linear combination is
coefficient(L1)+a coefficient(Q). It vanishes for every a only if
both coefficients are zero; the second-table e coefficient then
forces coefficient(L2)=0. Row-even integer variations surject onto
fixed-total Fp tables, since p is odd. Therefore these displayed
conditions have index 2^(2p-2)p^3 in L0.

To rule out a hidden larger index, let (theta1_ab,theta2_de), modulo
one and modulo additive constants on each table, be a character
annihilating every support difference. Vary b at fixed a,d. Then

  theta1_a,b+theta1_a,a+d-b

is independent of b for EVERY a+d. If f(b)=theta1_a,b-theta1_a,0,
the identity at center b+c is f(b)+f(c)=f(b+c). Consequently f is
an additive character of Fp:

  theta1_ab=alpha_a+u_a b/p,
  theta2_de=gamma_d+v_d e/p,             u_a,v_d in Fp.

The remaining local-support constancy equation is

  2alpha_a+2gamma_d+(u_a+v_d)(a+d)/p=constant mod 1.

Take a rectangular mixed difference in a,d. For distinct a,a' and
d,d' it gives

  (u_a-u_a')(d-d')+(v_d-v_d')(a-a')=0 mod p.

All divided differences of u therefore equal one t, and all those
of v equal -t. Hence u_a=t a+k and v_d=-t d+l. The remaining
separated equations are exactly

  2alpha_a=R-[t a^2+(k+l)a]/p,
  2gamma_d=S+[t d^2-(k+l)d]/p.

There are p^3 choices t,k,l. After quotienting the irrelevant
global constant in each table, each block has p-1 independent row
half-sign choices. Thus the annihilator has exactly

  2^(2p-2)p^3

characters. Full real rank was proved in Section 4. Finite-index
lattice duality now gives precisely this index, not just an upper
or lower bound. Therefore the source congruences describe the full
generated lattice, and

  covol(L)=p^5 2^(2p-2).                                  (A8)

This reconstruction is the essential all-prime check. Checking a few
primes by integer elimination would not have sufficed on its own.


6. Symmetry, even diagonals, and the valid lattice
-------------------------------------------------
Let S be symmetric real total-zero tables, dimension
s=p(p+1)/2-1. Put d=p(p-1)/2, so s=d+p-1 and r0=d+s.

The full symmetric integer lattice has covolume 2^(d/2), because
each off-diagonal basis vector has norm sqrt(2). The total functional
is primitive and its Euclidean gradient is J of norm p. Thus its
total-zero sublattice K0 has covolume

  covol(K0)=p 2^(d/2).

On this lattice the possible diagonal parity vectors have even sum,
giving index 2^(p-1) to make every diagonal entry even. Requiring
row sums even adds another factor 2^(p-1): after diagonals are even,
off-diagonal entries have the degree-parity map of the complete graph,
which has F2-rank p-1. These requirements are independent. Off-diagonal
variations can be compensated by changing one diagonal by an even
number to preserve the total.

Under symmetry L1=-L2=T, where
T=sum_a a n1_a-sum_d d n2_d. The Q congruence survives. These two
mod-p restrictions are independent on the already parity-constrained
lattice: changing first-table diagonal entries by +2 at (a,a) and
-2 at (0,0) changes (T,Q) by (a,a^2). At a=1,-1 the determinant
is 2, nonzero for every odd prime. Thus the remaining field index
is p^2.

For the PAIR of symmetric valid difference tables, the covolume is

  covol(K)=(p 2^(d/2))^2 2^(4p-4)p^2
          =p^4 2^(d+4p-4),
  covol(L)/covol(K)=p 2^(-d-2p+2).                         (A9)

Even diagonal entries are explicitly included here; omitting them
would change the coefficient exponentially in p. The actual source
definition includes them and the calculation is correct.

The valid affine lattice is nonempty for every N: take both tables
to have their only entry 2N at (0,0). Its differences are exactly K.
It has bounded covering radius in S times S, depending only on p.
Thus every N has valid lattice points within O_p(1) of the uniform
mean tables; no N-mod-p or N-parity subsequence is selected. At large
N these central points are entrywise positive. The local limit result
below additionally ensures they are genuine N-fold support points.


7. Stirling prefactor and weighted Gaussian
------------------------------------------
Put t=2N/p^2 and m=tJ+sqrt(N)X. For symmetric even-diagonal m in the
logarithmic window, direct Stirling expansion gives

  P(m)/(2N-1)!!
   =p^(-2N)(4pi N/p^2)^(d/2)2^((p-1)/2)
     exp[(p^2/8)||X||^2](1+o_p(1)).                      (A10)

The off-diagonal factorials supply (2pi t)^(d/2); each diagonal
double factorial supplies sqrt(2), and the denominator contributes
another sqrt(2) inversely. The power part is exp[-N H(m/(2N))].
Its constant is p^(-2N), its linear perturbation vanishes, and its
quadratic term is (p^2/8)||X||^2. The cubic exponent error is
O_p(log^3 N/sqrt(N)); all factorial relative errors are o(1) uniformly.

The inverse covariance on H is

  Sigma^(-1)=(p^2/2)(I-R/2).

Adding (A10) to the Gaussian exponent gives, on S,

  -1/2<X,Sigma^(-1)X>+(p^2/8)||X||^2
    =-(p^2/8)(||X||^2-||RX||^2).                         (A11)

The compression of R to S has eigenvalue 1/2 on tables v_a+v_b,
sum v=0 (dimension p-1), and zero on symmetric row-sum-zero tables
(dimension d). Consequently the weighted Gaussian precision has
eigenvalues p^2/8 with multiplicity p-1 and p^2/4 with multiplicity d.
It is strictly positive. Its one-block integral is

  I=(2pi)^(s/2)(8/p^2)^((p-1)/2)(4/p^2)^(d/2).           (A12)

No cancellation of a zero Gaussian direction is being assumed: all
valid symmetric directions have positive precision.

The joint local mass prefactor is
covol(L)(2pi N)^(-r0)(det Sigma)^(-1). The valid symmetric lattice,
scaled by sqrt(N), has point density N^s/covol(K). The pair of
pairing ratios contributes N^d. Thus powers cancel because d+s=r0.
The remaining coefficient is

  (4pi/p^2)^d 2^(p-1)(2pi)^(-r0)(det Sigma)^(-1)
       *[covol(L)/covol(K)] I^2.

Using (A7),(A12),

  I^2/det Sigma=(2pi)^s 2^(p-1)p^(2d).

The analytic part is therefore 2^(d+2p-2), and multiplication by
(A9) leaves EXACTLY p. I reproduce the stated constant, with all
Euclidean-volume factors and parity indices retained.


8. Relative local limit, semigroup support, and uniform tails
-------------------------------------------------------------
The local limit lemma in the source has the right strength: a
uniform RELATIVE error in ||m-Nmu||<=sqrt(N)log N. An absolute-error
central limit theorem would not justify the weighted summation.

For this finite-support law, covariance is positive on H times H,
and mu lies in the relative interior of the support convex hull.
The mean map under exponential tilt has derivative Sigma_total,
so the inverse function theorem supplies the unique small tilt with
mean m/N. Throughout the logarithmic window its parameter is
O_p(log N/sqrt(N)), and its covariance differs from the original
by the same order.

Because the support differences generate the MAXIMAL lattice L,
the tilted characteristic function has modulus one only at reciprocal
lattice points. On a reciprocal fundamental torus there is one such
maximum. Elsewhere a compactness bound gives modulus <=rho<1,
uniformly over the small tilts. Near zero there is the uniform
Gaussian modulus bound from positive covariance.

Fourier inversion at the tilted mean, splitting at N^(-2/5), gives

  P_tilt(sum W_i=m)
   =(1+o(1))covol(L)(2pi N)^(-r0)(det Sigma_tilt)^(-1).

The intermediate annulus has bound exp(-c N^(1/5)) and the distant
torus has rho^N. On the central integral, scaling by sqrt(N) and
Gaussian domination gives uniform relative error. Undoing the tilt
multiplies by exp[-N J(m/N)], whose cubic exponent error is
O_p(log^3 N/sqrt(N)). This proves the claimed relative approximation.
It also proves that all central affine lattice points have positive
mass eventually: there is no unproved normality assumption on the
semigroup of local profiles.

For the remainder of a small fixed uniform-table neighborhood, the
same tilt need not be estimated to relative precision. The finite-
support Cramer rate function J is analytic there, with Hessian
Sigma_total^(-1). The logarithm of the pairing weight, after its
constant p powers cancel, is also analytic. By (A11) their difference
has a strictly positive Hessian on S times S. Shrink the fixed
neighborhood once, depending on p. Taylor remainder is then at most
half the positive quadratic loss. Chernoff plus uniform Stirling
therefore bounds each weighted summand by

  C_p N^d exp[-c_p ||m-t(J,J)||^2/N].                       (A13)

This controls EVERY valid table in that fixed neighborhood, not only
ones whose empirical full-star law was assumed close to uniform.
Outside the sqrt(N)log N window there are polynomially many tables
and each has exp[-c_p log^2 N] decay. Their total is o(1).
Inside it, the valid-lattice Gaussian Riemann sum converges to the
integral (A12)^2. The shifted meshes have spacing O_p(N^-1/2), and
positive Gaussian precision controls their tails uniformly.

Hence the ENTIRE fixed uniform-edge-table neighborhood contributes
p+o_p(1). This tail argument is valid for each fixed p, and has no
unstated demand for a prime growing with N.


9. Region glue and direct simplicity conditioning
--------------------------------------------------
Take the central-pair neighborhoods of the p constant phases small
enough that their vectors lie in the sparse translated neighborhoods
of Section 3. Take the uniform edge-table neighborhood of Section 8
small enough to be disjoint from them. Disjointness can be checked
just from row masses: near a constant, one label carries almost all
2N stubs in its block; at uniformity each label carries 2N/p.

In the continuous two-star type space these are neighborhoods of all
p+1 zeros of F. The closure of their complementary region contains
no zero. By Section 2 it has a strict negative entropy maximum and
contributes exponentially little, including all Stirling polynomial
factors. If neighborhoods are written using closed inequalities,
one equivalently takes their interiors first or closes the complement;
the zeros lie strictly inside, so this does not affect the argument.

The uniform estimate counts all profile types whose edge tables lie
in its neighborhood. It does not require that every such type be
near the uniform full-star law. The sparse estimate counts all kernel
vectors close to a planted constant, irrespective of their neighbor
assignments. The two-star exact enumeration applies to the remaining
region. Thus these three estimates cover the same exact moment (A2)
without an omitted intermediate region or an inconsistent type model.
Their contributions are p+o(1), p+O(N^-1), and o(1), respectively:

  E_config |ker_Fp(A-I)|=2p+o_p(1).                         (A14)

For one degree-two configuration, the coefficient ratio of

  (1-z)^(-1/2) exp(-z/2-z^2/4)

and (1-z)^(-1/2) is the probability of simplicity. Every simple
degree-two graph has exactly 2^N distinguished-stub realizations.
The ratio tends to exp(-3/4). The argument works at all N: the entire
factor has absolutely summable coefficients with exponentially small
tails, whereas coefficients of (1-z)^(-1/2) are asymptotic to
1/sqrt(pi N), with the stated uniform ratio bounds. No parity-
restricted coefficient sequence occurs.

The independent two-block simplicity event has probability
s_N->exp(-3/2). Conditional on this event the internal graphs are
exactly independent UNIFORM SIMPLE degree-two graphs. Let K denote
the finite-field kernel size. The planted p vectors are present in
every configuration, so K-p>=0. Subtract them before conditioning:

  E_simple K
   =p+E_config[(K-p)1_simple]/s_N
   <=p+[E_config K-p]/s_N.

Together with (A14), this gives the claimed coefficient
(1+exp(3/2))p. This is exact conditioning, not an invalid contiguity
transfer of an unbounded expectation.
