PLAIN-LANGUAGE COMPANION: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
=========================================
November 15, 2026

Source: overview.tex in this directory. It is a six-page explanatory
companion, not a second mathematical proof paper.

The problem is to recover hidden groups in a network whose members
have exact prescribed numbers of neighbors in each group. The group
table, number of groups, and sizes are unknown to the algorithm.
Fixed upper bounds on group count and degree are supplied.

The guarantee assumes independent uniform random choices subject to
the degree constraints, a connected group-count table, compatible group sizes, and the manuscript's
symmetric omission-weight condition. It includes every fixed connected
simple regular template of degree at least three and some unequal-degree
models. It does not follow from minimum degree three alone.

The algorithm uses finite-field arithmetic to represent candidate
solutions compactly, certifies that its search is complete, and checks
the original integer neighbor counts before reporting a partition.
It selects the finest partition within the supplied group-count bound.
The proof charges rare large searches in its expected work bound.

Expected nearly quadratic work means approximately four times the work
when the vertex count doubles, for fixed parameters and ignoring
logarithmic factors. It is not a worst-case quadratic guarantee or a
practical benchmark. Exact equations remove a numerical eigenvalue-gap
assumption; they do not establish robustness to noisy or changed links.

For two groups of size N with two neighbors inside and one across,
ambiguity probability is asymptotic to 4/N^2 and optimal hidden-prior
Bayes error to 2/N^2. N is one group size, not the total size. The
algorithm attains the optimal hidden-prior error by exact listing and
selection. Some cycle and nonregular-template models are unidentifiable.

The potential contribution is the broad fixed-parameter guarantee and
its rigorous statistical and computational composition. Earlier direct
algebraic recovery ideas, including Kid Krypto (1993) and the 2023 IPCC
evaluation, are credited, as are classical methods. Historical firstness,
external peer review, practical speed, noise robustness, and growing-group
or growing-degree guarantees are not claimed.
