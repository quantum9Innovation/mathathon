Independent priority and logical-subsumption audit
2026-11-15

Scope. This report concerns the closure of the spectra of
Q_alpha(A,B)=A^(-alpha/2) B^alpha A^(-alpha/2), for 0<alpha<1,
when the spectrum p of A^(-1/2) B A^(-1/2) is fixed. The original
rank-one positive-update question is p=(1+tau,1,...,1). Only this
session directory has been written.

Verdict

The matrix construction is mathematically sound and extends to arbitrary
positive p. However, the general matrix result has a short proof assembled
entirely from classical inverse singular-value theory, Araki's inequality,
and existing diagonal-scaling asymptotics. I did not find an explicit
published statement of exactly this closure theorem. That negative search
result is not a novelty certificate. The unrestricted matrix theorem should
be presented as a sharp inverse/range corollary of Araki--Horn theory, with
careful credits, rather than as an independent breakthrough. Unit-mass path
realization is an additional structural assertion and is not supplied by
any of the matrix results below.

The stronger theorem

Fix an ordered positive list p=(p_1,...,p_n), p_1>=...>=p_n>0.
Let R_alpha(p) contain all ordered spectra q of Q_alpha(A,B), where
A,B are real SPD and spec(A^(-1/2) B A^(-1/2))=p exactly. Then

  closure R_alpha(p) = {q>0 : log q is majorized by alpha log p}.

There is no additional positivity-side constraint. In particular, if
p_n>=1, majorization already implies q_n>=p_n^alpha>=1: use the
prefix inequality k=n-1 and the equality of total sums. Likewise the
top inequality gives q_1<=p_1^alpha. For the rank-one list, the
majorization polytope reduces exactly to q_i>=1 and
product(q_i)=(1+tau)^alpha.

This is a closure statement. If p is not constant, the scalar point
q_i=(product p)^(alpha/n), which always belongs to the polytope,
cannot be attained by a finite SPD pair: Q=cI would force
B=c^(1/alpha) A, making p constant. Thus noncompact scaling is
essential even in the interior of the majorization polytope.

Logical dependency chain

1. Necessity is Araki's log-majorization.

   For X,Y>0 and 0<alpha<1,
   spec(X^(alpha/2)Y^alpha X^(alpha/2)) is log-majorized by
   spec(X^(1/2)Y X^(1/2))^alpha.
   Taking X=A^(-1), Y=B proves log q <=_maj alpha log p.
   Determinant equality is automatic. In rank one this adds no
   useful inequality beyond positivity plus the determinant.

2. Sufficiency of the desired reverse Cholesky pivots is old.

   Given d>0 with log d <=_maj log p, Weyl--Horn supplies a REAL
   triangular matrix R with diagonal sqrt(d) and singular values
   sqrt(p). Its transpose may be used to choose the upper-triangular
   orientation. Put S=R R^T. Then spec(S)=p and the reverse LDL^T
   pivots of S are d_i=R_ii^2. The real field and prescribed order
   are explicitly handled by Li--Mathias, cited below.

3. Separated diagonal scaling converts pivots into fractional
   relative eigenvalues.

   Set a_i=delta^(n-i), A_delta=diag(a_i), and
   B_delta=A_delta^(1/2) S A_delta^(1/2).
   Then spec(A_delta^(-1/2)B_delta A_delta^(-1/2))=p at every
   delta and

   A_delta^(-alpha/2) B_delta^alpha A_delta^(-alpha/2)
       --> diag(d_1^alpha,...,d_n^alpha).

   The eigenvalue and eigenvector asymptotics needed here are
   supplied by existing work (Usevich--Barthelme and
   Gissler--Auger--Hansen). A short elementary proof is recorded
   below to make the implication directly checkable.

For a target q use d_i=q_i^(1/alpha). This proves sufficiency.
The rank-one relative spectrum of S forces S-I to be PSD rank one,
so the construction retains the exact rank-one nature; no extra
rank approximation is needed.

Elementary fractional-pivot implication

Write the reverse principal-minor ratios as

  d_i = det S[i:n,i:n] / det S[i+1:n,i+1:n],

with the empty determinant equal to one. Denote the eigenvalues of
B_delta in increasing order by nu_i and choose orthonormal
eigenvectors v_i. Let m and M be the least and greatest eigenvalues
of fixed S. The inequalities m A_delta <= B_delta <= M A_delta
imply m a_i <= nu_i <= M a_i. In particular all adjacent ratios
nu_i/nu_(i+1) tend to zero.

For each k, expand the kth elementary symmetric polynomial as

  e_k(B_delta) = sum_{|I|=k} det S[I,I] product_{i in I} a_i.

Because the scales are separated, the trailing k indices dominate.
Also e_k(nu) is asymptotic to the product of the largest k nu_i.
Taking ratios for consecutive k gives nu_i/a_i --> d_i.

For every coordinate i and eigenvector v_k, positivity of the
spectral expansions of B_delta and its inverse gives

  |(v_k)_i|^2 <= (B_delta)_ii / nu_k <= C a_i/a_k,
  |(v_k)_i|^2 <= nu_k (B_delta^(-1))_ii <= C a_k/a_i.

Here C depends only on fixed S. Hence |(v_i)_i|^2-->1.
In the i-th diagonal entry of normalized B_delta^alpha, the
contribution of eigenvector k>i is bounded by
C(a_i/a_k)^(1-alpha), and the contribution k<i by
C(a_k/a_i)^(1+alpha). Both vanish, leaving d_i^alpha.
For each fixed spectral rank-one summand, an off-diagonal entry
has magnitude equal to the geometric mean of its two diagonal
entries. For i != j, at least one of those entries tends to zero,
while all entries remain bounded. Thus all off-diagonal entries
vanish. Finite-dimensional entrywise convergence implies norm
convergence. This proof works for every fixed SPD S; no rank-one
assumption is used.

Primary-source record

[A] Huzihiro Araki, "On an inequality of Lieb and Thirring,"
    Letters in Mathematical Physics 19 (1990), 167--170.
    DOI: 10.1007/BF01045887.
    Publisher page, inspected:
    https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01045887
    This is the original source for the comparison used in step 1.
    Its publisher abstract gives trace inequalities for all positive
    exponents and suitable test functions; exterior powers give the
    log-majorization formulation.

[B] Koenraad M. R. Audenaert, "On the Araki--Lieb--Thirring

    https://arxiv.org/html/math/0701129
    Theorem 1 states the direction for 0<=r<=1 explicitly. This
    primary paper is convenient for preventing a direction error;
    its complementary bounds are not a range theorem.

What has and has not been subsumed

* Positivity, determinant identity and strict-count upper bound:
  elementary matrix analysis; no novelty claim is tenable.
* Full rank-one closure: directly follows from the stronger
  majorization closure, which is the above synthesis.
* Complete fixed-p closure: not located verbatim in the sources
  searched, but follows without a new analytic ingredient from
  [A]+[H]/[LM]/[HT]+[UB]+[GAH]. A short self-contained treatment
  may still be useful. Its historical priority is unverified.
* The concrete z_i=(r_i-1) product_{j>i}r_j formula is an explicit
  rank-one evaluation of reverse pivots. It is valuable exposition
  and avoids inverse singular-value machinery; it should not be
  equated with a new general LDU asymptotic principle.
* A single edit on an ordinary unit-mass weighted path is not
  supplied by the cited matrix theorems. That constrained graph
  realization is the appropriate place to concentrate claims of
  additional content, subject to its own proof and priority audit.
