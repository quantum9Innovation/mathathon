2. SHARP MINIMUM SUPPORT AND THE UNIFORM UPPER COUNT
---------------------------------------------------
Every equitable sign vector tau satisfies A tau=delta tau, and is
balanced since A1=D1, A is symmetric, and D-delta=2b>0. Let sigma
be planted and w=(sigma-tau)/2. It is +1 on flipped plus vertices,
-1 on flipped minus vertices, and zero elsewhere. Balance gives the
same flip count k on both planted sides. Global exchange permits
1<=k<=N/2 for any alternative unordered partition.

At a flipped plus vertex, the equation Aw=delta w says

  delta=neighbors_in_Splus-neighbors_in_Sminus.

Thus at least delta distinct plus support neighbors are needed.
Simplicity forces k>=delta+1=:t. The minus equation gives the same
condition. Therefore the exact support S has K=2k>=2t. This minimum
is used only for the SIMPLE graph event, not falsely asserted for
the configuration model with loops or parallel edges.

At each vertex outside S, the sum of its nonzero support-neighbor
values is zero, so it cannot have exactly ONE incident edge into S.
For a fixed S of size K<=epsilon N in the colored configuration model,
expose its incident edges, skipping already paired stubs. There are
at most DK steps. A collision hits S or an exterior vertex already
seen. Every selected history has collision probability <=C_{a,b}K/N
when epsilon is sufficiently small: at most O_{a,b}(K) stubs belong
to collision vertices, while every remaining relevant internal or
cross pool retains Theta_{a,b}(N) stubs.

If e edges lie inside S and B edges leave it, there are e+B actual
exposures. Exactly |T| first touch exterior vertices. The necessary
condition gives |T|<=B/2, hence at least

  e+B-|T|>=e+B/2=DK/2

collisions. Internal edges consume two S stubs but only one exposure;
this accounting correctly includes that difference. It also includes
loops in the unconditioned witness count. Pad by noncollisions to DK
steps, apply conditional probability multiplication at selected indices,
and sum over at most 2^(DK) index sets. This yields the proof's bound
2^(DK)(C K/N)^(DK/2) for each fixed S.

Union bounding at most (2e_Euler N/K)^K supports gives
[A(K/N)^eta]^K, eta=(D-2)/2. Its consecutive ratio is
<=A exp(eta)((K+1)/N)^eta. Shrinking fixed epsilon makes this at most
1/2 throughout the desired range. Summing from K=2t therefore gives
O(N^(-2t eta))=O(N^(-nu)). Conditioning all three factors simple costs
only a fixed positive factor and yields the exact requested law.

For larger supports, r=k/N lies in [epsilon/2,1/2]. Slot symmetrization
of the INTEGER Boolean stars preserves all pairing tables. Removing one
internal sign gives

  L=-Phi=((a-2)/2)(I_++I_-)+b I_cross+J_++J_-.

The product comparison that preserves the actual internal conditional
laws has KL=J_++b I_cross<=L. Let W be the omitted internal sign and
m(X) its conditional mean. Its actual squared deviation has expectation
E Var(internal|X); under the comparison law it increases by at least
b Var(opposite_root), since a>=2. The same squared function lies in
[0,4a^2] under BOTH laws. Pinsker gives exactly

  Phi<=-(2b^2/a^4)r^2(1-r)^2.

The a=2,b=1 boundary is valid; no positive a-2 coefficient is required.
The exact finite-alphabet count has polynomially many types and uniform
zero-safe factorial errors, so this entire macroscopic range contributes
polynomial(N) exp(-cN)=o(N^(-nu)). Together these prove the asserted
sharp-rate upper bound at all admissible parities.

3. THE MOTIF REALLY PRODUCES AN ALTERNATIVE PARTITION
-----------------------------------------------------
Take t=delta+1 vertices Splus and t vertices Sminus. Put K_t cliques
on each. For every i in [t], j in [b], add distinct Wplus_ij and
Wminus_ij with edges

  Splus_i--Wplus_ij, Sminus_i--Wminus_ij (internal),
  Sminus_i--Wplus_ij, Splus_i--Wminus_ij (cross).

Each S vertex has delta clique neighbors, b internal W neighbors,
and b cross W neighbors. Its internal degree is delta+b=a and its
cross degree b: ALL of its physical stubs are used by the motif.
Each W vertex has one prescribed internal and one prescribed cross
edge, and total motif degree two. All vertices and prescribed edges
are distinct. Since t>=2 and b>=1, the two cliques and their connecting
two-step paths form a connected graph of minimum degree at least two.

Its counts are

  v=2t(1+b), e=t*delta+4bt,
  e-v=t(delta+2b-2)=t(D-2)=nu.

Flipping exactly the two S sets preserves all local degree counts:
each S exchanges its b internal W edges and b cross W edges, each
W exchanges one internal and one cross edge, and every other incident
edge has unchanged endpoint marks. There are no further S-neighbors
outside the motif because S is saturated. Extra edges among W or
from W to the remaining graph are allowed and do not spoil the flip.
Thus an INDUCED motif is not required. The equal t flip counts keep
balance, and at large N this partition differs from both orientations
of the plant. Every motif occurrence therefore implies ambiguity.

4. FIXED-EMBEDDING COUNTS AND RESIDUAL SIMPLE COMPLETION
-------------------------------------------------------
There are Theta(N^v) injective embeddings preserving planted plus/minus
types. For a fixed embedding, the finite positive number of allowed
stub assignments is

  c_H=product_u (a)_(h_int(u)) (b)_(h_cross(u)).

Each internal block has e_int=t*delta/2+bt prescribed edges; there
are e_cross=2bt prescribed cross edges. For any one assignment its
probability is EXACTLY

  product_(j=0)^(e_int-1)(aN-2j-1)^(-2)
        * [(bN)_(e_cross)]^(-1)=Theta(N^(-e)).

No additional edge orientation factor is needed. In fact assignments
are disjoint for this motif: because every required edge touches a
saturated S vertex, a configuration containing all required distinct
neighbors cannot have a parallel extra copy of any required edge.
Its physical stubs on those edges are consequently unique. Even a
one-assignment lower bound and finite-assignment upper bound would
suffice for the Theta estimate.

The conditional-simplicity argument is legitimate and important.
After one assignment is prescribed, remaining pairings/bijection are
uniform in the remaining pools. Every S residual degree is ZERO;
each W has residual internal degree a-1 and residual cross degree b-1;
other vertices retain a,b. Every prescribed edge has an endpoint in
S, so no residual edge can duplicate ANY prescribed edge. Therefore
full-graph simplicity is equivalent to residual-factor simplicity.
This equivalence would fail for an arbitrary motif with unsaturated
prescribed endpoints, but it is correct here.

These residual degree sequences differ at only O_{a,b}(1) vertices
from regular ones. Their total stub counts remain Theta(N), with
degrees bounded. Loop/double-edge factorial moments, or the bounded-
degree specialization of Janson's actual simplicity theorems, give
positive limiting residual simplicity probabilities. Finite degree
modifications change the loop and double-edge means only by o(1).
The original limits quoted in the proof are correct:
exp[-(a^2-1)/4] for each internal factor and
exp[-(b-1)^2/2] for the cross factor. When b=1, residual cross degrees
are at most one and that factor is automatically simple.
Reference: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.6344, Theorems 1.1--1.2 and 5.3.

There is also no new parity obstruction. Each residual internal total
is aN-tD, and
tD=(delta+1)(delta+2b)=delta(delta+1)+2b(delta+1)
is even. Cross totals on both sides are bN-2bt, hence equal. At all
sufficiently large admissible N these pools have their required sizes.
Symmetry under planted-vertex and stub relabeling makes the residual
lower bound uniform over embeddings and assignments.

Thus in the uniform-simple target model a fixed motif embedding has
probability Theta(N^(-e)), and its total labeled-embedding count Y
has EY=Theta(N^(v-e))=Theta(N^(-nu)). This supplies a genuine simple
completion lower bound, not a transfer from unconditional simplicity
that might collapse under the motif conditioning.

5. PROPER-INTERSECTION LEMMA AND THE SECOND MOMENT
--------------------------------------------------
The graph-theoretic lemma is valid: in any connected H of minimum
degree >=2, every proper subgraph F with nonempty vertex set obeys

  e(F)-v(F)<=e(H)-v(H)-1.

If F spans and omits an edge this is immediate. Otherwise remove
r>0 vertices R. Connectedness with a nonempty retained set gives
B>=1 crossing edges. If E_R are the removed-set internal edges,
minimum degree gives 2E_R+B>=2r, so E_R+B>=r+B/2>r. The deleted
edge count is integer and hence >=r+1. Further edge deletions can
only reduce excess. Isolated vertices of F are included in this
argument and cannot be dropped from its v(F).

For two motif embeddings, F is their COMMON VERTEX set and common
edge set, pulled back into one copy. It is a subgraph in exactly the
sense needed. If not the whole copy, its excess is <=nu-1. If all
vertices and edges coincide, the remaining multiplicity is bounded
by a fixed number of typed automorphisms and is a diagonal case.
No distinct large-overlap case escapes this dichotomy.

For any fixed finite typed union J, its prescribed-stub upper count
is <=C_J N^(-e(J)) at specified vertices in the simple model. A union
whose typed degrees exceed available degrees has probability zero.
For every fixed overlap identification type, the number of vertex
assignments is O(N^(v(J))). The union excess is

  e(J)-v(J)=2nu-[e(F)-v(F)].

Thus its contribution to E Y^2 is at most
O(N^[-2nu+e(F)-v(F)]). There are finitely many such types:

  no common vertices: O(N^(-2nu));
  proper nonempty intersection: O(N^(-nu-1));
  identical vertex-and-edge image: O(N^(-nu)).

Consequently E Y^2=O(N^(-nu)). The actual diagonal term EY already
has that order, consistent with this estimate. Cauchy-Schwarz yields
Pr(Y>0)>=(EY)^2/EY^2=Omega(N^(-nu)). There is no assumption of
independence for disjoint occurrences; the finite union probability
bound handles their global configuration correlations. Overlap types
that cannot coexist simply contribute zero.

Combined with Sections 2--4, this proves the claimed Theta ambiguity
rate for every fixed a>b>0, including (2,1), and every admissible N.

6. THE HIDDEN-UNIFORM PRIOR GIVES THE CLAIMED BAYES ERROR
--------------------------------------------------------
For each balanced ordered sign assignment sigma, the graph likelihood
is exactly 1/[G_a(N)^2 B_b(N)] when sigma is equitable (a,b), and zero
otherwise. Given sigma, its internal/cross decomposition is unique;
the factor counts depend only on N,a,b and not on the vertex labels.
Thus a uniform prior on hidden balanced assignments gives a posterior
uniform over admissible ordered assignments. Passing to unordered
partitions divides every admissible multiplicity by exactly two,
so the posterior remains uniform.

With K(G)>=1 valid unordered partitions, the best conditional success
probability is 1/K(G), including for randomized estimators. Hence

  (1/2)Pr[K(G)>=2] <= Bayes_error <= Pr[K(G)>=2].

Ambiguity is invariant under relabeling; its hidden-prior probability
equals the fixed-planted-block probability already analyzed. The Bayes
error is therefore Theta(N^(-nu)). This statement is correctly
qualified by the prior: with deterministically declared known planted
vertex sets a constant estimator would have zero error, so that
experiment could not support the same information-theoretic claim.

The motif also gives a compact nonzero w independent of sigma and
thus an extra real kernel vector, establishing a LOWER bound of this
order for real nullity >=2. It gives no matching real-nullity upper
bound, and the proof does not claim one.

7. RATE-ATTAINING POLYNOMIAL CUTOFF
----------------------------------
A polynomial fixed-prime moment suffices here; no sharp O(1) raw moment
or merely qualitative corank-one event is being used. At p=3 the
actual aggregate-fiber theorem gives O(N^50), so certainly the larger
kappa stated in the source is valid. Compute the F_3 kernel exactly
and cap its size at T_N=N^(kappa+nu+1). Markov bounds cap failure by
O(N^(-nu-1)). Every true sign solution reduces to this kernel since
the two residues 1 and -1 are distinct modulo three.

Enumerate kernel vectors, discard zero coordinates, lift 1,2 to
integer signs, and verify the original exact neighbor-count equations.
Passing candidates are automatically balanced by D!=delta; an explicit
balance check is harmless. Identify global exchanges. When the cap
does not trigger and the valid unordered partition is unique, this
returns the plant. Total failure is bounded by cap failure plus
ambiguity, namely O(N^(-nu)). Choosing an arbitrary verified partition
on ambiguity instead of reporting it gives the same-order bound in
the usual single-output estimation convention.

The stated O(N^(kappa+nu+2)+N^3) elementary-operation bound is
achievable using a base-three odometer/Gray update: over the whole
kernel pass it makes O(3^r) basis-column additions, each O(N), rather
than recomputing every combination at O(Nr) cost. Exact checks are
O_{a,b}(N) per vector. Even a looser implementation remains polynomial,
but this incremental enumeration supplies the particular exponent
shown in the proof. All degrees and exponents are fixed constants;
the bit overhead and Gaussian elimination are polynomial.
