Deterministic proof
--------------------
1. The common subspace H=1-perp is essential and correctly retained.
   Both connected Laplacians have the same one-dimensional kernel. All
   incidence update columns and every forward/backward recurrence vector
   lie in H. Sparse X=L/c-I and Y=L'/c-I therefore suffice; their norm-one
   constant eigenvectors never enter any estimate using r<1. No dense
   projector or uncharged orthogonalization is needed.

2. With c=(Lambda+gamma)/2, the assumed common interval gives
   ||X|H||,||Y|H||<=r=(Lambda-gamma)/(Lambda+gamma). The noncommutative
   identity Y^j-X^j=sum_l X^l(Y-X)Y^(j-1-l) is correctly ordered.
   Its norm bound is j r^(j-1)||E||/c on H and on the full space, since
   its restriction to constants is exactly zero.

3. For b_j=binom(alpha,j), the exact identity
     j|b_j|=alpha product_{i=1}^{j-1}(1-alpha/i)<=alpha
   holds for j>=1 and 0<alpha<1. The uniformly convergent binomial
   series on [-r,r] and the telescoping bound give tail
     alpha c^(alpha-1)||E|| r^q/(1-r).
   This treats the difference itself; it does not subtract two unscaled
   absolute approximations and lose control for tiny updates.

4. The common interval gives ||E||<=Lambda-gamma<=Lambda even for
   noncommuting endpoints: apply gamma I<=L,L'<=Lambda I to their
   difference. Consequently
     c^(alpha-1)||E||^(1-alpha)<=(Lambda/c)^(1-alpha)<=2.
   Equations (3),(10),(11) therefore prove intrinsic error
   epsilon||E||^alpha with no minimum update-size assumption.
   E=0 is separately and correctly handled.

5. Since log(r)<=-(1-r), the degree condition (4), together with q>=1,
   is sufficient for (3). Its constants depend on the supplied spectral
   interval ratio rather than the graph size or update magnitude. The
   geometric stopping test uses O(q) scalar operations, so no logarithm
   or arbitrary power oracle is silently needed to choose q.

Complete factors and work
--------------------------
6. The source's exact identity is
     F_q=c^(alpha-1)sum_{l=0}^{q-1} X^l U J W_l^T,
     W_l=sum_{j=l+1}^q b_j Y^(j-1-l)U.
   Symmetry of Y permits the displayed transpose. Backward Horner
   W_l=b_{l+1}U+Y W_{l+1}, W_q=0, computes all right blocks with only
   q sparse N-by-k block products. Forward powers compute the left
   blocks in the same order of work. There is no quadratic-in-q loop
   hidden by the polynomial expansion.

7. Concatenating these blocks gives width kq and O((M+N)kq) ordinary
   arithmetic including factor entries, coefficient generation and dense
   zero initialization if used. Diagonal J and a common scalar scaling
   cost O(Nkq), not O(Nk^2). Explicit output expansion is separately
   charged as O(N^2kq). The resulting exact product is symmetric because
   it equals the polynomial difference; termwise symmetrization gives
   a signed factor of width at most 2kq with no dense small-core solve.

8. The alpha=1/2 algorithm uses arithmetic and square roots. General
   fixed alpha explicitly charges one scalar c^(alpha-1) evaluation;
   the binomial coefficients need only arithmetic with alpha. Nothing
   here establishes floating-point stability or finite-word complexity.

Random-regular and adaptive-update corollary
---------------------------------------------
9. Independently inspected the ACTUAL PRIMARY SOURCE:
   Charles Bordenave, A new proof of Friedman's second eigenvalue Theorem
   and its extension to random lifts, arXiv:1502.04482v4, PDF page 2.
     https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.04482
   Theorem 1 is for the uniform SIMPLE d-regular graph, fixed d>=3,
   along admissible N with Nd even, and bounds max(lambda_2,|lambda_N|)
   by 2sqrt(d-1)+zeta with probability tending to one. Its equation (2)
   gives the stated N^-a bound, for fixed 0<a<1, at a deviation
   c(d,a)(log log N/log N)^2. The source distinguishes this second
   eigenvalue from a definition omitting repeated trivial eigenvalues.
   Thus the good event does ensure connectivity when zeta is chosen
   below d-2sqrt(d-1). The manuscript transfers the correct source model.

10. On that host event, L|H>=gamma I and L<=2d I. ANY conductance
    additions with total budget W0 have E>=0 and
    ||E||<=tr(E)=2sum_e d_e<=2W0. Therefore every such updated operator
    lies in the SAME interval [gamma,2d+2W0] on H. Additions preserve
    connectivity. This is a deterministic implication of one host event,
    so updates may be chosen adaptively after observing the host without
    a union bound over edge choices. Growing k is allowed with the
    stated k and input-size costs; W0 must remain fixed for the displayed
    fixed-constant corollary.

11. The imported project already reviews this spectral-gap input in
    snapshot/results/050-finite-defect.md and its batch-050 review.
    The new deduction therefore connects an ACTUAL imported spectral-gap
    result to the matrix-function update objective. It does not use the
    sharper SG-11 mixed permutation envelope or SG-04 cubic threshold.

12. For signed edits, total removed weight W_- produces a negative
    perturbation of norm at most 2W_-. Weyl gives the stated surviving
    lower gap gamma0-2W_->0; additions cannot reduce it. The endpoint
    upper bound is controlled by the base operator and added weight.
    If this gap condition fails, no logarithmic-degree claim is made.

Significance and remaining scope
---------------------------------
The log(1/epsilon) rank dependence improves the gap-free log-squared
bound when a common positive spectral interval is available. Its full
factor work and intrinsic update scaling are proved, including arbitrarily
small updates and the common-kernel issue. The graph-expansion corollary
is uniform over all budgeted additions and has an elementary deterministic
implementation once the gap event holds.

Polynomial and rational low-rank matrix-function updates are classical,
including Beckermann--Kressner--Schweitzer and later work. This report
does not determine whether the exact combined statement is already an
explicit corollary in that literature. It proves no new graph eigenvalue
theorem, no unrestricted quadratic matrix multiplication, no permutation
embedding theorem, and no lower bound establishing sharp logarithmic
rank for the fixed-gap graph class. Those limits are correctly preserved
in the reviewed source.
