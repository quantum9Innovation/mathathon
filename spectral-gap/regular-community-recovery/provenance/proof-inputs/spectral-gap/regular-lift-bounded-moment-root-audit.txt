2. ZERO PHASES: THE FINITE-GROUP ARGUMENT IS VALID

Let Z_1,...,Z_d take values in V=F_p^r, have a fixed sum, and have every
d-1 tuple mutually independent. Define H_j to be the linear span of support
differences of Z_j. For any j!=t, condition the other d-2 coordinates to
arbitrary supported values. The retained-product assumption makes this event
positive and leaves each conditional one-coordinate marginal unchanged.
The equation Z_j+Z_t=c therefore gives the reflection identity

  mu_t(z)=mu_j(c-z),

which makes H_j=H_t. These spaces are all a common H. A third coordinate
exists since d>=3. Changing its conditioned value changes c by every support
difference of that coordinate. Comparing the reflection identities makes a
marginal invariant under translation by these differences, hence all of H.
Since its support lies in one coset of H, that marginal is uniform on the
ENTIRE coset. This excludes both nonuniform subgroup measures and supports
which are proper subsets of a coset. Over F_p, the subgroup generated by
support differences is indeed an F_p-linear subspace.

At equality in the all-slot entropy identity, each root is independent of
each neighbor, and each retained neighbor tuple is conditionally independent
given the root. Thus conditional on ANY supported root value, the preceding
lemma applies with the same neighbor marginals. The root laws themselves
are seen as neighbor laws at other base vertices and therefore have the form

  mu_i=Uniform(a_i+H_i).

All neighbors of any old type i have the same H_j. Equality of subspaces
propagates along walks of even length. On a connected nonbipartite base all
vertices are joined by even walks, so there is ONE common H. The local
equation for every root value is equivalent to

  H R subseteq H,
  a_i R-sum_(j~i) a_j in H.

Conversely these conditions produce exactly one star law: a uniform root
and any d-1 independent uniform coset neighbors, with the last determined
by the equation. Its omitted neighbor remains in its prescribed coset, and
all required product marginals hold. This verifies necessity, sufficiency,
and uniqueness after choosing the cosets. The quotient offsets need NOT
lift to an exact base intertwiner over V. This distinction is handled by
affine fibers in the sparse module rather than assumed away.

There are finitely many phases for fixed B,p,r,R. Intermediate invariant
subspaces are essential when r>1; keeping only the full uniform and point
phases would be an error. The present sources keep all of them.

3. NEAR-COSET REDUCTION: THE HOMOGENEOUS KERNEL IS THE RIGHT FIBER

Fix a proper invariant H and a compatible family a_i+H. For a full solution
define q_v=Y_v mod H-a_type(v) mod H. Since the offsets solve the quotient
base equation, q solves the HOMOGENEOUS quotient intertwiner equation.
Its support is exactly the number of vertices outside the chosen cosets.

For each fixed quotient assignment q, choose arbitrary representatives in V.
The remaining equations for h_v in H form an affine linear system whose
homogeneous operator is

  L_(G,H): h -> A_G h-h R|_H.

The solution fiber is either empty or a translate of its kernel, so has size
at most Z_H(G)=|ker L_(G,H)|. This remains true for q=0 even when the offsets
are not exact V-valued base solutions. There is no unsupported assertion
that all quotient cosets lift, nor any replacement of affine equations by
homogeneous equations with a different cardinality.

If S=supp(q), then every outside vertex touched by S has at least two
neighbors in S: exactly one would give a nonzero vector equal to zero in V/H.
With t=|S|, internal edges e, outside vertices ell, and all incident edges
E=dt-e, the exact degree count gives

  ell<=(dt-2e)/2,
  E-(t+ell)>=(d-2)t/2.

This uses no injectivity of the induced R and no condition on equations
inside S. It also remains valid in characteristic two.

4. THE CONDITIONAL MOMENT COUPLING REALLY CONTROLS CORRELATION

For a prescribed matching pair u->v, swap the images of u and the current
preimage w of v. Earlier prescribed pairs remain fixed, because neither
the new domain nor the new target occurred earlier. Starting from the
uniform permutation conditional on t pairs, every permissible output after
the next forcing step has exactly N-t preimages: choose w from the unused
domains and undo the swap. Therefore the output is EXACTLY the conditional
uniform matching law, not merely a graph containing the certificate.
Independence between base-edge permutations is preserved by applying this
procedure separately on each edge.

For a certificate of E prescribed lift edges this coupling uses at most E
image swaps. Each swap changes at most four vertex rows of L_(G,H), each
of scalar block dimension s=dim H, giving the deterministic inequality

  rank(L_(G^C,H)-L_(G,H))<=4sE,
  Z_H(G^C)<=p^(4sE) Z_H(G).

The direction of the kernel inequality is correct: nullity can increase by
at most the perturbation rank. Taking expectation under this coupling yields

  E[Z_H(G) | C]<=p^(4sE) E Z_H(G).

This is the key step preventing a rare defect event from silently being
treated as independent of a possibly enormous bulk kernel. It is valid even
when that unconditional kernel moment has not yet been bounded. It then
enters the induction as precisely M_H(N), not a new conditioned model.

The weighted support enumeration also checks. For t<=N/(2d), a prescribed
certificate has probability at most (2/N)^E. Choose its t+ell vertices,
nonzero quotient values (at most p^(rt)), and stub pairs. The resulting bound
is

  E[#solutions with exactly t quotient defects]
    <=M_H(N) d t L^t (t/N)^(alpha t),
  alpha=(d-2)/2,

for fixed L depending only on B,p,r. The factor p^(4sE) is at most
p^(4rdt) and is absorbed in L^t. It is exponential in t, not in N after t
is summed over a sufficiently small fixed linear range. The factorial
denominators t!ell!, the inequalities t^t/t!<=exp(t) and
sum_ell t^ell/ell!<=exp(t), and E-(t+ell)>=alpha t produce exactly the
displayed negative support power. No missing factor N^(c t) remains.

For fixed small epsilon, consecutive terms decrease geometrically through
t<=epsilon N. Support one is impossible in a simple positive-degree lift.
The sum therefore starts at t=2 and is

  O_(B,p,r)(N^(-(d-2))) M_H(N).

The all-in-coset sector is at most M_H(N). Since H is proper, M_H is exactly
the same original model with vector dimension dim H<r and matrix R|_H.
The dimension-zero base case is identically one. This closes the intended
nonbipartite induction without any hidden spectral-gap assumption.

5. CENTRAL SECTOR: CHECKS RELEVANT TO THE BOUNDED UPPER, NOT ITS EXACT LIMIT

I checked the following facts in central-lift-uniform.md independently.

The two weighted counting identities are correctly normalized: the legal-
star model P has q^d local stars and the unrestricted-neighbor model Q has
q^(d+1), whereas the same matching-factorial weight W satisfies

  E Z_R=q^(dkN) E_P W,
  q^(dkN) E_Q W=1.

Under P, the root and any d-1 neighbors are independent uniform; therefore
any two neighbors are independent conditional on the root for d>=3. This
gives the same pair-table mean and covariance as Q, for arbitrary R. On the
real space with equal row margins across each old type's slots, the covariance
is q^(-2)[I+(d-1)P_row], positive definite. No inverse of R or division by d
in the finite field occurs.

The claimed maximal lattice index q^(kq) is consistent with, and follows
from, the character argument. For each root label a a constant character on
all legal neighbor tuples must be a common additive character of their sum,
plus slot constants. Varying two coordinates with a third compensating forces
this Cauchy equation; d>=3 is used here as well. Modulo the unrestricted
common-row lattice, there are q independent character choices per root
label, hence q^q per old type. This counts ALL unit-modulus periods, rather
than only exhibiting some congruences. The unrestricted real covariance has
already shown that the legal lattice has finite index and full real rank.

For the relative LLT used here, finite support, positive covariance, and
maximal lattice are enough: a tilt of size O(log N/sqrt(N)) centers any
lattice point in the logarithmic window; Fourier inversion has one Gaussian
maximum modulo the maximal reciprocal lattice, a uniform modulus gap away
from it, and a uniform cubic expansion near it. The cutoff N^(-2/5) makes
N times the cubic error tend to zero. Undoing the tilt contributes rate
Taylor error O(log^3 N/sqrt(N)), hence relative error o(1). Since P and Q
have identical covariance, their local probability ratio on the legal
affine lattice is q^(kq)(1+o(1)). Nonnegative W and the EXACT Q normalization
give the bounded central logarithmic-window contribution immediately.

The fixed-neighborhood tail does not rely on an invalid unweighted CLT:
on compatible deviations X, the combined Gaussian/factorial exponent is

  -(q/2)sum_i||r_i||^2-(q^2/2)sum_edges||Z_ij||^2,

strictly negative on every nonzero compatible direction. The dimension
identity e(q^2-1)-h+s=0 checks the powers of N. Analytic Cramer rates near
the mean preserve positive quadratic precision after shrinking the fixed
neighborhood. Chernoff plus Stirling gives a polynomial prefactor times
exp(-c||m-Nmu||^2/N); outside the sqrt(N)log N window this is summable.
Thus the required central upper is bounded. The scalar exact coefficient
p^dim ker(B-lambda I), which has an additional compatible-lattice rank
calculation, is not needed for (A) and is outside this audit's verdict.

6. EXPLICIT GLOBAL ASSEMBLY FOR THE ASSIGNED SCOPE

Induct on r. For each proper phase (H,a mod H), choose its sparse-neighborhood
epsilon as above. Its expected contribution is at most

  [1+C N^(-(d-2))] M_H(N),

bounded by the induction hypothesis. There are finitely many such phases.
Choose also the fixed full-uniform edge-table neighborhood from the central
module, whose expected contribution is bounded.

These sets are actual neighborhoods of every zero-entropy law in the compact
feasible star-law polytope: for a proper phase, sufficiently close root laws
have total mass outside its cosets below epsilon; for the full phase its
edge tables lie in the central neighborhood. They may overlap, which is
harmless for an upper bound. On the closed complement, the continuous entropy
exponent has maximum -c<0, because Section 2 classified every zero.

The exact ordered-star factorial formula and the polynomial number of types
then bound this complement by C(N+1)^B exp(-cN), using uniform Stirling errors
also at zero entries. This supplies the missing global tail without assuming
a quantitative entropy estimate across a boundary singularity. Adding the
central, proper-phase, and remaining-tail contributions gives bounded limsup
as N tends to infinity. The finitely many smaller N each have finitely many
assignments, so their maximum is finite and (A) follows. Uniformity over R
for fixed p,r follows simply by taking a maximum over its finite matrix set.

This assembly does not require disjoint phase neighborhoods or a unique
smallest H for a particular empirical assignment. It also does not infer
bounded expectation from a high-probability support event; the weighted
conditional estimate in Section 4 is essential.

DEGREE-TWO SANITY CHECK (A REAL COUNTEREXAMPLE OUTSIDE THE SCOPE)

The degree threshold cannot simply be removed. Let B be an odd cycle and
take scalar R=2 mod p. Its random N-lift is a disjoint union of cycles;
its number of connected components has the distribution of the number C_N
of cycles of a uniform permutation of N points (gauge away all but one
base-edge permutation). A separate constant value on each component is
always in ker(A-2I), so the expected kernel size is at least E p^(C_N).
Inserting the last permutation point either into an existing cycle or as
a new singleton gives exactly

  E p^(C_N)=p(p+1)...(p+N-1)/N!
            =binom(N+p-1,p-1),

which grows like N^(p-1)/(p-1)!. Thus even a connected nonbipartite base
does not rescue bounded moments at d=2. This agrees with the vanished
entropy coefficient and the failed sparse-support geometric exponent.
