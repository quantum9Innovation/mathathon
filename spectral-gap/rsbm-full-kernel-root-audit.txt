2. THE PRIMARY PRECONDITIONERS HAVE THE REQUIRED EXACT SCOPE
------------------------------------------------------------
Take K=F_q, D with independent uniform entries in K*, and T=DM.
Chen et al. Theorem 4.2 applies over any field and gives failure at
most n(n-1)/(2(q-1)) for cyclicity away from zero. Their Theorem 4.5
applies to symmetric M over any field, preserves rank, and bounds
nontrivial nilpotent zero blocks by r/(q-1). Their Theorem 4.3 has
a characteristic restriction and is not needed. The quoted statements
and the manuscript's application agree.

Thus, on the common good event, K^n=ker(T) direct-sum im(T), with
T restricted to im(T) invertible and cyclic of dimension k=rank M.
The SAME random D is used by both results; only a union bound is
needed, not independence of their success events. D is invertible,
so ker(T)=ker(M) and rank(T)=rank(M).

Zero semisimplicity supplies the direct sum and its k-dimensional
invertible part. Cyclicity supplies a degree-k minimal polynomial of
that part. Nonzero repeated roots are allowed: polynomial annihilation
and its nonzero constant coefficient suffice for every later step.
No diagonalizability/squarefreeness assertion is being silently used.

3. SHIFTED FINITE-SEQUENCE COMPLEXITY IS AN UNCONDITIONAL CERTIFICATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
For arbitrary D,u,v, consider a_j=u^T T^(j+1)v, 0<=j<2n.
The vector Tv lies in the invariant image space of T, of dimension
rank T. Cayley-Hamilton on that space gives a recurrence of order
at most rank T for the ENTIRE infinite scalar sequence and therefore
for this finite prefix. Exact BM shortest length ell consequently
satisfies

  ell<=rank(T)<=rank(M).

For the sampled invertible D equality holds in the last inequality.
This lower-rank certificate is valid even with nontrivial nilpotent
zero blocks, hidden modes, or arbitrary unlucky scalar probes.
It does not require that the returned polynomial annihilate all of T.

BM must supply its TRUE finite shortest-register length, with the
associated monic FORWARD recurrence polynomial of degree ell. If
connection coefficients are returned in backward order they must be
reversed; transient cases may have zero final connection coefficient.
The manuscript explicitly handles this and rejects g(0)=0. The
prototype retains the BM length separately, pads coefficients through
that length, reverses them for Horner evaluation, and rejects that
zero constant case. The zero sequence gives ell=0,g=1.

Suppose the trial has n-ell independent VERIFIED kernel columns.
They give rank(M)<=ell, exactly matching the unconditional lower bound.
Acceptance therefore ALWAYS means rank(M)=ell and a complete kernel
basis, even on every random draw outside the good event. If ell
underestimates rank, the requested n-ell columns exceed the actual
kernel dimension and no such trial can accept. If an erroneous
polynomial action misses the kernel, its residual check prevents
insertion; zero/dependent columns cannot inflate the verified rank.

When n-ell=0, the empty kernel is sound because ell=n already proves
full rank. Rejecting first if g(0)=0 can only postpone an otherwise
sound result; it cannot introduce false acceptance.

4. GOOD-PROBE PROBABILITY AND EXACT PROJECTION POLYNOMIAL
---------------------------------------------------------
Condition on a good diagonal preconditioner. Let C denote the
invertible cyclic k-dimensional image restriction. The projected
start Tv is uniform on im(T), and u restricts to a uniform linear
functional there. This holds even if im(T) is isotropic for the
ambient dot product: restriction of arbitrary ambient functionals
onto its dual is still surjective.

Controllability and observability determinants are nonzero degree-k
polynomials in v and u, respectively. Existence follows from cyclicity
of C and its transpose. Schwartz-Zippel bounds their combined failure
by 2k/q. If both are nonzero the leading k-by-k scalar Hankel matrix
is nonsingular, so the prefix complexity is at least k. Section 3
gives the matching upper bound. Its monic recurrence is uniquely fixed
by the first 2k terms and equals the actual minimal polynomial of C.
The 2n computed terms are sufficient since k<=n. This also reconstructs
the scalar recurrence fact independently of any strengthened reading
of the Wiedemann citation.

Hence the event G satisfies

  Prob(G^c)<=[n(n-1)/2+r]/(q-1)+2k/q=O(n^2/q),
  ell=k, g(0)!=0, g(T)=g(0)P_(ker T parallel im T).

The polynomial identity follows by evaluating on the direct-sum
components; it remains valid with repeated nonzero roots. At k=0
the image is zero, g=1, and the identity projection is obtained.

Fresh independent uniform b give independent uniform g(T)b in the
r-dimensional kernel. No dependence on the prior scalar-probe event
is introduced because these b are fresh and G does not involve them.
Their uncapped expected span time is

  sum_(j=0)^(r-1) 1/(1-q^(j-r)) <= r+2.

The r=0 case needs no samples. After n+1 samples, a union bound over
the (q^r-1)/(q-1) hyperplanes gives failure probability

  [(q^r-1)/(q-1)] q^[-(n+1)] <=1/[q(q-1)].

This includes the worst case r=n. Consequently every fixed input
matrix has per-trial acceptance probability at least 1/2 for the
chosen q>=64(n+1)^6. No input matrix is stranded outside a hidden
generic-rank promise.

5. EVERY TRIAL IS CAPPED AND ITS EXPECTED WORK IS FULLY CHARGED
--------------------------------------------------------------
Computing 2n shifted terms uses 2n sparse applications of T and
O(n^2) dot-product work. BM costs O(n^2) field operations and O(n)
field storage. Together these cost O(n(w+n)). A degree-ell Horner
action needs at most ell<=n applications, with the same O(n(w+n))
upper bound, including its vector additions. Residual verification
costs O(w+n); incremental independence costs O(n(j+1)) when j
columns are retained, including its initial nonzero scan.

On G, the truncated span procedure is no longer than its uncapped
span time. Since it retains at most r columns, its expected costs
are bounded by

  O(n(w+n)(r+1)+nr(r+1))=O(n(w+n)(r+1)).

On ANY bad trial, at most n+1 polynomial probes are attempted and
at most r verified independent vectors can be stored. This caps
its arithmetic at O(n^2(w+n)), including all independence operations.
Thus its bad-event contribution to expected arithmetic is

  O(n^2(w+n))*O(n^2/q)=O(n^4(w+n)/q),

absorbed by the stated bound. The cap also applies when ell is too
small to be certifiable or g(T) has an unfavorable range. An early
g(0)=0 rejection is cheaper than this bound.

Random-field sampling uses rejection only with uniformly bounded
expected overhead. The deterministic cap here is an arithmetic/
accepted-field-sample count; the bit-sampling implementation may
have an unbounded rejection count. This does not break the charge:
standard rejection's trial count is independent of its accepted
uniform value, so its conditional expected bit cost remains bounded
even on the bad algebraic event. Alternatively use uniform base-field
coefficients with the same property. Thus no rare-event conditioning
silently multiplies an uncontrolled random-bit cost.

Independent retries, conditional on the fixed verified extension,
obey E(total)=E(one trial)/Prob(one trial accepts), even when work
and acceptance within a trial are correlated. Since acceptance is
uniformly >=1/2, this proves finite expected work and almost-sure
termination for every fixed M. There is no graph-probability event
or dense rational fallback in this all-input Las Vegas argument.

Boundary cases check directly. At M=0, ell=0,g=1 and the algorithm
collects n independent uniform vectors; its possibly cubic work fits
n(w+n)(r+1) with r=n,w=0. At full rank, good trials certify ell=n
and accept an empty basis after the sequence work. Symmetric inputs
that are nilpotent before preconditioning remain within the theorem.

6. EXTENSION CONSTRUCTION AND BIT COMPLEXITY
-------------------------------------------
The least s with q=p^s>=64(n+1)^6 has s=O_p(log(n+1)) and
q<64p(n+1)^6. Rank is unchanged under extension since the same
nonzero minors determine it. Sample monic degree-s polynomials
and accept only after an EXACT irreducibility test.

The elementary test in the manuscript is valid as written: require
gcd(h,X^(p^i)-X mod h)=1 for EVERY 1<=i<=floor(s/2). Any reducible
degree-s polynomial has an irreducible factor of degree at most s/2,
which appears in its corresponding test; an irreducible degree-s
polynomial fails none. Repeated-factor cases are caught as well.
For s=1 all monic linear polynomials are irreducible. Hence this
all-small-degrees test does not need an additional Frobenius-at-s
condition; it is a valid elementary alternative to the abbreviated
prime-divisor criterion.

The monic irreducible count is at least p^s/(2s), so search succeeds
with probability >=1/(2s) per candidate. One elementary proof uses
p^s=sum_(d|s)d I_p(d) and bounds the proper-divisor contribution by
sum_(j<=s/2)p^j<=p^s/2 for fixed odd p and s>=2. Thus O(s) expected
candidates suffice. Successive modular p-th powers and classical
polynomial gcd take polynomial(s) deterministic bit work per test.
Construction is therefore expected polylog(n) bit work and almost
surely terminates, independently of any matrix rank.

Represent elements as s base-field coefficients. Schoolbook modular
polynomial operations, extended-Euclid inversion (or exponentiation),
zero tests, and uniform sampling cost polynomial(s)=polylog(n) bits
for fixed p. This justifies soft-O rather than unit-cost arithmetic
over an unbounded field. Construction is done once and reused; the
trial probability bounds hold for every field obtained this way.

7. CANONICAL DESCENT, MEMORY, AND THE DETERMINISTIC CERTIFICATE
-------------------------------------------------------------
An accepted B in K^(n by r) spans the full extension kernel. Select
r rows J with B_J invertible and set C=B(B_J)^(-1). Then C_J=I_r.
Frobenius x->x^p preserves the kernel because M has base coefficients,
and preserves this pivot identity. Restriction to J is an isomorphism
of the kernel onto K^r, so each normalized basis column is uniquely
determined by its pivot coordinates. Hence C^(p)=C and every entry
is in F_p. This proves descent of the normalized basis, not of each
arbitrary extension-field probe separately.

Row selection, inversion and multiplication cost O(nr^2+r^3), bounded
by O(n(w+n)(r+1)) because r<=n. Storage is O(nr) field elements.
Check the nonconstant representation coefficients vanish, extract
the base entries, and recheck MC=0 exactly. The prototype's maintained
canonical pivot columns implement the same normalization incrementally.

The certificate contains h,D,u,v,ell,C,J. Its size is O_p(nr+n log n)
bits: C is over the fixed base field, while the three extension
vectors have O(n log n) bits; J costs at most O(n log n). A verifier
enforces the prescribed least extension degree/field-size bounds and
encodings (as the prototype explicitly does), tests irreducibility,
recomputes the shifted sequence and exact BM length, and checks

  r=n-ell, MC=0, C_J=I_r, valid distinct pivot rows J.

These checks alone give both rank inequalities deterministically.
The verifier need not trust g(T), the preconditioner probability,
Frobenius descent, or the generation algorithm. It need not perform
dense elimination or any O(nr^2) rank test: the pivot identity already
certifies independence. Cost is O(n(w+n)+wr+r^2) field/base operations,
within soft-O_p(n(w+n)) bits since r<=n.

Workspace throughout is O(n(r+1)+w) field/base elements: input sparse
storage, D,u,v, sequence/BM data, current probes, and <=r retained
columns. Temporary normalization r^2 data fit within nr. Each has
O_p(log(n+1)) bits, with sparse indices covered by the same soft
bound. A retry counter may be stored separately; its expected size
is finite under the uniform geometric success bound. No complete
Krylov matrix or exponentially long output list is needed as scratch.

8. COMPLETE BASE-FIELD ENUMERATION WITHOUT AN EXTRA r
----------------------------------------------------
Enumerate a in F_p^r and maintain its image Ca in base-field
arithmetic. In an odometer, changing a digit from p-1 to zero is
also addition of one modulo p, so a carry is just one further
basis-column addition. Across a full pass, the TOTAL digit changes
are at most p^r sum_(j>=0)p^(-j)=O_p(p^r). Each costs O(n).
Initialization/counter management is absorbed by O(n p^r), since
r<=n. No fresh O(nr) matrix-vector multiply is done per candidate.

Retain only vectors whose coordinates are 1 or -1 and whose final
coordinate is 1. For odd p the residues lift uniquely to integer
signs, and global sign exchange has precisely one such orientation.
Check balance and M_Z sigma=0 over the INTEGERS; modular residuals
alone would be insufficient. At bounded degree and bounded known
lambda these checks and writing a passing vector cost O_{d,p}(n)
bits per candidate (or the same soft bound under a conventional
counter implementation). The full generation/check/output charge
is therefore O_{d,p}(n p^r), with no r*p^r moment required.

Every integer sign kernel maps to a base-field kernel vector, and
different signs remain distinct modulo odd p. Thus the list is
complete on every input, including matrices with extra real or
modular kernel dimensions. At r=0 the single field vector is zero
and correctly produces no sign vector for n>=1. Odd graph orders
can simply produce no balanced sign solutions.

9. AVERAGING IS VALID UNCONDITIONALLY, WITH THE STATED RAW INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------
For fixed bounded-degree input and actual base corank r, the
conditional expected total work is

  soft-O_(d,p)(n^2(r+1)+n p^r).

This bound is valid for EVERY fixed input matrix, with uniform
constants. Since r+1<=O_p(p^r), a single actual-law raw moment
sup_n E p^r<=C controls its expectation and proves unconditional
expected soft-O_(d,p,C)(n^2) bit work. It also controls expected
basis workspace and output bits by soft-O(n) and O(n), respectively.
Returning a streamed list avoids using output as scratch; even if
the list is stored, its expected size is covered by the same moment.

If the raw bound is proved in the exact configuration model and
simplicity probability is bounded below, its transfer is the direct
inequality E[p^r|simple]<=E[p^r]/Prob(simple). This is legitimate for
an unbounded random variable. Qualitative contiguity or high-probability
real corank one would NOT supply this expectation bound; the source
explicitly avoids both shortcuts. An asymptotic bounded moment plus
the finitely many initial admissible sizes gives a finite supremum.

For regular RSBM parameters a,b, d=a+b, lambda=a-b, an integer sign
eigenvector has exactly a same-side and b opposite-side neighbors.
Balance imposes the equal-block partition requirement. Thus the
verified list is exactly the full admissible partition list for these
KNOWN parameters. The planted assignment is always included.
Returning the unique partition when unique, otherwise its complete
ambiguity list, has no algorithmic error or computational abstention;
any ambiguity is a property of the graph distribution. A separate
Boolean-rigidity theorem supplies high-probability uniqueness if desired.
