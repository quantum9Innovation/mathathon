CONSTRUCTIVE SHARP FRACTIONAL GRAPH UPDATES WITH BOUNDED ENDPOINTS
================================================================

Working theorem and proof, 2026-11-15 UTC. Independent root reconstruction
passed; see bounded-spectrum-graph-algorithm-independent-audit.txt.
This note supplies a charged sparse algorithm for the improved leading
width in bounded-spectrum-fractional-counts.txt. Polynomial subtraction
and rational matrix-function updates are established mechanisms. No new
scalar approximation rate or historical-priority claim is asserted.

1. INPUT, GUARANTEE, AND COST
============================
Fix 0<alpha<1 and R>1. Let K,K' be graph conductance Laplacians on N
vertices with a common positive diagonal mass matrix H, held fixed.
Write A=H^-1/2 K H^-1/2 and B=H^-1/2 K' H^-1/2. Suppose k>=1 distinct
edge conductances are increased by positive amounts d_e, and promise

  max(||A||,||B||)<=R||E||, E=B-A.                       (1)

Let M be the number of edges in their union. For epsilon,delta in (0,1),
there is an algorithm returning real general factors P,Q with

  Pr{||PQ^T-(B^alpha-A^alpha)||<=epsilon||E||^alpha}
       >=1-delta,                                       (2)

in expected ~O_(alpha,R)((M+N)k) arithmetic, including every factor entry.
For each fixed k, uniformly over graph size, spectra and masses, its
width is at most

  [k/(4 pi^2 alpha)+o_(alpha,R,k)(1)]log^2(1/epsilon).   (3)

More explicitly, with L=log(1/epsilon)+O_(alpha,R)(log(2k)), width is

  k L^2/(4 pi^2 alpha)+O_(alpha,R)(k L^(15/8)).          (4)

The leading coefficient is optimal on this graph class: the matching
lower already holds for k edge increases on a connected mass-weighted
path, by bounded-spectrum-fractional-counts.txt, Section6. Optimality
concerns output width, not runtime. The returned general factors need
not give an exactly symmetric product. Straight symmetrization doubles
width and is not assigned the sharp coefficient in this note.

The output is the function correction and requires no base matrix
function. Dense expansion adds O(N^2*width) arithmetic. At alpha=1/2,
the algorithm uses field operations, comparisons, positive scalar square
roots and discrete randomness. At general alpha the node-dependent
scalar powers are supplied/charged as in sharp-matrix-function-updates.tex.
No finite-word stability or deterministic runtime cap is claimed.

2. INTRINSIC NORMALIZATION AND THE EXACT SUBTRACTION
==================================================
As in sharp-graph-fractional-updates.txt, set

  U_e=sqrt(d_e)H^-1/2(e_u-e_v), E=UU^T,
  a=||E||_F, b=||U||_F^2=tr(E), chi=b/a.

Sparse edge arithmetic computes a,b in O(M+N+k) work, and

  ||E||<=a<=sqrt(k)||E||, 1<=chi<=sqrt(k).              (5)

Zero E is returned exactly. Otherwise take T=R*a. By (1), both A and B
have spectrum in [0,T]. Put C_A=I-A/T, C_B=I-B/T, so 0<=C_A,C_B<=I.

For an integer p>=2 and t off the negative real axis, the finite geometric
identity gives

 (A+tI)^-1
  =sum_(j=0)^(p-1) T^j C_A^j/(t+T)^(j+1)
     +[T/(t+T)]^p C_A^p(A+tI)^-1.                    (6)

The same holds for B. Let

  D_t=(A+tI)^-1-(B+tI)^-1,
  D_t^(p)=D_t-sum_(j=1)^(p-1)
                T^j(C_A^j-C_B^j)/(t+T)^(j+1).         (7)

The j=0 terms cancel. Equivalently D_t^(p) is the difference of the two
last terms in (6). The original fractional-power integral therefore is

 B^alpha-A^alpha
   =T^alpha sum_(j=1)^(p-1) beta_j(C_A^j-C_B^j)
        +c_alpha int_0^infinity t^alpha D_t^(p)dt,     (8)

where c_alpha=sin(pi*alpha)/pi and

  beta_1=alpha,
  beta_(j+1)=beta_j(j-alpha)/(j+1).                    (9)

Indeed c_alpha*int_0^infinity t^alpha/(t+T)^(j+1)dt
= beta_j*T^(alpha-j), for j>=1. This scalar beta-integral identity
also follows from the binomial expansion (1-z)^alpha=1-sum beta_j z^j.
The finite polynomial is computed by recurrence (9), not a gamma oracle.

3. WHY THERE IS NO FACTOR-p LOSS IN OUTPUT WIDTH
================================================
Let a_nu>=0 and s_nu>0 be logarithmic quadrature coefficients/nodes for
the remainder, and put t_nu=T*s_nu,

  omega_nu=a_nu*c_alpha*t_nu^(alpha+1),
  delta_j=beta_j-sum_nu
            a_nu*c_alpha*s_nu^(alpha+1)/(1+s_nu)^(j+1).

Substitution of (7) in the quadrature for (8) gives EXACTLY

 F= sum_nu omega_nu D_(t_nu)
      +T^alpha sum_(j=1)^(p-1) delta_j(C_A^j-C_B^j).   (10)

Thus there are q ORIGINAL rank-k resolvent nodes, plus ONE polynomial
difference of degree p-1. It is unnecessary to represent every remainder
node separately by p update blocks, which would incorrectly charge kpq.

Since C_A-C_B=E/T, telescoping gives a rank-k(p-1) factor for the
polynomial in (10). Explicitly put, for 0<=l<=p-2,

  V_l=C_A^l U,
  Z_l=sum_(j=l+1)^(p-1) delta_j C_B^(j-1-l)U.

Its value is

  T^(alpha-1)sum_(l=0)^(p-2)V_l Z_l^T.                (11)

Generate V forward and Z backward by Z_(p-1)=0 and
Z_l=delta_(l+1)U+C_B Z_(l+1). This costs O((M+N)kp) sparse arithmetic
and O(Nkp) factor storage. Computing all delta_j costs O(pq) scalar
operations from powers of (1+s_nu)^-1. The beta_j require O(p) work.

The rank-k node is D_t=V_t W_t^T, with
V_t=(A+tI)^-1 U and W_t=(B+tI)^-1 U. The shifted actions use the
SDD systems K+tH and K'+tH, exactly as in the existing graph algorithm.

4. A VARIABLE-SLOPE STRIP ENVELOPE
=================================
Use t=T*exp(y), and divide the logarithmic remainder integrand by T^alpha:

  F_p(y)=c_alpha*T^(-alpha)*t^(alpha+1) D_t^(p).

For |Im y|<=d<pi, the sector inequality
|lambda+t|>=cos(d/2)(lambda+|t|) for lambda>=0 implies, via (6),

  ||F_p(x+i theta)||
   <=C_alpha*cos(d/2)^(-(p+1))*exp(-g_p(x)),

  g_p(x)=alpha*(-x) for x<=0,
         (p-alpha)*x for x>=0.                         (12)

Here C_alpha is independent of p, dimensions, graph data and spectra.
For example bound each C_A^p and C_B^p by one, each resolvent by
1/(|t|cos(d/2)), and |T/(t+T)| by
1/[cos(d/2)(1+exp(x))]. The two real asymptotic slopes in (12) follow.

On the real line the envelope prefactor is independent of p, and the
real logarithmic derivative has a bound C_alpha(p+2)*exp(-g_p(x)).
Differentiate t^(alpha+1), the scalar (t+T)^-p, and the endpoint
resolvent separately. Each logarithmic derivative changes the real-line
bound by at most alpha+1, p, or one. This derivative bound is sufficient
for actual scalar-node rounding below.

5. UNIFORM MOVING-STRIP QUADRATURE FOR GROWING p
===============================================
This section gives the needed uniform-in-p account, instead of invoking
a fixed-slope O-constant while p grows. For sufficiently large L, let p
be the least power of two with p^8>=L. Thus

  L^(1/8)<=p<2L^(1/8).                                 (13)

It is found by integer doubling and comparisons. Put ell=L^(3/4), and
take d=pi_minus-L^(-1/4), where pi_minus<=pi is the certified lower
approximation from the existing constructive scalar routine, to error
exp(-C L). Then d>pi/2 for sufficiently large L.

Use intervals I_j=[j*ell,(j+1)*ell] and the entire Gaussian windows

  phi_j(z)=pi^(-1/2)int_(I_j)exp(-(z-u)^2)du.

They sum to one on the real line. Retain windows meeting

  [-(L+ell)/alpha, (L+ell)/(p-alpha)].                  (14)

For each window put m_j=min_(I_j)g_p and
Q_j=max(ell,L-m_j+3ell), h_j=2*pi_minus*d/Q_j. Keep the lattice points
x=n*h_j in I_j+[-B,B], with B=4sqrt(L+1). The ideal coefficient is
a=h_j*phi_j(x), nonnegative.

Here are explicit error controls. Since g_p has Lipschitz constant at
most p, convolution against a Gaussian costs at most exp(p^2/4):

  int exp(-g_p(x))*exp(-(x-u)^2)dx
       <=2sqrt(pi)exp(-g_p(u)+p^2/4).

Using (12) on the strip boundary adds cos(d/2)^(-(p+1))*exp(d^2).
The logarithm of the complete envelope prefactor is consequently

  O_alpha(p log(L+1)+p^2+1)=o(ell).                    (15)

For large enough L this is at most ell/4, after absorbing fixed constants.
All thresholds here are computable from the displayed elementary bounds.
For smaller requested L, increase it to a fixed threshold depending only
on alpha. This does not alter the asymptotic coefficient or introduce
input spectral parameters.

Omitted windows have m_j>=L+ell; their summed integrals are bounded by
poly(L)*exp(-L-ell+o(ell)). For retained windows, the ordinary strip
trapezoid error is at most a fixed factor times
ell*exp(-m_j-Q_j+o(ell)). Since m_j+Q_j>=L+3ell and there are
O_alpha(L/ell+1) windows, these errors sum to at most
poly(L)*exp(-L-3ell+o(ell)). Gaussian lattice tails outside the enlarged
intervals are at most poly(L)*exp(-2L). Thus the total normalized error
is O_alpha(exp(-L)), with only a fixed additive budget shift in L.

For completeness, the node count is also uniform in p. On each linear
tail, replacing the integral of (L-g_p)_+ by interval minima costs
O_alpha(L*ell+p*ell^2). The exact integral is

  int (L-g_p(x))_+ dx = (L^2/2)(1/alpha+1/(p-alpha)).   (16)

The 3ell buffer in Q_j costs O_alpha(L*ell+ell^2). Enlarging the
windows adds at most
O_alpha(sqrt(L)*(L^2/ell+p*ell)), and integer rounding adds O_alpha(L/ell+1).
Substitution of ell=L^(3/4) and (13), followed by
1/(pi_minus*d)=1/pi^2+O(L^(-1/4)), proves

  q <= L^2/(4pi^2)*(1/alpha+1/(p-alpha))
                 +O_alpha(L^(7/4))
    <=L^2/(4pi^2 alpha)+O_alpha(L^(15/8)).             (17)

Every retained |x| is O_alpha(L), the total interval length is O_alpha(L),
and h_j<1. The Gaussian lattice-sum estimate from the existing scalar
construction gives sum a_nu=O_alpha(L+1), uniformly in p.

6. ACTUAL SCALAR OPERATIONS AND NODE ERRORS
==========================================
The positive polynomial-square Gaussian-window implementation in
sharp-matrix-function-updates.tex, Lemma constructive, applies with the
following explicit uniform bounds. The number of windows and nodes is
polynomial in L. Clip window integrals to [-4sqrt(L+1),4sqrt(L+1)],
approximate exp(-w^2/2) by its Taylor polynomial of degree O(L), square
that polynomial, and integrate its polynomial primitive at the clipped
endpoints. The quadrature weights are nonnegative because they are
integrals of polynomial squares. The
same Taylor remainder calculation gives absolute weight error
O(exp(-16L)), after a fixed increase of the degree constant.

The actual positive nodes approximate exp(x) with logarithmic error
exp(-C L), by scaled Taylor approximation and repeated squaring, as in
that lemma. The real-line derivative bound after (12) grows only
polynomially in L. Together with the O(L) coefficient mass, these scalar
errors contribute O(exp(-L)) normalized error. All scalar construction
work is polynomial in L (the cited direct implementation is O(L^3)).
No Gaussian, exponential, gamma, or best-rational-approximation oracle
is invoked. Pi, log2 and c_alpha are fixed constants approximated by
convergent series to the budgeted precision. At alpha=1/2 the required
node powers are obtained by square roots.

Approximate each original D_t node by cross factors Vhat_t What_t^T.
Columnwise relative energy error eta gives the already proved estimate

  ||Vhat_t What_t^T-D_t||<=3 eta b/t^2.                 (18)

Although the REMAINDER has been quadratured, (10) uses only these original
nodes and the explicit polynomial compensation. Consequently the total
node error is at most

  3c_alpha*eta*b*T^(alpha-1)
                         sum_nu a_nu*s_nu^(alpha-1)
   <=C_alpha*eta*chi*a^alpha*(L+1)*exp(C_alpha L).       (19)

Here T=Ra, alpha<1 and R>1. All actual log-nodes have magnitude O_alpha(L).
Set eta to a sufficiently small dyadic number with
log(1/eta)=O_alpha(L+log(2k)), computably by halving, so (19) is at most
a^alpha*exp(-L). This does not depend on a graph gap, mass ratio or
endpoint conditioning. The implemented small scalar constants and
polynomial compensation coefficients receive the same fixed error budget.
For j>=1, the real coefficient integrand
s^(alpha+1)/(1+s)^(j+1) is uniformly bounded, so sum a_nu=O(L)
also bounds the scalar coefficient sensitivity by a polynomial in L.

Choose L by repeated halving so

  exp(-L)<=epsilon/[C_(alpha,R)(k+1)],
  L=log(1/epsilon)+O_(alpha,R)(log(2k)).                (20)

The quadrature error is O(T^alpha exp(-L)), node and scalar errors are
of the same order, and a^alpha<=k^(alpha/2)||E||^alpha. Hence (20),
with a large enough fixed constant, gives the target error (2).

7. FULL GRAPH WORK, PROBABILITY, AND SHARPNESS
============================================
There are 2kq shifted column solves, q rank-k node factors, and one
polynomial factor of width k(p-1). The total width is therefore
k(q+p-1), which gives (4) and (3). Sparse forward and backward powers
for (11) cost O((M+N)kp), including their factor entries. Node factors
cost O(Nkq) to write. Scalar compensation costs O(pq) and the window
construction is polynomial in L. Both p and q are polynomial in
log(1/epsilon), and log(1/eta) has the same type of bound, so these terms fit
expected ~O_(alpha,R)((M+N)k).

Apply the existing shifted-SDD energy solver to K+tH and K'+tH. Its
expected-time guarantee is the one audited in sharp-graph-fractional-
updates.txt. For arbitrary confidence, independently repeat constant-
success solves and select a candidate by median pairwise system-energy
distance, as proved in graph-fractional-solver-audit.txt. Allocate failure
delta/(2kq) to each column solve and use a union bound. The algorithm
does not need its approximate solve maps to be linear or symmetric.

Finally, bounded-spectrum-fractional-counts.txt proves a matching
universal PSD lower, already on connected mass-weighted paths with k
positive conductance increases satisfying (1). Its normalization is
exactly ||E||=1. No rank below the strict singular count can meet the
operator-norm error on those fixed examples, regardless of randomization.
Thus the actual construction, not only existence of a rational approximant,
attains the optimal leading width under (1). The generic unbounded-endpoint
constant k/[4pi^2 alpha(1-alpha)] is not being reused for this class.

The source ingredients include classical polynomial/rational updates and
Gaussian localization. In particular pole-plus-polynomial scalar
approximation has substantial prior literature. This note concerns the
charged graph realization, uniform growing-subtraction accounting and
the matching restricted matrix/graph width theorem. Historical novelty
and finite-precision stability remain separate questions.

8. REPRODUCIBLE ALGEBRA CORROBORATION
====================================
scripts/check_bounded_spectrum_graph_algorithm.py and retained output
bounded-spectrum-graph-algorithm-checks.txt check six positive common-mass
graph update pairs. Their known common graph kernel is removed only for
stable dense diagnostics. A dense norm computation chooses a safe T only
inside that diagnostic; the actual algorithm uses the supplied promise
and sparse scale T=R*a, as in Section2.

There are 96 finite-geometric-identity trials and 72 collected-quadrature
trials at alpha=0.3,0.5,0.7 and p=2,3,4,8. The polynomial Horner factors,
global rank-collection identity, and actual fractional correction all
pass. Maximum scaled geometric identity error was 2.59e-15, maximum
polynomial-factor discrepancy 6.84e-17, and maximum collected-rule
discrepancy 2.56e-16. Uniform-grid quadrature error was below 0.000127
times the requested T^alpha*exp(-L) budget.

These computations check signs and noncommutative factor order. They do
not implement or numerically verify the optimized Gaussian node rule,
its asymptotic leading coefficient, or the sparse shifted-solver backend.
Those claims rest on the analytic proof and the audited component inputs.
