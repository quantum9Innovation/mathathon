3. ANALYTIC REMAINDER: DERIVATIVE SCALE, RANK AND TELESCOPING
==========================================================

The three nested fixed Bernstein ellipses give exponential polynomial
approximation on a contour a positive distance from the spectral
interval. Cauchy's formula and

 (zI-B)^(-1)-(zI-A)^(-1)
   =(zI-B)^(-1)(B-A)(zI-A)^(-1)

therefore yield the genuinely derivative-scaled bound

 ||(h-p_q)(B)-(h-p_q)(A)|| <= C exp(-cq)||B-A||.

All contour distances and constants are fixed independently of input
dimension, q and the increment. A scalar absolute uniform error alone
would not suffice; the source correctly uses the resolvent difference.

The polynomial rank bound is valid for noncommuting A,B: every term
in B^k-A^k lies in the span of columns of B^ell E, ell<q. Thus rank
at most q*rank(E), rather than an erroneous quadratic degree factor.
For rank-one E of normt it gives sigma_(q+1)<=Ct exp(-cq), q>=0.
Summing over q proves the geometric trace bound (Ct)^s/(1-exp(-cs)).

For arbitrary-rank endpoints in I, D=length(I) bounds ||E||. The
straight-line segments stay in I because the matrix order interval is
convex. Within each of K fixed segments, a partial spectral sum of E/K
has norm at most D/K, including mixed signs. Its endpoints consequently
stay in the fixed enlarged interval I_delta. This justifies applying
the rank-one bound to every step. The resulting factor is exactly

 C^s K^(1-s) tr|E|^s/(1-exp(-cs)).

The inequality tr|E|^s<=D^((1-alpha)s)tr|E|^(alpha*s) is valid
eigenvalue by eigenvalue, including zero values. Hence H_s^I(h)=O(1/s).
The same derivative bound and ||E||<=D give O(r(L+1)) correction rank.
No inadmissible intermediate spectra or missing update-scale factor
were found.

4. THE ACTUAL SINGLE RANK-ONE GLUING CONSTRUCTION
===============================================

For fixed blocks C=diag(c_j I), the chosen matrix

 S_ij=-T_ij/(c_i-c_j), i!=j; S_ii=0

is anti-Hermitian, because T is Hermitian and the distinct centers are
real. With U_t=exp(tS), its sign is correct:

 U_t^*(C+tT)U_t=C+t(T+[C,S])+O(t^2)
              =C+t*diag(T_jj)+O(t^2).

All matrices are fixed and finite BEFORE t tends tozero. The gap
constants may depend arbitrarily badly on the chosen centers. The
operator alpha-Hoelder estimate changes the O(t^2) remainder by
O(t^(2alpha)); undoing U_t changes the bounded f-output by O(t).
Both are o(t^alpha), since 0<alpha<1. Local scalar expansions pass
to each fixed Hermitian diagonal block by spectral calculus. This
proves the stated block limit, including complex-valued f.

The positive perturbation is genuinely rank ONE, not a concealed direct
sum. In the construction

 H_j=(A_j+B_j)/2, v=direct_sum sqrt(p_j)v_j,
 T_pm=diag(p_j H_j)+/-vv*/2,

one has ||v||=1, T_plus-T_minus=vv*, and diagonal blocks
(T_minus)_jj=p_j A_j, (T_plus)_jj=p_j B_j. Thus

 E_t=B_t-A_t=t vv*, ||E_t||=t, tr|E_t|^(alpha*s)=t^(alpha*s)

EXACTLY. Its normalized output limit is the direct sum of
p_j^alpha [F_j(B_j)-F_j(A_j)]. The centers being interior guarantees
both global endpoint spectra stay in I once t is sufficiently small.
No fixed input matrix, separation, accuracy or lower update scale is
claimed uniformly in this limiting step.

6. INFINITE SERIES AND THE MOMENT LAW
====================================

The amplitude hypothesis A(u)~C u^nu implies a polynomial bound on A,
finiteness of D(s)=sum exp(-s b_j) for every positives, and summability
of the original coefficients. Thus f converges locally uniformly and
inherits the global operator alpha-Hoelder bound by the operator norm
triangle, uniformly on every finite matrix input.

At any selected center c_j, a disk avoiding all other centers exists:
there are no interior accumulation points. For k!=j, choose the analytic
power branch agreeing with |x-c_k|^alpha on that disk's real diameter.
All these branches are uniformly bounded on a smaller disk, since the
centers stay in compact I. Weighted summability gives normal convergence
and an analytic local remainder. This verifies the block-limit hypotheses
for the FULL infinite f, not merely its finite partial sum.

The infinite power upper passes first through finite partial sums on
one fixed finite matrix pair, then uses norm continuity of the power
trace. It is valid before taking the supremum. The lower uses only
finite selected sets and their independently chosen witnesses before
increasing the set. Therefore

 H_0(s)D(s/(1-alpha*s))^(1-alpha*s)
       <=H_s^I(f)<=H_0(s)D(s)

is legitimate without a supremum/integral exchange. Integration by
parts yields D(s)=s integral exp(-su)A(u)du. After u=v/s, dominated
convergence applies with majorant C'(1+v^nu)exp(-v), giving
D(s)~C Gamma(nu+1)s^-nu. The optimized lower has the same leading
term: its logarithmic ratio is O(s log(1/s))+o(1). This proves the
stated exact moment coefficient and finiteness for all0<s<=1.

7. INFINITE COUNT UPPER AND LOWER
================================

Upper: the stated tail estimate follows from Stieltjes integration
and the polynomial bound on A. At
T=L+(nu+4)log(L+2)+O(1), its normalized correction norm is at most

over all input pairs because that count is already a supremum.
For depths above a fixed U_eta it is bounded by (r c_0+eta)u^2;
bounded or slightly negative depths have bounded cost per term, and
sufficiently negative depths return zero by the operator norm bound.
This proves the source's O_eta(J) remainder. The retained cutoff T
exceeds the effective phase cutoff for largeL, so extension of the
phase sum to all j introduces no missing nonzero terms.

The identity

 sum_j(V-b_j)_+^2=2 integral_0^V(V-u)A(u)du

is exact by Tonelli, and regular variation gives leading coefficient
2C/((nu+1)(nu+2)). The O(logL) shift in V and O(L^nu) finite-threshold
costs are lower order than L^(nu+2).

Lower: retain b_j<=(1-delta)L and take masses1/J. All effective depths

Choosing t after all witnesses preserves these counts with strict
margin. The O(logL) depth changes alter the sum by at most
O(L^(nu+1)logL)=o(L^(nu+2)).

The integration-by-parts formula for the truncated phase sum retains
