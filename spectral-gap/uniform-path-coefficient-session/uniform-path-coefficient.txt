              The sharp uniform coefficient for a doubled edge
                   on finite unit-weight paths and cycles
                                  Uniform path coefficient session

                                         November 15, 2026


                                               Abstract
         For every fixed 0 < α < 1, the supremal strict eigenvalue count of the energy-relative
     fractional-Laplacian correction for doubling one edge on a finite unit-weight path, or on a con-
     nected graph of maximum degree two, is asymptotic to log2 (1/ε)/(2π 2 ). The upper bound is
     uniform in the graph size and the edge location, including arbitrary coupled growth with the
     inverse tolerance. Its proof uses a finite-measure approximation lemma: periodization on each
     logarithmic spectral block has the sharp Fourier rank, and the periodization images have   √ a
     separate small-rank exponential expansion. Only the leverage-measure bound ν((0, δ]) ≤ δ is
     needed from the finite graphs. The centered-path limit supplies the lower bound.

Remark 4.2 (The general tail coefficient is sharp). For dµ(s) = e−βs ds on s ≥ 0, passage to
Lebesgue measure gives the weighted convolution e−β(s+t)/2 Kα (s − t). The Fourier symbols at α
and 1/2 are comparable above and below by fixed positive constants. For α = 1/2, the unitary
substitution u = e−s/2 gives the kernel 2uβ v β /(u + v) on (0, 1). Laptev’s parameter is ρ = 2β,
so its count coefficient is 1/(4π 2 β). Thus the constant in Theorem 4.1 cannot be reduced using
just the tail hypothesis, even for non-atomic measures. Multiplying the measure by a fixed constant
accommodates any prescribed positive tail prefactor A and does not change the leading coefficient.

    For finite paths and cycles, Lemma 2.1 gives Λ = 4, K = 1, β = 1/2, hence A = 2 after the
logarithmic change of variables. Corollary 4.3 proves (1). This proves the upper half of Theorem 1.1
for all paths and cycles, since those are exactly the connected graphs of maximum degree at most
two that have an edge. In particular, arbitrary functions n = n(ε) and p = p(ε) are covered by the
same bound.

6    Scope and source checks
The logarithmic localization and Fourier-decay method has classical antecedents. Laptev [1, Sec-
tions 3–4] transforms weighted Cauchy operators into weighted logarithmic convolutions, partitions
the half-line into cells, and controls diagonal and off-diagonal contributions; his paper explicitly
builds on Widom’s method. An earlier internal note, matmul/uniform-alpha-channel-search.
txt, Sections 6–8, already uses periodic cell kernels, positive Fourier decompositions for arbitrary
spectral measures, correction of the periodization images, and optimization of cell masses for uni-
form Schatten bounds. The internal channel-classification-priority-audit.txt expressly
credits these Laptev–Widom antecedents. Accordingly, no new periodization or phase-volume
principle is claimed here. These notes establish the explicit measure-tail count bound, its stated
uniformity and remainder, and its sharp finite-path/cycle application by a self-contained version of
that method.
    The upper bound requires no path eigenvalue monotonicity, quadrature ordering, or comparison
of a finite matrix below its continuum limit. The atomic measures are handled directly. Thus edges
at fixed distance from an endpoint, edges whose distances to the endpoints diverge at arbitrary
tolerance-dependent rates, and growing cycles all obey the same upper coefficient. The fixed-
endpoint limiting coefficient 1/(6π 2 ) from the earlier session is compatible with this supremal
statement and is not used in this proof.
    The uniform rank lemma and its block summation were independently checked in three internal
parallel reviews. One written check is periodization-independent-audit.tex in this directory.
These are internal proof checks, not external peer review or formal verification. No claim about
historical priority is made.
    For the lower bound, the primary Laptev PDF was inspected at pp. 1038–1039, including the
kernel, the parameter hypotheses, the strict count convention, and Theorem (3). Copies of these
rendered pages and the PDF are in sources/. For the Fourier symbol, the DLMF table entry and
its normalized Fourier convention were inspected and rescaled to (5). The upper proof otherwise
uses only the explicit expansions and estimates given here. All new artifacts from this session lie
in the present directory; the existing papers are unchanged.
