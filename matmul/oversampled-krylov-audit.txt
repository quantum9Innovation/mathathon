Audit of fast rectangular multiplication and oversampled block Krylov
2026-11-14

Conclusion
----------
The checked published guarantees in Kacham--Woodruff (2024), arXiv:2407.11959,
do not establish a faster general spectral rank-one product algorithm in the
candidate's parameter regime. Their improved block algorithm targets finite
Schatten-p norms. Its small-block iteration parameter is always at least
sqrt(p). Translating even its stronger pth-power error guarantee to a uniform
relative spectral guarantee either retains Omega(theta^{-1/2}) passes or
requires a block comparable to the full inner rank m.

This is a statement about what the checked theorem and proof certify. It is
not a computational lower bound for arbitrary large-block Krylov algorithms,
and it does not rule out a better spectral analysis of the same bases.
Scalar matvec lower bounds alone do not exclude faster arithmetic through
batching; the argument below does not make that inference.

1. Exact block algorithm and guarantees
--------------------------------------
Use p here for the Schatten exponent; output dimensions are denoted N.
For an explicit square matrix D, Kacham--Woodruff Algorithm 1 forms

  [D Omega, (DD^T)D Omega, ..., (DD^T)^q D Omega],

where Omega has b Gaussian columns, orthogonalizes the range, and extracts
exactly k Ritz vectors. Thus b>k is allowed; this audit does not incorrectly
restrict oversampling to b<=k.

Their Theorem 3.1 states two per-vector guarantees for the rank-k output Z:

  ||D^T z_i||^2 >= sigma_i(D)^2
                    - c log^2(N)/q^2 * sigma_{k+1}(D)^2,

and, if gap=sigma_k(D)/sigma_{b+1}(D)-1,

  q >= C log(N/epsilon)/sqrt(min(1,gap))

suffices for per-vector additive epsilon*sigma_{k+1}^2 error. The first
guarantee has no improvement in q as b grows; the second depends on a real
oversampling gap, not merely the block width. Arbitrary fixed inputs do not
have a uniform lower bound on that gap when b<m.

Their Algorithm 2 runs TWO branches:

  Z1 = BlockKrylov(rank=k, block=k, iterations=~O(q)),
  Z2 = BlockKrylov(rank=k, block=k+b', iterations=~O(sqrt(p))),
  b' = ceil((3/2) max(1,k/(q^2 epsilon))).

The displayed choices of q in every parameter regime satisfy q>=sqrt(p).
It computes W_i=colspan(D^T Z_i), then chooses W1 or W2 using estimated
singular values and the gap threshold approximately 1+1/(2p).

Theorem 3.2 proves

  ||D(I-WW^T)||_{S_p} <= (1+O(epsilon)) ||D-D_k||_{S_p}.

For k<=epsilon*N^alpha, its explicit-square running time is
~O(sqrt(p)*N^{2+eta}), for any fixed multiplication slack eta>0. The other
regimes use q=max(sqrt(p),...) and hence retain the same sqrt(p) lower
bound on the selected iteration parameter. No improved p=infinity bound is
stated for Algorithm 2. Their stable LazySVD Theorem 5.3 does cover
p in [2,infinity], but costs the usual ~nnz(D)k/sqrt(epsilon).

The proof in Appendix B is stronger than the simplified norm statement in
one useful respect: it bounds pth-power residual by
(1+O(p*epsilon))*||D-D_k||_{S_p}^p, plus explicitly negligible terms.
This stronger form is retained in the comparison below.

2. Finite-p to spectral conversion, including a stronger interlacing route
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Let rank(D)<=m and k=1. A direct norm-equivalence conversion gives

  ||D(I-WW^T)||_2
    <= (1+O(epsilon)) (m-1)^{1/p} sigma_2(D).

Thus ordinary conversion requires epsilon=O(theta) and p of order
log(m)/theta. Since q>=sqrt(p), this already retains the ordinary
theta^{-1/2} number of passes, up to logarithms.

One must also check a potentially stronger conversion. Write E=D(I-WW^T),
with rank(W)=1. Rank-one singular-value interlacing gives

  sigma_j(E) >= sigma_{j+1}(D).

If a pth-power guarantee is

  ||E||_{S_p}^p <= (1+rho) sum_{j>=2}sigma_j(D)^p,

then subtracting the lower bounds for j>=2 yields

  ||E||_2^p <= sigma_2(D)^p
                 +rho*sum_{j>=2}sigma_j(D)^p
             <= [1+rho*(m-1)] sigma_2(D)^p.

This conversion is sharp as an implication from that scalar error guarantee
and rank interlacing alone: D=diag(L,1,...,1), W=e_2, has residual largest
singular value L and pth-power excess L^p-1. Choosing
L^p=1+rho*(m-1) attains the displayed upper bound.

For the Appendix B guarantee rho=O(p*epsilon), the certified sufficient
parameter scaling for spectral 1+theta is therefore

  p*epsilon*m <= c*((1+theta)^p-1),

with a suitable small absolute constant c. Consider the two regimes:

  (a) p*theta >= c0>0. Then q>=sqrt(p)=Omega(theta^{-1/2}).

  (b) p*theta <= c0. Then ((1+theta)^p-1)=O(p*theta), so the above
      uniform spectral conversion needs epsilon=O(theta/m). Algorithm 2
      consequently chooses b'>=c*m/(q^2*theta). If q=o(theta^{-1/2}),
      the prescribed block exceeds m asymptotically. Even q smaller by a
      fixed constant makes b' comparable to m, with constants adjusted.

Hence the checked Schatten guarantees do not yield both a genuinely
sub-full-rank block and o(theta^{-1/2}) passes with a uniform spectral
rank-one error guarantee. This remains true regardless of whether fast
rectangular multiplication makes some substantial range of widths b free.

This calculation does not prove that Algorithm 2 fails on a particular
input, nor that its bases cannot admit a stronger analysis. It establishes
that the published finite-p bounds cannot simply be substituted to obtain
the claimed faster spectral endpoint.

3. Factor access and orthogonalization must be charged separately
-----------------------------------------------------------------
For M=A B with A in R^{N x m}, B in R^{m x N}, applying M or M^T to b
columns costs, up to constants,

  T_MM(N,m,b)+T_MM(m,N,b),

not b times a scalar matvec cost. A fair Krylov comparison must use these
batched costs. Its range width is at most L=min(m,(q+1)b). Constructing
and extracting the basis additionally incurs tall QR/Gram and projected
core work at width L, plus expansion of the final rank-one output in N^2.

The explicit-square simplification in Kacham--Woodruff Section 3.1,
O(q*T(N,b)), uses an inequality that absorbs QR cost into multiplication
by their EXPLICIT N x N matrix. When M is implicit and has an inner
dimension m<<N, the factorized block application may be cheaper than that
explicit multiplication. The absorption argument must not be copied
without checking the tall basis/core terms.

One can commute the product iterations into latent coefficients, e.g.

  M M^T (A X)=A (Q P X), P=A^T A, Q=B B^T.

This avoids lifting the full physical block on every iteration, but
applying Q P to a fresh block still requires original-factor block products
unless the true Grams (or another adequate representation) are computed.
Materializing exact P,Q restores the fast-Gram baseline. The paper does
not supply a cheaper exact Gram representation for arbitrary factors.

At full block width b>=m, the Gaussian product sketch spans range(M)
with probability one, so optimal rank-one extraction is possible. However,
forming/applying these full-width factor blocks has the same dimension
triple (N,m,m) as exact Gram formation, by symmetry of the matrix-
multiplication tensor. Under the supplied rectangular bound, this costs
the named Gram exponent, not the proposed output-optimal bound.

4. Candidate's latest regime
----------------------------
For N=m^{59/50}, theta=m^{-25/59}, h=m^{-7/100}, the proposed
preconditioned construction has verified exponents

  setup:     m^{3362/1425} = N^{6724/3363},
  tests:     m^{27811/11800} = N^{27811/13924},
  output:    m^{59/25} = N^2.

Both construction exponents are strictly below output. Ordinary scalar
Krylov and exact Gram have the named bound

  N^{7056/3481+o(1)} = N^{2+94/3481+o(1)}.

The audited finite-Schatten large-block theorem does not currently give a
smaller uniform spectral construction cost: the preceding parameter
analysis leads either back to theta^{-1/2} passes or to full-width blocks.
This comparison does not assert that no other existing or future
large-block spectral theorem could improve the frontier.

5. Related scope cautions
-------------------------
Chen et al. (2025), arXiv:2508.06486, primarily analyze block sizes up to
the target rank. That should not be used to dismiss b>r methods; the
Kacham--Woodruff algorithm above explicitly permits b>r.

Bakshi--Narayanan (2023), arXiv:2304.03191, prove scalar matrix-vector
query lower bounds in a dimension regime sufficiently large relative to
the accuracy parameter. These do not directly rule out arithmetic savings
from wide, cheaply batched queries, nor factor-aware preprocessing. No
claim of an appropriate large-block arithmetic lower bound is made here.

6. A direct spectral theorem with arbitrary oversampling
-------------------------------------------------------
J. A. Tropp, Analysis of Randomized Block Krylov Methods, Part III,
Caltech ACM Technical Report 2018-02 (public release June 2021),
https://tropp.caltech.edu/reports/Tro18-Analysis-Randomized-TR.pdf,
Theorem 2.5 on PDF page 48, directly analyzes spectral rank-k error with
arbitrary oversampling a=b-k>=2. Its failure bound has the form

  min(1, [(18*k*tail_content/t)
              *exp(-4*q2*sqrt(t/(2+t)))]^{a/2}).

The tail_content depends on an optional first depth q1; q=q1+q2. Before
this bound is nontrivial, q2 must exceed

  log(18*k*tail_content/t)/(4*sqrt(t/(2+t))).

The polynomial theta^{-1/2} dependence remains even as oversampling grows.
This source explicitly distinguishes the depth threshold from the failure
probability benefit of oversampling. The Part I Theorem 1.5 likewise gives
gap-free extreme-eigenvalue error O(log^2(stable_rank)/q^2), with block size
principally improving failure probability. These results support the
published-bound comparison above; neither is a block arithmetic lower bound.
