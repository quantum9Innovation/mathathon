Practical theoretical scope: factorized spectral low-rank approximation
====================================================================
2026-11-14, research_runs. Dedicated note; no manuscripts or state edited.

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
"Practical" here means an arithmetic algorithm using ordinary matrix
multiplication, QR, Cholesky, small SVD/eigendecomposition, and polynomial
degrees/storage that could plausibly be useful. It does not mean a measured
hardware speed claim. No benchmark or timing claim is made.

The clean classical one-level corollary below is a simplification of the
existing construction, not a new frontier over the previously audited
stochastic construction. Its optimized construction term is a geometric
interpolation between the stochastic and exact-Gram terms. The new linear
covariance surrogate has a smaller stochastic variance constant but the
same exponent under the currently justified signed-SVRG analysis. A useful
balanced-stack lemma gives an ordinary covariance-PCA wrapper without an
inverse lift; it does not by itself improve the raw-factor runtime.

Notation: M=AB, A is n-by-m, B is m-by-p, I=(n+p)m, target rank r,
and ||M-C||_2 <= (1+theta) sigma_(r+1)(M). All comparisons include
np*r for ordinary expansion of a rank-r output into its full matrix.
Polylogarithms and standard oversampling/confidence factors are suppressed.
Exact real arithmetic and customary numerical SVD flop counting must be
distinguished from a proved finite-precision, arbitrary-condition theorem.

1. Strong classical baseline routes and exact guarantee scope
------------------------------------------------------------
(a) Factor-aware economy QR plus small-core SVD is a mandatory comparator.
Write A=U_A R_A and B^T=U_B R_B. Then

  M=U_A (R_A R_B^T) U_B^T.

Ordinary arithmetic costs O(I*m+m^3), followed by skinny extraction/lifting
and np*r output. This avoids materializing AB. The construction uses standard
orthogonal factorizations and works for arbitrary factors, with numerical
rank/conditioning qualifications for a finite-precision implementation.

(b) Ordinary factor-access randomized block Krylov/subspace methods are the
other mandatory comparator. With q=~theta^(-1/2), block width ~r, they use
~I*r*q input arithmetic, plus the actual basis, Gram, extraction and output
work. Maintaining a latent basis of width w=~r*q costs O(m*w^2+w^3)
with ordinary QR; it must not be called free when w is large. At w<=m
these terms are at most O(m^3). One can fall back to the full QR/SVD route.
The spectral guarantee is product-only and gap-free; it is substantially
stronger than an error measured relative to factor norms or ||M||_F.

Primary: Cameron Musco and Christopher Musco, Randomized Block Krylov
Methods for Stronger and Faster Approximate Singular Value Decomposition,
NeurIPS 2015, arXiv:1504.05477 (especially Theorem 12).
https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05477
https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2015/hash/1efa39bcaec6f3900149160693694536-Abstract.html
The later oversampling literature improves useful spectrum-dependent
bounds and failure probabilities; it does not make this uniform tiny-theta
comparison a constant-pass theorem. See the existing oversampled-Krylov
audit rather than substituting finite-Schatten results for the spectral case.

(c) Generalized Nystrom is a serious simple sketch-and-solve construction,
but its clean near-optimal theorem is Frobenius. With independent skinny
sketches Omega,Psi one can form

  C=M Omega (Psi^T M Omega)^dagger Psi^T M

through A(B Omega) and (Psi^T A)B. Stable small-core solves and optional
rank-r truncation must be counted. Oversampled rank-r truncation generally
needs thin orthogonalizations O((n+p)*ell^2+ell^3), even if construction of
the untruncated GN factors was cheaper. Theorem 3.2 of the primary source
is a Frobenius error theorem. It is not a uniform (1+theta)*sigma_(r+1)
spectral result at width O(r).

Primary: Yuji Nakatsukasa, Fast and stable randomized low-rank matrix
approximation, arXiv:2009.11392, 2020; Theorems 3.2 and 4.1.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11392
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.11392

A direct limitation of plain non-power range finding is instructive.
For M=diag(1+2theta,1,...,1), a rank-one relative-(1+theta) spectral
approximation needs constant overlap with the first coordinate. The range
of M Omega for a Gaussian m-by-ell sketch has expected overlap O(ell/m),
since M has condition at most three. Thus ell=o(m) does not uniformly
supply that overlap with constant success. This is a limitation of that
one-pass range-restricted construction, not of arbitrary factor algorithms.

(d) Projector/ridge and generalized-eigenvalue reductions are already broad
and some are globally gap-free with a transition band. They must not be
dismissed as uniformly gap-dependent. However their true solve dependence
is material to the comparison:

* Frostig--Musco--Musco--Sidford 2016, Principal Component Projection
  Without Principal Component Analysis, ICML/PMLR 48. Theorems 1.2/3.2
  use ~gamma^(-2) ridge calls and their stated gap assumptions.
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/frostig16.html
* Allen-Zhu--Li 2017, Faster Principal Component Regression and Stable
  Matrix Chebyshev Approximation, ICML/PMLR 70. Theorem 7.3 is a global
  transition-band theorem, with O(gamma^(-1) log(1/(gamma*epsilon)))
  positive ridge calls. These are not sqrt(gamma)^(-1) calls.
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/allen-zhu17c.html
* Jin--Sidford 2019, Principal Component Projection and Regression in
  Nearly Linear Time through Asymmetric SVRG, NeurIPS. Theorem 1 is
  global with a gray band; Theorems 3 and 5 and full-version Theorems
  6--8 expose the squared-system/asymmetric-solver dependence. A squared
  denominator of width gamma has regularization gamma^2. Treating that
  as a gamma-strongly-convex ordinary quadratic would incorrectly remove
  a square root of the accuracy cost.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06517
* Allen-Zhu--Li 2017, Doubly Accelerated Methods for Faster CCA and
  Generalized Eigendecomposition, ICML/PMLR 70. Theorem 4.2 charges a
  B^{-1}A oracle; Theorem F.3 has a signed-sample accelerated pattern.
  Neither theorem makes a raw two-factor negative covariance complement
  free. Ordinary CCA of the two full inner column spaces may have all
  canonical correlations one and does not retain the Euclidean AB
  singular spectrum.
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/allen-zhu17b.html
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06017

These sources provide algorithmic components. The previous scoped audits
did not establish a reduction from their published input models to the
claimed two-factor threshold cost. This is a scope statement, not a novelty
certification by absence.

2. The classical one-level optimization is not a new frontier
------------------------------------------------------------
Replacing all fast multiplication and fast range revelation by ordinary
arithmetic gives the existing one-level threshold construction the term

  T(h)=~I + ~m^3/h^2 + ~I*r*sqrt(h/theta) + ordinary lifting/output,

provided its raw Krylov width w=~r*sqrt(h/theta)<=m. Ordinary latent
QR/core work then costs at most ~m^3. Sketch row count is ~m/h^2,
capped by using the corresponding original factor as an identity sketch.
The displayed optimum must be clipped to the allowed constant distortion,
row-cap and width regimes. At an interior optimum,

  h_*=(m^6*theta/(I^2*r^2))^(1/5),
  C_* = ~m^(3/5)*(I*r)^(4/5)*theta^(-2/5).

For r=1, if S=I^(3/4)*m^(1/2)*theta^(-1/2) is the old signed
stochastic term and G=I*m is the classical Gram term, then exactly

  C_*^5=S^4*G.

Consequently C_* cannot beat min(S,G); a useful simplified algorithm
does not constitute a new asymptotic frontier over both older routes.

A clean named-baseline corollary remains: n=p=N, m=N^(6/11), r=1,
theta=N^(-12/11), h=N^(-2/11) gives setup and input work ~N^2;
sketch rows N^(10/11)<N and width N^(5/11)<m. Classical Gram and
ordinary factor Krylov each cost N^(23/11). The older stochastic term
already costs N^(87/44)<N^2 before output, however. This is a simplicity
example, not a new improvement over every audited route.

The current absolute-pencil proof has deliberately loose constants in
its strong residual tolerance. Calling it practically useful would need
retightening those constants and supplying a clean ordinary SVD/LDL
wrapper. Stating the above exponents alone does not complete that work.

3. One-sided inverse linearization: correct, but no new exponent yet
-----------------------------------------------------------------
For SPD G with ||G-I||<=h<1,

  G^{-1}=2I-G+E,
  E=(G-I)G^{-1}(G-I)>=0,
  ||E||<=h^2/(1-h).

Thus K_lin=2tI-(tG+Q) is a lower surrogate for K=tG^{-1}-Q,
with 0<=K-K_lin<=t*h^2/(1-h) I. The matrix tG+Q is an ordinary
stacked covariance and its samples are linear rather than cubic products.
The identity is the inverse tangent/Newton--Schulz remainder; the identity
itself should not be described as a new mathematical principle.

Direction matters: n_-(K)<=r does NOT imply n_-(K_lin)<=r. Indeed a
flat critical band can acquire extra negatives. Safe inflation is available:
because (2I-G)G=I-(G-I)^2 >= (1-h^2)I,

  K_lin(t/(1-h^2)) >= K(t).

With h=O(sqrt(theta)) this costs only a 1+O(theta) threshold change.

The true stacked factors are analytical only: a dense transform of every
raw row/column would cost I*m. An action of a length-weighted minibatch
can transform the vector once, accumulate raw rank-one actions, and
transform back. A batch of m samples has constant normalized second
moment and costs O(m^2). This is exactly the same order as the earlier
independent-copy cubic pencil sampler. The linear sampler improves its
large universal variance constant but, under the same justified
nonconvex-component SVRG plus accelerated proximal-point analysis, still
gives ~I+I^(3/4)*m^(1/2)/sqrt(theta) per top-PCA stage.

The shifted component 2tI-C_hat can be indefinite even when its mean
is SPD. Convex-component acceleration cannot be invoked merely because
C_hat is PSD. Garber et al.'s nonconvex-component expected-variance
theorem is applicable, but that is the same theorem already used:

Dan Garber, Elad Hazan, Chi Jin, Sham M. Kakade, Cameron Musco,
Praneeth Netrapalli, Aaron Sidford, Faster Eigenvector Computation via
Shift-and-Invert Preconditioning, ICML 2016/full arXiv:1605.08754,
Theorems 9, 12 and 15.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08754
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/garber16.html

For thresholds far below lambda_1(Q), variance normalization also must
account for that large norm. A direct threshold sampler cannot silently
reuse the norm-comparable top-eigenvalue analysis. The earlier adaptive
top-PCA wrapper handles this by norm-comparable shifts and deflation;
an all-at-once threshold wrapper is an additional obligation.

4. Balanced-stack reverse AGM and a direct product-output lemma
--------------------------------------------------------------
This elementary lemma is useful for simplifying a wrapper, independent
of whether its runtime is new. Suppose active factors A_b,B_b have SPD
Grams P=A_b^T A_b and Q=B_b B_b^T. Put S=(P+Q)/2, Delta=(P-Q)/2,
and assume ||S^{-1/2}Delta S^{-1/2}||<=epsilon<1. Then for every j,

  (1-epsilon^2)*lambda_j(S) <= sigma_j(A_b B_b) <= lambda_j(S).  (L1)

Proof. For any s>0, rotate the symmetric block matrix
[[P,sI],[sI,Q]] to [[S+sI,Delta],[Delta,S-sI]]. Its Schur complement
is J_s=S-sI-Delta(S+sI)^{-1}Delta. Since

  0<=Delta(S+sI)^{-1}Delta<=Delta S^{-1}Delta<=epsilon^2*S,

we have (1-epsilon^2)S-sI<=J_s<=S-sI. Schur inertia in the original
coordinates identifies n_+(J_s) with the number of singular values of
A_b B_b exceeding s. Comparing these counts proves (L1), including
equalities by continuity. Singular factors can first be reduced to their
active product support, as in the existing constant-distortion reduction.

Now let V have orthonormal r columns and set C=A_b V V^T B_b. If
||(I-VV^T)S(I-VV^T)||<=s, then

  ||A_b B_b-C||<=s.                                         (L2)

To see this, on the complement write P_res,Q_res for the restricted
factor Grams. They satisfy P_res+Q_res<=2sI. Hence

  P_res^{1/2} Q_res P_res^{1/2}
    <=2s P_res-P_res^2<=s^2 I.

These are the squared nonzero singular values of the residual product.
There is no exact P^{-1} lifting and no need for a product eigenvector.
An ordinary covariance-PCA subspace with tail factor 1+zeta consequently
gives product error at most (1+zeta)/(1-epsilon^2)*sigma_(r+1)(AB).

Relative-h Gram sketches can be balanced exactly in their small core:
choose T with T^T P_tilde T=T^{-1}Q_tilde T^{-T}=D. The true balanced
Grams lie between D/(1+h) and D/(1-h); in fact epsilon<=h exactly:
(1-h)P<=(1+h)Q and the symmetric inequality imply -hS<=Delta<=hS.
Only skinny transformed directions need be materialized. The balancing
core and h-accurate classical sketch Grams cost ~m^3/h^2. Taking h~sqrt
theta makes the balanced-stack approximation error second order, but
one still needs relative-theta covariance PCA of S. Applying ordinary
Krylov to S costs ~I*r/sqrt(theta), not the improved preconditioned
sqrt(h/theta) term automatically. Applying the linear stochastic oracle
has the same exponent as Section 3. Thus no new total bound follows yet.

Primary algebraic antecedent, verified publisher metadata:
Rajendra Bhatia and Fuad Kittaneh, On the Singular Values of a Product
of Operators, SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications 11(2),
272--277 (April 1990), DOI 10.1137/0611018.
https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0611018
Their singular-value arithmetic--geometric mean domination supplies the
upper inequality's established context. The short Schur proof above is
self-contained; absence of an exact matched keyword result does not
certify novelty of its reverse estimate or its algorithmic use.

5. What a new practical theorem would still need
----------------------------------------------
A useful next mechanism must improve one of the real expensive steps:
fine Gram setup; raw-factor passes for the threshold subspace; or the
variance-times-action-cost product of a signed complement sampler.
Simply replacing a cubic unbiased sampler by a linear one improves
constants but does not change the proved cost exponent. Simply applying
PCP to a positive ridge oracle has its published accuracy-dependent call
count. Simply using GN does not give the required uniform spectral tail.

The independent critical-Schur note is a different direction: on a small
computable threshold-critical subspace, one-sided far-block inverse
polynomials and an arbitrary negative witness can certify the output
without a high-accuracy eigenvector. Its unresolved uniform obligation
is handling a large critical core cheaply. The current practical note
does not close that obligation or the original N=m^(9/8), theta=1/m,
rank-one N^2 target.

6. Closest spectral-shifting prior for the flat-critical-core extension
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary metadata:

Shusen Wang, Luo Luo, Zhihua Zhang, SPSD Matrix Approximation vis Column
Selection: Theories, Algorithms, and Extensions, JMLR 17(49):1--49, 2016.
https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v17/14-199.html
https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume17/14-199/14-199.pdf

Section 7/Algorithm 5 already approximates an SPSD matrix by scalar
identity plus low rank. Theorem 9/Corollary 10 quantify a Frobenius
improvement; Example 1 has exact recovery of a flat bulk with positive
spikes. Their center is a tail mean estimated from trace and a leading
singular-value sum (Theorem 11), rather than a compression median.

Shusen Wang, Chao Zhang, Hui Qian, Zhihua Zhang, Improving the Modified
Nystrom Method Using Spectral Shifting, KDD 2014, pp. 611--620,
DOI 10.1145/2623330.2623614.
https://wangshusen.github.io/papers/KDD2014.pdf
Its Theorem 3/Corollary 4 and Example 1 are an earlier direct antecedent
for shifted low-rank approximation and exact flat-tail recovery.

The note's indefinite matrix-free S=a0 I+L+E, rank(L)<=k, ||E||<=nu
can be handled by an elementary composition of standard tools:

* Any (2k+1)-dimensional orthogonal compression has its middle eigenvalue
  within nu of a0: a rank-k perturbation can put at most k compressed
  eigenvalues above the noise band and at most k below it. An additive-nu
  computed median a therefore leaves sigma_(k+1)(S-aI)<=3nu.
* Apply a standard constant-relative-error spectral range finder/power
  method to the SELF-ADJOINT operator S-aI. A logarithmic power depth
  suffices to turn the dimension factor in elementary range-finding
  bounds into a constant. This works for positive and negative spikes.
* If V captures the centered range to ||(I-VV^T)(S-aI)||<=c*nu, then
  ||S-[aI+VV^T(S-aI)VV^T]||<=2c*nu, by symmetry and the triangle
  inequality. The represented small core can be indefinite.
* Fresh residual norm tests and doubling rank estimates make the procedure
  adaptive without knowing k. Their query and ordinary QR/core costs,
  and the confidence union bound, still need to be charged explicitly.

This gives the same qualitative matrix-free scalar-plus-low-rank spectral
surrogate as the new critical-core note. The median's rank-interlacing
proof is elementary, and centering plus ordinary spectral range finding
is a simple composition. It should not be sold as a new general-purpose
LRA primitive merely because the cited spectral-shifting theorem uses
Frobenius norm or a different center. The potential factor-access result
is instead the ONE-SIDED Schur lift: the surrogate's inertia and arbitrary
negative witness certify an output space for the original product while
avoiding accurate PCA of the full critical operator. That transfer and
its actual adaptive raw-factor cost require separate proof and comparison.

Conversely, the cited Frobenius theorems alone do not establish this
operator-noise guarantee: a d-dimensional noise floor nu can contribute
sqrt(d)*nu Frobenius error. Dropping that factor by norm conversion would
be an invalid claimed subsumption. Both the simple standard composition
and the genuine one-sided certificate obligation should be stated.
