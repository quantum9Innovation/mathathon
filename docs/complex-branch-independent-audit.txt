1. PRECISE RESULT AND PARAMETER CONVENTIONS
------------------------------------------
Fix alpha in (0,1), beta=1-alpha, and FIXED a,b in C. Let

  f(x)=a x_+^alpha+b (-x)_+^alpha,
  g_+(x)=(-ix)^alpha, g_-(x)=(ix)^alpha,
  f=kappa g_+ +lambda g_-.

With phi=pi alpha/2, e=(a+b)/2 and o=(a-b)/2, solving the two
linear equations gives exactly

  kappa=(e/cos(phi)+i o/sin(phi))/2,
  lambda=(e/cos(phi)-i o/sin(phi))/2.

Both sin(phi) and cos(phi) are nonzero. Let m be the number of nonzero
coefficients kappa,lambda. Then m=0 only for f=0; m=1 on the two
nonzero lines b=a exp(+/-i pi alpha); otherwise m=2. The supported

  N_(f,r)(L)/L^2 -> m r/(2 pi^2 alpha beta),

for every fixed positive integer r. H is the POWER constant, not its

2. EXACT NONLINEAR FACTORIZATION AND ITS SIGNS
---------------------------------------------
For the centered pair on a bounded interval, put R_t=(D^2+t^2)^(-1).
The resolvent at -it has blocks [-it R_t, D R_t; D R_t,-it R_t].
The centered difference is -omega_I times its outer product through
(w,0). Inserting the one-pole formula for g_+ gives

  Delta g_+ = [ -i P, Q; Q*, i S ],

where P,S are positive, and Q is the integral of
c_alpha t^(alpha+1)omega_I R_t w w* R_t D.
Right multiplication by J=diag(-I,I) gives iR with

  R=[P,-iQ;iQ*,S]>=0.

Consequently Delta g_+=iRJ and Delta g_-=-iJR, so the singular values
of Delta f are exactly those of kappa R-lambda JRJ. All signs in the
source's formulas (3)--(4) are correct.

After t=exp(y), the first real column kernel in R has numerator
sqrt(c_alpha rho(x)) exp((alpha+3)y/2), and the second has numerator
sqrt(c_alpha rho(x)) exp(x+(alpha+1)y/2), both divided by
exp(2x)+exp(2y) and multiplied by sqrt(omega_I(exp(y))). These are
precisely u(x)k_+(x-y)v_I(y) and u(x)k_-(x-y)v_I(y).
Thus the displayed T_+ and T_- give R=V W W* V*, with V=diag(I,iI).
V commutes with J and [W,JW]=diag(T_+,T_-)H0. This proves the exact
nonlinear pencil identity. No Frechet approximation is substituted.

The positive lower bound for omega_I is uniform. Increasing I to the
whole-line density bounds M_I by M, and dropping a^(-delta)<=1 for
a>=1 bounds its tail by log(1+t^2)/(2t). This is bounded for all t>0.
The source's looser constant pi/2+1 is sufficient. The same weight
therefore occurs in both kernels and stays strictly positive.

3. UNIFORM HILBERT--SCHMIDT CONTROL AND OPERATOR DOMAINS
-------------------------------------------------------
The relevant common integral is

  integral_R u(x)^2 exp(alpha x)dx
    =c_alpha integral_R rho(x)exp(-beta x)dx <infinity.

Its left/right tails have slopes alpha and beta+delta. For z=x-y,
the weighted square kernel separates into this integral times
integral exp(-alpha z)|k_sigma(z)|^2 dz. The latter is finite:
the slowest one-sided decay of k_sigma is exp(-|z|/2), and alpha<1.
This proves a Hilbert--Schmidt norm bound independent of the bounded
output interval I, with delta fixed.

In particular the full-line factor notation is legitimate as the bounded

4. CROSS-CELL AND OUTSIDE-INPUT FINITE-RANK SEPARATION
-----------------------------------------------------
The polynomial

  P_n(t)=(2/3) sum_(j=0)^n [-(2t-1)/3]^j

approximates 1/(1+t) on [0,1] with error at most 3^(-n-1), by the
exact geometric-series remainder. On either fixed sign of z, multiplying
by the corresponding exp(-gamma |z|) gives n+1 separated exponentials,
with gamma=1/2 or 3/2.

For x in one output cell, y to its left has x-y>=0, and y to its
right has x-y<=0. Each entire half-line therefore needs only n+1
separated terms. Summing the two half-lines and J cells costs
2J(n+1) rank, not J^2(n+1). The y domains include all inputs outside
I. All separated input factors lie in L2: on the right, even the
slowest decay leaves exponent 1-alpha>0 after weighting; on the left
the exponent is automatically positive.

The error's weighted Hilbert--Schmidt square is bounded by a constant
times 3^(-2n) times the common integral in Section 3. Explicitly, after
z=x-y, the two scalar error integrals are bounded by

  integral_0^infinity exp(-(alpha+2gamma_+)z)dz,
  integral_0^infinity exp(-(2gamma_--alpha)z)dz.

Both are finite since gamma_+/- are at least 1/2 and alpha<1. These
bounds are independent of cell positions, J, and I. They justify the
claimed uniform outside-input and cross-cell remainder.

5. PERIODIZATION ERROR AND THE SHARP RANK/ERROR BALANCE
------------------------------------------------------
Within one cell, z is in (-ell,ell). Every positive periodic image
z+p ell has nonnegative sign and every negative image has nonpositive
sign. Summing each separated exponential over p gives a geometric
series and preserves rank n+1 per side. The remainder is bounded by

  C 3^(-n)[exp(-gamma(ell+z))+exp(-gamma'(ell-z))],

with C uniform for ell>=1. These two exponentials are at most one.
For x,y in the same cell, exp(alpha y)<=exp(alpha ell)exp(alpha x).
Integrating first in y and then summing x over all cells gives

  ||error||_HS <= C sqrt(ell) exp(alpha ell/2)3^(-n).

Combining this with Section 4 gives the source's (8), including its
rank <=4J(n+1). For ell=sqrt(L), J=O(L/ell), n=ceil(C1 L), the
discarded rank is O(L^(3/2))=o(L^2). The operator error can be made
smaller than exp(-C2 L) for any fixed target C2 by increasing C1.
The prefactor exp(O(sqrt(L))) causes no leading exponential loss.

The constants may depend on alpha,delta and the fixed threshold range.
They do not depend on I or the base-matrix dimension. No uniformity
as delta->0 is needed: delta is fixed before L->infinity.

6. EXACT PERIODIC SINGULAR-CHANNEL FACTORIZATION
------------------------------------------------
The complex Fourier shift of 1/(2 cosh z) gives

  h_+(xi)=(pi/2)sech(pi xi/2-i pi/4),
  h_-(xi)=conj(h_+(xi)),
  |h_+|^2=|h_-|^2=(pi^2/2)sech(pi xi).

The signs and factor pi^2/2 are correct. Periodic convolution on a
circle of length ell has these multipliers at xi_k=2pi k/ell, with
NO extra ell factor when the integral uses Lebesgue measure and the
Fourier basis is normalized by 1/sqrt(ell).

Each multiplier is nonzero, so its phase defines a unitary U_sigma.
Let H have multiplier |h_sigma| and Hhat=H0/sqrt(2). With A_sigma
the weighted periodic operators, their two-column combination is

  mathcal A=sqrt(2) M_u U Hhat diag(H M_v,H M_v).

The two blocks H M_v are identical and commute with the constant
matrix Hhat. This gives exactly

  mathcal A K mathcal A*
   =2 M_u U Hhat diag(kappa S,-lambda S)Hhat*U*M_u,
  S=H M_(v_I^2)H >= (inf_cell v_I)^2 H^2.

The central operator has singular values equal to the two positive
spectra multiplied by |kappa| and |lambda|. The outside multipliers
M_u can reduce every singular value by at most (inf_cell u)^2,
using the ideal inequality on the two bounded inverse multipliers.
This holds even when kappa and lambda are arbitrary complex numbers.
It needs neither an indefinite ALT inequality nor a positivity claim
for the pencil. Each active coefficient really supplies one channel.

7. MULTIPLYING THE LOCALIZED FACTORS WITHOUT A LARGE-NORM LOSS

  ||mathcal W K mathcal W* - Wtilde K Wtilde*||

  N_actual(eps)>=N_model(eps+||Z_L||)-rank R_L.
