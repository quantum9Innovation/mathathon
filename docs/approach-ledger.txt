OTHER MECHANISMS, DISPOSITIONS, AND EXACT REASONS

1. Constant-distortion two-sided sketch and lift.
   Proved (1+h)/(1-h) spectral factor; diagonal 2x2 example is sharp.
   Disposition: insufficient for tiny theta without refinement.
   Source: ridge-filters.tex, Propositions 1-2.

2. Uniform negative-shift ridge solves.
   Proved input-factor contraction independent of conditioning and lambda>0.
   Shared canonical-form setup plus batching avoids paying m^omega per shift.
   Disposition: valid primitive, not a spectral-cutoff theorem.
   Sources: ridge-filters.tex, parallel-ridge-audit.tex.

3. Uniform scalar filters with negative/left-half-plane poles.
   Chebyshev denominator comparison bounds degree by Omega(log(1/alpha)/sqrt(g))
   for an interval cutoff of gap g. Separate coefficient-growth bound controls
   naive termwise refinement. Disposition: no polylog-degree shortcut in this
   class; batching or spectrum-specific algorithms are not ruled out.

4. Reuse of constant sketch for interior shifted Richardson.
   Exact scalar example has error spectral radius h/gamma and diverges.
   Disposition: false shortcut. The active construction instead pays for
   more accurate proxies, inflates them, and uses global threshold Lanczos.

5. Clustered-tail centering.
   Conditional target proved; shifted Krylov space is unchanged by centering.
   Disposition: classical spectrum-adaptive Krylov analysis, not new SOTA.
   Source: clustered-tail-audit.tex.

6. Fast exact multiplication on a known sqrt(m)-wide subspace.
   In the original hard regime, rectangular products cost input time up to
   exponent slack. Disposition: the unknown true Krylov space still depends
   sequentially on the operator; this accounting fact is not its construction.

7. Balance factors using sketched Grams, then inner AMM/COD.
   Approximate nuclear-energy balancing is possible cheaply in small cores.
   Materializing transformed tall inputs restores Gram-scale cost; sampling
   only a few transformed directions retains unfavorable nuclear-tail terms.
   Disposition: no new uniform total-time gain; Gram balancing has prior art.

8. Bilateral residual/control-variate decomposition + COD.
   Exact tangent-space split leaves residual with nuclear norm <=S.
   Standard Gaussian range finding matches width ~h0+S/epsilon.
   Disposition: no superior variable-rank general theorem found.

9. Diagonal-plus-low-rank Gram recovery.
   Exact partial-Gram reconstruction gives the target on a structured class.
   Existing structured-matrix recovery covers the mechanism.
   Disposition: useful structured comparator, no novelty claim.

10. Stochastic/covariance PCA and sample reuse.
    Stronger methods already exist for B=A^T; they may be output optimal in
    our new regime. They need accessible covariance samples. Applying their
    bounds directly to arbitrary AB omits feature formation or true-Gram work.
    Disposition: retain as a specialization, not a contradiction or a universal
    dominance claim. See product-literature-audit.txt.

11. Large-block/finite-Schatten Krylov as a hidden spectral comparator.
    KW2024, Tropp direct spectral bounds, and their factor-access costs audited.
    Published guarantees retain theta^(-1/2) depth or force a full-width block
    for uniform rank-one spectral error. This is an implication audit of known
    theorems, not a lower bound against every possible large-block algorithm.
    Source: oversampled-krylov-audit.txt.

12. Recent joint-leverage/quadratic-AMM claims.
    Exact printed-lemma counterexamples and norm/shape limitations recorded.
    Disposition: do not treat invalid or inapplicable bounds as frontier results.
    Source: product-literature-audit.txt.
