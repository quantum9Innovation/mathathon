1. Experiment, admissibility and genie

The specified prior chooses an ordered balanced partition of the labelled
bN-vertex hidden side uniformly, conditional on the r revealed labelled
size-N fibers. Its rb matching choices are mutually independent and
uniform. This is precisely the random lift experiment with that hidden
partition randomized. All edges lie between different fixed base types,
so every resulting graph is simple, for every positive integer N. There
is no internal parity restriction. Giving the base and all r revealed
fibers can only assist an estimator.

Observable degrees distinguish the two sides because b>r. The entire
obstruction remains within one degree class, rather than confusing
vertices of different degrees. Revealing the r small-side fibers is
stronger than ordinary degree/color refinement.

2. Uniform marked-pair count

For a fixed ordered hidden partition, the rb matching choices give
distinct graphs: restriction of the graph to each revealed/hidden fiber
pair recovers the matching. Thus a compatible (G,ordered partition)
has exactly one matching realization, and all marked pairs have the
same probability. The exact total
  T=(bN)! (N!)^((r-1)b)
follows from (bN)!/(N!)^b partitions times (N!)^(rb) graphs per partition.

The r maps V -> L_i with b preimages at each revealed vertex determine
the graph uniquely. Their count is exactly [(bN)!/(b!)^N]^r. Not all
such graphs need be liftable. The source explicitly uses this count
only as an upper bound on observation support; surjectivity onto all
ambient balanced maps is not assumed.

3. Posterior multiplicity and arbitrary estimators

For a supported G, its Z compatible ordered partitions have identical
posterior mass 1/Z. Every compatible unordered partition has precisely
b! ordered versions because its b size-N classes are nonempty. Therefore
W=Z/b! is an integer and the unordered posterior is uniform. There is
no additional r! loss: the revealed fibers are already known, and the
remaining unknown output is only the hidden-side partition.

Conditional success of any randomized estimator is at most 1/W. The
identity E(1/W)=b!*|support|/T and the ambient-support bound are correct.
On W<=t, 1/W>=1/t, so P(W<=t)<=t E(1/W)<=t s_N. This proves a genuine
high-probability multiplicity result, not merely a lower bound on the
expected number of alternative partitions.

4. General r,b asymptotic constant

Reconstructed Stirling's formula directly from
  s_N=b! [(bN)!]^(r-1)/[(b!)^(rN)(N!)^((r-1)b)].
The exponential term is exp(-gamma_{r,b} N), with
  gamma_{r,b}=r log(b!)-(r-1)b log b.
The remaining factor is exactly
  b! b^((r-1)/2) (2*pi*N)^(-(r-1)(b-1)/2)
times 1+O_{r,b}(1/N). In particular, no factor of N!, r!, or b! is
missing from the main exponent or polynomial prefactor. Taking
t=exp(cN) for fixed 0<c<gamma gives the stated exponentially vanishing
bound on small W.

5. Fixed planted partition and structural ambiguity

Permutations of the hidden vertex set act transitively on ordered
balanced hidden partitions and preserve the number W of compatible
unordered partitions. They carry the independent matching law for any
fixed planted partition to that for any other. Thus the distribution
of W, and its high-probability exponential lower bound, are identical
for each fixed planted partition and for the prior mixture.

Every compatible hidden partition yields an equitable partition into
r+b size-N classes with quotient exactly K_{r,b}. Each hidden vertex
has one neighbor in every revealed fiber; each revealed vertex has one
neighbor in every hidden candidate fiber. Distinct unordered hidden
partitions remain distinct full partitions, since the degree classes
cannot be interchanged. Equal numbers of nonempty classes also exclude
a strict coarsening relationship. Thus the structural multiplicity
claim genuinely refutes uniqueness among that bounded class family.

6. Connectedness conditioning

A spanning-tree change of fiber coordinates makes all tree-edge
permutations identity. Conditional on tree edges, the remaining
beta=|E|-|V|+1=(r-1)(b-1) edge permutations remain independent uniform,
by left/right invariance of the uniform permutation law. Connectivity
is equivalent to transitivity of the subgroup they generate. For a
fixed subset of size s, a uniform permutation preserves it with
probability binom(N,s)^(-1). The union bound over all such subsets gives
the displayed sum binom(N,s)^(1-beta).

Here beta>=6 because b>r>=3. The s=1 term is N^(1-beta). For s>=2,
binom(N,s)>=binom(N,2) through s<=N/2, so their total is at most
O(N^(3-2beta))=O(N^(1-beta)). Thus connectivity has probability 1-o(1),
and conditioning either estimator success or the small-W event costs
only a multiplicative 1+o(1). No rate subtraction destroys the
exponentially small success bound.

7. Threshold and scope

The integer condition (12!)^3>12^24 certifies positive gamma for the
15-vertex K_{3,12} example. The finite comparisons through b=11 justify
calling b=12 the first positive-rate case in this counting family.
The source correctly does not call this a recovery threshold and draws
no positive recoverability conclusion from a nonpositive exponent.

The estimator lower bound is explicitly for a hidden-partition prior
or blind/relabeling-invariant inference. A publicly fixed canonical
partition could be printed by a nonblind estimator, which does not
contradict that result. The structural multiplicity theorem, separately,
holds for every fixed planted partition. These quantifiers are kept
distinct throughout the final source.
