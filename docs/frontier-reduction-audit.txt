Frontier reduction audit -- 2026-11-14
====================================

Current finding
---------------
A concrete stochastic reduction now appears to improve and simplify the
fixed-depth matrix-free threshold bound. It constructs an unbiased symmetric
estimator of the signed product pencil in O(m^2) arithmetic, with uniformly
bounded second moment after constant-distortion whitening. The literal
variance-oracle assumptions of Garber et al. (2016), Theorem 9, then give a
linear solver; the generic accelerated approximate proximal point theorem of
Frostig et al. (2015), Theorem 1.1, accelerates it. The sampler construction
and solver reduction below are proved here. The independent generalized
AppxPCA wrapper audit now closes the fixed-rank spectral-AMM implication;
see the completion addendum in Section10 for its explicit assumptions.

The resulting total rank-one bound is

  ~O(I + m^(omega+zeta) + I^(3/4)m^(1/2)/sqrt(theta) + np),
  I=m(n+p).

The optimization ingredients are published black boxes. Whether the explicit
two-factor stochastic-pencil sampler or its total-cost corollary is new
remains a separate literature question. This is a concrete stronger route,
not an absence-based novelty certificate.

1. Constant-distortion support coordinates and row probabilities
--------------------------------------------------------------
Use the main paper's constant-distortion sketches, exact support reduction
and whitening coordinates. Thus A is n-by-m, B is m-by-p,

  W is m-by-a, L=W^dagger is a-by-m, a=rank(A),
  G=W^T A^T A W, Q'=L BB^T L^T,
  (1+xi)^(-1)I <= G <= (1-xi)^(-1)I,
  lambda=||AB||_2^2=||G^(1/2)Q'G^(1/2)||_2.

Take fixed xi<=1/16. At a safe shift s>=lambda, ||G||<=2 and
||Q'||<=2s. The normalized positive-definite pencil is

  K_s/s = G-GQ'G/s.

It has minimum eigenvalue at least
(1+xi)^(-1)(s-lambda)/s and norm O(1).
All exact pencil actions cost O(I), including the small-coordinate products.
Constant-distortion setup costs ~O(I+m^(omega+zeta)). The extra
coarse-norm estimate may use constant relative precision, hence O(m^2)
actions in the already formed core, not m^2/sqrt(theta).

Let a_i be column i of A^T (the transpose of row i of A), and let b_j be
column j of B. Define analytical feature vectors

  u_i=W^T a_i, i=1,...,n; v_j=L b_j, j=1,...,p.

Then G=sum_i u_i u_i^T and Q'=sum_j v_j v_j^T. Neither transformed
feature matrix is formed. Independent Gaussian JL matrices with
k=O(log((n+p)/delta)) columns estimate all their squared row lengths to
within, say, [1/2,2], by first computing W*Omega and L^T*Omega and then

  A(W*Omega), B^T(L^T*Omega).

This costs O((I+m^2)k), including all probability setup. Alias tables cost
O(n+p). On the simultaneous good JL event the resulting distributions obey

  p_i >= ||u_i||^2/(4 tr(G)),
  q_j >= ||v_j||^2/(4 tr(Q')).

Zero analytical features have zero contribution. Kernel preservation and
the zero-product exit are as in the main paper. Bad setup events are charged
to the declared failure probability; all sample/iteration budgets are capped.

2. Raw-row minibatches and exact second moments
---------------------------------------------
Draw b=m independent indices i_l from p, and define

  Ghat=(1/b) sum_l u_{i_l}u_{i_l}^T/p_{i_l}.

Writing R=u_i u_i^T/p_i gives

  E[R^2]=sum_i (||u_i||^2/p_i) u_i u_i^T
        <=4 tr(G) G,
  E[Ghat^2]=(1-1/b)G^2+(1/b)E[R^2] <=20I.

The last constant uses ||G||<=2, tr(G)<=2m and b=m. Similarly an
independent Qhat from b=m q-samples satisfies

  E[Qhat]=Q', E[Qhat^2]<=20s^2 I.

These are uniform Loewner/second-moment inequalities, hence apply to any
fixed vector and conditionally to an independent random vector.

One Ghat action costs O(m^2), not m^3: first compute W*x once, sum
the b raw-row outer-product actions a_i(a_i^T W*x)/p_i in the ORIGINAL
m coordinates, then apply W^T once. Qhat uses L^T*x, b raw B-column
outer products, and L once. Sample generation and raw-row retrieval cost
O(bm), which is included. It is incorrect to transform each sampled row
separately and charge m^2 for every row; that is unnecessary here.

3. An unbiased symmetric signed-pencil estimator
------------------------------------------------
Draw independent Ghat_0,Ghat_1,Ghat_2,Qhat and set

  Khat_s=s Ghat_0
          -(Ghat_1 Qhat Ghat_2+Ghat_2 Qhat Ghat_1)/2.

Each realization is symmetric and linear, and E[Khat_s]=K_s. Conditioning
successively on independent factors gives

  E||Ghat_1 Qhat Ghat_2 x||^2 <=20^3 s^2 ||x||^2.

The reversed product has the same bound; convexity of squared norm handles
their average even though the two products reuse those same batches. Thus

  E||Khat_s x||^2 <=2(20+20^3)s^2||x||^2=16040s^2||x||^2.

Consequently Khat_s/s is an unbiased symmetric oracle with a UNIVERSAL
second-moment constant V=16040. It costs O(m^2) per action. The constants
can be improved or reduced using larger constant-size batches; no such
optimization affects the exponents.

Sampling distributions are fixed after setup; fresh batches are independent
of the current SVRG iterates and past random outcomes. Symmetrization is
essential for viewing individual samples as Hessians of scalar quadratics.

4. Literal published nonconvex-component SVRG implication
-------------------------------------------------------
Primary: Garber et al., Faster Eigenvector Computation via Shift-and-Invert
Preconditioning, full version, Theorem 9, Section 4.1, page 16.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08754
Published conference paper:
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/garber16.pdf

The full theorem permits nonconvex component functions. Its hypotheses are
strong convexity of their SUM and a gradient-difference second-moment
inequality (equation (7)), not positivity or a constant maximum norm for
each sampled Hessian. The supplied oracle fits this exact condition.

In detail, let D=K_s/s be SPD, mu I<=D<=O(1)I, and
f(x)=x^T D x/2-c^T x. For each possible finite batch outcome sigma of
probability p_sigma, use component

  psi_sigma(x)=p_sigma[x^T(Khat_sigma/s)x/2-c^T x].

Then sum_sigma psi_sigma=f. No enumeration of outcomes or evaluation of
their probability products is needed: the sampled, rescaled component
gradient difference is exactly (Khat_sigma/s)(x-w), and the full gradient
is D*w-c. If x_* minimizes f, then

  sum_sigma (1/p_sigma)||grad psi_sigma(x)-grad psi_sigma(x_*)||^2
    =E||(Khat_s/s)(x-x_*)||^2
    <=V||x-x_*||^2 <=(2V/mu)[f(x)-f(x_*)].

Thus the theorem's variance parameter is S=V/mu. A constant expected
function-gap contraction costs ~O(I+m^2/mu^2). All scalar/vector updates
are included. Repeating logarithmically many epochs gives arbitrarily small
expected relative error. Markov's inequality converts this to prescribed
failure probability with only logarithmic overhead. No gap in AB or condition
number of A or B enters this normalized estimate.

Use the standard DESCENT update explicitly:
  g=(Khat_s/s)(x-w)+(D*w-c), x_next=x-eta*g.
Do not copy the sign in the paper's displayed update literally: its printed
full-gradient term/explanatory sign is inconsistent with descent. The
variance assumption and claimed convergence are the relevant source facts;
the following direct argument independently verifies the correct algorithm.
Writing gap(x)=f(x)-f(x_*), unbiasedness gives
  E||g||^2<=4S[gap(x)+gap(w)].
Consequently
  E||x_next-x_*||^2
    <=||x-x_*||^2-2eta(1-2eta*S)gap(x)+4eta^2*S*gap(w).
Sum this recurrence over an epoch, use
||w-x_*||^2<=2gap(w)/mu, and return the average of its T iterates.
With eta=1/(8S), T=ceil(64S/mu), the expected function gap is at most
one half its starting value. This directly proves the needed solver lemma.

The exponentially large formal outcome index set causes no hidden exponent:
the displayed epoch bound has no dependence on its cardinality, and the
implementation samples its O(m) raw indices directly. In particular, no
log(number of all batch outcomes) is silently treated as polylog(m).

As an independent direct check, an SVRG distance recurrence with step
eta=mu/(8V) has

  E||e_{k+1}||^2
    <=(1-2eta mu+2eta^2 V)E||e_k||^2
       +2eta^2 V||w-x_*||^2.

Its limiting snapshot ratio is 1/7, and O(V/mu^2) steps give a constant
contraction. Function-gap conversions add only logarithms in 1/mu.

5. Published acceleration and the optimized solve cost
-----------------------------------------------------
Primary: Frostig, Ge, Kakade, Sidford, Un-regularizing: Approximate Proximal
Point and Faster Stochastic Algorithms for Empirical Risk Minimization,
full version Theorem 1.1 (Accelerated Approximate Proximal Point Algorithm).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.07512
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/frostig15.html

This generic theorem assumes only a strongly convex objective and an oracle
that linearly reduces regularized objective error. It does not impose
convexity of an underlying component representation. Applied to f with
regularization rho>=2mu, its outer cost is ~sqrt(rho/mu) times the cost
of reducing the regularized problem to the required polynomial accuracy.
The regularized sample Hessian is Khat_s/s+rho I, whose second moment
is still an absolute constant when rho<=O(1). Therefore

  T_solve(mu,rho)
    =~O(sqrt(rho/mu)[I+m^2/rho^2]).

Choose rho=Theta(max{mu,sqrt(m^2/I)}), adjusting constants to satisfy
rho>=2mu. Since I>=2m^2, this lies in a bounded range. This gives

  T_solve(mu)=~O(I+I^(3/4)m^(1/2)/sqrt(mu)).

Only logarithms in dimension, inverse mu, requested relative solve accuracy
and inverse failure probability are suppressed. In particular, no polynomial
factor in an original factor condition number is being hidden. The outer
Rayleigh wrapper must still guarantee that every true system is SPD with
mu>=c*theta, or supply the appropriate sum over its safer intermediate
shifts. Independent verification of that wrapper is in progress elsewhere.

8. Why generic centering or native product sampling is insufficient
------------------------------------------------------------------
These are audit calculations, not lower bounds against all stochastic
methods. If G=I and a length-squared sampled row outer product is
T=m e_j e_j^T with uniform j, then E[T]=I but

  E||(T-I)x||^2=(m-1)||x||^2.

Even an exact deterministic coarse proxy I does not reduce this variance
merely by centering: Var(T-C)=Var(T). A useful control variate needs a
correlated sample-level proxy, not just a norm approximation to the mean.
The O(m)-row batches above pay explicitly to reduce this variance.

Likewise native Hermitian-dilation sampling of AB=sum_i a_i b_i^T has
component norm envelope sum_i ||a_i||||b_i||. This may be arbitrarily
large relative to ||AB||: with a_1=a_2=u, b_1=K v and b_2=(1-K)v,
the product is u v^T while that envelope is approximately2K||u||||v||.
Invoking stochastic negative-curvature bounds with a constant component
smoothness parameter on this representation would be invalid. Coarse
whitening plus sampled FACTOR Grams resolves precisely that difficulty.

A constant-relative-accuracy coarse norm computation gives

The theorem needs only logarithmically many approximate shifted inverses,
and every shifted system alpha I-S/b_0 is positive definite with

  alpha/(alpha-lambda/b_0)<=12/gamma.

Thus s=alpha*b_0 has normalized pencil minimum eigenvalue at least
c*gamma. In coefficients, an inverse application to input v is implemented
by solving

  (K_s/s)x=Gv/alpha.

All AppxPCA inner products and normalizations are computed in the exact
G metric; each costs O(I). The algorithm never forms G^(1/2). The conceptual
Euclidean inverse error equals ||G^(1/2)(x-x_*)||, which differs from
coefficient Euclidean error by absolute factors. Initializing with a
coefficient Gaussian and G-normalizing gives an elliptical conceptual start
of condition number<2; the normalized Gaussian-overlap lemma changes the
source iteration constants only. It must not be described as literally a
uniform conceptual-sphere start.

A cleaner alternative preserves the source's literal initialization law up
to a separately budgeted total-variation error. Apply a fixed polynomial
J_G approximating G^(-1/2) to relative eta_0=p/(100a), draw g~N(0,I),
and start with c=J_G*g, normalized in G. Before normalization the conceptual

[(1-eta_0)^2,(1+eta_0)^2]. Its KL divergence to N(0,I) is at most
4a*eta_0^2, hence Pinsker's inequality gives TV<=sqrt(2a)*eta_0<p/10.
Normalization and the whole future randomized algorithm cannot increase
TV. Since G is uniformly conditioned, the explicit Legendre polynomial
needs only O(log(a/p)) original-factor actions, costing ~O(I). No exact
root, spectrum oracle or theta-dependent power is required. This is the
recommended version of the start for a literal invocation of AppxPCA.

The source inverse tolerances have logs bounded by polynomials in
log(a), log(1/gamma), log(1/leakage) and log(1/failure). For example its
phase tolerances include (gamma/6)^m1/(64m1) and
leakage*(gamma/6)^m2/(8m2), with m1,m2 logarithmic. Therefore absolute
solver precision costs only polylogarithmic factors. Since RHS norms are
allowed to grow during each UNNORMALIZED inverse-power phase, they must
not be treated as absolute constants. Each inverse has norm O(1/gamma),
and a phase has O(log(a/(p^2*leakage))) calls. Therefore
log(1+||RHS||) is still polylogarithmic in the stated parameters. A zero-start

current guarantee. The paper does not provide the missing uniform sampler
lemma after coarse support whitening. The exact attribution should be to
product-specific access-cost accounting, not to the general possibility of
unbiased product updates.

Xie--Liang--Song, Scale Up Nonlinear Component Analysis with Doubly
Stochastic Gradients, NeurIPS2015, Section5.1/Theorem1 (published version).
https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2015/file/79a49b3e3762632813f9e35f4ba53d6c-Paper.pdf
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.03655
It includes kernel PCA/CCA/SVD stochastic updates. The inspected theorem
assumes a good initial subspace and minibatch operator error below one eighth
of the eigengap; its O(1/t) angular rate treats the gap as a constant. Those
conditions do not give our global near-top leakage accuracy at arbitrary
tiny gaps. Dai et al.'s original doubly stochastic kernel gradients already
appear in NeurIPS2014:
https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2014/file/c6cc81e8589ebb6accf27b78afad82d9-Paper.pdf

Han--Avron--Shin, Stochastic Chebyshev Gradient Descent for Spectral

https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/file/898aef0932f6aaecda27aba8e9903991-Paper.pdf
It builds unbiased spectral-sum gradients through randomized trace probing
and random polynomial truncation. The implementation still charges actions
of the actual matrix and its derivatives. It is another reason not to claim

Very recent related sampling result, inspected at Theorems1/4 and Section8:
Wang--Li, Protected Unbiased Sparsification: A Sharp Loewner Curve,

Classification after these checks
---------------------------------
The new theorem is most accurately presented as an explicit separate-factor
access-cost construction followed by a corollary of established SVRG,
accelerated proximal point and gap-free inverse-power machinery. Importance
sampling, independent-copy unbiasing, symmetrization and finite-degree
moment composition are standard. The new proof obligation that was not
discharged by earlier covariance-only citations is the uniform O(m^2)
signed-pencil oracle from raw arbitrary A,B, including cheap probability
setup and product-relative scale. This note supplies that obligation.

The targeted primary sources above do not explicitly state the resulting
AB spectral-tail total bound. Whether this application-specific corollary
was previously known remains unconfirmed; a modest elementary reduction
can yield a new upper bound without constituting a new optimization method.
The search is not a global novelty certificate, and the improvement over
the named Gram/Krylov bounds alone must not be promoted to one.

13. Independent audit of the completed stochastic manuscript
------------------------------------------------------------
Reviewed docs/stochastic-product-spectral.tex from its theorem through its
bibliography, including the subsequent support-rank and fair growing-rank
revisions. This section supersedes the earlier full-m-rank action count
where the newer exact support compression is available. No mathematical
gap was found in the stated successful-event theorem. The claims concern
exact real arithmetic, not floating-point stability or bit complexity.

Support rank and oracle costs
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The initial compression first whitens range(A^T A), then restricts to the
range of the sketched reduced B Gram. If the computed dimension is k_hat,
then k_hat=rank(AB)=k on the simultaneous embedding event. Deterministically
k_hat<=k even outside that event: range(W0) is contained in range(A^T A),
and rank(L0 B T^T)<=rank(Pi_range(A^T A) B)=rank(AB). The identity required
after the second compression is AB=Z L B; A=ZL need not hold. The current
manuscript uses only the correct product identity.

Its implementation now explicitly uses k_hat in every dimension, batch
size, regularization choice and rank cap. Writing k=k_hat in the good-event
analysis is therefore legitimate. A batch of k raw rows or columns costs
O(mk), including the W/L transforms. With JL probabilities bounded below
by squared feature length divided by four times its total, the batch
second moments are at most 20I for G and Q/c. Independent-copy composition
and symmetric averaging yield E||Dhat_s x||^2 <= 20000||x||^2 for s<=2.
The support-aware raw-factor cost is consequently O(mk), not O(k^2)
unless the transformed features have actually been materialized.

The latest norm scale c=nu/[0.9(1-xi)^2], where nu is an actual core
Rayleigh quotient in [0.9||Qbar_c||,||Qbar_c||], is valid. It gives
0.9((1-xi)/(1+xi))^2 <= lambda/c <= 1, with lower endpoint above 1/2
for xi=1/64. Thus the literal normalized AppxPCA assumptions hold.

SVRG, acceleration, accuracy, and all outcomes
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The direct descent update is correct and avoids the printed sign problem
in Garber et al.'s displayed full-gradient update. If S=V/mu, its proved
recurrence is

 E||e_next||^2 <= ||e||^2
   -2 eta(1-2 eta S)(f(x)-f*)+4 eta^2 S(f(w)-f*).

At eta=1/(8S), averaging ceil(64S/mu) iterates gives factor 1/2 in
expected objective gap. Regularized sample Hessians Dhat_s+rho I still
have bounded second moment for bounded rho. Frostig et al.'s Theorem1.1
requires a strongly convex aggregate objective and its regularized solver,
not convexity of each sampled component. Choosing rho a sufficiently large
constant multiple of sqrt(mk/I), or using the base solver when mu is larger,
gives the stated ~O(I+I^(3/4)(mk)^(1/4)/sqrt(mu)) solve bound.

Arbitrarily small row probabilities do not add an operation-count penalty
in the stated real-arithmetic model. Component outcomes need not be
enumerated; probabilities cancel in sampled gradient differences.
Zero-start gap <= ||b||^2/(2mu), so Euclidean precision and confidence cost
only logarithmic repetitions and logarithmic dependence on ||b||.

The AppxPCA inverse equation is exactly D_s x=Gv. The G-metric simulation,
fixed-condition polynomial inverse-root initialization, Gaussian KL/TV
coupling, and conditional union bound are valid. Inverse iterates are
unnormalized; the manuscript correctly bounds RHS growth by a deterministic
(C/gamma)^polylog expression rather than assuming RHS norms stay constant.
It now uses this bound to preset solver epochs and abort on an exceeded
bound. This is necessary for the advertised operation cap on failed random
outcomes. All good-event oracle errors fit within those caps. No hidden
singular-value-ratio logarithm was introduced by this initialization.

Rank-r and adaptive-error finishing
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The Schur-inverse/min-max proof of the all-spectrum Nystrom lemma is
dimensionally correct: the constructed bottom-space image has dimension
d-j inside z-perp, so inverse compression controls the j-th largest
residual eigenvalue. PSD limits cover singular H, and the update drops
rank by one whenever z^T H z>0.

The leakage-sensitive counting induction gives

 lambda_1(H_r) <= lambda_(r+1)(H_0) /
                 ((1-gamma)(1-eps)^r).

With gamma=theta/4 and eps=theta/(4r), this implies the stated (1+theta)
spectral approximation. Leakage enters AppxPCA only through logarithms;
replacing it by Rayleigh approximation alone would lose a sqrt(r) factor.

The oblique coefficient updates S_new=(I-d c^T)S are correct, as are
the rank-two congruence updates of Qbar. Initial sketch validity survives
all adaptive congruences. Fresh B-side JL scores after each oblique update
are required and explicitly charged O(I); reusing their initial scores
without a separate featurewise domination lemma would be unjustified.
Applying S costs O(ki)<=O(mk) because i<=k. Thus the main sum

 ~O(I(1+ell)+m^(omega+zeta)
    +ell I^(3/4)(mk)^(1/4)/sqrt(theta)
    +np+TMM(n,ell,p)), ell=min(r,k),

accounts for the oracle, core updates, fresh score setup, true metrics,
construction, and explicit output. The adaptive absolute-error theorem
correctly includes one additional cutoff query, summable confidence and
leakage budgets, and distinguishes its successful-event small-rank bound
from the unconditional preset rank cap.

Fair growing-rank comparison
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The earlier example r=m^(1/10), theta=m^(-1/5), N=m^(7/6) does NOT
separate the new method from fast batched block Krylov. A free-input
factor-block multiplication removes the spurious multiplicative r there.
That example has been removed from the latest manuscript.

The replacement N=m^(7/6), r=m^(7/200), theta=m^(-17/50) does pass
the corrected comparison. Put q=~m^(17/100) and L=(q+1)r=~m^(41/200).
For the supplied rectangular-MM bound, both r and L are below the free
factor-block threshold m^(7/13). Hence every A/B block action costs ~I,
where I=m^(13/6), and q iterations cost m^(701/300)=N^(701/350).
Even conventional tall-basis QR costs O(N L^2), exponent 473/300 in m,
which is well below that iteration cost. Projecting the product at width L
costs ~I; projected Gram, dense small-core algebra, and skinny lifting also
have smaller exponents. The fast block comparison therefore includes its
basis and extraction, not merely a matrix-vector loop.

The new construction has leading exponent
7/200+(3/4)(13/6)+1/2+17/100=233/100 in m,
or 699/350=2-1/350 in N. Its Ir and core exponents are 1321/600 and 9/4.
Output expansion has exponent two since r<N^(1/2). For growing r this
gives N^(2+o(1)), not an asserted literal O(N^2) expansion.
The named Gram comparison remains N^(100/49), while corrected batched
Krylov is N^(701/350)=N^(2+1/350).

The checked finite-Schatten-to-spectral route does not improve this uniform
worst-case guarantee: q<~theta^(-1/2) forces oversampling to full width
under its certified accuracy conversion. Full-width blocking costs at
least the named Gram upper bound within the supplied MM formula. These
are comparisons of applicable published upper bounds, not lower bounds
on every possible Krylov or randomized algorithm.

Parameter and compression comparisons
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The fixed-rank open family 1/8<d<(sqrt(2)-1)/2 and 2d<b<5d/2 follows
directly from comparing the three construction exponents with output
2+2d. For alpha*=(3sqrt(5)-5)/2 and s*=(7-3sqrt(5))/8, the stated
theta=N^(-(10s*-2eta)), 0<eta<s*, gives construction N^(2-eta+o(1)),
Gram N^(2+s*+o(1)), and fixed-rank Krylov N^(2+s*-eta+o(1)). Thus
