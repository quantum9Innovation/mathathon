  * an exact invariant split need not be supplied or computed by an

  * the raw coupling width q*k can be replaced by q+k*polylog, by sharing

1. MODEL, PARAMETERS AND PRECISE NEW CLAIM
=========================================
Use the supported raw coordinates of raw-factor-fresh-search.txt:

  S=Z K Z^T,  T=Z^T Z=I+D,  ||D||<=eta<=1/128,

where K=K^T is an explicit invertible m-by-m core and exact actions of
Z,Z^T come from N-by-m raw factors. Put I_X=Nm, N>=m. A constant
accuracy OSE can produce this smaller fixed eta at the same asymptotic
input-plus-m^omega setup cost. Rank-deficient K is first reduced on its
explicit support as in the earlier solver.

Fix q>=1, 0<epsilon<=1, t>0 and supplied poles w_j=t+i y_j,
y_j nonzero. Define
mu=max_j |w_j|/|y_j|. All poles and supplied RHS h_j are batched; later
sequential RHS are not retroactively free. Let

  kappa_t=#{lambda in spec(K): t/4<=lambda<=4t}.

The algorithm constructs a coarse subspace P of dimension k<=kappa_t.
It contains approximately every eigenvector with lambda in[t/2,2t].
If r=q+k*p<=m, p=O(log(2mu/epsilon)), all normalized actions

  w_j(S-w_j I)^(-1)h_j

are approximated with error epsilon||h_j||. A safe work bound is

  ~O(m^omega)*polylog(2mu/epsilon)
   + O(I_X [q+k*p]^(omega-2) polylog(2mu/epsilon))
   + O(q*m*k*p + q*k^omega + q*k^2*p)
   + O(m*k^(omega-1)*polylog(2mu/epsilon)).                 (1.1)

The ~O(m^omega)*polylog(2mu/epsilon) term includes O(m^omega*p)
coefficient setup, inverse-polynomial-accuracy diagonalization of
a BOUNDED explicit coarse matrix, exact small-core QR and Frobenius
forms. Dimension/confidence logarithms are suppressed. No log(kappa(K))
or other unbounded raw condition-number parameter is hidden. Arithmetic
is exact field arithmetic, comparisons, positive scalar roots, and the
standard randomized small-matrix approximation primitive described in
Section2. This is NOT a finite-word stability statement for subsequent
exact, possibly badly scaled rational/core calculations.

The accuracy statement is on the successful preprocessing event, with
any fixed requested inverse-polynomial failure probability; confidence
logs are charged. The displayed arithmetic count uses the observed k.
For a strict budget under a promise kappa_t<=k_bar, impose a supplied
k_bar cap and return zero before any expensive raw query if k>k_bar or
q+k*p>m. All remaining loop counts are fixed in advance; exact-core
singularities or QR rank failure also return zero. On the successful
event the cap cannot reject a valid promised instance. This gives the
same charged budget with k_bar in place of observed k even on failure.

For N=m^(9/8), omega=9/4, q,k<=~sqrt(m), and polynomial mu,epsilon^-1,
all terms in(1.1) are ~O(m^(9/4)). Small positive input exponent slack
can be retained just as in the original research direction. The output
here is q resolver-action vectors, not an N-by-N LRA answer.

Poles with |y|>=t/32 can instead use the existing off-axis theorem:
their angular sine is at least1/sqrt(1025)>eta. Hence only

                    |y_j|<=t/32                          (1.2)

needs the construction below. The far and near blocks cost the same
displayed q term. Empty/whole coarse subspaces are discussed at the end.

2. COMPUTE A CONSTANT-ANGLE BAND SPACE FROM A BOUNDED CORE
========================================================
Form exactly, using ordinary fast small-core arithmetic,

                 C=t K(K^2+t^2 I)^(-1).

It is symmetric, ||C||<=1/2, and its eigenvalue at lambda is
c(lambda)=t lambda/(lambda^2+t^2). In particular

  lambda in[t/2,2t] implies c(lambda)>=2/5;
  c(lambda)>=1/4 implies lambda in[t/4,4t].                (2.1)

The second inclusion is conservative: the exact endpoints are
(2-sqrt3)t and(2+sqrt3)t. Negatives have c(lambda)<0.

Use a near-matrix-multiplication Hermitian diagonalization routine to
get nearly orthogonal U and diagonal A with absolute backward and
orthogonality errors <=tau. This standard small-core primitive is paid
at ~O(m^omega) operations when tau is inverse polynomial in m. A primary
reference actually inspected here is Shah2024, Theorem4.2:
  https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09880
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09880
It supplies a backward diagonalization and near-unitarity at that cost
with logarithmic precision. More precisely, first round the bounded
entries of C to a symmetric binary grid with operator error <=tau/10.
Binary search performs this in O(m^2 log(m/tau)) field comparisons.
Call its EIGH-INTERNAL (Algorithm3) with the KNOWN bounds R0=R=1,
and accuracy small enough compared with tau. Algorithm3 explicitly
accepts upper bounds, its residual guarantee is accuracy*(R0+R), and
the proof of Theorem4.2 with equal initial R,R0 gives the same cost.
Thus no exact spectral-norm oracle or raw condition ratio is charged
as a unit primitive. The finite-floating arithmetic on this bounded
core can be simulated, or used as a paid small-core subroutine; the
later exact-field algorithm claims no overall finite-word guarantee.

Here are explicit gap and angle margins. Set gamma=1/[1000(m+1)] and
take tau sufficiently below10^(-6)*gamma. Among2m+1 equally spaced
points a_l=1/4+l/[24(m+1)], 0<=l<=2m, choose one at distance greater
than4gamma from every computed diagonal entry of A. Each entry excludes
at most one candidate, since the spacing exceeds8gamma. Therefore a
choice exists and can be found by sorting/comparisons in polynomial
small-core work. The true C has no eigenvalue within3gamma of a_l.

Let U_s contain the columns of U whose A entry exceeds a_l, and let
P0 denote the EXACT spectral subspace of C above a_l, used for analysis
only. Near-unitarity permits a polar correction in analysis; its
difference from U is O(tau), and it gives a backward Hermitian model
within O(tau). Davis--Kahan across the3gamma gap, or the elementary
spectral resolvent projector identity, shows that

          H=Re(U_s U_s^*)  satisfies ||H-P0P0^T||=O(tau/gamma).

The REAL PART is needed: Shah's theorem is stated with complex-unitary
U, so simply assuming real columns would leave a gap. H is real
symmetric. Draw an independent real Gaussian m-by-k matrix J and
orthonormalize HJ EXACTLY inside the small core; complete the result
to a real orthogonal [P,Q]. This costs O(m^omega), with no raw calls.
Here k is the number of selected diagonal entries, which equals dim(P0)
by the backward-model gap. Reduce tau by a further polynomial in m and
the desired preprocessing failure probability rho. Then

                ||PP^T-P0P0^T||<=delta:=1/100.             (2.2)

For an elementary justification of this last probabilistic step, the
k-by-k matrix P0^T J is standard real Gaussian. Each column's distance
from the span of all other columns has the distribution |g|, g standard
normal. A union bound gives all distances >=rho/(2k) with probability
>=1-rho, and the inverse-row-distance identity then gives
sigma_min(P0^T J)>=rho/(2k^(3/2)). Separately, Markov applied to
||J||_F^2 gives ||J||<=m/sqrt(rho) with probability >=1-rho. Therefore
a sufficiently small O(delta*rho^(3/2)*m^(-5/2)) upper bound on
||H-P0P0^T|| gives(2.2) by the elementary range perturbation estimate.
Failure probabilities can be allocated separately to preprocessing.
When k=0 this step is empty. All accuracy reductions are polynomial
and add only dimension/confidence logarithms. Exact QR leaves the
selected real span unchanged; it is not an algebraic spectral oracle.
The dimension equals dim(P0), hence k<=kappa_t by(2.1). No exact
eigenvalue/eigenvector of K, and no accurate invariant basis for K^-1,
was computed. The choice of C avoids their unbounded scales.

3. THE KEY GRAPH-SUBSPACE ESTIMATE AVOIDS ||K^-1||
=================================================
For |w-t|<=t/16 put

 M0(w)=w K^-1-I,        A0(w)=M0(w)^(-1).

A0 itself is used only at nonreal actual poles. Define the coarse
compression F0(w)=Q^T M0(w)Q and its physical inverse

                    B(w)=Q F0(w)^(-1) Q^T.

We prove B extends analytically across the entire disk, including
coarse resonant real poles of A0, and ||B(w)||<=4 there.

In the exact invariant decomposition P0+Q0, write M0=diag(M_P,M_Q).
By(2.1) and the band inclusion,

  ||M_P||<=21/4,            ||M_Q^-1||<=3                 (3.1)

throughout that disk. The second estimate follows directly from
|lambda/(w-lambda)|<=3 for lambda outside[t/2,2t]. These inequalities
contain no ||K^-1|| term.

By(2.2), the approximate P-space has graph form [I;E] with ||E||<=2delta
over P0. Its orthogonal complement has graph form [-E^T;I]. Thus F0 is
unitarily equivalent to the congruence

 (I+EE^T)^(-1/2) [M_Q+E M_P E^T] (I+EE^T)^(-1/2).

The perturbation E M_P E^T has norm <=21delta^2; combining with(3.1)
and inverse perturbation bounds gives

               ||F0(w)^(-1)||<=4.                        (3.2)

This proves analyticity and the asserted B bound on the whole disk.
It is important NOT to bound Q^T K^-1 P by ||K^-1||. That would restore
an unbounded precision dependence and defeat the construction.

Define the coarse harmonic extension and its Schur coefficient by

 V0(w)=P-B(w)M0(w)P,
 G0(w)=P^T M0(w)P-P^T M0(w) B(w) M0(w)P.                 (3.3)

Both are computable from explicit small-core data, even at w=t.
They are analytic in the same disk and satisfy

                 ||V0(w)||<=2,    ||G0(w)||<=6.           (3.4)

For completeness, use graph P=[I;E] N^-1/2, N=I+E^T E, and
A_Q=M_Q^-1. Algebra yields

 G0=N^(1/2)[I+M_P E^T A_Q E]^(-1)M_P N^(1/2),
 V0=[I; A_Q E M_P][I+E^T A_Q E M_P]^(-1)N^(1/2).

These formulas, (3.1), and ||E||<=.02 give(3.4) with room to spare.
They also prove the analytic extensions at possible zeros of M_P.
They are proofs of bounds, not a demand to compute P0 or E.

At nonreal w define C0=P^T A0 P. Then ordinary block inversion gives

 G0=C0^-1,  V0=A0 P C0^-1,
 A0=B+V0 C0 V0^T.                                      (3.5)

All transposes are plain transposes of complex symmetric quantities.
P,Q are real orthonormal. No Hermitian claim is made for B at complex w.

4. TRUE REFINEMENT: ONLY A SMALL CORE RETAINS NEAR-REAL GROWTH
==========================================================
The true pencil is M=M0-D. Since ||B(w)D||<=4eta<=1/32, define

 R=(I-BD)^(-1)B,           V=(I-BD)^(-1)V0,
 G=G0-V0^T D V.

All are analytic in |w-t|<=t/16, with ||R||<=5 and ||V||<=3.
The true full inverse obeys the exact Dyson/low-rank identity

               M(w)^(-1)=R(w)+V(w)G(w)^(-1)V(w)^T.        (4.1)

One proof substitutes(3.5) into (I-A0D)^(-1)A0 and uses the matrix
determinant lemma. The safe factor I-BD is invertible by contraction;
the full M is invertible at nonreal w, so the k-by-k G is invertible.
The same conclusion follows by direct block elimination.

Moreover P^T B=0, P^T V0=I, hence P^T R=0 and P^T V=I. Therefore

                  G(w)^(-1)=P^T M(w)^(-1)P.

The previously proved physical-Hermitian comparison for T=I+D gives

 ||M(w)^(-1)||<=2|w|/[(1-eta)|Im w|]<=3mu,
                  ||G(w)^(-1)||<=3mu.                   (4.2)

Thus every large factor is isolated in a k-by-k core whose inverse
has a supplied polynomial-mu bound. The safe and coupling functions
themselves have constant bounds on a fixed disk. This is stronger
than merely replacing one exact K split by a near-invariant basis.

5. SHARE THE ANALYTIC COUPLING COEFFICIENTS ACROSS ALL q POLES
==========================================================
Use z=(w-t)/(t/16), so the functions above are analytic on |z|<=1
with the bounds in(3.2)--(3.4). Actual near poles have |z|<=1/2.
For any analytic matrix function of norm at most C on |z|<=1, its
degree-p Taylor truncation has error at most2C*2^(-p) on |z|<=1/2.
This is the elementary Cauchy coefficient estimate and geometric tail.

All coarse B,V0,G0 Taylor coefficients are exactly computable by field
operations. Put F=Q^T K^-1 Q, H0=(tF-I)^(-1). Then

 (wF-I)^(-1)
   =sum_(ell>=0) (-(w-t)H0 F)^ell H0.

Here ||H0||<=4 and H0 F=(I+H0)/t. Repeated small-core products give
the first p coefficients in O(m^omega*p) work. Formula(3.3) then gives
V0,G0 coefficients by exact polynomial matrix products. Intermediate
K^-1 values may be large, but their exact-field operation count is
charged and the FINAL analytic coefficients obey the uniform bounds.
No finite-precision cancellation guarantee is asserted.

Now compute V(z)=(I-B(z)D)^(-1)V0(z) modulo z^(p+1), using L steps
of the fixed Neumann iteration

             W_(ell+1)(z)=V0(z)+B(z)D W_ell(z), W_0=0.

Each true D-action is applied to ALL k(p+1) coefficient columns in
one raw batch. Coarse convolution has only polynomial-in-p overhead.
On the unit disk the exact L-step error is <=3*(1/32)^L. Its first
p coefficient errors inherit this bound by Cauchy. Evaluation on the
half disk adds only a factor2. Truncation of true V adds <=6*2^-p.
Choose L,p=O(log(2mu/epsilon)) so the total V error is <=a requested
delta_star. The coefficients are finite exact-field expressions in
the true raw data, not approximate oracle values treated as exact.

Apply D once to the final coefficient block, and form coefficients of

                     G=G0-V0^T D V

by polynomial convolution. Multiplying m-by-k coefficient blocks into
k-by-k blocks costs O(m*k^(omega-1)) times a polynomial in p by
elementary blocking. Its truncation/iteration error is O(delta_star),
because ||D||,||V0||,||V|| are uniformly bounded. The degree may be
enlarged by a constant factor to keep a shared polynomial error budget.
The coefficients are computed ONCE, not separately for each pole.

For the safe RHS solves R(w_j)b_j, where b_j=Z^T h_j, use direct
Neumann iteration with B(w_j) on the q supplied columns. B(w_j) can
be applied in one batch via Q, an exact Frobenius form of F, and Q^T.
The companion recurrences for (w_j F-I)^(-1) cost O(mq), and the
basis changes cost O(m^2 q^(omega-2)). This is the same shared-FNF
primitive proved in raw-factor-fresh-search.txt. It uses no q separate
dense inversions. The true D-action again uses one q-column raw batch.

Hence all true raw queries have width at most q+k(p+1), not q(k+1).
They can be concatenated on each iteration. All q data are supplied
before the batch and all future coefficient RHS are generated jointly.

6. ERROR PROPAGATION AND THE ACTUAL PHYSICAL OUTPUT
==================================================
Let approximations Rhat_j,Vhat_j,Ghat_j have operator errors <=delta_star
(after fixed constant allocations). Choose

                  delta_star<=epsilon/[10^6 mu^2].       (6.1)

Then (4.2) and inverse perturbation imply Ghat_j is invertible,
||Ghat_j^-1||<=6mu, and ||Ghat_j^-1-G_j^-1||<=18mu^2 delta_star.
Expanding(4.1), using ||V||<=3, gives an inverse-action error below
1000mu^2 delta_star||b_j||. The normalized physical Woodbury identity is

             w_j(S-w_jI)^(-1)h_j=-h_j-Z M(w_j)^(-1)b_j.

Since ||Z||^2<=1+eta, (6.1) leaves ample room for total physical error
epsilon||h_j||. All bounds are operator bounds, so h_j may depend on
the coarse sketch. A failed small-core inversion can return zero on
a bad preprocessing event; fixed loop and degree caps remain charged.

All pole evaluations of Vhat and Ghat cost O(qmk p+qk^2p), with
q inversions of k-by-k matrices costing O(q k^omega). The final lifts
through Z are one q-column raw batch. The safe RHS and coefficient
iterations have polylogarithmically many stages, each of width at most
q+k(p+1). Together with the explicit coarse coefficient preparation,
these costs prove(1.1).

One uses the standard cheap rectangular bound
T_MM(N,m,r)=O(Nm r^(omega-2)) when1<=r<=m<=N. Small-core transformations
fit because m^2<=Nm. No exact tall QR, true full Gram or unknown
eigendecomposition of T has been assumed. Exact QR occurs only on
the explicit m-dimensional coarse data.

7. WHAT THIS CHANGES AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
========================================
At q=k=~sqrt(m), the earlier q(k+1) raw width is ~m. Its generic raw
term is at least the named width-m multiplication route, beyond the
desired m^(9/4) bound when N=m^(9/8). Shared coefficients reduce the
raw width to ~sqrt(m), giving ~m^(9/4). The remaining qmk evaluations
cost ~m^2 and q k^omega cost ~m^(13/8), both below that budget.
The earlier ordinary Schur assembly qmk^2 can likewise be replaced by
shared coefficient products. The cost C_split is also no longer free:
it has been replaced by bounded-core approximate diagonalization and
a proved graph-subspace perturbation argument.

However the coarse critical band can still fill the entire support.
If k=m, Q is empty, B=0, V0=P and G=P^T(M0-D)P. Computing the
coefficient action D P then reveals the entire true Gram in that
basis. The algorithm correctly charges that width-m cost; it does not
turn it into input-plus-m^omega time. For K=tI, this reduces to raw
covariance PCA with T close to I. The original arbitrary-factor gate
already includes that problem via A=Z,B=Z^T.

The q-sharing refinement matters for a many-pole resolver, but a standard
well-separated rational sign filter may need only polylogarithmically
many near-real poles. Thus it does NOT by itself cure the main k=m
barrier for sharp LRA. A full threshold/filter/extraction theorem and
a genuinely different all-band information mechanism remain necessary.
The present construction must not be relabeled the requested seminal
or general quadratic result.
