VERDICT
-------
PASS: the cone-feature singular-value/Gram-conditioning estimates;
the positive prescribed-exterior-node moment construction and its
Theta(1/m) head weight; scalar/both-Gram approximation bounds; the
common-start finite realization; and the direct Section 8 rank-one
reconstruction obstruction.

The decisive construction in Section 8 has no substantive mathematical
gap. Its two incompatible rank-one solution sets are proved using
orthogonal heads with small cross-state deficit, not by a claim that
different scalar spectral measures alone force incompatible outputs.

PRECISE SCOPE: one fixed supplied starting vector, queries restricted to
a fixed cone, and bounded errors that may be correlated/adversarial.
The theorem is a robust-data/model discontinuity statement. It is NOT
a raw-input arithmetic lower bound, an independent-Gaussian-start lower
bound, a result about unbiased stochastic noise, or a proof that the
particular errors of an actual raw-factor solver attain the hard case.
The original general quadratic-time product gate remains OPEN.

Two statement clarifications are useful, neither changing the theorem:

1. The displayed exponentially small differences are UPPER bounds.
   Write <=exp(-Omega(sqrt(m))) when a two-sided rate is not established.
   A particular pole, for example z=0, can have exactly zero difference.
   The robust precision consequence is log(1/eps)=Omega(sqrt(m)), not
   a matching characterization of the optimal radius for every example.

2. Exact S0 action responses form one coherent positive transcript with
   S0-derived scalar/Gram responses, all exponentially close to the two
   hidden inputs. The exact TRUE scalar/Gram data of S1 and S2 are equal
   to each other, but generally not equal to S0's data. If those true exact
   extras are supplied as well, use the common oracle of exact S1 actions
   instead: S1 and S2 actions differ by at most twice the stated S0 radius.
   Reducing kappa_c absorbs this factor for sufficiently large m. This
   gives a fully coherent positive transcript that also matches all exact
   true scalar/Gram extras, and the same success-probability conclusion.

No novelty claim or numerical experiment is required for this verdict.

1. CONE NORMALIZATION AND UNIVERSAL FEATURE COLLAPSE
---------------------------------------------------
Fix c in (0,1] BEFORE letting m grow, and set a=c/4. For admissible z,
|1-z|>=|Im z|>=c|z|, so a_z=z/(1-z) has modulus <=1/c. The point
z=0 is harmless and gives H_0=I. The forbidden denominator z=1 is
not in the cone. Thus, on J_c=[1-a,1+a],

  H_z(t)=1/[1-a_z(t-1)], |a_z(t-1)|<=1/4.

Its modulus is between 4/5 and 4/3, and the degree-L geometric
remainder is at most (4/3)4^(-L-1). Spectral calculus gives the same
operator bound for every real symmetric S with spectrum in J_c.

All polynomial feature columns lie in a common subspace of dimension
<=L+1. Their block remainder has Frobenius norm at most
(4/3)sqrt(q)4^(-L-1)||h||. Best-rank approximation therefore gives
the source's sigma_(L+2) bound for 0<=L<=q-2.

For q>=2, taking L=q-2 and using sigma_1>=max column norm
>=(4/5)||h|| proves, for independent columns,

  kappa_2(U) >=(3/20)4^q/sqrt(q),
  kappa_2(U*U) >=(9/400)16^q/q.

Reducing the constants covers the trivial q=1 case. If columns are
dependent, the smallest singular value is zero. Additional ordinary
column normalization changes conditioning only by an absolute factor,
since the displayed columns already have comparable norms.

This is a theorem about the specified resolvent feature coordinates,
not a claim that every possible nonlinear algorithm must explicitly
whiten that Gram. Exact conjugates and repeats are included; repeats
can simply make the block dependent. Since the bound holds for EVERY
admissible pole set, it also holds after adaptive pole selection within
the same fixed cone. If c varies with m, the theorem's constants cannot
silently be treated as fixed.

2. THE PRESCRIBED-NODE POSITIVE RULE
------------------------------------
For probability dx/2 on [-1,1], the orthonormal Legendre polynomials
are p_k=sqrt(2k+1)P_k, and K_d is their degree-d reproducing kernel.
For x_*>1, the nodal polynomial (x-x_*)K_d(x,x_*) is a nonzero
constant multiple of p_(d+1)(x)-r p_d(x), with
r=p_(d+1)(x_*)/p_d(x_*)>0.

The ratio p_(d+1)/p_d has positive derivative between its poles, by
the diagonal Christoffel--Darboux identity. It has one solution of
ratio=r between each consecutive pair of p_d zeros. The rightmost
solution is x_*. Its leftmost solution lies after -1, because the
ratio at -1 is negative, while r is positive; all p_d zeros lie in
(-1,1). Hence the other d nodes are simple and strictly interior.

For deg r0<=d-1, reproduction annihilates the nodal polynomial times
r0. Polynomial division then proves degree-2d exactness. The interpolatory
weight at a node is integral ell_i^2 dnu>0, since deg ell_i=d and
exactness holds through 2d. In particular

  ell_*(x)=K_d(x,x_*)/K_d(x_*,x_*),
  alpha=int ell_*^2 dnu=1/K_d(x_*,x_*).

The ordinary (d+1)-node Gaussian rule supplies the other positive
probability measure, with all nodes interior and moment exactness
through 2d+1. Thus matching through 2d and positivity are rigorous.
The sufficiently-large-m theorem may take d>=1; the general quadrature
construction itself also has a harmless d=0 case.

3. HEAD WEIGHT AND MOMENT-BASED OBSERVATION BOUNDS
------------------------------------------------
The Legendre Laplace integral gives 1<=P_k(cosh eta)<=exp(k eta).
The lower bound can also follow from Jensen on the positive integrand.
Therefore

  (d+1)^2<=K_d(x_*,x_*)<=(d+1)^2 exp(2d eta),
  eta=arcosh(x_*)<=sqrt(2(x_*-1)).

At d=floor(b sqrt(m)), x_*-1=O(1/m), both d eta and the head-weight
constants are bounded for fixed c,b. This proves alpha=Theta(1/m)
in Section 3. Adding the common atom at one preserves moments and
sets the next largest observed state to one. Zero-mass padding at
eigenvalue one gives sigma_2=1 for the no-head operator as well.

Matching t^k through D also matches (t-1)^k through D. Applying the
geometric remainder to the two probabilities gives exactly (4.1).
For either Gram integrand, the coefficient of (t-1)^k is bounded by
(k+1)c^(-k). The tail bound

  sum_(k>D)(k+1)4^(-k)<=2(D+2)4^(-D-1)

and total variation at most two give (4.2). There is no division by
pole differences, so repeats/conjugates and all adaptive cone queries
are covered. An observed Q-by-Q Gram difference has norm at most Q
times the entrywise bound. For polynomial Q this is exponentially
small in sqrt(m). These are upper bounds, not pointwise two-sided rates.

4. SECTION 8: THE MIXTURE AND WEIGHTED KERNEL
--------------------------------------------
Now take b=sqrt(a)/1000, d=floor(b sqrt(m)), theta=1/m, and xi=1+4/m.
The exterior Legendre coordinate is x_*=1+8/(a m), giving d eta<=.004.
The mixture rho=((1-b^2)mu_G+b^2mu_R+delta_1)/2 has head mass

  beta=b^2 alpha/2=Theta(1/m).

The factor b^2 cancels the potentially large 1/b^2 head-weight
prefactor. For sufficiently large m, for example, the estimates give
beta between exp(-.008)/(8m) and 1/(2m). Its flat-state mass at one
is exactly 1/2. There are at most 2d+2 nonflat states, so padding to
dimension m leaves a flat eigenspace W of dimension at least two.

For p of degree <=d-1, exact moment matching gives the more explicit
identities behind (8.2):

  int p^2 d rho_b = (1/2)int p^2 dnu-beta p(xi)^2,
  int(1-t)p^2 d rho_b
    = (1/2)int(1-t)p^2 dnu+beta(xi-1)p(xi)^2.

The flat atom disappears in the second identity. Thus the weighted
Gram for omega=(1-t)rho_b dominates half the continuous weighted
Gram. Inversion on the degree-(d-1) polynomial space shows
k_omega(xi,xi)<=2k_((1-t)nu)(xi,xi). This comparison needs only that
finite polynomial space, and its positivity follows from the domination.

In x coordinates, (1-t)nu=a(1-x)dx/4. The squared norm of
P_n^(1,0) is a/[2(n+1)], and its value at one is n+1. The positive
Jacobi expansion in the source has the correct factorials. Its terms
are bounded by [(3/2)n^2 u]^r/(r!)^2, u=8/(a m), and their sum is
at most exp(sqrt(6)n sqrt(u))<=2 for n<d. Summing normalized squares
and then applying the factor two above gives k(xi,xi)<=16d^4/a.

5. THE SMALL-DEFICIT VECTOR AND THE ORTHOGONAL HEAD
--------------------------------------------------
For the source's u_b, weighted reproduction gives precisely

  <(S1-I)p(S1)h,u_b>=sqrt(beta)(xi-1)p(xi).

Adding the flat direction f with <h,f>=1/sqrt(2) enforces the
constant polynomial identity. Every degree-d polynomial is a constant
plus (t-1)p(t), so u and the head v1 have the same projection onto
K_d=span{h,S1 h,...,S1^d h}. The vector u has no head component.

Its deficit is exactly

  Delta=beta(xi-1)^2 k(xi,xi).

Using the loose beta<=1/[2(d+1)^2] is already enough to give
Delta<=128d^2/(a m^2)<=.000128 theta<theta/1000. The mixture's
stronger beta bound is not needed for that estimate.

The Legendre kernel bound |p|^2<=d^2 int p^2 dnu implies that at
most half the squared norm lies within a/(2d^2) of the upper endpoint.
It follows that int p^2 dnu <=(4d^2/a)int(1-t)p^2 dnu. Combining
the two exact comparisons in Section 4 transfers that inequality to
rho_b. Applied to k, it gives

  B^2=||u_b||^2 <=(4d^2/a)Delta
     <=512d^4/(a^2m^2)<=512*10^(-12).

Since the below-flat part of h has norm <=1/sqrt(2), the constant
constraint gives |u_f|<=sqrt(2beta)+B. Hence ||u||^2<=3B^2+4beta<1.
A unit vector in W perpendicular to f is perpendicular to K_d, u,
and v1. Completing u in that direction yields a real unit v2 with
v2 perpendicular to v1, the same projection onto K_d, and exactly
the same small deficit. Every dimension and norm requirement is met.

6. THE REFLECTION, COMMON S0, AND EXACT DATA
--------------------------------------------
Because v1-v2 is perpendicular to K_d, reflection in its perpendicular
hyperplane fixes K_d pointwise and swaps the orthonormal heads v1,v2.
It fixes h in particular. Set S2=R S1 R^T. Its spectrum and spectral
measure at h are identical to S1's, so ALL scalar resolvents and both
feature Grams are exactly equal, not merely exponentially close.
The degree-k powers at h are also exactly equal for k<=d.

The no-head measure (mu_G+delta_1)/2 has the same moments through 2d.
The degree-d moment Gram is positive definite. Identifying the two
orthonormal polynomial Krylov bases therefore identifies their actual
first d powers and unit starts. This partial isometry extends to a
real orthogonal map of the padded m-dimensional spaces. It constructs
S0 in the SAME coordinates as S1,S2 without changing their heads.

The Taylor remainder then gives, for j=1,2, every admissible z,

  ||H_z(Sj)h-H_z(S0)h||<=(8/3)4^(-d-1).

A smaller fixed kappa_c turns this into exp(-kappa_c sqrt(m)) for
all sufficiently large m. It holds simultaneously at every cone point,
so choosing later poles adaptively cannot invalidate the common oracle.
The positive measures also give the claimed exponentially close S0
scalar/Gram data. All spectra lie in [1-a,1+a], so the operators are
uniformly well-conditioned SPD; their required top/tail singular values
follow from the unique head and padded multiplicity at one.

7. INCOMPATIBILITY OF ALL COMMON RANK-ONE OUTPUTS
-------------------------------------------------
For any real rank-at-most-one C, its kernel meets the real plane
span{v1,v2} in a nonzero vector. Choose a unit x in that intersection.
The S1 cross term vanishes because v1 is its head; the S2 cross term
vanishes because v2 is its head. Reflection gives
||S2 v1||=||S1 v2||, so

  ||S1 x||^2+||S2 x||^2=xi^2+||S1 v2||^2
                      >=xi^2+1-2Delta.

Since Cx=0, at least one approximation error has square at least

  1+4theta+8theta^2-Delta.

This exceeds (1+theta)^2 by
2theta+7theta^2-Delta>theta. The proof therefore excludes every
common rank-one C, including nonsymmetric ones. It is a physical
output contradiction, not merely a failure to infer the spectral edge.

With a common admissible transcript, a randomized reconstruction has
the same output distribution on S1 and S2. Its two success sets are
disjoint, so their probabilities sum to at most one. Consequently one
input has success probability <=1/2. This statement is for the robust
oracle/data model specified below; it does not give an arithmetic bound.

8. ORACLE QUANTIFIERS AND EXACT-EXTRA CLARIFICATION
---------------------------------------------------
The allowed data are one supplied fixed h and its cone-resolvent actions
with an absolute error allowance; the errors may be coherently biased.
The oracle may return the exact S0 actions to every query. This remains
admissible for S1 and S2 under the uniform bound, however many times
the same or adaptive query is made. The full S0 transcript is itself
the exact transcript of one positive operator, including its derived
scalar/Gram values.

The S1/S2 true scalar/Gram data are identical, so adding those as exact
extras does not distinguish the hidden inputs either. To retain exact
joint consistency between such extras and the returned physical vectors,
use exact S1 actions as the common oracle for both inputs. The S1/S2
vector distance is at most twice their distance to S0; reduce kappa_c
and increase the lower threshold on m to absorb that factor. The exact
S1 transcript is positive-consistent and has the exact extras of BOTH
hidden inputs. Thus the strengthened interpretation also has the same
<=1/2 reconstruction conclusion.

No independently Gaussian start is proved hard: the matrices and their
hard h are constructed together. The head mass Theta(1/m) is a scale
comparison, not a Gaussian distribution statement. Additional independent
RHS queries, near-real poles, raw matrix/factor access, unbiased stochastic
noise, or special error correlations of a particular numerical solver
are outside this obstruction. Averaging adversarial repeated queries
does not reduce a fixed coherent bias.

9. COST INTERPRETATION AND THE ORIGINAL GATE
--------------------------------------------
The embedding with N=m^(9/8) (rounded to an integer when needed) has
AB=diag(Sj,0), and well-conditioned nonzero factors. A common ambient
rank-one approximation would give a common compressed m-by-m one,
contradicting Section 7. Nevertheless the different raw factors remain
available to a raw-input algorithm and can reveal information that this
one-start transcript omits. The embedding is not a raw-input lower bound.

The obstruction means a robust bridge certified ONLY by unrestricted
absolute action errors cannot use a polynomial error allowance here.
It needs a precision logarithm Omega(sqrt(m)). For the specific cited
source bound I q^(omega-2) log(q/eps)+m^omega, with
I=m^(17/8), q=Theta(sqrt(m)), omega=9/4, its leading block factor is
m^(9/4), and requesting that accuracy adds a sqrt(m) precision factor.
This gives the displayed m^(11/4) COST BOUND for that route. It does
not show that all algorithms must spend that time, that this sufficient
solver bound is sharp, or that actual solver errors realize the oracle.

Thus the note usefully rejects the proposed unrestricted-absolute-error
bridge. It does not settle the original theta=1/m product problem,
establish a general N^2 lower bound, or complete the user's broader
seminal-breakthrough objective.
