Theorem
-------
Let B denote the adjacency matrix of a fixed connected NONBIPARTITE
simple d-regular base graph, d>=3, with k vertices. Let A_N be the
adjacency matrix of its random N-lift: independently on each base
edge choose a uniform perfect matching between its two N-vertex fibers.
Fix an ODD prime p and lambda in F_p. Put

  t=dim_Fp ker(B-lambda I),
  D_N=dim_Fp ker(A_N-lambda I).

Then for every fixed integer r>=0,

  lim_(N->infinity) E p^(r D_N)
    =p^(r t) product_(j=0,...,r-1)(1+p^j).                (T)

The empty product at r=0 is one. Every positive lift size N is allowed;
there is no parity restriction or requirement p not dividing d or N.
The field, base, scalar and r are fixed before N tends to infinity.

The old base kernel injects into the lift kernel through fiber-constant
vectors, so D_N>=t. Equivalently all fixed p-power moments of the EXCESS
corank D_N-t converge to product_(j<r)(1+p^j). Section 9 proves that
these are the corresponding limiting moments for uniformly random
symmetric F_p matrices. This note asserts MOMENT EQUALITY, not a
limiting corank law, a cokernel/group law, or Cohen--Lenstra universality.
Such distribution claims require a separate determinacy argument.
Historical novelty is not certified.

1. Moments as vector labelings and exact type enumeration
---------------------------------------------------------
Set V=F_p^r, q=p^r. A matrix Y with one row in V at each lift vertex
satisfies A_NY=lambda Y exactly when all r of its columns belong to
ker(A_N-lambda I). Thus

  Z_r(A_N):=#{Y:A_NY=lambda Y}=p^(r D_N),
  M_r(N)=E Z_r(A_N).

An old-type-i ordered star consists of a root a in V and one neighbor
label b_j for every j adjacent to i. Its legal condition is

  sum_(j~i)b_j=lambda a.                                  (1)

There are q^d legal stars. Assigning their empirical counts T_i to
the N labeled vertices gives N!/product_s T_i(s)!. If m_ij(a,b)
records the directed root/neighbor table on base edge i->j, matching
compatibility is m_ji=m_ij^T. Its exact matching factor is
product_(a,b)m_ij(a,b)!/N!. Consequently

  M_r(N)=sum_(compatible types)
    product_i [N!/product_s T_i(s)!]
      *product_(edges ij)[product_(a,b)m_ij(a,b)!/N!].      (2)

The first multinomial already chooses root labels. No extra root
factor is inserted. The old base is simple, so all factors are cross
matchings: a candidate-color diagonal entry m_ij(a,a) has NO evenness
requirement. It connects two different old fibers, not a graph loop.

For star laws P_i and pair laws nu_ij=m_ij/N, uniform factorial
bounds give logarithm N Phi+O(log(N+1)), where

  Phi=sum_i H(P_i)-sum_(edges ij)H(nu_ij).

The number of types is polynomial in N for fixed B,p,r. At zero
counts, use 0 log 0=0. All errors are uniform. Lossless omission of
each physical neighbor slot, averaged over d omissions, gives

  Phi=-(d-2)/d sum_edges I(root;neighbor)
           -(1/d)sum_(i,t~i)J_it<=0,                     (3)

where J_it is conditional total correlation of retained d-1 neighbors.
These are REAL entropy operations, not division by d in F_p.


2. Analytic uniform-phase interface with all constants retained
---------------------------------------------------------------
Let P choose independent uniform LEGAL stars at every vertex, and Q
choose roots and all neighbor labels independently uniform. For a
compatible table array put

  W(m)=product_edges [product_(a,b)m_ij(a,b)!/N!],

and W=0 otherwise. The exact normalizations are

  M_r(N)=q^(dkN) E_P W,
          q^(dkN) E_Q W=1.                               (4)

For the second identity, every lift and all q^(kN) root labelings
determine their ordered neighbors uniquely. This normalization will
retain all Gaussian/factorial constants without an informal prefactor.

For one old type, let H_i be the real space of its d q-by-q total-zero
tables with common row sums. Put H=direct-sum_i H_i and let P_row be
the projection onto identical row-constant tables at a type. Under
BOTH P and Q, covariance on H_i is

  Sigma_i=q^(-2)[I+(d-1)P_row].                            (5)

It is positive definite. Conditional on the root, any two neighbor
labels are independent uniform even under P, since d>=3 leaves a
third slot to absorb their sum. This proves the equality of means
and covariances and the full real span. No inversion of lambda, d,
or a field-size-dependent graph parameter is used.

Let L0 be the full integer lattice in H. The Q support differences
generate L0 by within-row neighbor-label transfers and root changes.
The P difference lattice L has exactly the congruences

  C_ia(m)=sum_(j~i)sum_b b m_ij(a,b)-lambda a n_i(a)=0
                                                    in V, (6)

where n_i(a) is the common row margin. Before matching compatibility
these kq vector congruences are independent. Their complete lattice
index is

  [L0:L]=q^(kq)=p^(k q r).                               (7)

For completeness, character exhaustiveness follows at a fixed root
a from the Cauchy equation on sum_j b_j=lambda a: each slot character
is a row constant plus the SAME additive character chi_a(b). Variation
of a leaves precisely the character sum_a chi_a(C_ia). There are
q choices per chi_a. This proves that (6) supplies ALL periods, not
merely necessary congruences.

The finite-support tilted lattice local limit gives, uniformly in
||m-Nmu||<=sqrt(N)log N and on the legal affine lattice,

  P_P(M=m)=p^(k q r)(1+o(1))P_Q(M=m).                     (8)

This is relative error. Tilt size is O(log N/sqrt(N)); undoing it
has exponent error O(log^3 N/sqrt(N)). Fourier inversion has one
Gaussian maximum modulo the maximal reciprocal lattice and a
uniform modulus gap elsewhere. Positive covariance supplies the
local Gaussian bound. This proves genuine central support on those
affine lattices eventually; no semigroup-normality assumption is made.

On matching-compatible deviations write

  X_ij(a,b)=[r_i(a)+r_j(b)]/q+Z_ij(a,b),

where each r_i has zero total and Z has zero row/column sums.
Combining (5) with the exact Stirling expansion of W gives weighted
quadratic exponent

  -(q/2)sum_i||r_i||^2-(q^2/2)sum_edges||Z_ij||^2.        (9)

It is strictly negative in every nonzero compatible direction.
The ambient/compatible dimension difference equals the power from
the edge factorial prefactors. Taylor expansion of the analytic
rate function and the pairing entropy then bounds the part of any
sufficiently small fixed uniform neighborhood outside the logarithmic
window by polynomial(N) exp(-c log^2 N)=o(1).

These established local calculations apply to every fixed vector
dimension. The remaining issue for an EXACT coefficient is the rank
of (6) on the compatible integer lattice; that is derived next.


3. New calculation: all compatible vector congruence dependencies
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Let K0=L0 intersect the real matching-compatible subspace. It is the
integer lattice of edge tables with common root margins. Its reduction
mod p is the full compatible F_p table space: choose arbitrary integer
root margins of zero total, then freely choose the (q-1)-by-(q-1)
interior cells on each undirected edge and fill its last row/column.
No division is needed. Therefore there is no saturation or hidden
factor of p in reducing this lattice to a field-linear rank problem.

Regard V as column vectors solely for this dual calculation. A linear
dependence among the kqr scalar congruences has coefficients

  h_i(a) in V^*,           i in V(B), a in V.

Identify V^* with column covectors through the standard dot product.
An arbitrary 2-by-2 interaction transfer on edge ij has zero root
margins, hence imposes

 (h_i(a)-h_i(a')) dot (b-b')
   +(h_j(b)-h_j(b')) dot (a-a')=0                        (10)

for all a,a',b,b'. Take a'=b'=0. Testing against coordinate vectors
b makes every coordinate of h_i(a)-h_i(0) linear in a. Thus

  h_i(a)=K_i a+c_i,
  K_i^T+K_j=0               on every base edge ij.         (11)

Conversely (11) annihilates all interaction transfers. Along an even
walk K_i is unchanged; across one edge it becomes -K_i^T. Connected
nonbipartiteness supplies an odd cycle, forcing K_i=-K_i^T at its
vertices and then ONE COMMON SKEW-SYMMETRIC matrix K throughout the
base. Over odd p this space has dimension r(r-1)/2.

Now compute the remaining root-margin coefficient. On edge ij,

  h_i(a) dot b+h_j(b) dot a
    =(Ka) dot b+(Kb) dot a+c_i dot b+c_j dot a.

The two K terms cancel because K is skew. In the target term of
(6), lambda(Ka) dot a vanishes because a^TKa=0 over odd characteristic.
The coefficient on root margin n_i(a) is therefore

  [sum_(j~i)c_j-lambda c_i] dot a.                        (12)

Root margins at different old types are freely variable subject only
to their own total zero. A linear function of a annihilates all these
variations exactly when it is zero (its value at a=0 is zero). Hence

  sum_(j~i)c_j=lambda c_i          for every i.             (13)

This is r independent copies of the old base kernel, of dimension rt.
The parameters K and c are unique and independent: c_i=h_i(0), and
the remaining affine-linear part determines K. Thus the FULL dependence
dimension and congruence rank are respectively

  b_r=rt+r(r-1)/2,
  rho_r=k q r-b_r.                                       (14)

This exhausts both interaction and root-margin directions, which
generate the compatible space. A lower-bound count of explicit
dependencies alone would not have sufficed; (10)--(13) also prove
that none are missing.


4. Exact full-uniform contribution
-----------------------------------
The legal compatible sublattice has index p^(rho_r) in K0. The affine
lattice is nonempty for EVERY N: all-zero legal stars give a compatible
table array, and their N-fold sum has the required table totals.
The fixed-index lattice Gaussian Riemann sum in (9) assigns precisely
p^(-rho_r) of the full compatible Gaussian sum to this legal coset,
asymptotically. A fixed translate does not change that limit.
The affine translate may depend on N: reducing its offset to a
fixed fundamental cell gives a uniformly bounded representative,
which vanishes after scaling by sqrt(N). Thus the same statement
holds along every residue class and every sequence of lift sizes.

Multiplying this fraction by the local density ratio (8) leaves

  U_r=p^(k q r-rho_r)=p^(rt+r(r-1)/2).                    (15)

The normalized weighted Q central mass tends to ONE: it describes
a random lift and iid uniform root labels. On any one base matching,
its N matched label pairs are independent uniform pairs. Their count
table is within sqrt(N)log N of the mean with probability 1-o(1),
by Chebyshev; a finite union handles all base edges. Dependence of
different edge tables is immaterial. Thus (15) is an exact limit,
not merely an upper bound. The fixed-neighborhood tails in Section 2
extend it to any sufficiently small fixed uniform edge neighborhood.

At r=0 the alphabet has one label, the unique solution contributes
one exactly, and set U_0=1. This treats the zero-dimensional Gaussian
case directly.


5. The finite zero phases and exact scalar lifting of their offsets
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Equality in (3) forces every edge mutual information and every retained
conditional total correlation to vanish. The finite-group argument is
briefly recalled. For d random variables with fixed sum and every d-1
tuple independent, condition d-2 variables and compare the two remaining
marginals by reflection. Their support-difference spaces coincide.
Changing one conditioned value gives translation invariance under
that common space. Each marginal is therefore UNIFORM on a whole
affine coset, not a nonuniform subgroup measure.

At an old type all neighboring subspaces coincide. Even-walk propagation
and connected nonbipartiteness make them one common H<=V. Every H is
invariant under scalar lambda. The phase data are exactly

  H<=V,
  a_i in V/H with sum_(j~i)a_j=lambda a_i.                 (16)

They determine root laws Uniform(a_i+H), product edge laws, and the
unique uniform legal-star laws on their affine supports. There are
finitely many phases. Distinct coset data give distinct root laws,
and hence distinct edge-table centers because every old vertex has
positive degree.

An essential scalar simplification is that EVERY quotient map in
(16) lifts to an exact base intertwiner in V. Choose any F_p-linear
section sigma:V/H->V. It commutes with multiplication by lambda,
so b_i=sigma(a_i) satisfies

  sum_(j~i)b_j=lambda b_i.                                (17)

For labels inside those cosets, translate by the fiber-constant b_i.
This is a bijection to homogeneous H-valued solutions. An arbitrary
choice of representatives need not satisfy (17), but this linear
section supplies one. The argument would NOT automatically work for
a nonsplit general matrix target R; scalar action is crucial here.


6. Leaking outside a proper coset is negligible, with correlation charged
------------------------------------------------------------------------
For phase (H,a), let D_a(Y) count vertices whose label lies outside
the specified coset. Put h=dim H<r, and let M_h(N) be the homogeneous
H-labeling moment. Quotienting and subtracting (16) gives a homogeneous
quotient solution w with support size D_a(Y). For every fixed quotient
assignment, its lifts form an affine system over H; a nonempty fiber
has cardinality exactly Z_h(A_N). In particular it is bounded by that
homogeneous kernel count.

A nonzero quotient support S of size m has no outside vertex with
exactly one support neighbor. The certificate of all incident edges
satisfies E-v>=(d-2)m/2 and E<=dm. Support one is impossible because
the lift of a simple base is a simple positive-degree graph.

The possible correlation between this rare support and Z_h must be
charged. To force E prescribed matching edges, swap images in the
corresponding independent base-edge permutations one pair at a time.
Every step produces EXACTLY the uniform law conditional on the forced
pairs: each permissible output has the same number of prior preimages.
Earlier prescribed pairs stay fixed. Each image swap changes at most
four vertex rows of the H-valued operator A-lambda I, so its rank
changes by at most 4h. Under this coupling,

  E[Z_h(A_N)|certificate]<=p^(4hE) M_h(N).                 (18)

Counting support/exterior vertices, nonzero quotient labels, and
colored stub pairings, and using probability <=(2/N)^E, gives for
some fixed L and sufficiently small fixed epsilon>0,

 E #{Y: A_NY=lambda Y, D_a(Y)=m}
   <=M_h(N) d m L^m (m/N)^((d-2)m/2),
                   1<=m<=epsilon N.                     (19)

Here p^(4hE) is absorbed into L^m because E<=dm. The factorial
denominators for support/exterior choices and E-v>=(d-2)m/2 supply
the negative power of N. These are precisely the proved estimates
of the source sparse module. A geometric ratio bounds the sum
starting at m=2 by

 E #{Y:0<D_a(Y)<=epsilon N}
      <=C N^(-(d-2)) M_h(N).                             (20)

Thus leakage is o(1) whenever the lower-dimensional moment is bounded.
No independence of a defect event and the bulk kernel size is assumed.


7. Disjoint phase neighborhoods: the exact contribution is U_h
----------------------------------------------------------------
Fix r and consider its finite family of phase EDGE-table centers.
Choose sufficiently small PAIRWISE DISJOINT open neighborhoods of
these centers. Each contains its phase and is contained in the
appropriate fixed uniform-within-cosets neighborhood; shrink radii
to meet all intrinsic uniform-phase and sparse constants.

Small edge-table distance to a proper phase implies at most epsilon N
vertices lie outside its root cosets. Indeed a row margin is obtained
from any incident pair table. In a fixed finite alphabet its outside
mass is bounded by a fixed multiple of the table distance; summing
over the finitely many old types gives the claim.

Split a proper phase neighborhood into exactly-in-coset labels and
nonzero leakage. By (17), exactly-in-coset labels translate bijectively
to H-valued homogeneous solutions. The imposed edge neighborhood
becomes an intrinsic FULL-UNIFORM H edge neighborhood. By Section 4
its expected contribution tends to U_h, where

  U_h=p^(ht+h(h-1)/2).

The leakage tends to zero by (20) and boundedness of M_h. Hence the
phase contributes U_h+o(1). At h=0 this is the one exact point map;
at h=r there is only the full uniform phase and Section 4 applies
directly, without a leakage estimate.

It would be WRONG to assign the entire moment M_h(N) to each phase:
that would count its nested lower-subspace phases repeatedly. Only
the intrinsic H-uniform neighborhood is counted as this phase's
leading contribution. Disjoint edge-table neighborhoods make this
distinction literal at the level of actual empirical assignments.

The complement of these neighborhoods in the compact feasible
star-law space contains no zero of the continuous entropy exponent
(3). Its maximum is strictly negative. The exact count (2), zero-safe
factorial errors, and polynomially many types then bound its total
contribution by polynomial(N) exp(-cN)=o(1). Phase-neighborhood
boundaries introduce no exception, since every zero lies strictly
inside its own open neighborhood.


8. Closed scalar induction, phase count, and the Gaussian binomial sum
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Induct on r. At r=0, M_0(N)=1. Suppose (T) has been proved in every
dimension h<r. Their limits in particular give bounded M_h(N),
which is exactly the hypothesis needed in (20) for every proper H.
Sections 4--7 consequently evaluate all phase contributions and the
complement in dimension r. No same-dimensional bounded-moment input
or circular induction is used.

For fixed H of dimension h, the quotient V/H has dimension r-h.
The space of quotient base maps in (16) has dimension t(r-h), so
there are EXACTLY p^(t(r-h)) such coset phases. This counts cosets,
not chosen representatives or all their possible lifts; multiplying
again by p^(th) for different representatives would overcount.

The number of h-dimensional subspaces of V is the Gaussian binomial
[r choose h]_p. Therefore the evaluated limit is

  sum_(h=0)^r [r choose h]_p p^(t(r-h)) U_h
    =p^(rt) sum_(h=0)^r [r choose h]_p p^(h(h-1)/2).      (21)

To evaluate the sum without an imported identity, let F_r be its
last sum. The elementary subspace recurrence

  [r choose h]_p=[r-1 choose h]_p
                      +p^(r-h)[r-1 choose h-1]_p

follows by intersecting with a fixed hyperplane. With h=l+1 in
the second term, its power becomes
(l+1)l/2+r-l-1=l(l-1)/2+r-1. Hence

  F_r=(1+p^(r-1))F_(r-1),          F_0=1.

Thus F_r=product_(j=0)^(r-1)(1+p^j). Substitution in (21) proves
(T) and simultaneously gives the bounded lower-dimensional moments
required at the next induction step.

Examples:
  r=1:  E p^(D_N) ->2p^t;
  r=2:  E p^(2D_N) ->2(p+1)p^(2t);
  r=3:  E p^(3D_N) ->2(p+1)(p^2+1)p^(3t).


9. Exact comparator calculation for uniform symmetric matrices
--------------------------------------------------------------
Let S_m be uniformly distributed among all symmetric m-by-m matrices
over F_p. For a fixed m-by-r matrix X of rank h, the condition S_m X=0
means its symmetric bilinear form annihilates im X. By an invertible
congruence, this fixes the first h rows/columns to zero. It has

  mh-h(h-1)/2

independent linear constraints, so probability p^(-mh+h(h-1)/2).
The number of rank-h matrices X is

  [r choose h]_p product_(j=0)^(h-1)(p^m-p^j).

Consequently

  E p^(r nullity S_m)
   =sum_(h<=min(r,m)) [r choose h]_p p^(h(h-1)/2)
                           product_(j=0)^(h-1)(1-p^(j-m)).

For fixed r and m tending to infinity this converges to F_r, the
same product in (T). If a symmetric bilinear form is constrained
to annihilate a fixed t-dimensional subspace, it descends to a
uniform symmetric form on the quotient and adds t deterministic
kernel dimensions. Its limiting moments are therefore p^(rt)F_r.
This argument does not require that the fixed subspace be nonisotropic
for a chosen ambient dot product.

Thus the theorem gives exactly the symmetric-matrix excess-corank
MOMENTS with an old kernel of dimension t. It does not identify a
limiting law solely by invoking equality of these unbounded moments.

11. Boundaries and assessment
-----------------------------
The proof is for a fixed connected NONBIPARTITE SIMPLE regular base
of degree at least three and a fixed ODD prime. Bipartite bases can
have two distinct side-subspaces and additional interaction dependencies,
especially at lambda=0; this formula is not asserted there. At degree
two, componentwise constants already give divergent moments. At
characteristic two, skew/alternating constraints and quadratic terms
need a different count. A nonscalar target need not split its quotient
phases, and its skew compatibility space depends on that target.

No uniformity in growing r, p, or base size, and no quantitative N
error rate, is claimed. The local analytic constants can depend on
all fixed data. No full cokernel law, group law, distributional
universality, moment determinacy, or historical priority conclusion
is made. Within its stated scalar fixed-base scope, the formula
survives the algebra, lattice, phase-count, and leakage checks above.
