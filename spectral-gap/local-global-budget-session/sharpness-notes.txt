Flat escaped-budget construction for a doubled unit bridge
=========================================================
Research proof, 2026-11-15. Only this session file is modified.

Conclusion
----------
The single-port leaf obstruction can be avoided by giving each target
reservoir MANY ports. A repeated colored binary-tree gadget realizes,
uniformly over all reservoir contacts, an arbitrary weighted star
behind two compulsory access resistances of one. This gives

  liminf_{epsilon->0} epsilon W_bridge,3,alpha(epsilon)
       >= alpha log(6/5).

Combined with a separately proved matching local-budget upper, this
would establish the exact coefficient. The lower argument below uses
the separated-mass lemma already proved in the frozen independent
unweighted manuscript, and proves the multiport extension of its
reservoir realization. It does not assume that individual leaves
supply equal increments.

1. The target finite pair and exact equal allocation
--------------------------------------------------
Fix an arbitrary integer m>=2 and 0<s<log(6/5). Write

  c=1/3, c'=2/5, f(g)=(c'+g)/(c+g).

Thus f(0)=6/5 and f(g) decreases continuously to one. Define

  r_i=(6/5) exp(-i s/m),
  g_i=(c'-r_i c)/(r_i-1),    0<=i<=m,
  q_i=g_i-g_{i-1}>0,        1<=i<=m,
  t_i=1/q_i, T=diag(t_1,...,t_m).

All quantities are finite since r_m>1; g_0=0. Consider

  K=(T+3 11^T)^(-1),       K'=(T+(5/2)11^T)^(-1).

Both are SPD, and K'-K is positive rank one. Equivalently they are the
terminal stiffnesses of a star with terminal conductances q_i and
anchor branch conductances c and c'. If I_i={i,...,m}, the matrix
inversion/determinant lemma gives

  det K'_{I_i}/det K_{I_i} = f(g_{i-1})/f(g_m).

Indeed K=diag(q)-q q^T/(c+g_m), so
  det K_{I_i}=(product_{j in I_i}q_j)(c+g_{i-1})/(c+g_m),
and similarly for K'. Therefore the reverse Schur pivot ratios satisfy

  log(kappa_i'/kappa_i)
    =log f(g_{i-1})-log f(g_i)=s/m,  1<=i<=m.             (1)

The separated-mass lemma then makes all m relative fractional
correction eigenvalues tend to

                  exp(alpha s/m)-1.                     (2)

2. Exact bounded-degree repeated-block network
---------------------------------------------
Let B be ANY finite rooted full binary tree with exactly m leaves,
labeled 1,...,m. There is no restriction that m be a power of two.
For a terminal current vector j in R^m, let R_B be the positive
semidefinite matrix whose quadratic form is

  j^T R_B j = sum_{edges a of B} (sum_{i below a} j_i)^2.

Choose Q=2^h, h>=1. On the right of the edited bridge put a complete
binary tree of depth h, and put a copy of B at each of its Q leaves
(identify the two roots). The copies have corresponding leaf labels.
Every leaf labeled i is connected by a neck of ell_i unit edges to a
distinct port in reservoir i. All Q ports with label i belong to the
SAME connected reservoir i. Take ell_i to be any positive integer with
ell_i/Q -> t_i as Q->infinity, e.g. max(1,round(Q t_i)).

On the left of the bridge put a complete binary tree of depth h,
whose Q leaves are Q distinct ports in ONE anchor reservoir. The leaves
here are identified with the reservoir ports, not joined by an extra
edge. Thus the last tree edge is the external attachment to the port.
The roots of the left and right trees are joined by the edited unit
bridge. It separates the anchor reservoir from every target reservoir.

The reservoirs will have internal degree-two ports, each acquiring
exactly one external attachment. All other reservoir vertices have
degree three; all internal binary-tree vertices have degree at most
three; right leaves have degree two. The host is simple, unweighted,
connected, and has maximum degree three. Doubling changes only the
bridge conductance, from one to two.

First idealize each reservoir as a zero-resistance conductor at its
mean. Ground the anchor and inject j_i at target i; set J=sum_i j_i.
Uniqueness and tree-copy symmetry make each copy of B carry currents
j_i/Q at its colored leaves. The two top trees each contribute energy
J^2 sum_{d=1}^h 2^(-d)=J^2(1-1/Q). The B copies together contribute
j^T R_B j/Q; the necks contribute sum_i ell_i j_i^2/Q. Hence the exact
ideal terminal impedance matrices are

  Z_Q = diag(ell_i/Q)+(3-2/Q)11^T+R_B/Q,
  Z'_Q= diag(ell_i/Q)+(5/2-2/Q)11^T+R_B/Q.               (3)

They tend in norm to T+3 11^T and T+(5/2)11^T. Their inverses tend to
the target K,K'. This calculation is for arbitrary signed j, not only
positive injections, so it proves a matrix statement.

3. Multiport reservoir contacts are uniformly negligible
-------------------------------------------------------
Take cubic expander reservoirs with Laplacian gap at least gamma_0>0.
For each reservoir, subdivide Q distinct edges once and use the new
vertices as ports. All ports have degree two. The graph size is the
old even size plus Q, which can approximate any fixed positive mass
ratio after common scaling; Q is fixed before that volume limit.

The subdivided reservoirs have a gap gamma>0 independent of Q and
volume. Here is an elementary bound. Let E be the new graph energy,
mu the mean on the old vertices, and V_old their variance. Then
E_old<=2E and V_old<=2E/gamma_0. Assign every new port to one endpoint
of its subdivided old edge. Each old vertex is assigned at most three
ports. Therefore

  sum_ports (x_p-mu)^2 <=2E+6 V_old,
  sum_all_vertices (x_v-mu)^2 <=2E+7 V_old
                               <=(2+14/gamma_0)E.

Variance about the new mean is smaller. Thus one may use
  gamma=(2+14/gamma_0)^(-1), R=1/gamma.
The needed cubic expander existence is precisely the standard
Bordenave/Friedman input of the frozen manuscript; no stronger
expander property is required.

For one reservoir define the Q-by-Q port contact matrix

  H=P_ports^T L_res^dagger P_ports.

The columns of P_ports are the coordinate vectors at the ports.
The uniform gap gives 0<=H<=R I. If the port current vector is a,
the internal load, when mean current sum(a) is supplied uniformly,
is P_ports a - (sum(a)/n)1. Its minimum internal flow energy is
exactly a^T H a. This proves the contact formula even when the
reservoir mean is the terminal variable rather than a physical port.

Let K_Q(H),K'_Q(H) denote the true zero-frequency terminal stiffnesses,
with the reservoir MEANS as terminal coordinates and anchor mean
zero. Thomson's minimum-flow principle and the ideal symmetric flow
from Section 2 yield the uniform Loewner bounds

  0 <= K_Q(H)^(-1)-Z_Q <= (R/Q)(I+11^T),
  0 <= K'_Q(H)^(-1)-Z'_Q <= (R/Q)(I+11^T).              (4)

The lower inequality drops the nonnegative internal reservoir energies.
For the upper one use j_i/Q at each target-i port and -J/Q at each
anchor port; their contact cost is at most
(R/Q)(sum_i j_i^2+J^2). This ideal flow is an admissible trial flow
inside the real reservoirs, using their minimum-energy internal flows.

Consequently K_Q(H)->K and K'_Q(H)->K' uniformly over EVERY allowed
contact tuple H=(H_0,...,H_m), 0<=H_i<=R I. No convergence of the
individual contacts needs to be assumed at this stage. Positivity and
the rank-one update persist: eliminating a fixed graph core under a
single-edge rank-one update gives a positive rank-one change of its
terminal stiffness. Here it is nonzero because the bridge separates
anchor and targets.

In fact the exact true impedance identity is

  K'_Q(H)^(-1)=K_Q(H)^(-1)-(1/2)11^T.

Every injection vector j forces bridge current J=sum j, so changing its
resistance subtracts exactly J^2/2 from the minimum flow energy; all
other flow energies and constraints are identical. Sherman--Morrison
therefore gives an explicit continuous positive rank-one stiffness
update throughout the contact family.

Since m and the target pair are fixed, reverse pivot determinants are
continuous and strictly positive near K,K'. For any eta>0, choose one
finite Q so large that every allowed contact tuple satisfies

  log(kappa_i'(H)/kappa_i(H)) >= (s-eta)/m, all i.       (5)

This is the key uniform statement needed before choosing masses.

4. Passing to actual unit vertex masses
--------------------------------------
For the fixed finite network chosen above, give target i prescribed
mean mass M_i=delta^(-(m-i)). By the separated-mass lemma, its grounded
relative fractional correction approaches the diagonal pivot response.
The family of true terminal pairs is a compact subset of the SPD pairs
near K,K', parameterized by 0<=H_i<=R I. Thus the lemma's compact-family
uniformity applies. A single sufficiently small fixed delta realizes
all m grounded response eigenvalues arbitrarily close from below to
exp(alpha(s-eta)/m)-1, simultaneously for all allowed contacts.

Now give the anchor a sufficiently large but finite prescribed mass
M_0. As M_0->infinity, the mass-normalized full (m+1)-terminal stiffness
converges to zero direct-sum the grounded pair. Its common kernel
converges to the anchor coordinate and all other eigenvalues stay
positive. Finite-dimensional fractional and inverse-on-support
functional calculus thus transfer the m lower eigenvalues uniformly
in the same compact contact family.

Finally realize masses by expander reservoir sizes n_i(u) with
n_i(u)/u -> M_i as u->infinity. Use Q subdivided ports in every one;
Q and the entire core are fixed. The multiport version of the frozen
relative-reservoir lemma is proved exactly as follows.

Let P_u be the normalized reservoir indicators and use its complement
for mean-zero reservoir directions and core coordinates. The Laplacian
has blocks (A_u,B_u; B_u^T,F_u), with

  ||A_u||=O(1/u), ||B_u||=O(1/sqrt(u)), F_u>=eta_0 I.

The constants may depend on the now fixed core and Q. For the last
bound, the internal gaps control all mean-zero reservoir norms. Each
of the finitely many port deviations is controlled by that norm; the
fixed core's positive Dirichlet stiffness with all reservoir pins
zero controls its norm by external edge energy plus port deviations.
The same bounds hold after the bridge update. The static Schur
complement is exactly the true mean-coordinate network stiffness,
normalized by n_i(u)^(-1/2); this uses the contact calculation in
Section 3. Its u-scaled limit along any contact-convergent subsequence
is the corresponding prescribed-mass stiffness pair.

Block elimination gives m+1 low eigenvalues of order 1/u (one zero),
all other eigenvalues bounded below, and low isometries within
O(1/sqrt(u)) of P_u. Their updated-to-baseline high-space leakage is
O(1/sqrt(u)). Compressing the updated bounded high fractional power
therefore costs O(1/u), which becomes O(u^(alpha-1)) after relative
normalization and vanishes because alpha<1. The low fractional
parts and baseline inverse factors converge to the prescribed-mass
relative pair. This proves convergence of the baseline low-space
compression. Min-max transfers its m lower eigenvalues to the full
host correction. This argument asserts no convergence or disappearance
of the high response.

The contact matrices always lie in a compact set. Any failure of
uniform approximation to the contact-dependent mass pair would have
a contact-convergent subsequence and contradict the preceding block
argument. Thus one sufficiently large finite u realizes the desired
m lower eigenvalues, without needing to tune or predict contacts.

5. The strict-threshold coefficient
----------------------------------
For any fixed s<log(6/5), eta>0, and arbitrary m, the finite choices
Q,delta,M_0,u above can be made so that the full graph has m correction
eigenvalues as close from below as desired to

                 exp(alpha(s-eta)/m)-1.

Given small epsilon, choose ANY integer
  m < alpha(s-eta)/log(1+epsilon)
with m log(1+epsilon)->alpha(s-eta). There is a strict positive margin
between the displayed target eigenvalues and epsilon; perform all
finite approximations within that margin. Then

  liminf epsilon W_bridge,3,alpha(epsilon)>=alpha(s-eta).

Let eta decrease to zero and s increase to log(6/5). This proves the
lower coefficient stated at the start. The construction is existential
and may require enormous reservoirs; no efficiency claim is made.

6. Optional extension to every fixed maximum degree
--------------------------------------------------
The same lower proof works for every integer d>=3.
Use (d-1)-ary top trees with Q=(d-1)^h, while keeping any m-leaf binary
bottom block and cubic reservoirs. The two access resistances tend to
1/(d-2), giving R=d/(d-2), R'=(d+2)/(2(d-2)), hence ratio
2d/(d+2). Replace c,c' and log(6/5) throughout by 1/R,1/R' and
log(2d/(d+2)). This realizes the matching proposed bridge coefficient
alpha log(2d/(d+2)); maximum degree never exceeds d.

Primary input checked
---------------------
Charles Bordenave, A new proof of Friedman's second eigenvalue theorem
and its extension to random lifts, Ann. Sci. ENS 53 (2020), Theorem 1.
https://arxiv.org/html/1502.04482v4 (Theorem 1, lines 58--64 in the
browser text, inspected in this session). It bounds mu_2 and |mu_n|
by 2 sqrt(d-1)+epsilon with probability tending to one along every
admissible size sequence. Setting d=3 and epsilon=(3-2 sqrt(2))/2
supplies cubic expanders at every sufficiently large even size with
gamma_0=(3-2 sqrt(2))/2. The bound on mu_2<3 also ensures connectivity.
All new electrical and approximation steps above are derived here.
