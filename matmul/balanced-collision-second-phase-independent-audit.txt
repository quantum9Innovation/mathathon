1. Precise restricted-sketch statement
-------------------------------------
For d>=2 fixed, 0<epsilon<1 and 1<d*epsilon<sqrt(2), select t fixed
coordinates and their zero-sum contrast subspace of dimension r=t-1.
Let s/t^(3/2)->c>0, m=sq, q->Q in {2,3,...,infinity}. Average d independent
balanced partition Grams. Its limiting squared-norm embedding success is

  exp(-lambda_W-lambda_T),
  lambda_W=d(d-1)(1-1/Q)^2/(2c^2),
  lambda_T=d(1-1/Q)(1-2/Q)/(6c^2),

where 1/Q=0 at infinity. Counts, input subspace, tolerance and the
balanced finite-population model all match the statement. Total stacked
width is asymptotic to dc*r^(3/2). Genuine compression by a fixed factor
is assured when Q>d. The unmodified Gram law is distinguished from an
implementation that switches to exact identity when stacking is too wide.
Neither tolerance-window endpoint nor a complete phase diagram is claimed.

2. Exact probabilities and independent Poisson motifs
----------------------------------------------------
For specified disjoint groups in distinct buckets, the exact probability
is (s)_h*product(q)_(v_j)/(m)_(sum v_j). Merging groups adds an extra
inverse power of s. The resulting fixed-pattern expansion is uniform
over q>=2 as an ABSOLUTE error; no division by a zero triangle constant
occurs at q=2. A colored connected constraint of rank R has probability
O(s^-R), with a uniform fixed-pattern constant.

The wedge count uses a center, an unordered leaf pair and different
colors on its two edges: d(d-1)(t)_3/2 objects, each of probability p^2.
The triangle count has d(t)_3/6 objects, each of probability
(q-1)(q-2)/[(m-1)(m-2)]. These give the displayed means exactly.

Ordered vertex-disjoint motif tuples yield all leading mixed factorial
moments. For a connected union on v>=4 vertices, colored rank R>=v-1
because replacing every monochromatic component by a spanning tree
preserves connectivity. Thus its contribution is O(t^(3/2-v/2))=o(1).
On three vertices, two distinct motif objects require R>=3; a rank-two
pattern determines exactly one wedge or one monochromatic triangle.
This proves that overlapping tuples are negligible. Shared colors across
disjoint components are correctly handled by the joint assignment formula.

The source derives the joint Poisson limit by independent thinning and
finite Bonferroni bounds, rather than exchanging an unbounded series
with expectation. Tightness follows from bounded first moments. In
particular the zero-motif probability has the displayed exponential limit.

3. Collision graph spectrum and mean projection
-----------------------------------------------
Repeated colored pairs and rainbow triangles have vanishing expected
counts. A component on at least four vertices contains a four-vertex
tree, with colored constraint rank three and total expectation
O(t^4/s^3)=o(1). With high probability, the only components are isolated
vertices, edges, differently colored three-vertex paths and monochromatic
triangles. The raw motif counts then equal their corresponding components.

On the contrast space, the exact error operator is
  (1-p)^-1 P_t(A_col/d+pI)P_t.
A matching has adjacency norm at most one, hence is accepted eventually
because d*epsilon>1. A path has local Perron eigenvalue sqrt(2), a triangle
has eigenvalue two. Centering its unit nonnegative eigenvector loses only
O(1/t) in the Rayleigh quotient: with z=(1^Tv)^2/t<=3/t the quotient is
at least lambda*(1-2z)/(1-z). Nonnegative other components cannot worsen
that lower bound. Consequently either component causes failure eventually
because d*epsilon<sqrt(2). Success is therefore equivalent, up to an
event of probability o(1), to absence of both motifs.

The same subspace instance is a spectral AMM example via A=U^T,B=U,
with AB=I. This is an explicitly restricted input family, not a uniform
arbitrary-factor or relative-tail guarantee. The finite-q constants and
the stacked width arithmetic are consistent with that interpretation.
