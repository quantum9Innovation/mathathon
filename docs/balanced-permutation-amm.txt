Balanced permutation sketches for approximate matrix multiplication
==================================================================

Research continuation candidate, 2026-11-14. The identities and finite
variance formulas below are proved here. This is NOT a new embedding
width theorem, a relative-spectral AMM theorem, a priority claim, or a
completed breakthrough. It records a concrete route connecting the
matrix-product work in docs to the centered-permutation results in
spectral-gap, and identifies its remaining combinatorial obligation.

1. The sketch and its exact Gram factor
-------------------------------------

Let m=sq, with integers 2<=s<=m and q>=1. Partition the m coordinates
uniformly into s labeled buckets, each of size q. Equivalently, let P
be a uniform m-by-m permutation matrix, let H_0 be the s-by-m incidence
matrix of a fixed balanced partition, and set H=H_0 P. Each column of
H has one entry equal to one; all other entries are zero. Each row has
q entries equal to one. Define

  p=(q-1)/(m-1),  J_0=11^T/m,  Pi_0=I-J_0,
  W=(H^T H-p11^T)/(1-p).                                    (1)

For i!=j the probability that i and j occupy the same bucket is p.
The diagonal entries of H^T H are one. Hence

  E W=I,  W1=1.                                             (2)

Write J_s=1_s1_s^T/s and Pi_s=I_s-J_s. Since HH^T=qI_s,

  Pi_H=(1/q)H^T Pi_s H

is an orthogonal projection of rank s-1, with range orthogonal to 1.
Putting lambda=q/(1-p)=(m-1)/(s-1) gives the exact identity

  W=J_0+lambda Pi_H.                                        (3)

In particular W is PSD, has rank s, and has eigenvalues 1 on span{1},
lambda on the bucket-constant zero-sum subspace, and zero on the
within-bucket zero-sum subspace. When q=1, W=I and there is no compression.

The apparent rank s+1 in (1) can be reduced to s without a dense
factorization. An explicit s-by-m factor is

  S=[(1-p)^(-1/2)Pi_s+q^(-1/2)J_s]H,   S^T S=W.             (4)

Indeed the two row projections are orthogonal, so S^T S equals
(1-p)^(-1)H^T Pi_s H+q^(-1)H^T J_s H, which is (3). Applying S
requires unsigned bucket aggregation followed by subtraction and scaling
of the mean over bucket rows. Thus the bucket incidence is unsigned;
the complete Gram factor includes a rank-one centering operation.

2. An unbiased AMM estimator with complete arithmetic costs
----------------------------------------------------------

Let A be n-by-m and B be m-by-v, fixed before the random partition.
The estimator

  C=A W B
   =[(AH^T)(HB)-p(A1)(1^T B)]/(1-p)
   =(AS^T)(SB)                                               (5)

satisfies E C=AB and rank C<=s. Bucket aggregation and centering build
the factors in O(m(n+v)+s(n+v)) arithmetic operations, plus O(m)
permutation generation. Naive explicit expansion takes O(nvs); fast
rectangular multiplication can replace this term if desired. Storing
the factors takes O(s(n+v)). Unbiasedness alone is not an accuracy
guarantee.

For d independent partitions define Wbar=d^(-1)sum_c W_c and Cbar=A Wbar B.
The constant direction can be stored only once:

  Wbar=J_0+(lambda/d)sum_c Pi_(H_c),
  rank Wbar<=min(m,1+d(s-1)).                                 (6)

One explicit factor uses the row 1^T/sqrt(m), followed by the d blocks
sqrt(lambda/d) Q_c^T, where the columns of Q_c form an orthonormal
basis of range Pi_(H_c). Such a basis need not be constructed densely:
the s bucket rows with their common row mean removed give a redundant
factor of the same asymptotic size, and a fixed Householder transform
on the s bucket coordinates can remove the zero row in O(s) work per
input/output column. The complete naive cost is

  O(d m(n+v)+nv[1+d(s-1)]),                                  (7)

with O((n+v)[1+d(s-1)]) output-factor storage. For fixed total width
and n=v=N, explicit output therefore costs O(N^2). No assertion is
made that fixed width achieves the requested accuracy on arbitrary A,B.

3. Exact matrix variance and a basic rigorous AMM consequence
------------------------------------------------------------

Let T=W-I. Permutation invariance, or (2)-(3), gives

  E Pi_H=Pi_0/lambda,
  T=lambda Pi_H-Pi_0,
  E T^2=(lambda-1)Pi_0=c Pi_0,
  c=(m-s)/(s-1).                                            (8)

For independent centered T_c and Tbar=d^(-1)sum_c T_c, the cross
expectations vanish, entrywise and as matrix products. Consequently

  E Tbar^2=(c/d)Pi_0,
  E||Tbar x||_2^2=(c/d)||Pi_0 x||_2^2.                        (9)

For the AMM error E_AMM=A Tbar B, positivity and the Frobenius trace
identity yield

  E||E_AMM||_F^2
   <= (c/d) min{ ||A Pi_0||^2 ||Pi_0 B||_F^2,
                 ||A Pi_0||_F^2 ||Pi_0 B||^2 }.             (10)

For example, ||A Tbar B||_F<=||A Pi_0|| ||Tbar B||_F and
E||Tbar B||_F^2=tr(B^T E Tbar^2 B); the other inequality follows
by transposing. Markov's inequality and ||X||<=||X||_F therefore give
the valid probability bound

  Pr{||E_AMM||>epsilon}
  <= [c/(d epsilon^2)]
       min{ ||A Pi_0||^2 ||Pi_0 B||_F^2,
            ||A Pi_0||_F^2 ||Pi_0 B||^2 }.                   (11)

This elementary bound can be large when s<<m. It neither proves nor
disproves that higher moments give substantially better subspace or
spectral AMM bounds. In particular (9) measures the entire vector
Tbar x, not just its projection back into a target subspace; it is
not itself the variance of the squared-norm embedding error.

4. Exact squared-norm variance by collision classes
--------------------------------------------------

Assume m>=4. For a zero-sum unit vector x put t4=sum_i x_i^4 and
Z=x^T(W-I)x. Let

  r3=(q-1)(q-2)/[(m-1)(m-2)],
  r22=p[(q-2)(q-3)+(m-q)(q-1)]/[(m-2)(m-3)].

Then E Z=0 and

  Var Z = [2p(1-t4)+8r3(t4-1/2)+r22(3-6t4)-p^2]/(1-p)^2.  (12)

Proof. Write I_ij for the same-bucket indicator and
Q=2sum_(i<j) I_ij x_i x_j. Since sum x_i=0 and ||x||=1,
Z=(Q+p)/(1-p) and E Q=-p. In Q^2 the two unordered coordinate
pairs can be equal, have one shared vertex, or be disjoint.

The equal-pair contribution is
4p sum_(i<j)x_i^2x_j^2=2p(1-t4).

For the shared-vertex terms the ordered sum of pair weights is
sum_i x_i^2[(sum_(j!=i)x_j)^2-sum_(j!=i)x_j^2]=2t4-1.
Their probability is r3, yielding 4r3(2t4-1).

The sum of all unordered pair weights is -1/2. Thus the ordered sum
over disjoint pairs is
1/4-(1-t4)/2-(2t4-1)=3/4-3t4/2.
Condition on the first pair being in one bucket. The third vertex is
either in that bucket, followed by a fourth in the same bucket, or
outside it, followed by a fourth in the third vertex's bucket. This
gives exactly r22 and the remaining contribution r22(3-6t4).
Subtracting (E Q)^2=p^2 proves (12). Independent averaging divides
the variance of x^T(Wbar-I)x by d.

For m=4,q=2 the unit coordinate contrast has variance 1/2; the unit
balanced sign vector (1,1,-1,-1)/2 has variance 2. The dependence on
t4 shows why matrix variance (8) alone does not determine norm-error
variance for a fixed subspace.

5. A collision obstruction that any proposed embedding must respect
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Take x=e_i-e_j, i!=j. If those coordinates collide in one bucket,
Sx=0. This occurs with probability exactly p. If they do not collide,

  ||Sx||^2/||x||^2=1/(1-p).

Thus any single-sketch (epsilon,delta) norm-preservation claim with
epsilon<1 must have delta>=p. If epsilon<p/(1-p), both outcomes fail,
so the failure probability is one. For d independent sketches, the
distortion of the squared norm is exactly

  [p-K/d]/(1-p),  K~Binomial(d,p),                            (13)

and the event that all d sketches erase x has probability p^d.
These elementary obstructions are not lower bounds for all AMM
algorithms or all sketches; they concern this precise balanced scheme.

6. An exact centered-permutation trace expansion
------------------------------------------------

This section specifies where the imported spectral-gap results apply.
Let K=H_0^T H_0 and K_0=K-qJ_0. For color c put

  Y_c=P_c-J_0,  gamma=1/(1-p).

Since K_0 1=0 and P_c fixes the constant direction, (3) becomes

  T_c=gamma Y_c^T K_0 Y_c-Pi_0,
  (T_c)_(ij)=gamma sum_(a,b)(K_0)_(ab)
                   (Y_c)_(ai)(Y_c)_(bj) -(Pi_0)_(ij).       (14)

Every entry in Y_c is a centered cell of a uniform permutation;
different c are independent permutation colors.

For an integer t>=1 write F=A^T A and G=BB^T. Cyclicity of trace gives

  E tr[(E_AMM E_AMM^T)^t]
    = E tr[(Tbar G Tbar F)^t]
    = d^(-2t) sum_(c_1,...,c_(2t))
           E tr[T_(c_1)G T_(c_2)F ... T_(c_(2t-1))G T_(c_(2t))F]. (15)

To make the scalar moment connection entirely explicit, set L=2t,
let D_l=G for odd l and D_l=F for even l, and take i_(2L+1)=i_1.
Each trace in (15) is the sum over i_1,...,i_(2L) of

  product_(l=1)^L (T_(c_l))_(i_(2l-1),i_(2l))
                   (D_l)_(i_(2l),i_(2l+1)).                (16)

Substitute (14) and select a subset J of the L factors at which the
quadratic Y term, rather than -Pi_0, is used. The expectation attached
to that term is exactly

  E product_(l in J)
          (Y_(c_l))_(a_l,i_(2l-1)) (Y_(c_l))_(b_l,i_(2l)),  (17)

with deterministic coefficient

  gamma^|J| (-1)^(L-|J|)
  product_l (D_l)_(i_(2l),i_(2l+1))
  product_(l notin J) (Pi_0)_(i_(2l-1),i_(2l))
  product_(l in J) (K_0)_(a_l,b_l),                          (18)

summed over all a_l,b_l for l in J and all the i indices.

The monomial (17) has 2|J|<=4t centered-permutation occurrences.
Group occurrences that have the same color, row and column into
powers. Let a be the number of distinct colored cells. A centered
singleton has multiplicity one. It is isolated if no other distinct
cell of its color shares its row OR column; let h be the number of
such isolated singletons and b the number of other singletons.

The exact mixed envelopes SG-11, SG-14 and SG-16 in
spectral-gap/CLAIM_LEDGER.md apply to every such monomial, including
repeated powers and arbitrarily many colors. For clarity write
P_h(m,r) for the positive partition polynomial with block-size-j weight
[((j-1)m+r)(j-1)!], with P_0=1. In the regime 2<=a<=m they give

  SG-11: |E(17)| <= 2^b m^(-h)P_h(m,a-h)/(m)_a;

  SG-14, b>=1:
    |E(17)| <= 2^(b-1)m^(-h-1)P_h(m,a-h-1)/(m)_(a-1);

  SG-16, b>=2:
    |E(17)| <= 2^(b-2) [
      2m^(-h-1)P_h(m,a-h-1)/(m)_(a-1)
       -m^(-h-2)P_h(m,a-h-2)/(m)_(a-2)].                    (19)

Here (m)_a=m(m-1)...(m-a+1). The bound for an empty monomial is one.
For a=1 a singleton has expectation zero; a higher centered power has
absolute expectation at most 1/m. Taking 4t<=m ensures that every
nonempty monomial is in one of these stated ranges. The bounds in
(19) are attained in their respective full admissible classes when
b=0,1,2 as recorded in the source. This does NOT say the extremizing
supports necessarily occur with dominant coefficient in (15).

7. The unresolved step toward a useful AMM or embedding theorem
---------------------------------------------------------------

Equations (14)-(19) are an exact reduction to the imported moment
theorems, not a completed trace bound. To prove a new width theorem,
one must sum (18) over collision patterns and colors while retaining
enough of the matrix structure of F,G and the bucket kernel K_0.
Taking absolute values of all deterministic entries immediately can
destroy orthogonality and create prohibitive dimension factors.

A successful analysis needs an explicit bound on that entire weighted
pattern sum, including repeated cells, shared rows/columns, singleton
gains, color choices, and dependence on rank, stable rank or coherence.
It must beat the elementary baseline (11), obey the collision obstruction
(13), and then supply the actual spectral AMM/Gram guarantee required
by the existing product algorithms. Finally one must count the total
width 1+d(s-1), factor construction, confidence, and explicit expansion.

No such improved pattern-sum theorem is presently established here.
Balanced hashing, centering, averaging, and the second-moment methods
have substantial prior-art risk; the formulas above are a verified
starting point, not a declaration of first discovery.

Verification
------------

scripts/check_balanced_permutation_amm.py enumerates all unlabeled
balanced partitions for several m<=8 and checks with exact rational
arithmetic: E W=I, W1=1, the projection polynomial, covariance (8),
quadratic variance (12), and coordinate-contrast collision probabilities.
Its recorded output is docs/balanced-permutation-checks.txt. Finite
enumeration corroborates these identities and does not prove the
unresolved trace-pattern sum.
