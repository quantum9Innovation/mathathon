VERDICT
-------
PASS in the exact randomized arithmetic model: the positive-update,
promised PSD downdate, and intrinsic signed-update theorems. The final
signed guarantee is genuinely normalized by ||B-A||^(1/2), despite
arbitrary cancellation in the supplied signed factors. No spectral
condition number, gap, downdate-margin, factor-condition, or base/update
scale ratio enters the arithmetic bound.

Both the direct signed-core construction and the two-PSD-call reduction
are valid. The latter gives the simplest route to the strongest theorem.
The alternative exact downdate covariance refactorization also passes.

No hidden norm, eigenvalue, base-root, pseudoinverse, or shifted-solve
oracle is required. Arithmetic, comparisons, scalar square roots, and
standard discrete random sampling suffice. The existing sparse-OSE
citation has the claimed logarithmic failure dependence. A dense SRHT
alternative is recorded below as well.

No substantive mathematical repair is needed. For a completely literal
statement, use log(2r/eps) rather than log(r/eps), and select the smallest
integer tail cutoff satisfying the displayed inequality. The source's
O(log^2) and Theta(rho^2) conventions have these harmless choices. The
direct indefinite alternative also needs the precise pivot-search
convention described in Section 7 below; the preferred PSD proof does
not use that alternative.

NOT AUDITED/NOT CLAIMED: finite-word stability, input bit complexity,
hardware performance, global priority, optimal running time, or a
seminal new mechanism. The main theorem remains a fully costed synthesis
of established tools. The exact-Gram comparison below is a proved
baseline, not a lower bound on prior methods or a certificate of SOTA.

1. MINIMAL EXACT-REAL THEOREM SUPPORTED BY THE PROOF
--------------------------------------------------
Let X be real N-by-m and U real N-by-r, 1<=r<=m<=N. Let J be an
explicit real symmetric involution of order r. Set

  A=XX^T, E=U J U^T, B=A+E,

and promise B>=0. For eps,delta in (0,1), an algorithm returns factors
Z_+,Z_- of total width

  K=O(r log^2(2r/eps))

such that, with probability at least 1-delta,

  ||Z_+Z_+^T-Z_-Z_-^T - [B^(1/2)-A^(1/2)]||_2
       <= eps ||E||_2^(1/2).

It uses conventional exact arithmetic, comparisons, positive scalar
square roots, and discrete randomness in

  ~O(Nmr+m^3)

operations; only dimension, accuracy, and failure logarithms are hidden.
The operation cap can hold on every random outcome. On a failed pivot
test it returns zero; all necessary tests pass on the OSE event. The
promise is not verified as part of the theorem.

If E=UU^T, a single PSD factor Z suffices with error eps||U||_2.
If E=-UU^T and B>=0, a single PSD factor approximates A^(1/2)-B^(1/2).
Neither endpoint needs to be invertible. The principal root of A is
not an input. Explicit N-by-N output costs an additional O(N^2 K).

2. QUADRATURE AND ITS ACTUAL SCALAR PRIMITIVES
---------------------------------------------
For PSD endpoints the difference-of-resolvents Stieltjes formula for
the square-root correction is valid, and its integrand has rank <=r.
For a>=||E||>0, the logarithmic integrand at t=a exp(y) has norm

  <=C_d sqrt(a) min(exp(Re(y)/2),exp(-Re(y)/2))

uniformly on any fixed strip |Im(y)|<=d<pi. The two bounds come from
separate resolvents and their difference identity; both remain valid
at singular endpoints and without a bound on ||A||/a.

The analytic trapezoid estimate applies in operator norm: scalarize
against arbitrary unit left/right vectors, use the same uniform bound,
then take the supremum. There is no entrywise dimension loss. Both the
infinite discretization error and the finite geometric tails are
O(rho sqrt(a)) with q=O(log^2(2/rho)) nodes.

One fully specified grid is t_j=a 2^(j/L), -LK<=j<=LK, where L is a
power of two comparable to log(2/rho), and K is the LEAST integer with
2^(-K/2)<=rho/C_0. Then s_min=t_min/a=Theta(rho^2) and
s_max=Theta(rho^-2). The least-cutoff convention prevents an unnecessary
overtruncation from invalidating the later Theta notation.

No input-dependent exponential/logarithm is computed: 2^(1/L) uses
repeated scalar square roots, and the remaining nodes use multiplication
and division. Integer loop budgets are selected by comparisons with
successive powers of two. With a=||U||_F^2, computing a costs O(Nr).
When a=0 the correct output is zero.

The weights are

  w_j=[log(2)/(pi L)] t_j sqrt(t_j)>0.

The fixed coefficient log(2)/pi need not be an exact primitive. For
example log(2)=2 sum_(k>=0)(1/3)^(2k+1)/(2k+1), and the Machin identity
pi=16 atan(1/5)-4 atan(1/239), with the usual alternating power series,
give certified relative O(rho) rational approximations in O(log(2/rho))
field operations. These have positive ratio after a fixed initial number
of terms. The weighted sum of exact node norms is O(sqrt(a)); relative
O(rho) coefficient error therefore stays inside the error budget.
The final sqrt(w_j) uses only one further positive scalar square root.

Taking rho=eps/(C sqrt(r)) converts rho sqrt(a) into at most a fixed
fraction of eps||U||, since sqrt(a)<=sqrt(r)||U||. This is a computable
normalization and does not assume an operator-norm oracle.

3. THE OSE, INCLUDING RANK DEFICIENCY AND FAILURE DEPENDENCE
----------------------------------------------------------
The only probabilistic ingredient needed for a positive/downdate call
is a Gram proxy G0 with

  (1-1/4)G0 <= G=X^T X <= (1+1/4)G0.

Nelson--Nguyen, OSNAP, Theorem 9, was checked in the actual primary PDF:
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.1002
At fixed distortion it gives O(m log^8(2m/delta)) sketch rows and
O(log^3(2m/delta)) nonzeros per column, success at least 1-delta.
The theorem explicitly allows arbitrary delta with logarithmic dependence;
there is no polynomial 1/delta setup hidden here. Taking a sufficiently
small fixed norm distortion gives the displayed reverse Gram sandwich.
Applying the sketch costs ~O(Nm) and forming its Gram costs ~O(m^3),
using conventional products. Listing or sampling its entries adds only
the permitted logarithmic overhead.

Rank deficiency is harmless. The embedding applies to the actual fixed
column space of X, of dimension <=m. The Gram sandwich also identifies
the kernels exactly on its success event. It is not necessary to construct
an orthonormal basis or discover the rank first. The same event controls
every subsequently chosen positive shift and every RHS.

Optional dense-input implementation: pad N to N'<2N, a power of two,
and use normalized Hadamard/sign mixing followed by row sampling. Tropp's
parameterized Lemmas 3.3--3.4 and matrix Chernoff theorem imply that

  Mbar=(sqrt(m)+sqrt(8 log(2N'/delta)))^2,
  ell>=C Mbar log(4m/delta)

rows give a sufficiently small fixed Gram distortion, with failure delta.
If ell>N', compute the exact Gram instead; its N'm^2 cost is at most
ell m^2=~O(m^3). Hadamard application costs O(Nm log(2N)). This is a
direct corollary of the parameterized lemmas, not a literal restatement
of Tropp's Theorem 3.1, which prints failure 3/k. Primary source:
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.1595

Standard discrete randomness is part of the randomized arithmetic model.
No draw from an input-dependent continuous distribution is needed. Random
signs and finite row/hash choices can be generated with logarithmic bit
overhead; a fixed sampling budget or capped rejection adds only a chosen
small failure allowance. This does not entail a numerical bit-complexity
guarantee for the subsequent real arithmetic.

4. COMMON EVEN-NEUMANN POLYNOMIAL AND THE PHYSICAL INVERSE
---------------------------------------------------------
Write P=G0+tI, F=P^(-1/2)(G-G0)P^(-1/2). The sandwich gives ||F||<=1/4.
For even ell,

  Q_ell=P^(-1/2) sum_(j=0)^(ell-1)(-F)^j P^(-1/2),
  Q_*=(G+tI)^(-1),
  0<=Q_*-Q_ell<=eta Q_*, eta=4^(-ell).

This follows by diagonalizing F: the residual is F^ell(I+F)^(-1),
which is positive and bounded by eta(I+F)^(-1). The same identity also
gives Q_ell>=0 on the success event.

No matrix inverse square root is computed. To apply Q_ell to W=X^T U,
start Y_0=0 and perform the fixed recursion

  Y_(j+1)=P^(-1)[W-(G-G0)Y_j], j=0,...,ell-1.

Each P solve is a Cholesky solve, each G action is X^T(XY_j), and
G0 is stored. This is precisely the same symmetric polynomial for every
column; RHS-dependent stopping is not being used.

The exact Woodbury formula gives

  R=(XX^T+tI)^(-1)=t^(-1)(I-XQ_*X^T).

Therefore Rhat=t^(-1)(I-XQ_ell X^T) satisfies

  0<=Rhat-R<=eta I/t.

This uses XQ_*X^T<=I and is uniform on the ambient nullspace as well.
In fact Rhat<=I/t on the good event since Q_ell>=0. The looser source
bound (1+eta)/t also suffices. No condition number of X or positive
smallest eigenvalue enters this argument.

5. POSITIVE NODES: FACTOR AND ERROR
-----------------------------------
For V=RU and S=I+U^TV, Woodbury gives

  D=R-(A+UU^T+tI)^(-1)=VS^(-1)V^T>=0.

The approximate Vhat=Rhat U, Shat=I+U^T Vhat obey Shat>=I. Expanding
the two V factors and using the inverse-difference identity proves

  ||Dhat-D||<=3 eta a/t^2+eta a^2/t^3.

The coefficient 3 follows from ||Vhat||<=2sqrt(a)/t and
||Vhat-V||<=eta sqrt(a)/t; both inverse cores have norm at most one.
Weighting by t^(3/2) and summing the geometric grid gives

  C eta sqrt(a)[rho^-1+rho^-3].

Thus eta<=c rho^4 suffices, with even ell=O(log(2/rho)). Cholesky
Shat=C C^T and the factor sqrt(w)Vhat C^(-T) have the correct
orientation: their product with its transpose is w Dhat exactly.

6. DOWNDATES, INCLUDING EXACTLY CRITICAL ENDPOINTS
-------------------------------------------------
For B=A-UU^T>=0, S=I-U^T R U is SPD for every t>0. Exact algebra gives

  S^(-1)=I+U^T(B+tI)^(-1)U,
  ||S^(-1)||<=M=1+a/t,
  D=(B+tI)^(-1)-R=VS^(-1)V^T,
  0<=D<=I/t.

This eliminates any need for a downdate-margin parameter. The perturbation
F_c=U^T(Rhat-R)U is PSD with norm <=eta a/t. Hence
Shat=S-F_c is SPD whenever eta(a/t)(1+a/t)<=1/2. The source's
eta<=s_min^2/[4(1+s_min)] is a sufficient simultaneous condition.

Set Y=VS^(-1/2), T=(Vhat-V)S^(-1/2) for analysis only. Then
||Y||^2<=1/t, ||Y||<=sqrt(aM)/t, ||T||<=eta sqrt(aM)/t.
Expanding (Y+T)(I-S^(-1/2)F_cS^(-1/2))^(-1)(Y+T)^T proves

  ||Dhat-D||<=8 eta[a/t^2+a^2/t^3].

The use of ||Y||^2<=1/t in the first term is valid and essential to
this power. The same eta=c rho^4 handles both SPD and total error.
The output uses only the small SPD Cholesky, not the analytical S^(-1/2).

7. SIGNED UPDATES AND INTRINSIC CANCELLATION NORMALIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------
The direct signed core S=J+U^T R U is invertible and satisfies
S^(-1)=J-JU^T(B+tI)^(-1)UJ. Its norm is <=1+a/t. The source's
perturbation bound (S2) follows by an ordinary inverse-difference
expansion; eta=c rho^8 suffices. Symmetric congruence elimination and
scalar square roots give signed factors without an eigenvalue oracle.

For a fully specified elimination, FIRST search all remaining diagonal
entries and permute any nonzero one into the pivot. Only if ALL those
diagonals vanish, use a nonzero off-diagonal a_ij and replace e_i by
e_i+e_j, giving pivot 2a_ij!=0. The source's shorter instruction is
otherwise ambiguous: replacing an arbitrary zero-diagonal vector by
its sum with another does not always create a pivot; [[0,1],[1,-2]]
is a counterexample. The stated search convention repairs that local
implementation detail in O(r^3). Alternatively try e_i+e_j and then
e_i+2e_j; one succeeds whenever a_ij!=0. None of this affects the
strongest theorem, whose simpler two-PSD-call proof avoids the issue.

An easier complete proof uses P_+/P_-=(I+/-J)/2 and C=A+U_+U_+^T.
Then C>=A,B and has raw factor [X,U_+]. A positive call for C-A and
a downdate call for C-B, subtracted in this order, give B^(1/2)-A^(1/2).
The base width grows by at most a constant factor. If it exceeds N,
QR of the transpose reduces the covariance factor to N columns in
O(m^3), since then N=O(m). Two independent sketches with delta/2
give the claimed union bound. No arbitrary indefinite solver is needed.

For intrinsic error, compute exact thin QR U=QR, form C0=RJR^T and
c=||C0||_F. These cost O(Nr^2+r^3), inside the claimed budget.
If c=0 then E=0 exactly. Otherwise

  ||E||=||C0||<=c<=sqrt(r)||E||.

PSD Cholesky of cI+C0 is valid with zero pivots: a zero diagonal of
a PSD Schur residual forces its row/column to vanish, so that column
can be skipped. Let LL^T=cI+C0 and set

  Uc=Q[L,sqrt(c)I], Jc=diag(I,-I).

Then Uc Jc Uc^T=E and ||Uc||^2<=3c<=3sqrt(r)||E||. Invoke the
signed representation-scale theorem with eps'=eps/(sqrt(3) r^(1/4)).
It gives exactly eps||E||^(1/2). The quarter root of r uses two scalar
square roots. The factor width at most 2r and this polynomial change
of eps alter only constants and logarithmic budgets. In the two-call
implementation the positive and negative column groups each have at
most r columns, so the dimension bookkeeping is especially direct.

All cancellation is performed inside the exact r-dimensional core.
There is no factor-condition-dependent iteration count. In finite
precision this cancellation would require a separate analysis, which
is correctly not claimed.

8. THE ALTERNATIVE EXACT DOWNDATE REFACTORIZATION
-------------------------------------------------
On an injective sketch of span(X), the solution sets of
(S0 X)C=S0 U and XC=U coincide, and their kernels agree. Exact
rank-revealing QR plus SPD solves implements the minimum-norm solution
without a pseudoinverse oracle. Its range lies in range(X^T), and the
PSD downdate promise forces CC^T<=I on that support.

For thin QR C=Q R, PSD Cholesky L L^T=I-RR^T and
T=I+Q(L-I)Q^T give TT^T=I-CC^T. Thus

  Y=XT=X+(XQ)(L-I)Q^T

satisfies YY^T=A-UU^T. The displayed low-rank products cost O(Nmr),
not O(Nm^2); the other work is ~O(Nm+m^3). A positive-update call
on (Y,U) proves the downdate theorem again. Failure tests and sketches
can be budgeted sequentially with a union bound. This is a covariance
factorization, not the principal square root, as the source correctly says.

9. FULL COST, STORAGE, AND FAILURE CAP
-------------------------------------
For each semidefinite call:

  sketch/Gram setup:                  ~Nm+~m^3,
  reusable X^T U:                     Nmr,
  q positive shifts:                  q m^3,
  ell fixed raw iterations per shift: q ell Nmr,
  core construction/normalization:    q(Nr^2+r^3).

Since r<=m<=N, these give ~O(Nmr+m^3). Both q=O(log^2(2r/eps))
and ell=O(log(2r/eps)) are fixed before the solve loops. Repeated
cubic shifted-core factorizations are logarithmic overhead, not hidden
rank dependence. Intrinsic normalization and a constant number of calls
preserve the bound. All wide-base exceptions have the constant-factor
or QR handling stated above.

Every loop is capped. The sketched Gram is always PSD and P=G0+tI
is always SPD, even if the OSE is bad. Any later forbidden Cholesky or
zero pivot causes immediate zero output. On the embedding success
event the proved inequalities ensure that no such test fails. Large
intermediate values on a bad event do not change the exact-real operation
count; their bit cost is outside the model.

Storage is O(Nm+Nr polylog(2r/eps)+m^2 polylog(2m/delta)), with
streamed shifted cores. Explicit dense expansion needs additional N^2
storage if retained and O(N^2 K) arithmetic. The theorem returns factors.

10. EXACT-GRAM COMPARISON AND PRIORITY LIMITS
--------------------------------------------
Forming G=X^T X exactly and applying the SAME quadrature and Woodbury
factors gives a concrete deterministic baseline

  ~O(Nm^2+m^3).

The same intrinsic signed normalization costs O(Nr^2+r^3), so this
comparison extends to the strongest signed theorem as well. The candidate
replaces the tall Gram cost Nm^2 with Nmr. Ignoring logarithms, the ratio is

  (Nm^2+m^3)/(Nmr+m^3),

at most order m/r. For N=Theta(m), both are cubic; r=m also removes
the order gain. For N=m^2,r=1, factor construction improves from
order m^4 to order m^3. Dense output in that example still costs
order N^2 and can dominate both constructions.

This comparison proves a gain over that specified exact-Gram route.
It does not prove dominance over all algorithms composed from prior work.
BCKS2021 already computes general principal matrix-function corrections
without a supplied base root; its projected square-root approximant is
PSD in the positive-update case. SDA2023/2024 already explicitly treats
downdates and splitting indefinite perturbations into two semidefinite
ones. Their existence makes signed closure and no-base-root claims
insufficient novelty arguments. Primary PDFs checked:
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.11501
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13156

The dedicated SOTA audit correctly identifies the routine-composition
hazard and the distinction between update-relative and correction-relative
error. This audit confirms the precise arithmetic theorem; it does not
certify its novelty or call it a seminal advance.

11. OTHER SCOPE CHECKS
-----------------------
The scalar-model caveat for general alpha is real. Fixed dyadic powers
use repeated square roots; arbitrary fixed alpha needs an explicitly
charged scalar-power evaluation or an added primitive. The pure square-
root theorem audited here has no such missing primitive.

The standard strip estimate used above was checked against Trefethen--
Weideman, The Exponentially Convergent Trapezoidal Rule, Theorem 5.1:
  https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/trefethen/publication/PDF/2014_149.pdf
Its decay and uniform strip L1 hypotheses hold for this resolvent
integrand. The matrix operator-norm deduction is the scalarization
argument already given above.

No numerical experiment was needed to replace a proof step in this audit.
