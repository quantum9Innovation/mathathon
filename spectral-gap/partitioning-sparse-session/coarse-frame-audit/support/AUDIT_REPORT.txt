1. Uniform small-support bound, arbitrary block composition
---------------------------------------------------------
Let n be the TOTAL vertex count, delta>=3 the minimum total degree,
D the maximum total degree, and min_i Ni>=rho*n. Independent uniform
internal configuration matchings and cross-factor stub bijections are
the exact probability model. All degree/parity/balance conditions are
assumed admissible. No balanced support or prescribed per-block error
counts are needed.

Call S bad if no vertex outside S has exactly one incident edge into S,
counting multiplicity. For |S|=s, let W be its outside neighborhood,
w=|W|, a the number of internal S edges (each loop once), b the boundary
edge count, and e=a+b. Then
  2a+b>=delta*s, b>=2w, w<=D*s/2, e<=D*s,
  e-s-w>=(delta-2)*s/2.
The integer excess is therefore at least ceil((delta-2)*s/2).

For s<=rho*n/(4D), every prescribed partial typed configuration with e
pairs has probability at most(2/(rho*n))^e: each positive factor pool
has size>=rho*n, and at most D*s pairs are prescribed. Internal pairs
have denominators L-1,L-3,...; cross bijections have L,L-1,... . Thus
loops, multiple edges, unequal planted sizes and arbitrary typed stub
composition are all covered, rather than silently replaced with an
untyped uniform graph.

There are at most binomial(n,s)binomial(n,w)2^(D*w)(2D*s)^e witness
patterns. Invalid typed patterns only enlarge this upper count. Bounding
(s/w)^w<=exp(s/e), and summing e,w, gives the explicit estimate
  E[bad sets of size s] <= C^s(s/n)^ceil(c*s), c=(delta-2)/2,
where one valid constant is
  C=4(D+1)^2 exp(1+D/2+1/e) 2^(D^2/2)(4D/rho)^D.
For eta<=min{rho/(4D),(2C exp(c))^(-1/c)}, the total sum is at most
  C*n^-ceil(c)+2C^2*(2/n)^(delta-2)=o(1).
In particular it is O(1/n) uniformly when only delta>=3 is used.

For uniform SIMPLE factors, conditioning costs a positive fixed constant.
Singleton sets cannot be bad in a simple graph of positive degree, so
their term disappears and the failure probability is O(n^(-(delta-2))).
Only the classical positive fixed-degree simplicity probability is used
in this conditioning step. The unconditioned proof itself includes loops
and multiple edges. Adjacency diagonal entries count a loop TWICE.

2. Every small-support intertwiner is excluded simultaneously
-----------------------------------------------------------
If AV=VR, let S be the nonzero row support of V. At v outside S,
  sum_u A_vu V_u=0.
Exactly one incident edge from v to S would make this equal one nonzero
row, impossible. Thus S must be bad. On the single good graph event,
therefore, no nonzero small-row-support intertwiner exists for ANY R,
ANY finite column dimension, or ANY field. No finite union over vector
entries or candidate frames is required for this local conclusion.

The argument does not require internal degrees ai>=2 separately: minimum
TOTAL degree>=3 and linear-sized positive typed pools suffice here.
The internal-degree assumption remains relevant to the separate entropy
step. Minimum total degree2 would be insufficient: small cycle components
give counterexamples, so this hypothesis must remain explicit.

3. Near-point compatibility QT=TR
--------------------------------
AJ=JQ holds exactly. Let Y be a verified INTEGER one-hot solution AY=YR
and T an old-block point map. If S is the set of changed rows and
s_i=|S intersection Bi|, the sharper sufficient condition is
  Ni > s_i + sum_j Q_ji*s_j, every i.
The right side counts all possibly changed vertices and all vertices
adjacent to them inside old block i. It is at most(D+1)|S|. Therefore
  |S|<min_i Ni/(D+1)
leaves a vertex in EACH old block whose own row and all neighbor rows
are unchanged. Its exact degree equation forces row i of QT=TR.
Then V=Y-JT satisfies AV=VR, and the uniform small-support event gives
V=0. Taking eta also<=rho/[2(D+1)] closes every point-map neighborhood.

This is uniform in arbitrary support composition. It does NOT rely on
balanced errors or on bounding the size/norm of R. If one has only a
modular equation, the clean-vertex argument gives compatibility only
modulo p; the root's real uniqueness argument must be applied to candidates
verified over the integers. Its stated verification step does this.

4. Unknown connected quotient and class-size lower bound
--------------------------------------------------------
For nonempty candidate classes of sizes Ma and nonnegative INTEGER R,
undirected edge counts give Ma*R_ab=Mb*R_ba. Off-diagonal positivity is
therefore symmetric. Along a path of length at most r-1 in a connected
quotient, successive mass ratios are ratios of integers in[1,D], hence
at most D. Thus
  min_a Ma >= n/[r*D^(r-1)] >= n/[K*D^(K-1)], r<=K,D>=1.
This remains valid with loops and unequal block sizes; diagonal entries
do not affect the path argument. Connectedness, integrality and nonempty
classes are necessary to this reasoning. The stated root formulation
includes them. The bound supplies the desired compactness exclusion of
vanishing candidate classes, if the entropy argument needs it.

5. Conditional identification of unknown k and Q
------------------------------------------------
Assume the separate macroscopic proof shows every accepted partition lies
within eta*n rows of some JT, uniformly over the finite connected candidate
frames r<=K and row sums<=D. Our local results then force Y=JT exactly.
Since every candidate class is nonempty, T is onto, so r<=k. The planted
pair(J,Q) itself is accepted, hence the maximum accepted r equals k.
Every maximizing T is a bijection and recovers the planted blocks up to
permutation. Their quotient entries are then uniquely determined by
integer neighbor counts. There are at most sum_(r<=K)(D+1)^(r^2) candidate
matrices, a finite number independent of n.

This proves the identification reduction conditional on the unreviewed
macroscopic step. It does not by itself prove that step, or expected
polynomial enumeration of modular intertwiners. A fixed upper bound K is
part of the scope; an unbounded unknown number of classes is not handled.
