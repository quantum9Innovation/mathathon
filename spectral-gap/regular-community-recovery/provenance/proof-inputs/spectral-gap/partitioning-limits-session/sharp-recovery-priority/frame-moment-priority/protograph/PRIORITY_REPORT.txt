Theorem 91, printed p106, condition (6.1); Appendix E, printed pp149--151,
especially (E.17)--(E.19), Lemmas 105--106. This is the earliest version of
this particular dominance argument inspected here; no earliest-priority
claim beyond that is made. Lemma106 abbreviates the upper proof as similar
to Lemma105. The 2026 revision below supplies the upper raw bound explicitly.

Yuwen Huang and Pascal O. Vontobel, Graph-Cover-based Characterization of
the Bethe Partition Function of Double-Edge Factor Graphs,
arXiv:2506.16250v2, revised 17 August 2026.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16250v2
  https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16250v2
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.16250v2
Full actual TeX and Appendix G were inspected, not just the abstract.
Theorem46, Eq(40); Definition50; Eqs(60)--(65); Lemma57.

Write B=Z_B,SPA^*>0, and f_LCT for the transformed local functions. Their
condition is

  B > (2/3) product_f sum_z |f_LCT(z)|.

In Appendix G, split endpoint variables are distinct. Definition50 takes
g_1 to be the product of local functions with only the all-zero split
configuration removed. Thus Eq(61) is an EQUALITY, not just an upper bound:

  alpha = [product_f sum_z |f_LCT(z)| - B]/B < 1/2.

The quantity in Eq(62) is

  R_M = E_cover Z(cover) / B^M.

The proof of Lemma57 gives, for every M,

  0 <= R_M <= 1 + sum_(j=1)^M alpha^j <= 1/(1-alpha) < 2.

Consequently this is a genuine uniform raw expectation theorem whenever
B=1 and the dominance condition holds. It is substantially stronger than
the displayed M-th-root limit. This estimate does NOT by itself imply
R_M -> 1. The sufficient upper-bound calculation works already for
alpha<1; the stated two-sided root-limit argument uses alpha<1/2.

The formalism is relevant to classical hard constraints: after Definition12
the 2026 source explicitly embeds a nonnegative ordinary factor graph by
replacing each y_e with (y_e,y'_e) and multiplying every incident factor by
[y_e=y'_e]. The partition function and diagonal marginal are preserved.
The fixed-point convention uses PSD-normalized messages; in this diagonal
embedding an update kills off-diagonal message entries, so its fixed points
reduce to the original nonnegative classical fixed points. It would be
incorrect to dismiss this source as applicable only to quantum models.

4. AN INDEPENDENT OBSTRUCTION TO BLANKET HV SUBSUMPTION

The following check uses neither the new full moment bound nor its claimed
scalar sharp constant. It concerns the natural classical star/pair-state
representation and its diagonal DE embedding. It does not assert failure
of every possible alternative factorization or gauge.

Take the simple base graph K_4, p=3, and R=0. This is a genuine fixed-base
cover subset of the target model: four equal-size types, d_ij=1 for i!=j,
q_ij=1/3. At each base edge use pair state (x_u,x_v); at a vertex require
that its incident copies of x_v agree and that the three neighboring
values sum to zero. This is an ordinary nonnegative normal factor graph.

For ANY feasible local star/pair beliefs, the lossless-omission entropy
calculation with weights 1/3 gives exactly

  H_B = -(1/3) sum_edges I(X_u;X_v)
        -(1/3) sum_vertices sum_(three omitted slots) J_(v,slot) <= 0.

Here J is the conditional total correlation of the two retained neighbors.
No symmetrization is needed since the three slots are distinct base edges.
The all-zero beliefs and the uniform legal-star beliefs both attain zero.
Hence the classical Bethe partition function is exactly 1. More explicitly,
the uniform edge messages have 9 states, each legal vertex has 27 stars,
Z_f=27/9^3=1/27 and Z_e=1/9; on K_4 the SPA ratio is
(1/27)^4/(1/9)^6=1. Every nonnegative admissible SPA fixed point induces
feasible beliefs, so the entropy inequality also bounds its SPA value by1.
Thus B=Z_B,SPA^*=1, including in the natural diagonal DE embedding just
described (all its fixed-point messages are diagonal).

On the base itself, A_K4=J-I over F_3. Its kernel consists of the three
constant vectors, so Z(base)=3. LCT preserves this finite-base partition
function. By the triangle inequality, for ANY such LCT of this natural
representation,

  product_f sum_z |f_LCT(z)| >= Z(base)=3.

The HV condition would require the left side to be less than 3B/2=1.5,
which is impossible. Equivalently M=1 in their raw upper bound would give
Z(base)/B<2, contradicting 3. This exhibits an actual certified simple-base
instance outside the inspected dominance theorem's scope. There is
therefore no uniform implication from the target omission certificate to
the HV dominance condition, even before the more general pooled mixed
frame or inactive-channel issues arise.

5. MORI--TANAKA RAW CENTRAL ASYMPTOTICS: REAL PRIOR, MISSING BOUNDARY

Ryuhei Mori and Toshiyuki Tanaka, New Generalizations of the Bethe
Approximation via Asymptotic Expansion, arXiv:1210.2592v2 (10 October 2012),
SITA2012.
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.2592v2
Actual source, Section VI and Lemmas15--16, printed p5, inspected.
Lemma16 states an asymptotic for E Z(M) itself: Z_B^M times a finite sum of
Hessian/determinant (equivalently graph-zeta) constants, times 1+o(1).
Its assumptions require finitely many global minima, EVERY minimizing
variable marginal positive on its full alphabet, EVERY minimizing factor
marginal positive on the legal support, and positive Hessian determinant.
Hard local zeros are allowed; positivity is required on the legal support.
Thus it already yields a raw O(1) result in its domain when Z_B=1.

Ryuhei Mori and Toshiyuki Tanaka, Central Approximation in Statistical
Physics and Information Theory, arXiv:1202.0655v1 (3 February 2012),
ISIT2012 pp1652--1656.
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.0655
Actual source, Theorem5, Section IV, printed p4, inspected. Its regular
bipartite factor-graph ensemble uses uniform (Nl)! stub connections,
so it is important pooled-configuration prior, not only colored-lift prior.
The unique optimizer has full positive variable law and a nonsingular
stated determinant; the result retains the exact integer lattice constant
and gives the first moment to constant order. It does not establish an
all-phase result for the present symmetric mixed-frame ensemble.

Ryuhei Mori, New Understanding of the Bethe Approximation and the Replica
Method, PhD thesis, arXiv:1303.2168 (March 2013).
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.2168
Theorem5.4 printed p55 (PDF p63), Corollary5.5 printed p57 (PDF p65),
Section5.3.1 printed p59 (PDF p67) inspected in full actual text.
Theorem5.4 explicitly assumes Min(F_B) is finite and contained in Int(F_B),
plus affine-constraint dimension and Hessian hypotheses. Corollary5.5
retains a structural lattice/Jacobian factor K(G). These are substantial
precursors of the central normalizer and congruence analysis.

The boundary obstruction to using them here is substantive. In a nontrivial
homogeneous target, the all-zero point is a proper-support entropy-zero
phase; the full-uniform legal-star phase also has exponent zero under the
certificate. Thus a global-minimum hypothesis requiring EVERY minimum to
be interior fails already in the K_4 example above. Applying an interior
formula separately on the exact supports of finitely many phases does not
bound profiles approaching those faces with a small nonzero defect count.
The required estimate is uniform in those neighborhoods, rather than only
at the face itself. The selected sparse certificate and dimension induction
are precisely additional ingredients to compare, not consequences of a
determinant formula at an interior saddle.

IMPORTANT linear-constraint remark: Section5.3.1 states, in its linear
constraint setting with factor degrees greater than two, that the uniform
messages minimize the Bethe free energy, sqrt(zeta)=1,
K(G)=|X|^(|F|-r), and Z_B(G)=|X|^(N-|F|). Its formal first asymptotic Bethe
approximation therefore satisfies

  Z_AB^(1)(G)=Z(G)=|X|^(N-r)

when the finite-base constraint system is consistent. Here r is its
FINITE-BASE constraint rank. This is directly relevant prior algebraic
normalization and must be cited. It is not an unconditional theorem that
the expected number of solutions in ALL random covers is bounded: the
preceding asymptotic theorem still has its interior-minimum hypotheses,
and the discussion defines an approximation by formally evaluating an
asymptotic expansion at cover degree1. Nor should its rank factor be
identified with the new mixed-frame constant without an explicit encoding
and lattice comparison.

7. MODEL CONVERSION AND THE PRECISE PRIOR-ART BOUNDARY

A fixed-base random cover chooses one independent N-permutation for EACH
base edge. The target cross factor chooses one uniform bijection over
n_i d_ij stubs, allowing physical slot labels to be mixed. An internal
factor is one uniform pairing of n_i d_ii stubs. Replacing a d_ij factor by
d_ij independently colored matchings changes that ensemble. Unequal a_i,
pooled parallel slots and internal pairings are not automatically converted
into a fixed-protograph lift. A proposed reduction must prove equality of
laws, or a quantitative bound that controls this unbounded count. Generic
contiguity, a rank/n theorem, and a normalized log-partition limit are
insufficient substitutions.

There is an exact overlap: for a simple base with d_ij in {0,1}, no internal
factors and equal type sizes, the target is a standard random cover. The
star/pair-state normal-factor encoding preserves the adjacency symmetry
there: a shared edge carries both endpoint labels, and a vertex factor
enforces equality of its root copies and the linear equation. This is why
general factor-graph theorems, including the diagonal DE construction,
cannot be dismissed for lacking the literal matrix notation AY=YR.
Similarly, arbitrary fixed matrices R and finite vector alphabets can be
encoded by finite local constraints; that feature alone is not a novelty
distinction. The missing estimate and the precise random ensemble matter.

Within the primary sources actually inspected, the remaining comparison
target is therefore: a raw all-phase moment theorem under the symmetric
strict omission certificate for pooled reversible mixed frames, including
inactive factors and internal pairings, proved through a phase class closed
under restriction. Its proposed mechanisms are the exact heterogeneous
normalizer, complete uniform-coset classification, maximal-lattice relative
LLT, and sparse-boundary induction with conditional rank control. The first
and central-counting ingredients have strong antecedents; the exhaustive
boundary estimate and its applicability under this certificate are where
subsumption has not been found. This conclusion does not certify novelty
against uninspected literature or certify algorithmic recovery consequences.
