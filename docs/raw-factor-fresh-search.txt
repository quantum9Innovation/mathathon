CURRENT RESULT AND SCOPE

There is a constructive batched off-axis resolvent theorem, proved below.
It handles the fixed swap coupling J needed for arbitrary products AB,
allows rank deficiency, and uses only a constant-distortion raw sketch.
For q supplied off-axis shifts and supplied right hand sides, its cost is

  ~O(I_X q^(omega-2) log(1/eps) + m^omega),       1 <= q <= m,

with dimension/confidence logarithms suppressed. The small-core setup is
an EXACT Frobenius normal form, not an exact eigenvalue oracle. Thus no
condition-number logarithm or per-shift m^omega cost is hidden. Arithmetic
is exact; this is not yet a backward-stability/finite-word theorem.

Section11 further proves an order-q APPROXIMATE bounded Cayley-Krylov
construction for S^2, hence for MM^T of arbitrary raw AB, at the same
~O(I_X q^(omega-2) log(q/eps)+m^omega) cost. Fast Möbius/Taylor basis
changes eliminate a provisional m q^2 composition term. This is a genuine
constructive finite algorithm; its conversion to sharp LRA remains open.

This is NOT yet a general LRA or N^2 algorithm. Imaginary-axis poles are
far from a nonzero real spectral threshold. The remaining substantive
question is a stable filter/representation with the required sharpness,
or a new way to move the resolvent center towards the real threshold.
Root is independently investigating the filter and coefficient growth.

The original partial monomial Krylov primitive remains open. Two exact
summary/data-structure barriers are recorded after the constructive result,
with their precise restricted models. Neither rules out special dependent
Krylov columns or approximate query algorithms.


1. RAW FACTOR AND SIGNED DILATION MODEL

Let X be an arbitrary real N-by-m matrix; write I_X=Nm, G=X^T X.
Let J be a supplied real symmetric involution: J^T=J, J^2=I.
The main case is a fixed coordinate permutation, so J-actions cost O(mq).
Define the real symmetric physical operator

  S = X J X^T.

For arbitrary A(n,d), B(d,p), use

  X = diag(A,B^T),      J = [0 I_d; I_d 0],
  S = [0 AB; (AB)^T 0].

Then N=n+p, m=2d, and the raw input size is O(d(n+p)); the zero blocks
need not be stored. Everything below applies to the product dilation
without forming P=A^T A, Q=BB^T, or either true Gram.

For z off the real axis and a physical right hand side h, Woodbury gives

  (I-zS)^(-1)h = h + z X (J-zG)^(-1) X^T h.             (1)

This identity is valid with rank-deficient X. The matrix J-zG is invertible:
JG has only real nonzero eigenvalues because they coincide with those of
G^(1/2) J G^(1/2), and zero eigenvalues cannot make I-zJG singular.
Alternatively (1) follows directly by multiplying both sides.


2. COARSE SKETCH, INCLUDING ITS ACTUAL INPUT COST

Choose a fixed eta in (0,1), e.g. eta=1/4. A standard sparse OSE forms
Y=Pi X in ~O(I_X) arithmetic, with ~O(m) rows, so that with probability
at least 1-delta its Gram G0=Y^T Y satisfies

  (1-eta)G0 <= G <= (1+eta)G0.                           (2)

Forming THIS sketched Gram and rank-revealing exact LDL/Gram factorization
costs ~O(m^omega), since Y has only ~O(m) rows. Obtain a full-row-rank
L(rho,m), rho<=m, with

  G0=L^T L.

No true Gram is formed. The same coarse event works for all subsequent
shifts, right hand sides, and iterations, including adaptive choices.
The cost statement only needs a classical OSNAP with polylogarithmic
row/sparsity overhead; no 2026 optimal embedding theorem is needed.

Primary source: Nelson--Nguyen, OSNAP, arXiv:1211.1002:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.1002 . The abstract and main theorems give
~O(m/eps^2) rows and polylog(m)/eps nonzeros per input-coordinate column,
and near-input-plus-rank^omega regression preprocessing. Here eps is a
fixed numerical constant and supplied failure is handled by the standard
logarithmic-confidence parameterization. Equivalently, any supplied
constant-distortion coarse factor L satisfying (2) is sufficient for the
deterministic theorem below.

The rank-deficient LDL step uses field operations, comparisons/zero tests,
and positive scalar square roots. It can also use an exact small QR of Y;
all its dimensions are ~O(m), so this does not hide tall QR of X.


3. CONTRACTION FOR THE SIGNED RAW SADDLE

Put E=G-G0. By (2) and full row rank of L there exists a real symmetric
D(rho,rho) with ||D||_2<=eta and

  E=L^T D L.                                             (3)

For example D=(LL^T)^(-1)L E L^T(LL^T)^(-1). The Loewner inequalities
show ||D||<=eta; equality (3) uses that E and G0 have the same nullspace
annihilation. D is ANALYTICAL and is never computed.

Let K=L J L^T, a supplied rho-by-rho real symmetric matrix. Define

  A=J-zG,                  A0=J-zG0.

The coarse inverse is

  A0^(-1) = J + z J L^T (I-zK)^(-1) L J,                 (4)

and in particular

  L A0^(-1) L^T = K(I-zK)^(-1).                          (5)

For b supplied, start w_0=0 and perform ordinary preconditioned Richardson

  w_(t+1) = w_t + A0^(-1)[b-A w_t].                      (6)

For w_*=A^(-1)b and e_t=w_t-w_*, (3)--(5) imply

  L e_(t+1) = z K(I-zK)^(-1) D L e_t.                    (7)

For z purely imaginary,

  ||z K(I-zK)^(-1)||_2
    = max_(lambda in spec K) |z lambda|/|1-z lambda| <= 1.

Hence ||L e_t|| <= eta^t ||Lw_*||. Transferring using (2),

  ||X(w_t-w_*)||
       <= sqrt((1+eta)/(1-eta)) eta^t ||Xw_*||.            (8)

This is a SEMINORM argument and does not invert G0. Nullspace components
of w are harmless because the required physical output is Xw. It is
therefore valid with rank-deficient A or B and with ill-conditioned raw
factors, without a support-coordinate change on the tall input.

More generally set c(z)=|Im z|/|z|. For any nonreal z,

  max_(lambda real) |z lambda|/|1-z lambda| = 1/c(z).

Thus (7) contracts by eta/c(z) whenever c(z)>eta.


4. UNIFORM PHYSICAL RESOLVENT ERROR, NO SPECTRAL-RATIO LOG

Take b=X^T h and form y_t=h+zXw_t. Equation (1) says y_*=h+zXw_*.
For pure imaginary z, diagonalizing S only for ANALYSIS shows

  ||zXw_*|| = ||[(I-zS)^(-1)-I]h|| <= ||h||.

Combine with (8):

  ||y_t-(I-zS)^(-1)h||
        <= C_eta eta^t ||h||,
  C_eta=sqrt((1+eta)/(1-eta)).                            (9)

The bound is uniform over the magnitude of z, over all eigenvalues and
condition numbers of S, and over all physical h. In particular t=O(log
(1/eps)) produces additive operator-action error eps||h||; there is no
log(||S||/target-scale) term. For c(z)>=c0>eta, the corresponding bound is

  ||y_t-y_*|| <= (C_eta/c0)(eta/c0)^t ||h||.              (10)

The statement is per-column; simultaneously it gives Frobenius error
<=eps||Hrhs||_F for a block of q right hand sides. To obtain a spectral
operator approximation on one prescribed q-dimensional input subspace,
apply to an orthonormal basis with per-column eps/sqrt(q), costing only
an extra logarithm in q. One must not claim a spectral block bound with
the same eps from independent per-column bounds without that adjustment.


5. ALL SHIFTED PRECONDITIONERS SHARE ONE EXACT SMALL-CORE SETUP

The potentially fatal per-shift setup in (4) can be avoided. Compute ONCE
a Frobenius normal form and its transformation

  K = P C P^(-1),

where C is block diagonal with companion blocks. This costs
O(rho^omega (loglog rho)^2) deterministic field operations and zero tests,
including the transformation matrix. Computing P^(-1) costs O(rho^omega).

Primary theorem: Neiger--Pernet--Villard, Computing Krylov iterates in the
time of matrix multiplication, ISSAC2024, Corollary5.2:
https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07345v2#S5.SS1 . The abstract explicitly states
the Frobenius form WITH transformation in that complexity. This theorem
is applied only to the explicit COARSE rho-core K, never to the true G.

Given a block R=[r_j] and shifts z_j, (4) requires

  a_j=LJr_j,
  d_j=P^(-1)a_j,
  f_j=(I-z_j C)^(-1)d_j,
  output_j=Jr_j+z_j J L^T P f_j.

All four dense basis/factor products can be batched across q columns.
For each companion block of size d, solving (I-z_j C_d)f=d_j costs O(d)
field operations by a forward recurrence plus one scalar division.
For the convention C has subdiagonal1 and final column -c_i, compute

  a(z)=1+c_(d-1)z+...+c_0 z^d,
  f_d=[d_d+z d_(d-1)+...+z^(d-1)d_1]/a(z),
  f_1=d_1-z c_0 f_d,
  f_i=d_i+z f_(i-1)-z c_(i-1) f_d  (2<=i<d).

The nonreal shifts ensure a(z)!=0 since K is symmetric and hence all
roots of its characteristic polynomial are real. Summing block sizes,
all companion solves cost O(mq) per iteration. No exact eigenvalues,
approximate eigensolver, matrix-function oracle, or per-shift dense inverse
has been assumed. Complex field operations have constant real cost.

This FNF construction can be badly conditioned in finite precision.
Equation (9) is an exact-arithmetic mathematical guarantee. A numerical
implementation can instead investigate orthogonal Schur/eigenbasis
preparation, but its finite-word conditioning/precision analysis is a
separate obligation and is not silently included here.


6. RAW BLOCK ARITHMETIC COST AND STORAGE

For 1<=q<=m<=N, splitting a rectangular product into q-sized square blocks
gives the elementary bound

  T_MM(N,m,q) = O(N m q^(omega-2))

up to harmless ceilings and fixed exponent slack. Thus one raw G-action
on the current block W costs O(I_X q^(omega-2)) via XW and X^T(XW).
The small-core/factor products in Section5 cost O(m^2 q^(omega-2)),
which fits this bound since N>=m. J actions, recurrences and additions
cost O(mq). Initial X^T Hrhs and final XW cost the same raw block bound.

Consequently all q supplied imaginary-axis resolvents with eps||h_j||
per-column error, failure<=delta, are obtained in

  ~O( I_X q^(omega-2) log(1/eps) + m^omega ),             (11)

with additional logq for a spectral subspace guarantee if desired.
Storage can reuse physical N-by-q block buffers, coefficient m-by-q
buffers, and O(m^2 polylogm) coarse/core data, in addition to X. The
Frobenius routine's temporary storage is charged at its arithmetic bound;
this note does not assert a sharper in-place FNF implementation.

The q shifts and right hand sides may depend on the coarse sketch. They
must be available together when each block action is performed. A later
adaptive sequential right hand side cannot be retroactively batched for
free. This nonadaptive-width requirement is precisely why (11) does not
already solve ordinary sequential monomial Krylov.

At m=2d with A,B of shape N0-by-d,d-by-N0, q=~sqrt(d), omega=9/4 and
N0=d^(9/8), (11) is ~O(d^(9/4) log(1/eps)); it matches the decisive
output exponent. This proves that MANY suitable resolvents can be built
at that cost. It does not yet prove that their span or a stable combination
attains the requested rank-one spectral approximation at theta=1/d.

With conventional multiplication omega=3, (11) is O(I_X q log(1/eps)
+ m^3 polylogs), so its speedup over separate solves is chiefly shared
preconditioner setup. It is not a new conventional-arithmetic LRA frontier.


7. EXACT ONLINE ARBITRARY QUERIES HAVE A DIFFERENT BARRIER

This section is a scoped arithmetic-circuit statement, not a lower bound
for the requested approximate algorithm or dependent Krylov sequence.

Fix a scalar arithmetic field K containing the algorithm's finitely many
input-independent constants. Choose a positive-definite symmetric H with
d=m(m+1)/2 entries algebraically independent over K. (There are such
matrices in the open SPD cone.) Allow arbitrary preprocessing of H into
stored real scalar values. A query arrives as a new independent symbolic
vector v and must return Hv exactly, using a finite straight-line program
of t binary field gates and freely reading stored scalar values. On its
generic branch, it can use at most 2t+m distinct stored scalars: every
used scalar must enter a gate or be a direct output.

Let F be the field generated over K by those accessed scalars. Every
output is in F(v). Exact equality with Hv on a nonempty open query cell
implies a rational-function identity, hence every coefficient H_ij is in
F. Thus d<=trdeg_K(F)<=2t+m, so

  t >= (m(m+1)/2-m)/2 = Omega(m^2).                      (12)

The same count holds for a finite algebraic decision tree on an open
branch. Allowing a fixed number of algebraic root gates only replaces F
by an algebraic extension, which does not increase transcendence degree.
No assertion is made for arbitrary nonalgebraic primitives, free vector
gates, unbounded real-encoding/decoding operations, or approximate outputs.

For q genuinely independent ONLINE queries, specialize all earlier query
vectors to values in K and apply the same generic branch argument to the
new symbolic vector at each stage. The earlier stored state is still
preprocessing independent of that new vector. Each query has Omega(m^2)
arithmetic cost. This excludes an exact arbitrary-query data structure
with total o(m^2 q), even with unbounded preprocessing. In particular a
total m^(9/4) for q=sqrt(m) cannot be obtained by this model. A claim about
randomized exact algorithms would need its own branch/measure argument;
the clean proposition here is deterministic and generic-circuit based.

This does NOT apply to v,Hv,H^2v,... as independent query variables. Their
dependence on H invalidates the coefficient-field argument. It also does
not prevent the NONADAPTIVE batched procedure of Sections1--6: a block of
q independent queries can be processed using fast matrix multiplication,
without returning each answer before the next query becomes available.


8. A CLOSED ONE-RHS ROW-MERGE SUMMARY MUST ENCODE THE FULL GRAM

Let v!=0 be fixed. Suppose a summary s(H) permits exact rank-one updates
H -> H+xx^T for arbitrary x, and after an update returns BOTH
(H+xx^T)v and (H+xx^T)^2v. Assume it can also return baseline u=Hv and
t=H^2v. Put b=x^T v. Direct expansion gives

  (H+xx^T)^2v
       = t + b Hx + (x^T u + ||x||^2 b)x.                 (13)

Whenever b!=0, its output recovers

  Hx = [(H+xx^T)^2v-t-(x^T u+||x||^2b)x]/b.             (14)

Thus the summary determines Hx on an open dense set of x, hence all of H.
For an algebraic summary of s scalar values, transcendence degree gives
s>=m(m+1)/2 on generic SPD inputs. The same dimensional obstruction holds
for continuous summaries on an open set by the usual topological dimension
argument. Even order-three one-start Krylov cannot admit a uniformly
smaller-dimensional exact summary closed under arbitrary row updates.

This strengthens the elementary two-dimensional counterexample already
recorded in factor-algebra-search.txt. Its limitation is explicit: a merge
algorithm may retain the raw factors, which have MORE than m^2 scalars,
or use multipole/new-seed summaries with a full-dimensional state. The
theorem does not prove that such a richer representation costs a true Gram
to compute, nor does it rule out a globally computed short Krylov sequence.


9. A RIGOROUS EXPONENTIAL RESIDUE-MASS OBSTRUCTION

This rules out a direct stable linear combination of only imaginary-axis
resolvents as a free sharp filter. It does not rule out quotient operations,
coherent common-backward-operator constructions, or a useful subspace with
an independently proved perturbation theorem.

Let 0<theta<=1/2 and

  r(x)=a0+sum_j a_j/(1-i s_j x),       s_j real,
  K=sum_j |a_j|.

Suppose |r(x)|<=1 throughout x in [1/2,1], but |r(1+theta)|>=2. Then

  K >= (1/4) 2^[ arcosh(7/5)/sqrt(8theta) ].             (15)

Proof. Differentiating each summand, |r'(x)|<=K/(2x) for x>=1,
since max_s |s|/(1+s^2 x^2)=1/(2x). The required jump therefore gives
K>=2/log(1+theta)>1. Put L=ceil(log2(4K)). For any imaginary z=i s,
the Taylor expansion around x=1 has exact remainder

  1/(1-zx) - [1/(1-z)]sum_(k=0)^(L-1)[z(x-1)/(1-z)]^k
    = [z(x-1)/(1-z)]^L/(1-zx).

For real x in [1/2,3/2], |z/(1-z)|<=1 and |1/(1-zx)|<=1, so its
absolute value is <=2^(-L). Summing yields a degree-(L-1) polynomial p
with |r-p|<=K2^(-L)<=1/4 on the same interval. Thus |p|<=5/4 on
[1/2,1] and |p(1+theta)|>=7/4. Chebyshev extremality gives

  7/5 <= T_(L-1)(1+4theta)
        =cosh((L-1)arcosh(1+4theta))
        <=cosh((L-1)sqrt(8theta)).

It applies to complex p too: rotate its value at 1+theta to be positive
real and take the real part, preserving its bound on the real interval.
Since L-1<=log2(4K), rearrangement gives (15).

Consequently a termwise evaluation error budget for this type of filter
requires log(1/eps)=Omega(theta^(-1/2)) even when each scalar resolvent
itself is cheaply computable. Degree/pole count alone does not certify
stable filtering. This is the exact obstruction behind the earlier
observation that, with scalar coarse G0, L Richardson steps produce only
a degree-L polynomial of the true operator no matter how many shifts
are evaluated. The coefficient result does not require scalar G0.


10. APPROXIMATE TRUNCATED CAYLEY KRYLOV IN THE PSD CASE

There IS a further order-sensitive constructive theorem. It generates
bounded Cayley powers to polynomial absolute accuracy at the desired
q-sensitive input cost. It does not yet furnish a stable sharp LRA filter.

Specialize to J=I, so S=X X^T is PSD. Let s>0 be arbitrary and

  T=(S-sI)(S+sI)^(-1),             spec(T) subset [-1,1].

For a supplied physical h and integer 2<=q<=m, the first q vectors

  [h,Th,...,T^(q-1)h]

can be approximated, in Frobenius norm to eps||h||, with probability
>=1-delta in

  ~O( I_X q^(omega-2) log(q/eps) + m^omega ).             (16)

No log(condition number of S), no restriction on s, and no supplied exact
eigenvalues enter. The output vectors are approximate; this is NOT the
exact partial raw monomial Krylov oracle requested in the main problem.

10.1 Holomorphic contraction

For PSD K and Re z<=0,

  ||zK(I-zK)^(-1)||<=1,
  ||[(I-zS)^(-1)-I]h||<=||h||.

Thus Sections3--4 give error <=C_eta eta^L||h|| uniformly on the ENTIRE
left half-plane after L coarse Richardson iterations. For |t|<1 put

  z(t)=-(1-t)/[(1+t)s],             Re z(t)<0.

The Cayley generating function has the exact identity

  V(t)=(I-tT)^(-1)h
      =h/(1-t) - [2t/(1-t^2)](I-z(t)S)^(-1)h.           (17)

Construct the raw Richardson iteration simultaneously as a truncated
power series in t, to order q. It is algebraically the SAME iteration
as evaluating at each complex t, so its full rational-function error
bound on |t|=r<1 is

  ||V_L(t)-V(t)|| <= [2r/(1-r^2)] C_eta eta^L ||h||.

Choose r=1-1/q. Cauchy's coefficient inequality and
r^(-(q-1))<=e imply that each of the first q coefficient errors is at
most e C_eta q eta^L||h||. Their combined Frobenius error is therefore
<=e C_eta q^(3/2)eta^L||h||. Taking L=O(log(q/eps)) proves the accuracy
claim without an exponential-in-q tolerance.

10.2 The polynomial-matrix operations really fit (16)

The raw saddle equation, with its scalar denominator cleared, is

  B(t) w(t)=(1+t)s X^T h,
  B(t)=(1+t)s I+(1-t)G.

Its coarse preconditioner is the explicit degree-one core

  B0(t)=(1+t)s I+(1-t)G0
       =(sI+G0)(I-tT0),
  T0=(sI+G0)^(-1)(G0-sI).

Compute T0, the inverse of sI+G0, and ONE Frobenius form T0=P C P^(-1)
in ~O(m^omega). All are coarse m-cores. To apply B0(t)^(-1) to ANY
m-vector polynomial R(t) modulo t^q, first multiply its q-column
coefficient block by P^(-1)(sI+G0)^(-1), then solve

  (I-t C)F(t)=D(t) mod t^q,

then multiply the coefficient block by P. The two transforms cost
O(m^2 q^(omega-2)). The companion solve is efficient even though its
right hand side is a polynomial, rather than a constant vector:

  f_d(t) = [sum_(j=1)^d t^(d-j)d_j(t)]
           /[1+c_(d-1)t+...+c_0t^d]  mod t^q,
  f_1=d_1-t c_0f_d,
  f_i=d_i+t f_(i-1)-t c_(i-1)f_d.

Forming the numerator/recurrences costs O(dq); invert the scalar
denominator modulo t^q once (its constant coefficient is1), then use
fast polynomial multiplication. Summed over all companion blocks, this
costs ~O(mq) per application, with ~O(mq) precomputed series data.

Every true B(t)-action is a raw G multiplication on q coefficients,
costing O(I_X q^(omega-2)); multiplication by (1+-t) only shifts/adds
coefficient columns. The final X lift and scalar series manipulations
in (17) have the same or smaller cost. Iterating L times modulo t^q
produces exactly the first q coefficients of the rational Richardson
iterate, because all inverses expanded have nonzero constant term.
This proves (16), including the degree-sensitive preconditioner cost.

10.3 Why this does not already settle sharp spectral LRA

The monomials T^j are uniformly bounded, but a Chebyshev filter tailored
to a shorter tail interval, e.g. [-1,0], can have exponentially large
coefficients in that basis. Polynomial absolute accuracy of the Cayley
Krylov block alone therefore does not establish a stable approximate
Krylov subspace or a useful sharp filter.

The obvious Chebyshev generating function does not preserve the cheap
holomorphic domain. Its denominator is I-2tT+t^2I; after Cayley mapping,
it contains

  (1-t)^2 S + (1+t)^2 sI.

The corresponding raw scalar shift is
-[(1-t)/(1+t)]^2/s, which approaches the positive real axis as t approaches
the unit circle. Uniform coarse contraction then fails. This is exactly
the missing stability, not an uncharged polynomial manipulation.

The theorem may be useful for stable matrix-function classes whose
coefficients in bounded Cayley powers have polynomial mass, or as a
building block for a common-backward-operator construction. No priority
or novelty claim is made without a primary-literature audit.


11. SIGNED EXTENSION: BOUNDED CAYLEY POWERS OF S^2 FOR ARBITRARY AB

The same q-sensitive approximate-series theorem DOES extend to arbitrary
products, by a quarter-plane square-root mapping. An initial implementation
had an m q^2 composition term; the fast Möbius/Taylor transformations in
Section11.4 remove it. The resulting bound for approximate bounded rational
Krylov powers is

  ~O(I_X q^(omega-2) log(q/eps) + m^omega).               (18)

Here J is again any fixed symmetric involution, S=X J X^T, s>0 arbitrary,
and the returned q columns approximate

  [h,T h,...,T^(q-1)h],
  T=(S^2-s^2I)(S^2+s^2I)^(-1),

to Frobenius error eps||h||. It allows rank deficiency and has no spectral
condition-number logarithm. For the product dilation, S^2 is block diagonal
with blocks MM^T and M^T M. Starting h=[g;0] therefore computes the bounded
Cayley-Krylov powers of MM^T in its first physical block. No true Gram or
full transformed tall factor is formed.

11.1 A uniform analytic domain for the SIGNED saddle

For |t|<1 choose the analytic branch

  z(t)=(i/s) sqrt((1-t)/(1+t)),            z(0)=z0=i/s.

The quotient (1-t)/(1+t) lies in the open right half-plane. Its square
root has argument between -pi/4 and pi/4; hence z(t) has argument between
pi/4 and 3pi/4, so c(z(t))>=1/sqrt(2) uniformly throughout the disk.
The same holds for -z(t). With eta=1/4, the signed raw Richardson
iteration therefore contracts by at most sqrt(2)eta<1 at EVERY t.

Define

  R2(t)=(I-z(t)^2 S^2)^(-1)
       =(1/2)[(I-z(t)S)^(-1)+(I+z(t)S)^(-1)].

The average identity follows by multiplying the commuting resolvents.
Since z(t)^2=-(1-t)/[(1+t)s^2], the generating function of T obeys

  (I-tT)^(-1)h
       =h/(1-t)-[2t/(1-t^2)]R2(t)h.                     (19)

Apply the uniform error (10) to each of the two raw resolvents, then use
the same Cauchy coefficient argument as Section10. All q coefficient
errors together are bounded by

  e sqrt(2) C_eta q^(3/2)(sqrt(2)eta)^L ||h||.

Thus L=O(log(q/eps)) iterations suffice. The square root here is solely
a scalar formal series/analytic mapping; no matrix square root is taken.

11.2 Efficient application of a NONLINEAR coarse series inverse

It remains to justify that A0(t)=J-z(t)G0 can be inverted on arbitrary
polynomial vector right hand sides without q copies of an m-core solve.

Set u(t)=z(t)-z0. Then u(0)=0 and u'(0)=-z0!=0. Its compositional inverse
is the explicit rational series

  t(u)=[z0^2-(z0+u)^2]/[z0^2+(z0+u)^2].                (20)

The denominator has constant coefficient 2z0^2!=0. Put

  A00=J-z0G0,                 T0=A00^(-1)G0.

Both are explicit m-cores, A00 is invertible, and

  A0(t)=A00(I-u(t)T0).

Compute one exact complex-field Frobenius form T0=P C P^(-1), together
with the fused transform P^(-1)A00^(-1), in ~O(m^omega). No symmetry of
T0 is required by the Frobenius theorem. This setup is independent of the
Richardson iteration and of its polynomial right hand sides.

For each input polynomial R(t), to compute A0(t)^(-1)R(t) mod t^q:

  (a) Transform its q coefficient columns by P^(-1)A00^(-1).
  (b) Compose each resulting scalar coordinate polynomial with t(u).
  (c) Solve (I-u C)F(u)=D(u) mod u^q by companion series recurrences.
  (d) Compose each coordinate of F(u) with u(t), and transform by P.

The two basis transformations cost O(m^2 q^(omega-2)); (c) costs ~O(mq)
as in Section10. Both composition maps are fixed linear maps on q scalar
coefficients, so precompute their q-by-q matrices. Their application to
m coordinate polynomials costs O(mq^2) conventionally. The matrices can
be prepared in ~O(q^2) using q successive truncated polynomial products;
the coefficients of u(t) follow in O(q) from the scalar square-root
differential equation. Equation (20) uses ordinary scalar-series inversion.
Thus neither composition preparation nor application exceeds the m q^2
term claimed in (18).

The true residual A(t)w(t) requires one raw G-action on its q coefficient
columns, plus multiplication by the scalar series z(t), costing
O(I_X q^(omega-2))+~O(mq). The final X lift and scalar convolutions in
(19) are equally charged. As before, truncating every arithmetic operation
modulo t^q gives exactly the first q coefficients of the full analytic
Richardson iterate, so the analytic estimate applies to the actual finite
algorithm. For real h, the two signs have conjugate coefficient series;
one complex solve and coefficientwise real parts suffice. Two solves also
fit the same bound and handle arbitrary complex h.

11.3 Scope of this constructive step

This is a product-aware, q-sensitive APPROXIMATE rational Krylov theorem
with the requested raw-factor tradeoff, not an exact monomial Krylov theorem
and not yet a relative spectral LRA result. Basis conversion to a sharp
filter can still need exponential accuracy. No such conversion or common
nearby-operator perturbation theorem is asserted. All costs above are
exact-field costs; a stable finite-precision implementation of the FNF
and formal composition steps remains separate work.

11.4 Quasi-linear composition by Möbius maps and fast Taylor shifts

The O(mq^2) bound in Section11.2 can be improved to ~O(mq). All arguments
below are over characteristic-zero fields and use exact scalar arithmetic.
Fast Taylor shifts cost O(M(q)) through the standard factorial convolution:
the coefficient of x^j in f(x+a) is

  (1/j!) sum_(k>=j) f_k k! a^(k-j)/(k-j)!.

All q such sums are one convolution after reversing/scaling coefficients.
Factorials, powers and their inverses cost O(q) preparatory arithmetic.

First, any Möbius substitution x=(1-w)/(1+w) on a degree-d polynomial
f can be performed rationally in ~O(q), d<q. Let h(y)=f(-1+y), obtained
by one Taylor shift. Define the reversed/scaled polynomial

  k(v)=sum_(j=0)^d h_j 2^j v^(d-j).

Then

  (1+w)^d f((1-w)/(1+w)) = k(1+w).

The right side is a second Taylor shift. Dividing by the known scalar
series (1+w)^d modulo the requested order uses O(M(q)) arithmetic.
The numerator can be retained polynomially when convenient.

FORWARD composition R(t(u)). Write v=1+u/z0 and w=v^2; then
t=(1-w)/(1+w). For each coordinate polynomial f(t), d=q-1, compute

  F(w)=(1+w)^d f((1-w)/(1+w)),

using the two shifts above. Interleave F's coefficients to obtain F(v^2),
of degree at most2d, then Taylor-shift v=1+a in O(M(2q)) arithmetic.
Multiply its first q a-coefficients by (2+2a+a^2)^(-d) mod a^q, and scale
a=u/z0. This is the desired f(t(u)) mod u^q. The denominator series costs
only O(q) after f_0=2^(-d): its coefficients obey

  2(k+1) f_(k+1)
     =-(2k+2d) f_k-(k-1+2d) f_(k-1),       f_(-1)=0,

by differentiating (2+2a+a^2)^(-d). This also treats d=0.

REVERSE composition R(u(t)). Given g(u) of degree<q, compute
G(v)=g(z0(v-1)) by scaling and one Taylor shift, then split

  G(v)=E(v^2)+v O(v^2).

Substitute w=(1-t)/(1+t) into E(w),O(w) by the same Möbius routine, then
multiply the odd part by the known v(t)=sqrt((1-t)/(1+t)) series.
All degrees are O(q), and all scalar products/inverses are quasi-linear.

Thus BOTH compositions cost ~O(q) per coordinate, not O(q^2). Truncation
is legitimate because u(0)=t(0)=0: terms discarded at degree>=q cannot
contribute to any of the first q coefficients under either substitution.
The temporary polynomial interleaving has degree<2q, so it changes only
constant factors. All coordinates together cost ~O(mq), which is bounded
by O(I_X q^(omega-2)) for 1<=q<=m<=N. Combining with the already counted
raw block products, core transforms and companion solves proves (18).

The Möbius composition idea was supplied by the root agent; the finite
factorial-convolution/denominator derivation above closes its cost proof.


12. ACTUAL APPLICATION: LOW-POSITIVE-INDEX SIGNED PSD PROJECTION

The following is an actual approximation algorithm, not only a primitive.
It was proposed by root, and the raw resolvent theorem supplies its solver.
It is a narrower problem than unrestricted product LRA; no seminal novelty
claim is made without the further literature comparison noted below.

THEOREM (exact-field, low positive index). Let S=XJX^T with raw X(N,m),
N>=m, J a fixed symmetric involution, and assume n_+(S)<=r, 1<=r<=m.
For every eps,delta in (0,1), one can construct a PSD matrix C=ZZ^T of
rank at most r with

  P{ ||C-S_+||_2 <= eps||S||_2 } >= 1-delta

at construction cost

  ~O(I_X r + m^3)                 conventional arithmetic,
  ~O(I_X r^(omega-2)+m^omega)      with fast small-core linear algebra.

All suppressed accuracy/confidence dependencies are logarithmic; no gap,
smallest-positive-eigenvalue, or factor-energy promise is imposed. The fast
row's small-core approximate PSD truncation uses the same standard fast
symmetric-core primitive already cited in the main research direction;
the conventional row has the explicit scalar-root Jacobi implementation
below. Both rows use exact-field coarse Frobenius preparation. This is not
yet a floating-point backward-stability theorem. Expanding the N-by-N
answer adds O(N^2 r), or its usual fast rectangular multiplication bound.

12.1 A computable scale from the COARSE core

On the coarse event (2), X=Tphys L with
(1-eta)I<=Tphys^T Tphys<=(1+eta)I. Consequently

  S=Tphys K Tphys^T,       K=L J L^T,
  (1-eta)||K|| <= ||S|| <= (1+eta)||K||.

Let U=(1+eta)||K||_F and c0=(1+eta)/(1-eta). Then

  ||S||<=U<=c0 sqrt(m)||S||.

This uses only the coarse core and a positive scalar square root. If K=0,
then S=0 on the event and return zero. Otherwise the known quantity
ell=U/(c0 sqrt(m)) is a positive lower bound on ||S||. Thus a requested
operator error proportional to eps ell implies the theorem's relative
error; the extra sqrt(m) only adds a logarithm to all filter tolerances.

12.2 Explicit rational |x| approximation with controlled coefficient mass

For real x,

  |x|=(2/pi) integral_(0)^infinity x^2/(x^2+t^2) dt.

After t=Ue^y, the integrand is a translated scaled sech:
x^2 Ue^y/(x^2+U^2e^(2y))=|x|/[2cosh(y-log(|x|/U))].
The infinite logarithmic trapezoid rule with spacing h has uniform error
at most C U exp(-pi^2/h), by Poisson summation and the elementary Fourier
transform of sech. Truncating at t in [U2^(-K),U2^K] adds at most C U2^(-K)
uniformly for |x|<=U, since the integrand is <=t at the small-t end and
<=U^2/t at the large-t end.

For target alpha U, choose K=ceil(log2(C/alpha)), L a power of two at
least K, h=log(2)/L, and indices -LK<=j<=LK. Then

  a(x)=sum_j w_j x^2/(x^2+t_j^2),
  t_j=U2^(j/L),     w_j=(2h/pi)t_j,

satisfies sup_(|x|<=U)||x|-a(x)|<=alpha U. The number of terms is
O(log^2(1/alpha)); their nonnegative coefficient mass obeys

  W=sum_j w_j <= C U/alpha.

Nodes use only O(log L) repeated positive square roots to form 2^(1/L),
then scalar multiplication. pi and log(2) are fixed constants, not new
matrix-function primitives. Parameter caps are chosen by repeated scalar
doubling/comparisons. The construction is finite.

Every matrix term is

  S^2(S^2+t_j^2I)^(-1)
   =I-(1/2)[(I-iS/t_j)^(-1)+(I+iS/t_j)^(-1)].

Thus the raw imaginary-resolvent theorem evaluates
F=(S+a(S))/2 to ||F-S_+||<=nu using only polylog(U/nu) shifted solves
and Richardson iterations: the scalar approximation takes alpha≈nu/U,
and each resolvent needs accuracy O(nu/W)=Omega(alpha^2). This precision
has logarithmic iteration cost. The function approximation is uniform at
zero, so no tiny-positive-eigenvalue condition occurs. The finite solver
is one common linear operator F on all right hand sides, although F need
not be exactly self-adjoint; its operator error bound is uniform.

12.3 Two filtered sketches give the rank-r PSD output

For r<m draw a physical Gaussian Omega(N,r+1), independent of the coarse
sketch. If A=S_+ has true rank k<=r, a standard square Gaussian head
inverse bound and a Frobenius norm bound imply, with failure<=delta/3,

  ||Omega|| ||(U_+^T Omega)^dagger|| <= B,
  B=(100N(m+1)/delta)^3.

This deliberately loose polynomial avoids additional oversampling or any
unknown k in the algorithm. The k=0 case is immediate. Choose

  nu=eps ell/[100(B+1)].

Use the filter to compute Y=F Omega and an exact orthonormal basis Q of
range(Y), with P=QQ^T. Since (I-P)Y=0 and A has rank k,

  ||(I-P)A||
   =||(I-P)A Omega (U_+^T Omega)^dagger U_+^T||
   <=nu B.

It follows ||A-PAP||<=2nu B. Use a SECOND filter pass on Q, set
R=Q^T F Q, and symmetrize R. It differs from the exact PSD rank-<=r core
Q^T A Q by at most nu. Compute a rank-<=r PSD approximation Rhat with
error <=Cnu to that core, and output C=Q Rhat Q^T=ZZ^T. Therefore

  ||C-A|| <=2nu B+Cnu <=eps ell <=eps||S||.

The second pass is needed: replacing R by Q^T S Q and clipping its
negative eigenvalues would require another perturbation argument, because
the negative part of S can contaminate Q. Filtering Q directly avoids it.

For conventional arithmetic, diagonalize the small symmetric R by Jacobi
until off-diagonal Frobenius norm<=nu, then keep the largest r positive
diagonal values and their orthogonal columns. This takes O(s^3 log(sU/nu))
operations up to an additional log(s) factor, s<=r+1, uses ordinary field
operations and positive roots, and does not assume eigenvalue separation.
For an explicit finite realization, use maximum-pivot Jacobi and maintain
off-diagonal magnitudes in a heap. Each rotation reduces squared
off-diagonal norm by at least its current fraction 2/[s(s-1)], requires
O(s) arithmetic for matrix/eigenvector updates and O(s log s) heap work,
so O(s^2 log(sU/nu)) rotations give the asserted ~O(s^3) cost.
Its approximation to the exact PSD
rank-r core has error O(nu) by Weyl and truncation. Fast symmetric-core
linear algebra gives the corresponding ~O(m^omega) option.

If r=m, the exact Gram/core route costs O(I_X m^(omega-2)+m^omega), or
O(I_X m+m^3) conventionally, so it fits the displayed bounds directly.
For r<m, all sketch, QR, compressed products, small truncation and lifting
costs fit the displayed r-sensitive bounds; r+1 is within a constant of r.
There are only polylog(Nm/(eps delta)) poles and precision iterations,
and one common coarse/Frobenius setup. No hidden per-pole core setup occurs.

12.4 Comparison and limitation

Jones--Anderson, Approximate Projection onto the PSD Cone Using
Randomization, arXiv:2410.19208v2, Aug18,2026, Algorithms2--3 and
Corollary1/Proposition2, is a close primary comparison:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.19208 . It uses randomized range finding on
the explicit symmetric matrix or a shifted version; its residual bounds
depend on singular tails or shifted eigenvalue tails. It does not by itself
give the raw-factor/polylog-accuracy bound just proved from low positive
index alone. Kang et al., arXiv:2507.09165v2, uses composite polynomial
matrix products for PSD projection, another relevant comparison.

However, low-positive-index matrices also have a classical secular form:
S=UU^T-VV^T with U of width<=r, and positive eigenvalues lambda solve
det[I-U^T(VV^T+lambda I)^(-1)U]=0. Rank-one secular bisection and ridge
solves are established tools. A wider literature audit is needed before
claiming that the result or its conventional implementation is new.

Most decisively, this theorem does NOT solve the original rank-r relative
LRA problem: the unshifted product dilation has m positive eigenvalues,
while subtracting a nonzero spectral threshold expands the raw identity
factor or requires the unknown true support metric. Neither operation is
free. The broad quadratic objective remains open despite this useful
gap-free signed-covariance application.


13. TENSOR-LEVEL NEW INGREDIENT CANDIDATE: TANGENT-BUNDLE MULTIPLICATION

This changes the family of ingredients rather than optimizing the already
barrier-limited univariate D0--D4 rules. It is an actual degeneration and
rank computation, but it has NOT yet yielded a better multiplication
exponent or a broad quadratic algorithm. These are standard geometric
constructions applied as candidate tensor ingredients, not a priority claim.

Work over C (or any suitable infinite characteristic-zero field). On P^2,
let P2_(a,b), a>=1,b>=1, denote multiplication

  H0(O(a-1)) x H0(O(b-1)) -> H0(O(a+b-2)).

Its three leg dimensions are

  binom(a+1,2), binom(b+1,2), binom(a+b,2).

Define E_(a,b), a>=1,b>=0, as multiplication by a scalar section f on
the tangent-bundle section module:

  H0(O(a-1)) x H0(T_P2(b-1)) -> H0(T_P2(a+b-2)).

Its three leg dimensions are

  binom(a+1,2), (b+1)(b+3), (a+b)(a+b+2).

The Euler sequence 0->O->O(1)^3->T_P2->0, twisted by b-1 and a+b-2,
gives exact global-section rows because H1(O(k))=0 on P^2. Multiplication
by f gives a commuting diagram between those rows. Choosing bases adapted
to the subspaces in each row makes the middle multiplication tensor block
upper triangular with diagonal blocks P2_(a,b) and E_(a,b). Scaling away
the off-diagonal block yields the shared-first-leg degeneration

  P2_(a,b) partialsum_1 E_(a,b)
       degenerates from partialsum_1^3 P2_(a,b+1).        (20)

Here P2_(a,0)=0 by convention. The first leg is unchanged. The dimensions
match on both unshared legs, e.g.

  binom(b+1,2)+(b+1)(b+3)=3binom(b+2,2).

This is the P^2/Euler analogue of the binary-polynomial concavity relation,
but the tangent bundle has rank2 and does not split into line bundles.
Consequently E is a genuinely additional tensor family, not merely a
different numerical choice in the old scalar-P rules.

13.1 Minimal tensor rank by geometric interpolation

In fact the tensor rank of E_(a,b) is EXACTLY its output dimension:

  rank(E_(a,b))=(a+b)(a+b+2).                             (21)

Upper bound: the dual of the target section space is spanned by point/fiber
evaluations ev_(x,ell), where x is a point of P^2 and ell a fiber covector.
A nonzero section is nonzero at some point, so these evaluations separate
sections; select a basis of D=dim(target) such evaluation functionals.
For each selected evaluation,

  ev_(x,ell)(f*g) = f(x) ell(g(x)),

after fixed line-bundle trivialization. This is a rank-one bilinear form.
The D coordinate outputs thus give rank<=D. No assertion about grouping
full rank2 fibers or generic vector-bundle interpolation is needed: one
may use distinct points/covectors for each of the D scalar evaluations.

Lower bound: the multiplication map onto the target is surjective. The
Euler description writes each tangent section as a triple of homogeneous
polynomials modulo the radial triple; scalar polynomial multiplication
spans every target triple. Thus its output flattening rank is D.

For a=2,b=0, E is the 3-by-3 outer product modulo scalar identity, with
output dimension8 and rank8. Explicitly compute six off-diagonal products;
two additional products (f1+f2)(g1-g2) and (f2+f3)(g2-g3), together with
those off-diagonals, give two independent diagonal differences. This is a
small concrete check of (21), not a claimed new rank-8 algorithm.

The same rank<=output-dimension argument applies to multiplication of
scalar sections with sections of any vector bundle, whenever the target
is separated by fiber evaluations. Surjective multiplication gives equality.

13.2 Duality and the next actual closure question

On P^2, T^* is isomorphic to T(-3). Serre duality therefore identifies

  H0(T(k))^* with H2(T(-k-6)).

If E^i_(a,b) denotes the analogous multiplication on H^i(T(b-1)), then
swapping E^0's unshared legs gives

  (23) E^0_(a,b)  isomorphic to E^2_(a,-a-b-3).          (22)

The tangent bundle has H0(T(k)) for k>=-1, H2(T(k)) for k<=-5, and the
isolated middle group H1(T(-3))=C; T(-2) and T(-4) are acyclic. These
facts follow directly from the Euler sequence and line-bundle cohomology.
The isolated middle channel and the nonsplit rank2 module are absent from
the P^1 line-bundle picture. A useful new tripling/separation relation would
have to exploit this closure, rather than merely replacing one-dimensional
intervals by boxes. No such exponent-improving relation is proved yet.

The one proved degeneration (20), cheap rank (21), and duality (22) are
the concrete current tensor-level output. Dimension counts alone do not
show that they beat the 9/4 operational barrier for the older ingredients.

13.3 Complementary Euler relation (root's construction)

The dual Euler sequence, using T^*=T(-3), is

  0->T(b-1)->O(b+1)^3->O(b+2)->0.

For b>=0 the relevant H1 tangent terms vanish, so its global-section
multiplication diagram similarly gives

  E_(a,b) partialsum_1 P2_(a,b+3)
       degenerates from partialsum_1^3 P2_(a,b+2).        (23)

Root supplied this complementary sequence. It is not sufficient to infer
a better exponent: for a quadratic scalar dimension function u(b), the
two expressions 3u(b+1)-u(b) and 3u(b+2)-u(b+3) coincide exactly. Any
successful spectral argument must exploit more than those leading
dimension identities, for example a genuinely useful duality/boundary
closure or a new matrix-multiplication extraction.

13.4 A fully explicit TERNARY QUADRUPLING degeneration

There is another concrete new-family packing relation:

  partialsum_1^3 P2_(2,b) partialsum_1 (23)P2_(2,b)
       degenerates_1 from P2_(2,2b+1),       b>=1.        (24)

Proof. Put L=b+1 and R=C[x,y,z]/(x^L,y^L,z^L). The right tensor is
multiplication by an arbitrary linear f from degree2b to degree2b+1.
The subspace of degree2b polynomials divisible by x^L, y^L, or z^L is
a direct sum of three corner spaces: no monomial is divisible by two
such powers, because 2b+1<2L. The corresponding output corner spaces
are also disjoint and multiplication by f preserves each corner. Each
corner map is ordinary degree(b-1)->degree b multiplication by f,
namely P2_(2,b).

The quotient diagonal block is f:R_(2b)->R_(2b+1). The complete
intersection R is Gorenstein with socle degree3b and perfect coefficient
pairing R_j x R_(3b-j)->C. Thus this quotient block is the transpose of
f:R_(b-1)->R_b. Both these low degrees are below L, so it is precisely
the transpose of P2_(2,b). Scaling the off-diagonal blocks away proves
(24), without transforming the shared first leg.

Both unshared leg dimensions are saturated:

  3binom(b+1,2)+binom(b+2,2)=binom(2b+2,2),
  3binom(b+2,2)+binom(b+1,2)=binom(2b+3,2).

For b=1, the (3,6,10)-dimensional tensor P2_(2,3) degenerates to three
normal length3 vector channels and one transposed length3 channel.

The restriction to first degree1 is substantive for the NAIVE identification
of the whole central block with an ordinary polynomial tensor: for first
degree a-1, disjoint corners require L>=a+b-1, whereas BOTH dual degrees
being ordinary require L<b+2. Only a<=2 satisfies these. The stronger
restriction argument in Section13.6 nevertheless recovers a fourth ordinary
fragment for ALL a, by strong Lefschetz rather than by this identification.

13.5 The actual central module for arbitrary first degree

For arbitrary a>=2,b>=1 put L=a+b-1 and B=a+2b-1. Decomposing
P2_(a,B) into the three disjoint corner blocks gives three P2_(a,b)
and the quotient map f:R_(L+b-1)->R_(2L-1). Its Gorenstein transpose is

  f: Sym^(a+b-3) V ->
       Sym^(2a+b-4) V / (x^L,y^L,z^L)_(2a+b-4).         (25)

The source is ordinary because its degree is L-2; the target has the
three corner relations x^L,y^L,z^L times Sym^(a-3)V. Keeping this
quotient, rather than silently calling it an ordinary P2 tensor, is the
correct general-degree closure obligation.

Geometrically it is an intermediate-cohomology module. Let K_L be the
rank2 syzygy bundle in

  0->K_L->O(-L)^3 --(x^L,y^L,z^L)-->O->0.

Then H1(K_L(k))=R_k. Equivalently K_L is the pullback of Omega^1_P2
under [x:y:z]->[x^L:y^L:z^L]. Its self-duality up to twist recovers
the Gorenstein pairing. This explicitly links the tangent/cohomology
line with the new quadrupling relation. No rank-minimal interpolation
bound is asserted for these H1 quotients; the point-evaluation argument
in Section13.1 applies to H0 target sections and does not automatically
apply to intermediate cohomology.

Relation (24) has fixed first dimension3, so by itself it is not a proof
of omega<=2 or even of an improvement over9/4. The unresolved mathematical
task is to couple growing first-leg representations to this central-module
closure and prove a violated operational constraint or an actual useful
MM degeneration. The exact relations (20)--(25) are current proved data.


13.6 GENERAL ternary quadrupling by strong Lefschetz

The central-module obstruction to IDENTIFYING the whole quotient does not
prevent RESTRICTING a fourth ordinary fragment. In fact, for all a>=2,b>=1,

  partialsum_1^3 P2_(a,b) partialsum_1 (23)P2_(a,b)
       degenerates_1 from P2_(a,a+2b-1).                 (26)

Use L=a+b-1 and the decomposition of Section13.5. Its central transpose
is the map (25). Put ell=x+y+z and restrict its input to

  g=ell^(a-2) g0,       g0 in Sym^(b-1)V.

Then fg=ell^(a-2)(f g0), where f g0 ranges in the ordinary degree L-1
piece R_(L-1). The map

  ell^(a-2): R_(L-1) -> R_(L+a-3)

is injective by strong Lefschetz for the monomial complete intersection.
Here is a short representation proof of the needed form. Each univariate
ring C[x]/x^L is an irreducible sl2 module with raising operator multiply
by x; tensor the three modules. Its raising operator is multiply by ell,
and its weight is twice the total degree minus the socle degree3L-3.
On every irreducible summand a raising power is injective before the
middle symmetric weight. Thus multiplication ell^d from degree j is
injective whenever 2j+d<=3L-3. With j=L-1,d=a-2 this inequality is
a-1<=L, which holds for b>=1.

Choose a fixed linear left inverse on this image. It recovers the full
ordinary product f g0, so the central transpose restricts to P2_(a,b).
Transposing back supplies the fourth, permuted fragment in (26). No map
depends on f, and the shared first leg is untouched. The three original
corner blocks remain separated by the preceding triangular degeneration.

For a>2 there is unused dimension; for a=2, (26) reduces to the saturated
relation (24). This is an actual growing-first-leg new-family degeneration,
not an asserted improvement of omega. In particular, root checked that a
naive squared lift of the old p=3/4 operational barrier has slack in this
quadrupling relation, so (26) alone is insufficient to break9/4.

13.7 Cheap central-module rank by root's apolar quadrature

Root supplied an independent rank bound for ANY graded trilinear slice of
R=C[x,y,z]/(x^L,y^L,z^L). Its socle functional is coefficient extraction
at x^(L-1)y^(L-1)z^(L-1). For homogeneous f,g,h of total degree3L-3,

  coeff_(L-1,L-1,L-1)(fgh)
   =L^(-2) sum_(zeta^L=xi^L=1)
      zeta^(-(L-1)) xi^(-(L-1))
      f(1,zeta,xi)g(1,zeta,xi)h(1,zeta,xi).              (27)

The two root-of-unity sums select y,z exponents congruent to L-1 modL.
The total degree permits only the pair (L-1,L-1), since replacing either
by2L-1 already exceeds3L-3 when the other is at leastL-1. Then the x
exponent is also L-1. Thus each of the L^2 summands is rankone and every
graded slice, including the central tensor in (25), has tensor rank<=L^2.
The formula annihilates the complete-intersection ideal in each input at
the relevant total degree, so it descends to the quotient pieces.

This closes a real rank-cost issue for intermediate cohomology without
misapplying H0 point interpolation. It is the elementary roots-of-unity
form of the monomial apolar/Waring bound. It still needs a suitable
catalyst/fragment extraction to yield a new multiplication exponent.

13.8 Full-flag Cartan/Pieri closure

Let C_(lambda,mu) be the Cartan multiplication tensor of line-bundle
sections on the SL3 full flag variety, with lambda,mu dominant weights.
Its output is the irreducible V_(lambda+mu), and geometric evaluation
as in Section13.1 shows rank exactly dim V_(lambda+mu). For Dynkin
coordinates (p,q), dim V_(p,q)=(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)/2.

The standard trivial rank3 bundle and its dual have tautological Borel
filtrations with line-quotient weights given, up to the usual dual sign
convention, by

  epsilon1=(1,0), epsilon2=(-1,1), epsilon3=(0,-1)

and their negatives. Twist the filtration by L_mu and compare
multiplication by f in H0(L_lambda). If all shifted weights are dominant
(e.g. mu has both coordinates>=1), higher cohomology vanishes, so the
adapted section filtrations give the compatible shared-first-leg relations

  partialsum_1^3 C_(lambda,mu)
    degenerates_1 to partialsum_(1,i) C_(lambda,mu+epsilon_i),

and the analogous relation with all epsilon_i negated. This is a
compatible multiplication-diagram statement; it does not infer a tensor
degeneration merely from the Pieri dimension identity.

After the usual shared-leg p-norm spectral transformation, these become
two discrete superharmonicity inequalities on the triangular weight lattice.
The cubic Weyl dimension function itself satisfies the interior dimension
identities. An exponent improvement would require a nontrivial boundary,
Borel--Weil--Bott, or permutation/cap-product closure. In particular no
claim p<=2/3 follows just from having a rank3 trivial bundle. This is the
current more general representation-theoretic search, still unresolved.

13.9 A PROVED barrier for the relaxed Cartan/Pieri inequalities

The interior Pieri constraints plus dimension bounds do not themselves
force the hoped-for uniform spectral exponent2/3. Here is an explicit
scalar model at p=3/4 satisfying all those relaxed constraints.

For A,B>=1 define x=A-1,y=B-1,c=2^(-4/3), and

  v(x,y)=min(x,y)^(1/3)[min(x,y)+2max(x,y)],
  u(A,B)=A+B-1+c v(x,y),
  Phi(C_(lambda,mu))=u(dim V_lambda,dim V_mu)^(3/4).

Assign every permutation of a Cartan tensor the same value in this model.
It is NOT claimed to be an actual spectral point; in particular arbitrary
cross-family tensor-product/degeneration relations have not been checked.
The statement concerns exactly the relaxed constraints specified below.

First, u is increasing and separately concave. For x<=y, the curved
piece v=x^(4/3)+2y x^(1/3) has

  d^2v/dx^2=(4/9)x^(-5/3)(x-y)<=0,

and joins the affine branch at x=y with the same first derivative.
At x=0 use the continuous extension. The Weyl dimension identity gives
3 D(mu)=sum_i D(mu+epsilon_i), and likewise for the opposite steps.
Jensen's inequality therefore proves ALL the interior shared-leg
superharmonicity inequalities in Section13.8, in either argument.

The vector boundary is exact: u(A,1)=A, so Phi(C_(lambda,0))=A^(3/4),
as required when all three vector-leg exponents are3/4.

The generic evaluation restriction gives diagonal subrank at least
min(A,B). The model satisfies Phi>=min(A,B): if A<=B and A>=2,

  u(A,B)>=2A-1+3c(A-1)^(4/3)>=A^(4/3).

For the last step use Bernoulli's inequality
(A-1)^(4/3)>=A^(4/3)-(4/3)A^(1/3), then 3c>1 and
2A-1>=A>=4c A^(1/3) for A>=2. A=1 is immediate.

The rank upper bound also holds. For nontrivial SL3 representations
A,B>=3, put s=A+B-1>=5. At fixed x+y=s-1,
v(x,y)<=3((s-1)/2)^(4/3), hence

  u(A,B)<=s+[3/2^(8/3)](s-1)^(4/3)<=s^(4/3).

For the final inequality, g(s)=(s^(4/3)-s)/(s-1)^(4/3) is increasing
on s>=5 (differentiate; its sign reduces to t^3-4t+3 at t=s^(1/3)).
Also g(5)>1/2>3/2^(8/3). If A=1 or B=1, rank upper bound is immediate.
Finally D(lambda+mu)>=A+B-1 because the SL3 Weyl dimension polynomial
has nonnegative mixed coefficients after its constant1 is removed.
Thus Phi<=D(lambda+mu), the exact-rank bound.

Consequently the rank-minimal Cartan tensors, exact vector boundary,
diagonal subrank, and ALL interior standard/dual Pieri constraints admit
the uniform p=3/4 model. Cubic Weyl dimension by itself cannot improve
the exponent through these relations. This is an analytic relaxed-model
barrier, not an assertion that no stronger bundle/BWB relation can help.

The ternary quadrupling inequality also passes a finite diagnostic for
this model for every2<=a<=300,1<=b<=300; its minimum is equality at
a=b=2. That finite computation is NOT a universal proof and is not part
of the claimed barrier theorem. Root has a more structured product-of-
binary-barrier candidate that preserves additional old-family data; its
Pieri validity is currently a separate piecewise analytic question.

13.10 A small-Coppersmith--Winograd connection for later extraction

The symmetric central slice

  T_L(f,g,h)=coeff_(L-1,L-1,L-1)(fgh),
  deg f=deg g=deg h=L-1,

has balanced leg dimension L(L+1)/2 and rank<=L^2 by (27). For L=2 its
six monomial terms are the xyz/permanent tensor. Over C this is linearly
equivalent to the small CW_2 tensor, since x0(x1^2+x2^2) splits into a
product of three independent linear forms and polarization preserves the
symmetric tensor equivalence. Its rank4 agrees with the L^2 bound.

This suggests a hybrid polynomial/CW/apolar extraction question for the
higher L family, not a known useful MM restriction. In particular balanced
leg dimensions and near-minimal rank do not by themselves show that an
L-by-L matrix multiplication tensor occurs as a restriction/degeneration.
No such embedding, asymptotic extraction, or improved exponent is proved.


14. ACTIVE NEXT MATHEMATICAL GATE

The constructive result moves the missing work from simultaneous raw
solves to spectral filtering: can q off-axis resolvents, with preparation
and stable combination costs counted, provide a useful sharp threshold
subspace at q substantially below theta^(-1/2), or can a new structured
representation move the center close to the real axis while keeping
the contraction independent of theta? Direct use of a fixed coarse
preconditioner at a shift with c(z)≈theta only gives contractioneta/theta,

accuracy cost and is not a solution.

No general quadratic matrix-product algorithm, no omega=2 claim, and no
general near-input-plus-core LRA theorem has been proved in this file.
The broad objective remains active. The proved outputs include the signed
