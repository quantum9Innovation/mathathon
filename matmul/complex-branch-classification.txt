1. PROPOSED COMPLETE THEOREM

Fix 0<alpha<1, beta=1-alpha, and a,b in C, and define

  f(x)=a (x_+)^alpha+b ((-x)_+)^alpha, x in R.

The power constant H_s(f) is the supremum over finite Hermitian
A!=B of tr|f(B)-f(A)|^s/tr|B-A|^(alpha s). The count supremum
N_(f,r)(L) uses rank(B-A)<=r and ||B-A||=1, as in the checked paper.

Put phi=pi alpha/2 and g_+(x)=(-ix)^alpha, g_-(x)=(ix)^alpha.
There are unique complex numbers kappa,lambda with

  f=kappa g_+ + lambda g_-,
  kappa=( e/cos(phi)+i o/sin(phi))/2,
  lambda=(e/cos(phi)-i o/sin(phi))/2,
  e=(a+b)/2, o=(a-b)/2.

Let m be the number of NONZERO coefficients among kappa,lambda.
Thus m=0 for f=0; m=1 on the two nonzero analytic branch lines
b=a exp(+/- i pi alpha); m=2 everywhere else.

THEOREM (derived below, pending independent review).

  lim_(s->0) s^2 H_s(f)=m/(pi^2 alpha beta),          (1)
  lim_(L->infinity) N_(f,r)(L)/L^2
      =m r/(2 pi^2 alpha beta), fixed r.             (2)

Consequently there is no continuous leading-coefficient law in FIXED
complex a,b. For any nonzero coefficient on both analytic branches,
however small one is, the leading coefficient is the full one.
The intermediate coefficient occurs precisely on the two analytic
lines. Limits in which a,b themselves depend on s are not included.
The zero function is immediate; the proof below assumes m>=1.

2. THE EXACT NONLINEAR CENTERED FACTORIZATION

Fix delta>0 and use the checked density

  rho(x)=c0 exp(x), x<=0; rho(x)=c0 exp(-delta x), x>=0,
  c0=(1+1/delta)^(-1).

Let I be any finite interval, D multiplication by exp(x) on L2(I),
w=sqrt(rho)|I, H=[[0,D],[D,0]], v=(w,0), E=vv*, and
A=H-E/2, B=H+E/2. The increment has norm ||v||^2<=1.
The actual nonlinear Woodbury weight is

  omega_I(t)=[1+M_I(t)^2/4]^(-1),
  M_I(t)=t int_I rho(x)/(exp(2x)+t^2) dx.

There is a positive constant omega0, independent of I and t, with
omega0<=omega_I(t)<=1. Indeed M_I<=M, and

  M(t)<=c0[pi/2+log(1+t^2)/(2t)]<=pi/2+1.

All following factorizations retain THIS ACTUAL omega_I. No weight
is deleted inside an indefinite expression.

Set J=diag(-I,I). The exact centered resolvent formula for g_+ gives

  [g_+(B)-g_+(A)]J=i R,  R>=0,
  g_-(B)-g_-(A)=[g_+(B)-g_+(A)]*.

Therefore the singular values of the correction of f are those of

  T=kappa R-lambda J R J.                            (3)

Put c_alpha=sin(pi alpha)/pi, and, for x in I and y in R, define

  u(x)=sqrt(c_alpha rho(x)) exp(-x/2),
  v_I(y)=exp(alpha y/2) sqrt(omega_I(exp(y))),
  k_+(z)=exp(-z/2)/(2 cosh z),
  k_-(z)=exp(+z/2)/(2 cosh z),
  T_+=M_u C_+ M_v, T_-=M_u C_- M_v,

where C_+,C_- are convolution with k_+,k_- from L2(R) to L2(I).
These products are kernel notation: multiplication by v_I alone is
unbounded on the full line. Define T_+,T_- by their displayed
Hilbert--Schmidt kernels, or on compactly supported functions followed
by their bounded extensions. On finite cells all multipliers are bounded.
Direct substitution t=exp(y) in the rank-one resolvent integral gives

  R=V W W* V*, W=[T_+;T_-], V=diag(I,iI).

The matrix V commutes with J. With

  mathcal W=[W,JW]
     =diag(T_+,T_-) H0, H0=[[1,-1],[1,1]],
  K=diag(kappa,-lambda),

(3) is unitarily equivalent to

  mathcal W K mathcal W*.                            (4)

This is an identity for the actual NONLINEAR fractional correction.
It is not merely its Frechet derivative or a first-order model.

The operators T_+,T_- are Hilbert--Schmidt, with norms bounded
independently of I. To check this, omega_I<=1 and

  int_R exp(alpha y)|k_+(x-y)|^2 dy
       =exp(alpha x) int_R exp(-alpha z)|k_+(z)|^2 dz,

and likewise for k_-. Both last integrals are finite because
0<alpha<1; int_I u(x)^2 exp(alpha x)dx is bounded by the integral
of a constant times exp(alpha x) on the left and exp(-(beta+delta)x)
on the right. In particular the resulting correction is compact.

3. SHARP UPPER BOUND: ONE OR TWO ACTIVE POLES

The checked one-pole formula and its adjoint are

  Delta g_+=-i c_alpha int_0^infinity t^alpha
             [(B+it)^(-1)-(A+it)^(-1)]dt,
  Delta g_-=adjoint(Delta g_+).

At each t, the combined node has rank at most m rank(B-A), since
only the nonzero coefficients kappa,lambda require poles. The log-t
strip has half-width pi/2, and the decay slopes are alpha,beta.
For each fixed d<pi/2, the Gaussian lemma therefore gives

  N_(f,r)(L)
    <=m r L^2/(4 pi d alpha beta)+O_(f,d,r)(L^(7/4)). (5)

The envelope prefactor depends on f but not on matrices or dimension.
Taking d up to pi/2 gives the upper in (2). Rank-one reduction and
layer cake give the upper in (1). This repeats the checked mechanism
with the exact number of active analytic poles.

4. ELEMENTARY FINITE-RANK SEPARATION OF CAUCHY KERNELS

For z>=0,

  k_+(z)=exp(-3z/2)/(1+exp(-2z)),
  k_-(z)=exp(- z/2)/(1+exp(-2z));                    (6)

for z<=0 use reflection and interchange +,-. There is an explicit
degree-n polynomial P_n on [0,1] satisfying

  sup |(1+t)^(-1)-P_n(t)|<=3^(-n-1).

For example, truncate (2/3) sum_(j>=0)[-(2t-1)/3]^j.
Thus on each fixed sign of z, k_+,k_- can be approximated with
error <=3^(-n-1) exp(-gamma |z|), gamma=1/2 or 3/2,
by n+1 separated exponentials exp(-gamma |z|-2j|z|).

Let L be large, ell=sqrt(L), and let I be a union of J consecutive
cells I_j=[j ell,(j+1)ell], with J=O(L). In the applications below
J is actually O(L/ell)=O(sqrt(L)). Compare T_+ with its block diagonal
restriction retaining x,y in the SAME cell and zero outside I.

For x in a fixed output cell and y outside that cell, x-y has fixed
sign on each of its two domain pieces. Formula (6) therefore gives
a rank <=2(n+1) approximation on that output cell. Summing cells
gives rank <=2J(n+1). The error has Hilbert--Schmidt norm <=C 3^(-n),
uniform in I, by the same weighted integrability calculation as in
Section 2. Multiplication by the actual v_I only uses its bound
v_I(y)^2<=exp(alpha y). This includes ALL t variables outside I.

Next replace the within-cell convolution by its periodization

  k_sigma^per(z)=sum_(p in Z) k_sigma(z+p ell).

For -ell<z<ell, every p>=1 term has nonnegative argument, and
every p<=-1 term has nonpositive argument. Summing the exponential
approximants in (6) over p is a geometric series, still of rank at
most 2(n+1) on the cell. The remaining pointwise error is bounded by

  C 3^(-n)[exp(-gamma(ell+z))
                +exp(-gamma'(ell-z))],

with constants uniform for ell>=1. The weighted Hilbert--Schmidt
error, summed over all cells, is at most

  C sqrt(ell) exp(alpha ell/2) 3^(-n).               (7)

For (7), use exp(alpha y)<=exp(alpha ell)exp(alpha x) within each
cell and the uniform integral of u(x)^2 exp(alpha x). A cruder
exp(O(ell)) prefactor would suffice.

Consequently, with A_sigma the weighted BLOCK-PERIODIC operator,

  T_sigma=A_sigma+F_sigma+E_sigma,
  rank F_sigma<=4J(n+1),
  ||E_sigma||<=C sqrt(ell) exp(alpha ell/2)3^(-n).    (8)

These are actual operator-norm/finite-rank bounds. No sign comparison
or removal of a positive integration weight is used. Taking n=C1 L
with any sufficiently large fixed C1 gives error exp(-C2 L), while
J=O(L/ell) gives total discarded rank O(L^2/ell)=o(L^2).

5. EXACT SINGULAR CHANNELS ON A PERIODIC CELL

The Fourier multipliers of C_+,C_- are

  h_+(xi)=(pi/2)sech(pi xi/2-i pi/4),
  h_-(xi)=conj(h_+(xi)),
  |h_+(xi)|^2=|h_-(xi)|^2=(pi^2/2)sech(pi xi).      (9)

These follow by an allowed complex shift in the checked C_1 Fourier
formula. On a circle of length ell the multipliers are evaluated at
xi_k=2pi k/ell. Let H be the positive convolution multiplier |h_+|,
and U=diag(U_+,U_-) its two unitary phase multipliers. All multipliers
in (9) are nonzero, so these phases are genuinely unitary.

Write Hhat=H0/sqrt(2), a constant unitary. On one cell,

  mathcal A=diag(A_+,A_-)
                times H0
    =sqrt(2) M_u U Hhat diag(H M_v,H M_v).

The scalar multipliers M_u,M_v act identically on both copies, and
the common H commutes with the constant matrix Hhat. Therefore

  mathcal A K mathcal A*
    =2 M_u U Hhat diag(kappa S,-lambda S) Hhat* U* M_u,
  S=H M_(v_I^2) H >= (inf_cell v_I^2) H^2.          (10)

Formula (10) is the missing positive singular-value lower. Even for
complex kappa,lambda, the middle block has singular values equal to
|kappa| and |lambda| times the eigenvalues of the SAME positive S.
The phase multipliers and Hhat are unitary. The two outer M_u factors
can lower singular values by at most the factor (inf_cell u)^2,
since their inverses have norm (inf_cell u)^(-1).

Thus EVERY nonzero kappa or lambda supplies one full scalar channel.
No positivity of K, indefinite ALT inequality, or Schatten pinching
is being assumed. In particular the troublesome positive-r pencil
rR-JRJ is handled by (10) without any abstract lower for such pencils.

6. FROM THE CELL MODELS BACK TO THE ACTUAL NONLINEAR CORRECTION

Apply (8) to mathcal W. Write mathcal W=mathcal A+F+E, with
rank F=O(Jn), ||E||<=exp(-C2 L). The Hilbert--Schmidt bound in
Section 2 gives a uniform bound on ||mathcal W||. Hence

  mathcal W K mathcal W*
    =mathcal A K mathcal A*+R_L+Z_L,
  rank R_L<=2 rank F=O(Jn), ||Z_L||<=C exp(-C2 L).   (11)

For example first set Wtilde=mathcal A+F, which is within ||E|| of
mathcal W and therefore has uniformly bounded norm. The difference
Wtilde K Wtilde* - mathcal A K mathcal A* has range contained in
range F plus range(mathcal A K F*), so its rank is <=2 rank F.
This avoids an uncontrolled error factor from a possibly large
finite-rank approximation F by itself.

The compact-operator singular-value inequalities now give

  N_actual(eps)>=N_model(eps+||Z_L||)-rank R_L.      (12)

The model is the direct sum of the independent periodic cells.
The difference R_L may be indefinite and arbitrarily large in norm;
only its finite rank is charged. Its rank is o(L^2) for ell=sqrt L
and n=O(L). Thus the earlier false routes of deleting a weight inside
an indefinite integral or using quasi-Banach pinching are unnecessary.

7. SHARP COUNT LOWER

Use cells covering [-A L,B L], with fixed A>1/alpha and
B>1/(beta+delta), enlarged to cell endpoints. The actual centered
pair is defined on this bounded interval I_L.

Define the piecewise linear envelope

  g(x)=alpha(-x)_++(beta+delta)x_+.

By omega_I>=omega0 and the explicit density, on a cell starting at
x_j one has

  (inf_cell u)^2 (inf_cell v_I)^2
      >=c exp(-g(x_j)-C ell),                       (13)

with fixed positive c,C. For a cell crossing zero this remains true
after increasing C, or that single cell may be omitted.

Let gamma be the minimum absolute value of the ACTIVE coefficients
kappa,lambda; gamma>0 is fixed. Combining (9)--(10), each active
channel on that cell has singular values bounded below, in decreasing
order, by the multiset

  c gamma exp(-g(x_j)-C ell)
        exp(-2pi^2 |k|/ell), k in Z.                 (14)

For a threshold exp(-(1-eta)L), 0<eta<1 fixed, its count is at least

  (ell/pi^2)[(1-eta)L-g(x_j)-C ell-C0]_+-1.

There are m channels. Riemann summation, with error O(L ell), gives

  liminf N_model(exp(-(1-eta)L))/L^2
    >=m(1-eta)^2/(2pi^2)
           [1/alpha+1/(beta+delta)].                (15)

Indeed int_R[(1-eta)L-g(x)]_+dx is exactly
(1-eta)^2 L^2[1/alpha+1/(beta+delta)]/2. The chosen I_L contains
its support, and the O(ell) losses contribute only O(L ell)=o(L^2).

Choose n in (8) so C2>1. In (12) the exponentially small operator
error is then negligible compared with exp(-L), and the larger model
threshold exp(-(1-eta)L) can be used. The rank loss is o(L^2).
Thus (15) is also a lower for the actual correction at exp(-L).

Transfer this bounded-interval construction to finite matrices exactly
as in the checked paper. At each fixed L approximate D by finite step
functions with correction error much smaller than the strict threshold
margin. The map f is continuous, including at zero, so functional
calculus converges in norm. The perturbation remains rank one of norm
<=1. If necessary normalize both finite endpoints AFTER the transfer;
this only increases output singular values. Let eta->0 and delta->0.
This proves the rank-one lower in (2); direct sums prove fixed rank r.

Matching worst-case counts at separately chosen L would not by itself

and an arbitrarily small strict margin gives

  liminf L^(-2)N_actual(xL)
    >=c_delta x^2,
  c_delta=m/(2pi^2)[1/alpha+1/(beta+delta)].          (16)

Use finite transfers with error exp(-(K0+2)L), and set s=1/L.
The denominator is at most one before any normalization. Layer cake

  liminf s^2 H_s(f)>=c_delta int_0^K0 exp(-x)x^2 dx.

Let K0->infinity and delta->0 to get m/(pi^2 alpha beta), matching
the upper. All underlying endpoints remain bounded and finite at
each construction/transfer stage. This proves (1) without assuming a
spectral asymptotic for one fixed infinite operator.

9. AN INDEPENDENT OPEN-CLASS PROOF AND THE EXCEPTION IT LEAVES

There is a short alternative lower for most coefficients. If both
kappa,lambda are nonzero and kappa/lambda is NOT a positive real,
the coefficients kappa and -lambda fit strictly inside one open
half-plane. A scalar phase rotation therefore makes

  Re(kappa R-lambda JRJ)>=c(R+JRJ), c>0.

The right side is positive. Singular values dominate the positive
eigenvalues of a positive real part (by the norm/eigenvalue min--max
principles). Since R+JRJ is block diagonal with the two audited
positive channels, its full leading coefficient follows immediately.

When kappa/lambda is positive real, this argument fails and the pencil
is selfadjoint indefinite. An abstract comparison with R-JRJ is false:
2-by-2 positive R can make rR-JRJ singular for any chosen r!=1.
The periodic block argument above uses the ACTUAL Cauchy factors to
resolve that remaining case, rather than inferring an invalid general
positive-matrix inequality.

10. LINEARIZED MELLIN SYMBOL AS A CHECK, NOT A NONLINEAR TRANSFER

For the symmetric two-sign spectral measure, the linearized divided-
difference kernel has a 2-by-2 convolution symbol. Put u=pi xi,
phi=pi alpha/2, D0=cosh(2u)+cos(2phi), and
c_+=cosh(u+i phi), c_-=cosh(u-i phi). With the probability factor
1/2 from the two signs, the symbol is

  M_f(xi)=(pi/D0)
      [[a sin(2phi), a c_+-b c_-],
       [a c_--b c_+, -b sin(2phi)]].

Its determinant is exactly

  det M_f(xi)=-pi^2/(2D0)
                 (a-b exp(i pi alpha))(a-b exp(-i pi alpha)).

Thus exactly the analytic branch directions lose a Fourier channel.
For other a,b the two large-frequency singular channels have the same
exponential rate. This calculation suggested (1)--(2), but is not their
nonlinear proof; that role is played by (3)--(16) with omega_I intact.
