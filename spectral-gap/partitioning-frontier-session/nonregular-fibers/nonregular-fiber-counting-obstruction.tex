\begin{abstract}
For a random $N$-lift of $K_{r,b}$, reveal all $r$ fibers on one side
and hide the $b$ fibers on the other. A finite counting argument bounds
the success probability of every estimator, even with the base known.
When $\gamma_{r,b}=r\log(b!)-(r-1)b\log b>0$, success decays
exponentially and a typical planted lift has exponentially many
alternative fiber partitions with the same quotient and the same
fiber sizes. The first positive example with $r=3$ is $b=12$.
Thus the regular-base recovery theorem does not extend to all fixed
nonregular bases of minimum degree at least three. This is an
identifiability obstruction, not only a failure of a modular kernel
enumeration or an omission-weight entropy proof. Historical novelty
is not certified.
\end{abstract}

All logarithms are natural. Fix $b>r\ge3$, independently of $N$.
The base $K_{r,b}$ is connected and simple, has minimum degree $r$,
and has $r+b$ vertices. There are no internal edges. Every positive
integer $N$ is an admissible lift size.

\section{The statistical experiment and the genie}
Let $L_1,\ldots,L_r$ be disjoint, fixed, labelled sets of size $N$.
Let $V$ be a disjoint labelled set of size $bN$. The genie reveals
the sets $L_1,\ldots,L_r$, as well as $r,b,N$ and the full base.
Select an \emph{ordered} partition
\[
 \mathcal R=(R_1,\ldots,R_b),\qquad |R_j|=N,
\]
of $V$ uniformly from its $(bN)!/(N!)^b$ possibilities.
Independently for every $i\in[r]$ and $j\in[b]$, choose a uniform
perfect matching between $L_i$ and $R_j$. Their union is $G$.
This is the ordinary random-lift law of $K_{r,b}$ with the hidden
right fiber partition randomized. Matchings are between distinct
old types, so $G$ is simple automatically.

An estimator receives only $G$ and the genie information. Its task
is to output the \emph{unordered} partition $\{R_1,\ldots,R_b\}$.
This removes all $b!$ permutations of the hidden base labels from
the success criterion. Randomization and unlimited computational
work are allowed. Failure in this easier experiment implies failure
of blind recovery of the full planted fiber partition.

The two sides have different observable degrees, $b$ and $r$.
Consequently this experiment already gives the estimator all degree
information, and substantially more: the exact $r$ small-side
fibers. The obstruction is not caused by concealing the degree classes.

\section{Counting marked lifts and possible observations}
Call $(G,\mathcal R)$ a marked lift. For a fixed $\mathcal R$, the
$rb$ matchings determine distinct graphs: the matching between
$L_i$ and $R_j$ is recoverable by restricting $G$ to those two sets.
Thus the number of marked lifts is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
 T_N=\frac{(bN)!}{(N!)^b}(N!)^{rb}
     =(bN)!(N!)^{(r-1)b}.
\end{equation}
Every marked lift has probability $1/T_N$ in the experiment.

Every observed graph has one neighbor in each $L_i$ at every
vertex of $V$, and each vertex in $L_i$ has $b$ neighbors in $V$.
It is therefore specified uniquely by $r$ maps
\[
 f_i:V\longrightarrow L_i,\qquad |f_i^{-1}(v)|=b
       \quad(v\in L_i).
\]
There are exactly $(bN)!/(b!)^N$ such maps for a given $i$.
The number of graphs with these degree and genie constraints is
therefore exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:U}
 U_N=\left[\frac{(bN)!}{(b!)^N}\right]^r.
\end{equation}
Some maps give graphs that have no compatible right fiber partition.
The observation support therefore has cardinality at most $U_N$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform posterior and fiber-count bound]\label{lem:count}
For a supported observation $G$, let $Z(G)$ be the number of its
compatible \emph{ordered} right partitions, and let
$W(G)=Z(G)/b!$ count compatible unordered right partitions.
Every estimator $\widehat{\mathcal R}$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 \Prob(\widehat{\mathcal R}=\{R_1,\ldots,R_b\})
 &\le\E\frac1{W(G)}
 =\frac{b!\,|\operatorname{supp}(G)|}{T_N}
 \le s_N,\label{eq:success}\\
 s_N&:=\frac{b!\,[(bN)!]^{r-1}}
              {(b!)^{rN}(N!)^{(r-1)b}}.\label{eq:sN}
\end{align}
Moreover, for every real $t\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smallW}
 \Prob(W(G)\le t)\le t\,s_N.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Given $G$, every compatible ordered partition has exactly one marked
lift and the same probability $1/T_N$. Hence the conditional posterior
is uniform on its $Z(G)$ ordered partitions, or equivalently on
its $W(G)$ unordered partitions. A randomized estimator cannot have
conditional success greater than $1/W(G)$. Also
$\Prob(G)=Z(G)/T_N$. Summing gives
\[
 \E\frac1{W(G)}
 =\sum_{G\in\operatorname{supp}}
      \frac{Z(G)}{T_N}\frac{b!}{Z(G)}
 =\frac{b!\,|\operatorname{supp}(G)|}{T_N}.
\]
Insert \eqref{eq:T}--\eqref{eq:U} to obtain
\eqref{eq:success}--\eqref{eq:sN}. Finally, on $W\le t$,
$1/W\ge1/t$, so Markov's inequality applied to $1/W$ proves
\eqref{eq:smallW}. Equivalently one can count at most $b!t$
marked lifts per such observation.
\end{proof}

\section{Exponential nonrecoverability and many alternative fibers}
Put
\[
 \gamma_{r,b}=r\log(b!)-(r-1)b\log b,
 \qquad \theta_{r,b}=\frac{(r-1)(b-1)}2.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Nonregular blind-recovery obstruction]\label{thm:main}
If $\gamma_{r,b}>0$, every estimator in the genie experiment has
success probability at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:asympt}
 s_N=b!\,b^{(r-1)/2}(2\pi N)^{-\theta_{r,b}}
            e^{-\gamma_{r,b}N}(1+O_{r,b}(N^{-1})).
\end{equation}
For every fixed $0<c<\gamma_{r,b}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:many}
 \Prob(W(G)\le e^{cN})
 \le O_{r,b}(N^{-\theta_{r,b}})
                 e^{-(\gamma_{r,b}-c)N}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The assertion about $W(G)$ holds also for every \emph{fixed}
planted right partition with the independent uniform lift matchings.
In particular a typical such fixed-partition lift has exponentially
many distinct equitable partitions into the same $r+b$ size-$N$
classes and with the same quotient $K_{r,b}$, even while every
small-side fiber is kept fixed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Stirling's formula applied to \eqref{eq:sN} gives
\begin{align*}
 s_N&=b!\,
  \frac{(2\pi bN)^{(r-1)/2}(bN/e)^{(r-1)bN}}
       {(2\pi N)^{(r-1)b/2}(N/e)^{(r-1)bN}(b!)^{rN}}
            (1+O_{r,b}(N^{-1}))\\
 &=b!\,b^{(r-1)/2}(2\pi N)^{-\theta_{r,b}}
      \left[\frac{b^{(r-1)b}}{(b!)^r}\right]^N
            (1+O_{r,b}(N^{-1})),
\end{align*}
which is \eqref{eq:asympt}. Lemma~\ref{lem:count} proves the
success bound and, with $t=e^{cN}$, proves \eqref{eq:many}.

For the fixed-partition claim, the permutation group of $V$ acts
transitively on its ordered balanced right partitions. Relabelling
$V$ takes the conditional lift law of any fixed partition to that
of any other, and $W(G)$ is invariant under that relabelling.
Thus the distribution of $W$ is the same for every fixed planted
partition and for their uniform mixture. This transfers
\eqref{eq:many} to each fixed-partition lift law.

Each compatible right partition has $b$ nonempty classes of size
$N$; adjoining the revealed $L_i$ gives an equitable partition with
quotient exactly $K_{r,b}$. Two distinct such partitions cannot be
coarsenings of one another because they have the same number of
nonempty classes. They are distinct even after permutations of
the hidden base labels, since $W$ already takes that quotient.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A fifteen-vertex base of minimum degree three]
For $H=K_{3,12}$,
\[
 \gamma_{3,12}=3\log(12!)-24\log12
                 \approx0.32388389207363844>0.
\]
Every blind fiber estimator has exponentially small success under
the stated hidden-partition model, even with the base and the three
degree-twelve fibers revealed. With high probability a random
lift of this fixed base has exponentially many distinct compatible
fifteen-class fiber partitions. The maximum degree is only twelve.
\end{corollary}
The sign can be certified with integers alone:
$(12!)^3>12^{24}$. Checking the finitely many integers $3\le b\le11$
gives $(b!)^3\le b^{2b}$, so $b=12$ is the first positive-rate
case in this \emph{counting family} with $r=3$.
This is not a claim of a sharp recovery threshold at $b=12$;
a nonpositive counting exponent does not establish recoverability.
For every fixed $r$, positive examples exist for sufficiently large
$b$, because
\[
 \gamma_{r,b}=b\log b-rb+\tfrac r2\log(2\pi b)+O_r(b^{-1}).
\]

\section{Connected lifts and precise scope}
The obstruction persists if the observed lift is required to be
connected. Here is a direct check. A spanning-tree change of fiber
coordinates sets all tree-edge permutations to the identity and
leaves
\[
 \beta=|E(H)|-|V(H)|+1=(r-1)(b-1)
\]
independent uniform permutations on the non-tree edges. The lift is
connected precisely when these generate a transitive action on
$[N]$. If it is disconnected, a nonempty subset of size at most
$N/2$ is preserved by all $\beta$ permutations. Therefore
\[
 \Prob(G\text{ disconnected})
 \le\sum_{s=1}^{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}\binom Ns^{1-\beta}
 =O_{r,b}(N^{1-\beta})=o(1).
\]
For the last estimate, separate $s=1$ and bound the remaining at
most $N/2$ terms using $\binom Ns\ge\binom N2$.
Conditioning the success bound or \eqref{eq:many} on connectedness
therefore costs only $1+o(1)$.

The estimator impossibility is explicitly a hidden-partition or
relabelling-invariant statement. For a publicly fixed canonical
partition, a nonblind estimator could simply print that partition
without looking at the graph; that is not the inference experiment
considered here. In contrast, the structural multiplicity statement
\eqref{eq:many} does hold for every fixed planted partition and
directly refutes unique-finest-partition rigidity in the bounded
class family.

The base stays fixed, all fibers have the same size, and all equations
hold exactly. The proof does not rely on spectral degeneracy or the
separate exponential modular kernel phenomenon for $K_{3,4}$.
It rules out the general extension
to \emph{all} fixed nonregular bases of minimum degree three; it
does not classify which nonregular bases remain recoverable.
The weighted omission criterion can still prove positive results
for a restricted class of bases. Failure of that criterion alone
does not imply the information obstruction proved here.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
This is a finite counting argument, using uniform marked lifts,
posterior multiplicity, Stirling's formula and the elementary
spanning-tree permutation description of a lift. A targeted primary
literature comparison is retained separately. No assertion of
historical firstness is made.
