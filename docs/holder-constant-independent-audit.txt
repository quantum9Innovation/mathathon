1. EXACT STATEMENT AND NOTATION
------------------------------
Fix alpha in (0,1), throughout held fixed as s decreases to zero. Let

 f_e(x)=|x|^alpha,   f_o(x)=sgn(x)|x|^alpha,   f_o(0)=0.

For either map let K_(s,alpha) be the least dimension-independent
constant for all finite Hermitian A,B in

 ||f(A)-f(B)||_(S_s)
    <= K_(s,alpha) ||A-B||_(S_(alpha*s))^alpha.

The audited conclusion is

 c_alpha/s^2 <= K_(s,alpha)^s <= C_alpha/s^2,  0<s<=1.       (1)

Consequently, with natural logarithms,

 log K_(s,alpha)=(2/s)log(1/s)+O_alpha(1/s).

The constants in (1) may depend on fixed alpha. Neither their optimal
values nor any assertion uniform in alpha approaching 0 or 1 is proved.
For Ricard's source exponent p=alpha*s, the logarithmic statement is
log C_(p,alpha)=(2alpha/p)log(1/p)+O_alpha(1/p).

2. WHOLE-LINE RANK-ONE UPPER BOUND: COMPLETE CHECK
------------------------------------------------
Use the scalar spectral-calculus integrals

 f_e(H)=c_e int_0^infinity t^(alpha-1) H^2/(H^2+t^2) dt,
 c_e=2 sin(pi*alpha/2)/pi,

 f_o(H)=c_o int_0^infinity t^alpha H/(H^2+t^2) dt,
 c_o=2 cos(pi*alpha/2)/pi.

Both constants check by substitution t=|x|u in the scalar integrals;
the two resulting beta integrals are pi/(2 sin(pi*alpha/2)) and
pi/(2 cos(pi*alpha/2)), respectively. Zero eigenvalues cause no issue.

Let E=A-B have rank r and norm a>0. Write R_H(z)=(H-zI)^(-1), and

 D_e(t)=A^2/(A^2+t^2)-B^2/(B^2+t^2),
 D_o(t)=t A/(A^2+t^2)-t B/(B^2+t^2).

The exact identities, including signs, are

 D_e(t)=-t/(2i) [(R_A(it)-R_B(it))
                              -(R_A(-it)-R_B(-it))],
 D_o(t)= t/2   [(R_A(it)-R_B(it))
                              +(R_A(-it)-R_B(-it))].

The resolvent difference has rank at most r and norm at most
a/dist(z,R)^2. Thus each D has rank at most 2r. For real t>0,

 ||D_e(t)||, ||D_o(t)|| <= min(1,a/t).                    (2)

For the first bound, the even scalar rational function has range [0,1]
and the odd rational function t*x/(x^2+t^2) has range [-1/2,1/2].
The resolvent identities give the second bound with constant exactly 1.

For COMPLEX t with |arg t|<=d<pi/2, the corresponding uniform bounds
hold with a factor depending only on d. It is helpful to make the
constant-envelope argument explicit: for real x,

 |x^2+t^2| >= cos(d)(x^2+|t|^2).

This directly bounds each even rational function by sec(d), and each
odd rational function by sec(d)/2. The two shifted resolvents are at
distance at least |t|cos(d) from the real line, so their differences
give the other bound a/(|t|cos(d)^2). The distance argument alone does
not bound a numerator involving an unbounded x; the displayed sector
inequality supplies that missing explicit detail in a short proof.

Put t=a exp(y). In both cases the DIFFERENCE integral becomes

 int_R F(y) dy,   F(y)=c_(e or o) t^alpha D_(e or o)(t).

As a function of y, F is analytic for |Im y|<pi/2, and, for any fixed
d<pi/2, satisfies on its closed substrip

 ||F(x+i eta)|| <= C_(alpha,d) a^alpha
                       min(exp(alpha*x),exp(-(1-alpha)*x)).    (3)

All bounds are independent of dimension and of both endpoint spectra.
The boundary L1 norms and the vertical-side decay required by the
strip trapezoid estimate follow directly from (3).

Apply the scalar strip-contour trapezoid bound to u*F(y)v, for arbitrary
unit vectors u,v. Its uniform constant gives an OPERATOR-NORM error,
bounded by C_alpha a^alpha/(exp(2*pi*d/h)-1). Taking the supremum over
u,v is legitimate after this scalar bound. No Schatten integration is
used here.

For n>=1 choose h=n^(-1/2) and retain nodes jh, -n<=j<=n. The geometric
tails satisfy, for beta>0,

 h sum_(j>n) exp(-beta*j*h)
       <= beta^(-1) exp(-beta*n*h).

Therefore the finite sum Q_n has

 rank(Q_n)<=2r(2n+1),
 ||f(A)-f(B)-Q_n|| <= C_alpha a^alpha exp(-c_alpha sqrt(n)). (4)

The integral of (3) also bounds the norm of the exact difference by
C_alpha a^alpha. Equation (4), the best-rank characterization of
singular values, and the initial norm bound give, after changing only
alpha-dependent constants,

 sigma_j(f(A)-f(B))
       <= C_alpha a^alpha exp(-c_alpha sqrt(j/r)),  j>=1.    (5)

The dimension cap simply sets subsequent singular values to zero.

For rank one, sum the s-th powers in (5). The elementary decreasing-
function integral bound gives

 sum_(j>=0) exp(-c_alpha*s*sqrt(j))
          <=1+2/(c_alpha^2*s^2).

Since C_alpha^s<=max(1,C_alpha) for 0<s<=1,

 ||f(A)-f(B)||_(S_s)^s <= C'_alpha s^(-2) a^(alpha*s)
             whenever rank(A-B)=1.                         (6)

For negative rank-one perturbations the same calculation applies:
neither endpoint needs to be positive in (2)--(6).

3. TELESCOPE AND THE EXACT QUASI-TRIANGLE CONSTANT
------------------------------------------------
For an arbitrary finite Hermitian E=B-A, take its ordinary spectral
decomposition E=sum_j t_j v_jv_j*, with the v_j orthonormal and t_j real.
Set A_0=A, A_j=A+sum_(i<=j) t_i v_iv_i*. Each A_j is Hermitian.
The Schatten s-power triangle inequality, valid with constant exactly 1
for 0<s<=1, says

 ||sum_j T_j||_(S_s)^s <= sum_j ||T_j||_(S_s)^s.

Applying it to the FINITE telescope and (6) gives

 ||f(B)-f(A)||_(S_s)^s
    <=C_alpha s^(-2) sum_j |t_j|^(alpha*s)
     =C_alpha s^(-2) ||E||_(S_(alpha*s))^(alpha*s).

There is no count-of-summands loss, no rank factor, and no application
of a quasi-norm triangle inequality to a continuum integral.

For PSD powers alone, signed spectral increments need not preserve PSD.
Use the common upper endpoint C=A+E_+=B+E_- instead. The two positive
rank-one paths A to C and B to C remain PSD; their combined update sum
is exactly sum_j |t_j|^(alpha*s). This also has no numerical loss.

4. ALL-ALPHA LOWER BOUND: NORMALIZATION AND AN EXACT SIMPLIFICATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The nonlinear remainder argument in the original working notes checks
algebraically, including its beta integral and final exponent. It can
be replaced by the following exact comparison, which is simpler and
improves the displayed lower exponent.

If H>=I and E=d vv*>=0 is rank one, put a=||E||. Woodbury gives

 (H+t)^(-1)-(H+E+t)^(-1)
   = d R_H(t) vv* R_H(t)/(1+d v*R_H(t)v).

The derivative L=D(H^alpha)[E] has the fractional-power integral with
the same numerator and no denominator. Since

 0<=d v*R_H(t)v<=a/(1+t)<=a,

the true correction Delta=(H+E)^alpha-H^alpha satisfies the exact
Loewner bounds

             L/(1+a) <= Delta <= L.                       (7)

In fact the same comparison holds for arbitrary positive E, by writing
the resolvent difference using T=R_H(t)^(1/2) E R_H(t)^(1/2) and using
T/(1+a)<=T(I+T)^(-1)<=T. Rank one is all the lower proof needs.

Now take any integer k>=1, a spacing h>0, beta=1-alpha, indices
0<=i<k, and

 a_i=exp(h*i),    H=diag(a_i),
 v_i=alpha^(-1/2) a_i^(beta/2),
 N=||v||^2,      E=vv*/N.

Thus H>=I, E>=0 has rank one, and ||E||=1. The exact divided-difference
formula gives

 L=K/N,   K_ij=k_alpha(h*(i-j)),
 k_alpha(x)=sinh(alpha*x/2)/(alpha*sinh(x/2)), k_alpha(0)=1.

The normalization alpha^(-1/2) is correct: the factor alpha^(-1) from
v_iv_j cancels the alpha in the diagonal derivative, and the powers of
a_i*a_j cancel the corresponding divided-difference powers.

With Fourier transform hat k(omega)=int_R k(x)exp(-i omega x) dx,

 hat k_alpha(omega)
   =(2*pi/alpha) sin(pi*alpha)/(cosh(2*pi*omega)+cos(pi*alpha)). (8)

An independent normalization check is alpha=1/2: k_(1/2)(x)=sech(x/4),
whose transform is 4*pi*sech(2*pi*omega), exactly (8).
The transform is strictly positive and exponentially decaying.

Poisson summation gives the absolutely convergent periodic symbol

 F_h(theta)=h^(-1) sum_(ell in Z)
                         hat k_alpha((theta+2*pi*ell)/h).

For -pi<=theta<=pi, keep the nonnegative ell=0 term and use
cosh(2*pi*theta/h)+cos(pi*alpha)<=2 exp(2*pi^2/h). Then

 F_h(theta)>=c0 h^(-1) exp(-2*pi^2/h),
 c0=pi sin(pi*alpha)/alpha.

For any vector z in C^k, integrate F_h(theta) times
|sum_i z_i exp(i*i*theta)|^2/(2*pi). This is exactly z*K*z, and Parseval
therefore proves the FINITE matrix bound

 lambda_min(K)>=c0 h^(-1) exp(-2*pi^2/h).               (9)

No infinite-section eigenvalue limit or boundary asymptotics is needed.
Also, because exp(beta*h)-1>=beta*h,

 N=alpha^(-1) [exp(beta*h*k)-1]/[exp(beta*h)-1]
       <= exp(beta*h*k)/(alpha*beta*h).                (10)

Equations (7)--(10) yield, for EVERY positive h,

 lambda_min(Delta)
   >= c_* exp(-2*pi^2/h-beta*h*k),
 c_*=pi(1-alpha)sin(pi*alpha)/2,
                                                        (11)

Root's optimization h=pi*sqrt(2/(beta*k)) minimizes the exponent and
gives the clean all-k bound

 lambda_min(Delta)>=c_* exp(-C_* sqrt(k)),
 C_*=2*pi*sqrt(2(1-alpha)).                            (12)

Every one of the k output singular values has this lower bound, and
every Schatten norm of the rank-one input E equals 1. Hence

 K_(s,alpha)^s >= k c_*^s exp(-C_* s sqrt(k)).

Choose k=ceil(s^(-2)). For 0<s<=1, k>=s^(-2), s*sqrt(k)<=sqrt(2),
and c_*^s>=min(1,c_*). This proves the lower half of (1), for the PSD
map and therefore for BOTH whole-line maps simultaneously. Optimizing
k asymptotically as k~4/(C_*^2*s^2) also yields the explicit consequence

 liminf_(s->0) s^2 K_(s,alpha)^s
                  >=1/[2*pi^2*(1-alpha)*exp(2)].

The same lower family shows necessary rank Omega_alpha(log^2(1/eps))
at error eps||E||^alpha. Direct sums give the matching linear rank-r
factor. This is an output-rank lower bound, not an arithmetic or oracle
lower bound.

5. ORIGINAL NONLINEAR REMAINDER: ADVERSARIAL CROSS-CHECK
-----------------------------------------------------
For completeness, the original proof is independently valid. Its exact
identity is

 L-Delta=c_alpha int_0^infinity
       t^alpha R_H(t) E R_H(t) E R_(H+E)(t) dt.

The norm is at most alpha(1-alpha)||E||^2/2 when H>=I, since

 int_0^infinity t^alpha/(1+t)^3 dt
     =pi*alpha*(1-alpha)/(2 sin(pi*alpha)).

With D0=1/(alpha(1-alpha)), R0=1/(2alpha(1-alpha)),
d0=min(1,c0/(4R0)) and
d=(d0/q)exp(-(2*pi^2+2(1-alpha))*q), its remainder is at most one
quarter of the displayed derivative eigenvalue lower bound. Dividing
by ||E||^alpha gives the exponent

 (1-alpha)(2*pi^2+2(1-alpha))+2*pi^2+alpha(1-alpha)
       =(2-alpha)(2*pi^2+1-alpha),

as asserted in the original notes. Individual resolvent-product
integrands in the remainder identity need not be Hermitian; norm
submultiplicativity is sufficient, and the total Loewner nonnegativity
comes from operator concavity. Equation (7) makes these extra steps
unnecessary for this family.

6. DILATION: EXACT EQUALITY OF ODD-MAP BEST CONSTANTS
--------------------------------------------------
For a rectangular matrix X, let

 D(X)=[0 X; X* 0],    M_alpha(X)=U|X|^alpha, X=U|X|.

The odd functional calculus gives

 f_o(D(X))=[0 M_alpha(X); M_alpha(X)* 0].

Each singular value of X is repeated twice in D(X), with any rectangular
zero padding irrelevant. Thus, AFTER raising the Holder inequality to s,
both its output Schatten power sum and its input Schatten power sum
gain exactly the same factor 2. Dilation proves that the best constant
for all rectangular matrices is at most the Hermitian odd-map constant.
The reverse inequality holds because Hermitian matrices are a subset
of square matrices and M_alpha(H)=f_o(H). The two best constants are
therefore EXACTLY EQUAL, not merely of the same asymptotic order.

7. SEMIFINITE EXTENSION: AUDITED PROOF ROUTE
------------------------------------------
Let (M,tau) have a faithful normal semifinite trace, and let A,B be
self-adjoint tau-measurable operators. The following is a legitimate
extension route; no type III extension is claimed here.

First suppose E=B-A=tP, where P is a finite-trace projection and
r=tau(P)<infinity. Every resolvent product R_A(z) E R_B(z) has range
support of trace at most r: multiplication cannot increase the trace
dimension of a support, by the polar-decomposition equivalence of left
and right supports. Consequently each rational node above has range
support of trace at most 2r, and a q-node sum has trace rank at most 2rq.

The operator-norm integral and quadrature bounds are unchanged. For
unbounded endpoints, identify the norm-convergent DIFFERENCE integral
with f(A)-f(B) by taking truncated scalar integrals: on bounded spectral
sets they converge uniformly, and tau-measurability makes the remaining
spectral tails converge to zero in measure. The difference integrals
also converge in norm by the uniform envelope. The two limits agree.

The generalized singular value function consequently satisfies

 mu_u(f(A)-f(B)) <= C_alpha |t|^alpha
                           exp(-c_alpha sqrt(u/r)), u>0,

after adjusting constants for small u. Integrating its s-th powers gives

 ||f(A)-f(B)||_(L_s)^s
       <=C_alpha s^(-2) tau(P)|t|^(alpha*s).

For finite simple Hermitian E=sum_j t_j P_j with finite-trace mutually
orthogonal P_j, use the same finite telescope. All sums lie in the
*-algebra of tau-measurable operators. For E in L_(alpha*s), take bounded
finite-trace simple spectral approximants E_n with |E_n|<=|E| and
E_n converging in measure to E. Then A+E_n converges in measure to B.
Continuity of continuous self-adjoint functional calculus in measure,
followed by the Fatou property of L_s, passes the same inequality to E.

For PSD powers, preserve positivity by the common upper endpoint
A+E_+=B+E_- and approximate positive paths separately. An arbitrary
signed approximation A+E_n does not necessarily remain PSD.

Finite matrices supply the same lower bound, so the order in (1) also
holds for the best uniform semifinite-trace constants. This observation
does not establish equality of their exact constants with matrix ones.

difference of fractional powers of self-adjoint operators. Original
Russian primary PDF, printed pp.3--10:
https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=ivm&option_lang=eng&paperid=6330&what=fullt

Section 3, Lemma 3, formula (3.2) gives root-exponential singular-value
decay for the rank-one resolvent integral Q_h from h to infinity, with
size factor h^(alpha-1). Its proof constructs rank m^2 approximants with
error exp(-c m), formula (3.10). This already implies the PSD rank-one
upper bound: the omitted interval (0,h) has norm O_alpha(h^alpha), while
the tail has singular values O_alpha(a h^(alpha-1)exp(-c sqrt(n))).
Taking h=a exp(-c sqrt(n)) balances them. Summation and finite positive
telescoping give K_(s,alpha)^s=O_alpha(s^-2). Theorem 4 and the remainder
of the article give upper spectral inequalities. No matching lower
example or sharp small-s growth is present in the inspected article.

Theorems 3.2 and 3.4 already establish both whole-line Holder maps for

https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.09708

not rule out a sharper specialization or reorganization of their proof.
