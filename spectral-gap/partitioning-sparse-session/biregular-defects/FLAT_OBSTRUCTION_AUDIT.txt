1. Graph construction and complete adjacency spectrum
----------------------------------------------------
The construction has no loops or repeated edges: its F edges stay inside
one(i,s) copy, while its R edges join different i copies and preserve a.
Each vertex has q internal neighbors and two neighbors per R neighbor,
hence degree d=q+2r. Because F and R are connected and r>=1, the graph
is connected: one can move within F, along R, and can change the s
coordinate by going to an R neighbor and returning through the other
matching choice. The sum-mod2 coloring from the two factor colors proves
bipartiteness whenever both factors are bipartite.

I reconstructed the tensor identity
  A = I_k tensor I_2 tensor A_F + A_R tensor J_2 tensor I_h.
The s-antisymmetric sector has dimension kh and eigenvalues lambda_j(F),
each repeated k times. The symmetric sector has dimension kh and has
eigenvalues lambda_j(F)+2 theta_i(R). These two sectors exhaust N=2hk
dimensions and prove the displayed full spectrum with multiplicities.

The vectors e_i have disjoint supports of size2h and are normalized.
They are q-eigenvectors because J_2 annihilates the antisymmetric sign
and F's constant vector has eigenvalue q. Their projector's diagonal is
1/(2h)=k/N everywhere, giving exactly mu=1. Trace(P)=k proves mu>=1
for any rank-k projector under this normalization. A normalized real
Hadamard rotation gives entries +/-1/sqrtN and IPR1/N, the exact minimum
by Cauchy--Schwarz. This concerns a displayed basis, not every possible
basis of the repeated eigenspace; the source correctly distinguishes this.

For F=K_{q,q}, q>2r and nonsingular A_R, no symmetric-sector eigenvalue
can equal q: the three cases would require theta=0,theta=q,theta=q/2.
The first is excluded by nonsingularity and the others by |theta|<=r.
Only the simple top F eigenvalue in each antisymmetric cell contributes.
Thus the ENTIRE q-eigenspace is exactly E of rank k, as claimed.

Since the graph is d-regular its universal cover is the d-regular tree.
The outlier and BH calculation is exact:
  H(sqrt(d-1))|E = [2(d-1)-q sqrt(d-1)]I.
It is strictly negative when q>2sqrt(d-1). No random-graph threshold is
being inferred from this deterministic observation.

2. Expansion formula
--------------------
The top antisymmetric eigenvalue is q. The unique top symmetric eigenvalue
is q+2r=d. Its next-largest value is
  max{q+2 lambda_2(R), lambda_2(F)+2r}.
Therefore d minus the largest nonprincipal adjacency eigenvalue equals
  min{2r,2 gamma_R,gamma_F}.
This proves the exact ordinary Laplacian gap. Bipartite negative endpoints
do not interfere: the ordinary gap uses the largest nonprincipal adjacency
eigenvalue, not the maximum absolute value. The source does not claim an
absolute adjacency gap for the bipartite family.

3. Statistical ambiguity and exact Bayes overlap
-----------------------------------------------
For x_sigma(i,s,a)=s sigma_i, each neighboring R cell supplies exactly one
same-label and one opposite-label neighbor, while all q F neighbors have
the same label. Thus every hidden labeling is balanced and has exact
within/across degrees(q+r,r), independent of sigma. There are2^(k-1)
distinct unordered bisections. These statements hold pointwise, before
any random permutation.

Conditioning on the final permutation, observed graph, covering coordinates
when present, and the estimator's independent random seed leaves all k
signs independent and uniform. Their graph was sampled independently of
these signs. The overlap numerator has the form sum_i w_i sigma_i with
|w_i|<=2h. The expected absolute sum is separately convex in each w_i.
Moving coordinates to endpoints cannot decrease its maximum; every box
vertex has the same distribution, namely2h times an unweighted Rademacher
sum. Hence the exact oracle optimum is E|sum sigma_i|/k and is attained
by the canonical orientation estimate. An estimator without the oracle
cannot do better. This also covers randomized estimators by conditioning.

For even k, independently summing the binomial law yields
  E|sum sigma_i|/k = 2^-k binomial(k,k/2).
Stirling gives sqrt(2/(pi k)), while Cauchy--Schwarz gives the simpler
upper bound1/sqrtk. The two-sided Hoeffding bound with sum w_i^2<=4h^2 k
is exactly2exp(-k delta^2/2). Thus the stated weak-recovery impossibility
is valid as k tends to infinity, even with the stronger oracle.

The uniformly permuted prior is uniform on balanced vertex labelings:
every fixed x_sigma is balanced and uniform permutation acts transitively
on such vectors. Conditional on a balanced labeling, the graph law is
invariant under permutations within each label class; the deterministic
degrees then give the exact one-edge marginals a/(N/2-1),b/(N/2).
The model nevertheless has strong edge correlations. It is not the
independent-edge SBM or uniform RSBM, and the source explicitly says so.

4. Explicit nonsingular degree29 expander family
----------------------------------------------

into symmetric and antisymmetric pair sectors gives eigenvalues2theta+1
and -1, with the latter repeated|S_m| times. A zero would force the
integer adjacency matrix of S_m to have rational eigenvalue-1/2, impossible
by the rational-root theorem for its monic characteristic polynomial.
Thus B_m is nonsingular. Its minimum eigenvalue is at least-5, and its
largest nonprincipal eigenvalue is at most7-2gamma; the repeated-1 sector

B_m is connected and contains a triangle: use one doubled internal edge
and a vertex in a neighboring doubled cell. Its bipartite double cover

6. Growing-base independent random-lift corollary
-----------------------------------------------
The existing input has coefficient/base dimension D, lift degree n, and
maximum degree d, with no dependence on the number of permutations:
  Pr{||A_new||>=rho(T)+epsilon} <= C(D/n)(d/epsilon)^9.
It is uniform for each deterministic connected loopless base selected
BEFORE the independent permutations. The present bases satisfy all these
hypotheses: simple connected bipartite29-regular, D_m=60k_m=o(n_m).
The generator-independent feature is essential since the number of base
edges grows with k_m. No fixed-base result alone is substituted here.

Choose epsilon0=(15-2sqrt28)/2>0. This lies within the input's admissible
range epsilon<29-2sqrt28. Since both d and epsilon0 are fixed, the failure
bound becomes C'k_m/n_m. Its complement has ||A_new||<15. All old adjacency
eigenvalues are exactly those of the base, so the previously proved entire
base15-eigenspace remains the ENTIRE lifted15-eigenspace; no new mode at15
is possible. Each normalized old vector is divided by sqrt(n_m) on each
fiber, giving rank k_m, diagonal1/(60n_m)=k_m/M_m, incoherence one and a
flat Hadamard eigenbasis with entries +/-1/sqrt(M_m).

Exact equitable neighbor counts are preserved by every covering, with
within/across degrees(22,7). The hidden k_m signs are independent of all
lift matchings. Conditional on the entire sampled graph and oracle data,
the previous Bayes argument applies with half-cell size30n_m. Thus its
exact optimum and Hoeffding tail survive independent lifting unchanged.

The base29 eigenvalue is simple and its other old eigenvalues are at most
29-c for uniform c>0. On the same fixed-epsilon0 event, all new eigenvalues
are<15. The lifted Laplacian gap is therefore at least min{c,14}>0.
An extra component would create an additional29 eigenvalue, impossible on
that event. Simplicity and bipartiteness hold for EVERY lift of a simple
bipartite base. For each fixed epsilon>0, the same uniform tail bound tends
to zero, proving new-space almost-Ramanujan convergence in probability.
The O(k_m/n_m) rate is for the fixed cutoff<15; the source does not assert
that rate for an unspecified vanishing spectral error. Conditioning on
the fixed-cutoff event does not affect the convergence-in-probability claim
since that event has probability tending to one.

For a fixed-radius ball, only C_29,ell matching entries are exposed, and
each fresh endpoint is uniform among at least n_m-C_29,ell unused sheets
in its base fiber. There are at most C_29,ell previously exposed vertices,
so the total collision probability is O(1/n_m), uniformly in base size.
Averaging over roots and Markov's inequality give the claimed local weak
limit, even though the base contains many short cycles. Because m grows,
k_m tends to infinity, while k_m/M_m=1/(60n_m) tends to zero. Choosing n_m
arbitrarily large permits the rank to diverge arbitrarily slowly relative
to total size. The rank is NOT fixed, and the source expressly retains this
limitation.
