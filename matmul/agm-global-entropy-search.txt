1. PROPOSED THEOREM AND SCOPE
=============================

Let f(x)=x log x-x+1 and b(x)=(x-1)^2/2. For every 0<eta<1 there
are explicit normalized operator convex algebraic generators f^-_eta,
f^+_eta satisfying globally on x>0

 0<=f^-_eta(x)<=f(x)<=f^+_eta(x)=f^-_eta(x)+eta*b(x).    (1.1)

Their matrix perspectives in dimension N have lifts using
O(log(2+log(1/eta))) PSD blocks of order at most2N. Their STANDARD
PETZ divergences have lifts with that NUMBER of blocks of order at
most2N^2, plus one order2N block for the upper Chi2 correction.

Moreover the lift can use rational coefficients of bit length
O(log(1/eta)), including exact value/slope normalization. Section9
gives a primal-dual amplification device avoiding the pitfall of
rounding a tangent constant and destroying the double zero at1.
It introduces only a constant multiple of the original block count.

For positive definite matrices A,B, the resulting divergences obey

 0<=D^-(A||B)<=D(A||B)<=D^+(A||B),
 D^+-D^-=eta*Tr[(A-B)B^(-1)(A-B)]/2,                  (1.2)

where D=Tr[A(logA-logB)]-TrA+TrB; trace-one inputs give Umegaki
relative entropy. Both endpoints are jointly convex and SDP
representable. The denominator is Chi2, which can be arbitrarily
large; there is no uniform bounded absolute-error claim for all states.

The underlying AGM/elliptic logarithm approximation is CLASSICAL:

3. CLASSICAL ELLIPTIC INPUT WITH SUFFICIENT EXPLICIT BOUNDS
===========================================================

Define, using the complementary modulus convention,

 K(q)=integral_0^infty dt/sqrt((t^2+1)(t^2+q^2))
     =pi/[2 AGM(1,q)],  q>0,
 e(q)=K(q)-log(4/q).

This K is the usual complete elliptic integral evaluated at modulus
sqrt(1-q^2) for 0<q<=1. It is NOT the usual K at modulus q.
For 0<q<1 the classical convergent series is

 K(q)=sum_(n>=0) A_n q^(2n)[log(1/q)+d_n],
 A_n=((1/2)_n/n!)^2<=1,
 d_0=log4, d_(n+1)=d_n-2/[(2n+1)(2n+2)], d_n>0.       (3.1)

Primary authoritative formula actually checked:
 https://dlmf.nist.gov/19.12.E1
 https://dlmf.nist.gov/19.12.E3
The positivity follows also from the digamma formula for d_n.

It follows termwise that e(q)>=0 for 0<q<=1. Homogeneity gives
K(q)=K(1/q)/q. For q>=1,

 K(q)>=log(4q)/q>=log(4/q),

because (1+q)logq+(1-q)log4 is0 at1 and has positive derivative
for q>=1. Thus

 e(q)>=0 for ALL q>0.                                (3.2)

Termwise differentiation of (3.1), bounding A_n<=1,d_n<=log4,
and summing geometric series for q<=1/2 gives the useful bounds

 0<=e(q)<=2q^2 log(4/q),
 |e'(q)|<=6q log(4/q),
 |e''(q)|<=16 log(4/q).                              (3.3)

For example sum n^2 z^(n-1)=(1+z)/(1-z)^3<=80/27 for
0<=z<=1/4 suffices for the last displayed constant.

Splitting the defining integral at t=q and t=1 shows

 K(q)<=2+log(1/q) for 0<q<=1,
 K(q)<=(2+logq)/q<=2 for q>=1.                       (3.4)

Combining (3.3)--(3.4) on q<=1/2, 1/2<=q<=1,
1<=q<=4 and q>=4 gives the convenient GLOBAL bound

 0<=e(q)<=10q,  q>0.                                (3.5)

5. DERIVATIVES NEAR1 WITHOUT ASSUMING COMPLEX ERROR CONTROL
===========================================================

C_i,E_i and their limit are positive operator monotone and operator
concave. This follows inductively from positivity, joint operator
monotonicity/concavity of harmonic and geometric means, and the same
composition rule. Functional calculus identifies the scalar iterations
with their free matrix evaluations, since all iterates of one input
commute.

A positive operator monotone p on (0,infinity) has its positive
Loewner representation p(y)=alpha+beta*y+integral y/(y+t) dmu(t),
with alpha,beta>=0. Differentiating the kernels gives for k>=1

 |p^(k)(y)|<=k!*p(y)/y^k.                            (5.1)

This includes the beta term for k=1. For k>=2 it follows from
t*y^(k-1)<=(y+t)^k. Thus no derivative estimate for a difference of
positive functions is being assumed from value convergence alone.

Put H_j=(pi/2)C_j and g=H_j-H. On [1/4,2] these positive

 |g''|<=128L,  |g''''|<=16384L on [1/4,2],

9. EXACT NORMALIZATION WITH RATIONAL SDP DATA
==============================================

Simply rounding c=C_j(1) or d=C'_j(1) as fixed coefficients is
INVALID for a global b-weighted bound: a residual constant or slope
at1 makes the ratio diverge. Exact algebraic coefficients are one
possible model, but the following rational SDP device avoids them.

9a. A general constant-and-sensitivity graph lemma
-------------------------------------------------

Suppose a scalar rational SDP has the form

 v(y)=max{ u^T z : Mz=b0+y*b1, z in K },              (9.1)

where K is a product of real PSD cones of order at most2 and
one-dimensional nonnegative cones. Assume primal and dual optima
are attained at y=1, and v is differentiable there. Put
c=v(1), d=v'(1). Its dual at1 is

 min{ (b0+b1)^T lambda : M^T lambda-u in K }.

For ANY dual optimum lambda*, sensitivity gives b1^T lambda*=d:
its affine dual objective supports v at1, so differentiability
identifies the slope. This uses only weak duality and equality at1.

For a positive definite N-by-N matrix A, introduce matrix-amplified
primal/dual variables Z,Lambda, replace every scalar coordinate by
an N-by-N Hermitian coordinate, and impose

 M Z=(b0+b1) A,  Z in K_N,
 M^T Lambda-u A in K_N,
 P=u^T Z=(b0+b1)^T Lambda,
 Q=b1^T Lambda.                                    (9.2)

Here K_N replaces each order2 block by an order2N block; all scalar
linear maps act on the small block index, leaving the N index.

CLAIM. The projection of (9.2) is EXACTLY P=cA,Q=dA.

Proof. Compress all variables against an arbitrary nonzero vector w.
Let alpha=w^*Aw>0. The compressed primal and dual variables, divided
by alpha, are feasible for the original scalar primal/dual at1.
The matrix objective equality makes their scalar objectives equal.
They are therefore both optimal. The compressed P equals c*alpha,
and the compressed Q equals d*alpha by slope uniqueness. Since this
holds for every w, P=cA,Q=dA. Conversely tensor any fixed scalar
primal/dual optimal solutions with A to obtain feasible variables
in (9.2), yielding exactly these outputs. QED.

This argument does NOT rely on a false operator-valued weak-duality
formula involving products of variable matrices; scalar compression
and scalar weak duality are sufficient. It doubles the PSD block
count, preserves maximum order2N, and uses only the rational data
of the original SDP. A positive-definite input suffices here.

9b. Application to the finite adjoint AGM
-----------------------------------------

The scalar hypograph C_j(y)>=z has a rational SDP with O(j)
order2 blocks and affine input dependence y. For each step use
nonnegative auxiliary c_i,e_i, initialized by c0=1,e0=y/a, and

 c_(i+1)<=harmonic(c_i,e_i),
 e_(i+1)<=geometric(c_i,e_i).

The first hypograph has a rational Schur-complement LMI, for example

 [ c_i+e_i   c_i ; c_i   c_i-c_(i+1)/2 ]>=0,

and the second uses [c_i e_(i+1); e_(i+1) e_i]>=0. Set output
z<=c_j. Monotonicity proves that its maximum is precisely C_j(y).
For y>0 choose all intermediate outputs strictly positive and
strictly below the actual means, and the final output strictly
below c_j; this supplies strict feasibility. The value is bounded
above and attained at the true iterates, so standard Slater duality
gives an attained dual optimum. Smoothness of the finite iteration
gives the required sensitivity uniqueness at1.

Convert this rational SDP to (9.1) by introducing PSD slack blocks;
the count and the coefficient bit lengths change only by constants.
Thus (9.2) represents the exact maps A->cA and A->dA with O(j)
blocks of order2N. No numerical c or d enters the lift coefficients.

The raw tangent generator

 -C_j(y)+c+d(y-1)

is now represented exactly, including its double zero at1. For its
matrix perspective use the graph device on both matrix arguments;
linear outputs give cX and d(Y-X), and the ordinary AGM hypograph
supplies the nonlinear perspective term. This preserves O(j)
blocks and maximum order2N. Multiply the raw tangent generator by
the rational q of (2.5), rather than using an irrational1/d.

9c. Effective coefficient preparation
--------------------------------------

The only nontrivial numerical preparation is q. At y=1, evaluate
the O(j) scalar iteration and its derivatives by forward automatic
differentiation, using certified rational intervals for square roots.
All means and derivatives are positive; d remains bounded above
and away from0 by (5.6). Initial data have O(L) bits and magnitude
at most1/a. Denominators along this constant evaluation are bounded
away from0, and O(L+j) guard bits suffice for an interval of d of
width O(a). Alternatively algebraic interval arithmetic gives the
same polynomial-bit bound without any numerical conditioning claim.

Select a positive dyadic q below the reciprocal of the upper bound
for d and sufficiently close that q*d>=1-a. O(L) bits suffice.
All remaining lift coefficients are rational numbers arising from
a,1/a,1/2,q,1/(1+a), eta, and the sparse mean/dual equations.
For rational eta of input bit length B, coefficient sizes are
O(B+log(1/eta)); no transcendental pi or logL is a lift coefficient.
The integer j uses only binary ceilings of the integers3s and8s.

This establishes effective rational input preparation and exact
mathematical lift identities. It does not establish that a generic
SDP solver has a favorable condition number: the constant graph
uses exact zero duality gap and can be degenerate. Any numerical
implementation needs ordinary certified optimization-error handling.

10. MATRIX BLOCK COUNTS AND THE PETZ/PERSPECTIVE DISTINCTION
============================================================

For N-dimensional matrix perspectives, the two mean hypographs and
their recursion use O(j) order2N blocks. The constant-and-sensitivity
graphs use another O(j) such blocks on each of the two inputs.
Affine output corrections, rational scalings and the bracket lower
rescaling add no PSD order. The upper b perspective adds one order2N
Schur-complement block. This proves the claimed O(loglog)-stage
order2N representation for matrix perspectives.

For STANDARD PETZ, use the commuting superoperator matrices

 X=A tensor I, Y=I tensor bar(B),
 phi(T)=vec(I)^* T vec(I).

Apply the previous construction in dimension N^2 and the positive
linear functional phi. The raw entropy generator then has value

 -phi(P_(C_j)(Y,X)) + c*TrA + d*(TrB-TrA).

This is precisely the standard Petz divergence of the raw tangent
generator, not its maximal noncommutative perspective on A,B.
The rational q and 1/(1+a) scalings give D^-. Scalar inequalities
transfer through the commuting positive modular superoperator,
establishing (1.2). Operator convexity gives joint convexity.

The upper correction Chi2/2 has one order2N block using

 [ W A-B ; A-B B ]>=0,

and the output TrW/2. Thus there are O(j) order2N^2 blocks plus one
order2N block. No valid reduction to order2N for the joint standard
Petz root/AGM construction is established here. Quantum affinity
must not be replaced by fidelity to claim such a reduction.
