Classical-arithmetic gate for the raw partial-Krylov proposal
2026-11-14. Scope update after the user's practical-theory clarification.

No benchmark, implementation heuristic, or practical SOTA claim is made.
The task asks for a new theoretical algorithm with reasonable primitives,
not a hardware-specific speedup or a replacement of GEMV by GEMM. No such
new classical-arithmetic theorem has been proved in this search.

1. The proposed exact raw short-Krylov primitive is still useful

The candidate in factor-algebra-search.txt Section9 is

  K_q(J X^T X,v)
    in ~O(I_X*q^(omega-2)+m*q^2+m^omega),

including the fixed-permutation J extension needed for arbitrary products.
At omega=9/4 it would solve the original hard total-time instance. It is
not currently proved. At classical omega=3 its input term becomes I_X*q,
so merely proving that formula uniformly in omega would NOT itself give
a new classical pass complexity. The m^omega setup must also be paid.
Replacing it by a small projected q-core without proof would erase the
principal missing algorithmic step.

A genuinely practical-strength target would instead be

  ~O(I_X*polylog(q)+m*q^2+q^3)

or a weaker concrete bound that saves a polynomial number of raw passes
without a full m^3 setup. An approximate routine may suffice, but needs a
certified spectral-subspace error bound independent of the conditioning of
the monomial Krylov basis and of the true leading-to-tail ratio. Elementwise
small errors in raw monomial vectors do not provide that guarantee.

In the original shape N=m^(9/8), q=~sqrt(m), this stronger expression is
~O(m^(17/8)+m^2), with output m^(9/4), so it would meet the total-time
gate using conventional arithmetic. This is an OPEN target, not a result.

2. Tempting immediately available methods do not meet that theorem

Orthogonalized Chebyshev/power recurrences still use q dependent true
operator actions. Blocking already known independent columns reduces their
GEMM cost but does not construct unknown future iterates in one call.
The same is true of a batch polynomial recurrence or a compact WY update
whose generating vectors have not yet been obtained. Communication-avoiding
Arnoldi can reduce synchronization or basis-maintenance costs while retaining
the sequential operator-action work; that is not the requested arithmetic
advance.

Stable block Davidson/LOBPCG and sketch-preconditioned eigensolvers are useful
practical comparators. A favorable local convergence experiment, approximate
Gram preconditioner, or reused dense core eigenbasis does not establish a
gap-free arbitrary-factor high-accuracy runtime. No new such convergence
theorem was obtained here. They are therefore not offered as a substitute
for the requested advance.

Exact QR-SVD avoids normal-equation conditioning and is the appropriate
stable exact reference when the factor Grams would square condition numbers.
Classical tall QR costs O((n+p)m^2) plus its small-core work. TSQR changes
communication and constant factors; sketch-preconditioned QR followed by
exact transforms must charge those full tall transforms. This is standard
linear algebra, not a new smaller arithmetic exponent. Lifting only r
retained coefficient vectors is already charged efficiently in the existing
comparators.

Batched negative-shift/ridge solves can be cheap on many RHSs. Constructing
the desired sharp filter from them must nevertheless pay for the needed
precision and extraction. Existing uniform coefficient-growth results show
that termwise refinement can restore the polynomial pass count; degree
alone is not a batching lower bound. A new stable extraction theorem remains
possible, but has not been supplied by ordinary reassociation.

3. Concrete algebraic work that remains

The promising focused question is still the order-q raw saddle expansion

  [ I_N      -X      ] [u(z)] = [0]
  [-z J X^T  I_m     ] [w(z)]   [v]   modulo z^q,

without computing the full degree-m rational fraction or dense Gram.
The output-sensitive polynomial-kernel/fraction algorithm must be given,
not assumed. The exact one-RHS row-update counterexample in the companion
search note explains why naive child Padé summaries fail to close under
row/block merges. General polynomial-matrix algorithms currently inspected
still pay their full kernel setup before low-order series extraction.

The full-determinant AD reduction does not rule out this short-order task.
For example, differentiating a scalar weighted short trajectory with respect
to X produces X times a coefficient matrix of rank at most 2q: one factor
in every term belongs to the forward q-dimensional Krylov span, while reverse
adjoints collect the other factors into at most q columns. At q<<m this
does not directly recover a full generic Gram as the exact trace/determinant
gradients do. This is a scope observation, not a lower bound against some
different reduction.

No new classical theorem beyond standard block Lanczos, orthogonalized
Chebyshev, or known sketch-and-solve was established. The note preserves
the practical success bar explicitly rather than labeling the unproved
galactic-exponent candidate as a practical algorithm. The original hard
instance and unrestricted near-input-plus-core goal remain open.

4. Lazy signed elimination: an exact potential and a failed sampling rule

New route requested by the parent: process the raw bordered threshold pencil
with its known m negative baseline, maintaining only the at-most-r excess
negative directions, in O(mr) work per raw row plus O(m^3) core work. The
following audit concerns an actual proposed mechanism, not an impossibility
claim for all offline or adaptive algorithms.

4.1 What the Schur and signed-URV algorithms actually cost

The 1996 algorithm supplies all threshold-spectral approximants from a
hyperbolic factorization of [epsilon I,H], for an explicit m-by-n H. Its
Fig.1 processes every column through all m pivots; the signed triangular
state retains m-d positive and d negative columns. Sections4.2--4.3 charge
about (m^2 n)/2 elementary rotations overall, and about m^2/2 per update.
Section4.4 also exhibits prefix breakdown at an exact threshold even when
the final matrix is not at threshold. The small final d does not remove
the positive triangular state or its rotations. [Source-derived summary:
94 words; no long quotation.]

The 2011 signed-URV Table3 explicitly eliminates against both the m-d
positive and d negative pivots, and states O(m^2) per vector update. Its
stable treatment uses only one hyperbolic rotation; the ordinary rotations
remain. The 2012 journal abstract likewise promises QR-update order, not
O(md). [Source-derived summary: 46 words from the conference paper; 17
words from the journal abstract.]

Thus applying these algorithms to H=AB directly first needs the explicit
product; reducing to a true m-dimensional covariance first needs its exact
Gram/QR information. Applying their signed elimination to the larger raw
bordered pencil does not by itself make its m baseline negative directions
free. A raw-factor rank-sensitive extension would be a new ingredient.

4.2 The attractive logdet counting bound is correct

Consider the easier all-positive prefix case

  K_i=t I-sum_{j<=i} x_j x_j^T > 0,  K_N >= g I,  K_0=t I,
  ell_i=x_i^T K_(i-1)^(-1) x_i.

The matrix determinant lemma gives 0<=ell_i<1 and

  det K_i = det K_(i-1) (1-ell_i).

Consequently

  sum_i ell_i <= sum_i -log(1-ell_i)
               = log(det K_0/det K_N) <= m log(t/g).             (4.1)

There are at most m log(t/g)/tau rows with ell_i>=tau. If each such row
could be recognized cheaply, processed for O(m^2), and every other row
handled safely for O(mr), this would give the desired type of heavy-row
count with only logarithmic accuracy dependence. Two unproved steps are
essential: a cheap exact or certified ell_i query, and preservation of the
final threshold certificate after ignoring or sampling the light rows.

4.3 A constant-probability late-cancellation counterexample

The following independently derived example disproves the most direct
light-row rule: independently retain row i with probability

  p_i=min(1,c ell_i),

and use its Horvitz--Thompson covariance contribution x_i x_i^T/p_i. It
does not disprove scalar summation, alternative samplers, adaptive revisits,
correlated rounding, or a global offline algorithm.

Take dimension1, t=1, 0<g<=1/2, c>=1, even N>=4c, and identical squared
row lengths w=(1-g)/N. All true prefixes are positive:

  K_i=1-iw,  K_N=g,  ell_i=w/K_(i-1).

Let xi_i be independent Bernoulli(p_i), and let

  S=sum_i (xi_i w/p_i-w),  Khat_N=g-S.

For i<=N/2, K_(i-1)>=1/2 and p_i<=2c/N<=1/2. Hence

  Var(xi_i w/p_i-w)
    =w^2(1-p_i)/p_i >= w K_(i-1)/(2c) >= 1/(8cN),

so V=E S^2>=1/(16c). Every nonzero centered increment has magnitude at
most 1/c: if p_i<1 then w/p_i=K_(i-1)/c<=1/c, while p_i=1 gives zero.
Independence and centering imply

  E S^4 <= 3V^2 + V/c^2 <= 19V^2.

Paley--Zygmund applied to S^2 therefore yields

  Pr{|S| >= 1/sqrt(32c)} >= 1/76.                              (4.2)

Thus whenever eta g < 1/sqrt(32c), this estimator fails the final relative
margin requirement |Khat_N-K_N|<=eta K_N with probability at least1/76.
Achieving that requirement at smaller constant failure probability requires
c=Omega((eta g)^(-2)) for this rule, despite sum ell_i<=log(1/g).
The failure is precisely that errors small in an early positive metric are
amplified by later cancellation. The potential (4.1) alone does not supply
a gap-logarithmic lazy Schur algorithm.

4.4 Negative count does not bound the relevant positive barrier mass

For the final metric, even the entirely positive case gives

  sum_i x_i^T K_N^(-1) x_i
    =tr(K_N^(-1)(t I-K_N)).

If K_N=g I this is m(t-g)/g although the negative index is zero. More
generally K_N with m-r eigenvalues +g and r eigenvalues -g has negative
index r but

  tr(|K_N|^(-1)(t I-K_N)) = m t/g-m+2r.                       (4.3)

Arbitrary orthogonal rotation preserves this example. In particular, a
count of excess negatives does not control either absolute-inverse leverage
mass or the dimension of a positive block lying close to the threshold.
This is a limitation of this barrier strategy, not a complexity lower bound:
the displayed isotropic examples themselves have easy structure.

A negative-space-only summary also cannot close exact rank-one updates.
For example K_a=diag(a,1), K_b=diag(b,1), with 0<a<c^2<b<1, initially
have the same empty negative certificate. After appending x=c e_1 as a
downdate, K_a-xx^T has one negative direction and K_b-xx^T has none.
The positive state is necessary for the next decision. A full dense
positive state can be stored with O(m^2) memory, but neither this example
nor the signed-URV algorithm tells how to update it in O(mr) arithmetic.

4.5 Precise missing mechanism

One possible escape is a correlated/resparsified representation that makes
earlier errors shrink relative to all later Schur complements. Its proof
must pay for revisiting old rows or transforming their raw coordinates;
computing each dense inverse quadratic form still costs O(m^2) by direct
arithmetic. Another is to batch many unresolved rows and exploit genuine
structure in their joint negative certificate. The count r by itself does
not provide the needed numerical-rank or positive-gap bound.

No new O(Nmr+m^3) raw-factor threshold/inertia algorithm has been obtained.
The concrete progress is the exact potential (4.1), the failure theorem
(4.2) for its natural sampling implementation, and the verified costs of
the closest hyperbolic-Schur / signed-URV algorithms. The broad practical
and original total-output targets remain open.

5.1 One-sided order and physical dual orientation: PASS

Write the normalized true pencil in critical/far coordinates as

  Kbar_U=[C B^T;B A],  (1/4)I<=A<=2I,  ||B||<=2.

The fixed polynomial T_-<=A^-1<=T_+ has T_+-T_-=delta I. Hence
S=S_+=C-B^T T_-B and S_-=S-delta B^TB. For an operator-norm-tau
approximation Stilde, the actual definitions imply

  Slo=Stilde-(tau+4delta)I <= S_-,
  Shi=Stilde+tau I >= S.

The analytical lower full surrogate changes the critical diagonal block
by Slo-S_- and the far block from A to T_+^-1. Both changes are negative
semidefinite. Their norms are at most 2tau+4delta and 4delta, respectively.
The upper surrogate changes the two diagonal blocks by PSD errors at
most 2tau and 8delta. Thus epsilon=2tau+8delta safely bounds each full
surrogate error; no noncommuting inverse subtraction has been assumed.

For any negative pivot W_c of Slo, the analytical lifted pivot
[W_c;-T_+B W_c] has lower-surrogate image [Slo W_c;0]. Congruence back
through U and J_D yields the physical dual space

  range(Z J_D U_C Slo W_c).

This direction is correct. In particular it is J_D, not J_D^-T. For a
physical x, y=Z^T x and xi=J_D^T y, so x orthogonal to the displayed
space is exactly xi orthogonal to U_C Slo W_c. Positivity of the full
surrogate on that complement transfers to the true pencil. The same
reasoning works for all q=n_-(Slo)<=r negative directions at once, with
no greedy factor r in the required additive precision. Zero pivots are
excluded from the negative pivot, not inverted.

5.3 Median and adaptive approximation: PASS

For S=a0 I+L+E, rank L<=ell, ||E||<=nu, every (2s+1)-dimensional
orthogonal compression at s>=ell has a middle eigenvalue within nu of
a0, by rank perturbation and min--max. The additive-nu core bisection
therefore produces |a-a0|<=2nu. At most ell singular values of S-aI
exceed3nu. The fact that the compression may be deterministic causes
no problem. At ell=0 the s=0 trial is covered by this same argument.

The range argument remains valid for signed E_a. Solve its full head
Gaussian coordinates with a pseudoinverse, zero the weak head coordinates,
and retain only eigenvalues with |lambda|>tau/32. All remaining tail
eigenvalues have |lambda|<=tau/64, so the power leakage ratio is at most
2^-q. The strong/tail split gives the stated operator bound, and
||E_a-P E_a P||<=2||(I-P)E_a|| is valid for a self-adjoint E_a.

The fresh power test is an upper certificate with its allocated failure
probability: the top absolute eigenvector has Gaussian coefficient at
least beta/100 except O(beta), while ||g||<=sqrt(d/beta) except beta.
The selected power-of-two u makes Gamma between ||R|| and2||R|| on this
event. A fresh g conditional on the built Stilde is required and is
explicitly specified. The test also returns zero exactly for R=0.

For nu=tau/512, a successful range event at s>=c_nu(S) gives residual
below tau/4, so its fresh norm test accepts. Doubling reaches width at

exact full S if 2s+1>=d is a valid finite fallback. The d=0 case takes
this full-core branch immediately; it must not invoke log(d) or a norm

5.5 Raw-input and adaptive-rank costs: PASS, conditional scope retained

A block of s Schur actions uses fixed-degree far and mass polynomials,
so all raw applications are to the SAME s-column block at each stage.
Its cost is logarithmic factors times Acal(s), plus core algebra. The
norm tests add Acal(1); the median uses2s+1 columns. Summing doubling
widths costs the same order as the final width by column tiling, and
there are only logarithmically many thresholds. Thin QR and explicit
small-core inertia are bounded by m^omega with fast algebra or m^3
conventionally. Physical q-column lifting and right-factor projection cost
Acal(r), then the actual dense output costs T_MM(n,r,p) (or npr).

This verifies the stated construction bounds

  ~O(I+m^(omega+zeta)+Acal(c_*+1)+Acal(r)),
  ~O(I+m^3+I(c_*+r+1)) with conventional arithmetic,

on the correctness/efficiency event, subject to the inherited n,p>=m
model. Widths are capped by m. On failed events fixed polynomial degrees,
trial counts, guarded ranks and the full-core fallback still bound the
runtime; they do not guarantee the small-c_* bound or correctness.
