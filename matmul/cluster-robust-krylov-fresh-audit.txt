VERDICT
-------

PASS for the direct interpolation theorem as stated, the displayed
graph-angle corollary, and the displayed scalar-filter inequality. I
found no fatal mathematical flaw after checking all the proof steps.
In particular, the dangerous noncommutative evaluation and conditional
independence steps are correct in this source, and the stated numerical
constants are sufficient.

There is one missing dependence in the prose description of acceleration:
lines 345--347 omit the log(n-bd) term inherited from the angle prefactor.
The displayed inequalities remain valid. Two additional clarifications
would make the proof less vulnerable to misreading: explicitly reuse the
joint event constructed in the main proof when proving the corollary,
and explicitly declare that Q and Q_perp are orthonormal eigenvector
matrices. Details and exact replacement formulas follow below.

The equal-size, fixed-partition hypothesis is material. This result does
not establish a general multiplicity-only or sliding-b-th-gap theorem.
It also does not prove the refuted intermediate-factor conjecture or a
new matrix-product runtime. The manuscript currently keeps these limits
appropriately explicit. No priority certification is supplied here.

1. ISSUES AND RECOMMENDED CLARIFICATIONS
--------------------------------------

1.1 Missing dimension dependence in the additional-depth prose

At lines 345--347 the listed depth dependencies include log(1/epsilon),
d log(1/g), d log(db/beta), and the outer separation. The prefactor in
the displayed angle corollary also contains sqrt(n-bd). Consequently,
the sufficient depth obtained from those inequalities contains
(1/2) log(n-bd). If the list is intended to be complete, it needs a
dimension term. This is a reporting omission, not a counterexample to
any displayed theorem.

For n>bd define exactly

  C_beta = [8 d b^(3/2)/beta]
           sqrt[2 d b (n-bd)/beta]
           [16000 d^8 b^5/(beta^4 g)]^(d-1),

and a_gamma = arcosh(1+2 gamma). For 0<epsilon<1 and gamma>0,

  t >= ceil( log(2 C_beta/epsilon) / a_gamma )

is a sufficient nonnegative integer filter degree for tan(angle)<=epsilon
on the event of the angle corollary. More generally one can take the
maximum of this expression and zero. The exact numerator expands as

  log(16 sqrt(2)) + (3/2)log d + 2 log b
  + (3/2)log(1/beta) + (1/2)log(n-bd) + log(1/epsilon)
  + (d-1)[log 16000 + 8 log d + 5 log b
           + 4 log(1/beta) + log(1/g)].

Thus a complete concise dependence statement includes log(n) in addition
to the terms already listed. For 0<gamma<=1, a_gamma is of order
sqrt(gamma); for arbitrary gamma the displayed arcosh expression is
the correct denominator.

1.2 Explicitly use the joint head event in the corollary

Lines 322--323 say to use the head theorem and its Gaussian norm event
at failure beta/2. This is valid when interpreted as reusing the
intersection event constructed in the proof of the theorem, with its
confidence parameter set to beta/2. That proof event simultaneously
controls G_H and max_i ||Omega_i^-1||, and has failure at most beta/2.

The statement of the theorem alone only bounds G_H; blindly appending
a separately allocated inverse-norm event could overcount the failure
budget. A clear replacement is:

  "Apply the joint event constructed in the proof of Theorem 1 with
   confidence parameter beta/2. It simultaneously bounds G_H by the
   theorem's right-hand side with beta replaced by beta/2 and bounds
   max_i ||Omega_i^-1|| by 8 d b^(3/2)/beta."

The subsequent tail Markov event costs beta/2, as written, and no
independence between these events is needed for their union bound.
The displayed angle constants need no alteration.

1.3 Declare the eigenbasis and dimensions explicitly

At lines 283--289 Q is first called a subspace and then used as a matrix.
The standard intended interpretation is clear: Q is n-by-bd with
orthonormal eigenvector columns, Q_perp completes it to an orthogonal
eigenvector matrix, and A Q=Q Lambda_H with Lambda_H diagonal and
partitioned into the supplied blocks. State this directly. Otherwise
an arbitrary basis for an invariant subspace would not give a standard
Gaussian Q^T Omega or the displayed diagonal head representation.

1.4 Small edge-case wording

The previous-block term in R is declared absent for q=1 and is handled
correctly in the proof. Writing that convention next to the definition
of R, or defining the term to be zero, would avoid the undefined symbol
P_{-1} in a literal reading. Likewise, the filtered inequality can be
introduced for n>bd, since the n=bd case was already settled and a maximum
over an empty tail is otherwise undefined without a convention. Neither
point affects the substantive result.

2. LEFT EVALUATION AND GENERIC INTERPOLATION
-------------------------------------------

All coefficients are on the right: P(B)=sum_j B^j C_j. The proof uses
only operations that respect that convention. In particular,

  [P(z)D](B)=P(B)D

for a constant right factor D. It never substitutes B into an arbitrary
product of two matrix polynomials as though evaluation were a ring
homomorphism.

For a subset S of s blocks, let

  K_S=[Gamma_S,A_S Gamma_S,...,A_S^(s-1) Gamma_S].

This is square of order sb. Its determinant is polynomial in all entries
of the original Gaussian blocks. The witness Omega_i=I_b produces, after
row and column permutations, b scalar Vandermonde matrices. Each has
one node from every block, so its nodes are distinct under precisely the
stated cross-block disjointness. Repeated eigenvalues within a block do
not spoil that witness. Thus det K_S is a nonzero polynomial and K_S is
nonsingular almost surely.

Multiplication by diag(Omega_i^-1:i in S) turns the block columns into
[I,B_i,...,B_i^(s-1)] in block row i. This is exactly the linear map from
stacked right coefficients to left evaluations, acting independently on
each output column. Its invertibility proves uniqueness and existence
of the actual fundamental polynomials. A finite union of null sets covers
all subsets. There is no uncountable union over spectra here.

Every prefix of an invertible K_S has independent columns. Hence each
block orthogonalization step adds exactly b independent columns, and
every leading coefficient C_{j,j} is invertible. The stipulated fixed
measurable QR rule is enough; no hidden spectral or root randomness is
used in constructing the basis of a subset.

3. ROW SMALL-BALL LEMMA
----------------------

Independent centered Gaussian rows with covariance >=h^2 I are jointly
absolutely continuous, so any b-1 of them are linearly independent almost
surely. Conditional on the other rows, projection of row i onto a unit
normal to their span is a centered Gaussian of variance at least h^2.
Therefore its absolute value has probability at most sqrt(2/pi) u/h of
being <=u. This remains valid for any measurably selected unit normal;
the sign does not matter.

For an invertible row matrix N the norm of column i of N^-1 is D_i^-1,
where D_i is that row's distance to the other rows. Consequently

  ||N^-1||_F^2=sum_i D_i^-2.

If every D_i>sqrt(b) h t, then ||N^-1||_2<1/(h t), so the event
sigma_min(N)<=h t forces at least one D_i<=sqrt(b) h t. The union bound
is <=sqrt(2/pi) b^(3/2)t<=b^(3/2)t. The proof includes b=1 and works
under the conditioning used later. No identical-covariance assumption
is needed.

4. TERMINAL BLOCK-LANCZOS POLYNOMIAL
-----------------------------------

For q blocks, V_0,...,V_{q-1} is a complete orthonormal basis for R^(qb).
For j<=q-3, symmetry gives

  V_j^T A V_{q-1}=(A V_j)^T V_{q-1}=0,

because A V_j lies in the Krylov prefix through degree j+1<=q-2.
The complete basis therefore gives the terminal recurrence with only
V_{q-2} and V_{q-1}, and no residual block. Its coefficient matrices are
compressions of A and have norm at most one after normalization.

The polynomial

  R(z)=z P_{q-1}(z)-P_{q-2}(z) E^T-P_{q-1}(z) D

has degree q with leading coefficient C_q=C_{q-1,q-1}. Its realization
sum_l A^l Gamma C_l is zero by the recurrence. The i-th block row of
this identity is Omega_i R(B_i)=0. Thus it annihilates each selected
matrix under the correct left evaluation.

For z outside spec A, the scalar division identity in lines 170--174
gives

  (zI-A)^-1 Gamma R(z)
    =sum_(l=1)^q sum_(t=0)^(l-1)
          z^(l-1-t) A^t Gamma C_l.

Left multiplication by V_{q-1}^T eliminates every term except l=q,
t=q-1. The remaining term is I_b because

  V_{q-1}=A^(q-1) Gamma C_q + lower-degree Krylov terms

and V_{q-1} has orthonormal columns. The order is C_q on the right,
exactly as required. Therefore

  W(z) R(z)=I_b,
  R(z)^-1=W(z)=V_{q-1}^T (zI-A)^-1 Gamma,
  ||R(z)^-1||_2<=||Gamma||_2/dist(z,spec A).

There is no omitted condition number of Gamma, a leading polynomial
coefficient, or a transformed similarity in this estimate.

For P_j of degree <=q-1, interpolation uniqueness gives

  P_j(z)=sum_(i in S) F_i^S(z) Omega_i^-1 (V_j)_i.

This identity is valid because every final factor is constant and on the
right. The block rows of V_j have norm at most one. Thus each P_j has
supremum norm <=q G_S max_i||Omega_i^-1||, and the three terms in R
give the asserted factor 3. For q=1 there are only two terms, so the
same bound remains valid. No induction estimate was assumed in proving
the inverse identity.

5. FRESH ROOT AND COVARIANCE
----------------------------

Fix T and k, and construct R exclusively from S=T\{k}. Conditional on
the Gaussian matrices in S, all its coefficients and Gamma_S are fixed.
Since Omega_k B_k^j=Lambda_k^j Omega_k,

  N_{T,k}=Omega_k R(B_k)

has row l equal to omega_{k,l}^T R(lambda_{k,l}). Each row is a fixed
linear transform of a different standard Gaussian row. Its covariance
is R(lambda)^T R(lambda), not R(lambda)R(lambda)^T; both have the same
smallest eigenvalue, and the manuscript needs only the valid lower bound

  R(lambda)^T R(lambda)
      >= [delta_{T,k}/||Gamma_S||]^2 I_b.

The resolvent estimate proves precisely this bound. The fresh row lemma
therefore applies conditionally and proves nonsingularity. With

  Z_{T,k}=delta_{T,k} ||N_{T,k}^-1||/||Gamma_S||,

its conditional tail is <=min(1,b^(3/2)/z). Integrating the tail for
the half moment gives exactly 2 b^(3/4). These estimates hold before
intersecting any success event.

The expression

  F_k^T(z)=R(z)N_{T,k}^-1 Omega_k

has degree at most |T|-1, evaluates to zero on S, and evaluates at B_k
to R(B_k)[Omega_k R(B_k)]^-1 Omega_k=I_b. All multipliers used after
R are constant right factors. Thus this is the actual fundamental
polynomial. The proof does not evaluate a polynomial product illegally.

6. DETERMINISTIC RECURSION AND PROBABILITY CONSTANTS
--------------------------------------------------

For each d original blocks,

  E||Omega_i||_F^2=b^2,
  Pr{||Omega_i||>2 b sqrt(d/beta)}<=beta/(4d),
  Pr{||Omega_i^-1||>4 d b^(3/2)/beta}<=beta/(4d).

The union of all 2d failures costs at most beta/2. One such event E
controls every subset without an extra subset union bound.

For q=|T|-1 on E,

  ||Gamma_S||<=sqrt(q)L_0,
  ||R||_sup<=3q G_S U_0,
  ||N_{T,k}^-1||=Z_{T,k}||Gamma_S||/delta_{T,k},
  ||Omega_k||<=L_0,
  delta_{T,k}>=Delta.

Multiplication gives exactly

  G_T <= [3 q^(3/2) U_0 L_0^2/Delta]
           max_(k in T) Z_{T,k} G_{T\{k}}.

The singleton value G_T=1 is correct. In particular the minimum
cross-block gap appears only once per elimination step. There is no
extra factor of Delta^-1 concealed in the resolvent estimate or in the
Gaussian norm bounds.

For a fixed elimination permutation pi, every factor after Z_{T_1,pi_1}
is measurable in the remaining original Gaussian blocks T_2. Conditional
integration of Omega_{pi_1} therefore bounds the first half moment by
2 b^(3/4) while leaving the later factors intact. Iterating proves

  E[(product_j Z_{T_j,pi_j})^(1/2)]
       <=(2 b^(3/4))^(d-1).

This argument does not assume independent Z variables. It is crucial
that no good event E is included in the conditioning; the manuscript
does this correctly. Markov and a union bound over at most d! paths
produce the displayed threshold with total failure <=beta/2.

The deterministic factors multiply to

  [3 U_0 L_0^2/Delta]^(d-1) ((d-1)!)^(3/2),
  3 U_0 L_0^2=48 d^2 b^(7/2) beta^-2.

The path moment threshold adds 4 b^(3/2) per step and the remaining
factor 4 beta^-2(d!)^2. Thus the unsimplified bound in lines 270--272
is arithmetically correct. The listed factorial estimates give a base
power d^(15/2), which is <=d^8; the constant is 768, safely rounded up
to 1000. The estimates also hold for d=2. The logarithm of the
spectrum-independent prefactor is O(d log(db/beta)), as stated.

Affine normalization preserves left evaluation: the polynomial
F_i((x-a)/L) has the desired values at the original B_j if F_i has the
desired values at (B_j-aI)/L. Replacing the normalized gap by Delta/L
therefore restores exactly (L/Delta)^(d-1).

7. GRAPH ANGLE AND FILTER CONSEQUENCES
-------------------------------------

In an orthonormal eigenbasis, K_H is square and nonsingular almost
surely. The entire depth-d Krylov subspace can be written as the graph

  range [I; X],  X=K_T K_H^-1.

For the largest principal angle to the head coordinate subspace,
tan(theta_max)=||X||_2. For y partitioned into head blocks,

  p_y(z)=sum_i F_i(z) Omega_i^-1 y_i

has coefficient stack K_H^-1 y because its head values reproduce y
after multiplication by the original Omega_i. For ||y||=1,
sum_i||y_i||<=sqrt(d). The row bound and its Frobenius summation
therefore give exactly

  ||X||<=sqrt(d)||Omega_T||_F G_H max_i||Omega_i^-1||.

The theorem can be applied on the full spectral interval of A, so this
controls evaluations at every tail eigenvalue, including a tail that
is not ordered relative to the head. No outer spectral gap is needed
for this initialization bound.

With confidence beta/2 the joint head event gives

  G_H<=[16000 d^8 b^5/(beta^4 g)]^(d-1),
  max_i||Omega_i^-1||<=8 d b^(3/2)/beta.

The tail Markov event gives ||Omega_T||_F<=sqrt(2b(n-bd)/beta) with
failure <=beta/2. Their combination is precisely the printed corollary.
For n=bd, the full square Krylov matrix is nonsingular almost surely,
so the angle is zero.

For scalar f nonzero on the head, the filtered graph matrix is

  X_f=f(Lambda_T) X f(Lambda_H)^-1.

Its norm has the printed ratio bound. The filtered graph is contained
in the depth-(d+t) Krylov space; enlarging the approximating subspace
cannot increase its largest principal angle to the fixed target. This
justifies the filtered inequality even when the larger Krylov space
has more than bd dimensions or has already saturated.

For the top bd target with mu>ell and lambda_bd>mu, mapping [ell,mu]
to [-1,1] and applying T_t gives absolute value <=1 on the tail and
at least (1/2) exp[t arcosh(1+2gamma)] on the head. The stated ratio
2 exp[-t arcosh(1+2gamma)] is correct. If every tail eigenvalue equals
one value mu distinct from the target, the factor z-mu annihilates the
tail and is nonzero on the head. Outer separation is necessary for
this convergence consequence; it was not silently inserted into the
initial angle theorem.

The theorem certifies existence of accurate vectors in the ordinary
block Krylov space. It does not by itself analyze finite precision,
restarted algorithms, a specific Rayleigh--Ritz vector selection for
arbitrary interior targets, or faster arithmetic per iteration. The
manuscript does not presently claim those conclusions.

8. FIXED PARTITION, QUANTIFIERS, AND PRIMARY-SOURCE SCOPE
-------------------------------------------------------

The stated result allows arbitrary within-block repetitions and arbitrary
ordering of blocks, but it requires a supplied partition into d full
blocks of size b whose spectra are pairwise disjoint. This implies that
no eigenvalue has multiplicity greater than b among the target rows.
The converse is false: with b=3,d=2, three distinct values of multiplicity
two cannot be put into two size-three blocks without splitting some
repeated value, which would make the cross-block gap zero. The current
caveat at lines 68--70 is correct and necessary.

The probability statement is also correct: for each deterministic
admissible input, the claimed event has probability at least 1-beta.
There is no claim that a single Gaussian draw works for all possible
spectra, or that a partition selected adaptively from the Gaussian draw
inherits this bound without an additional argument.

Primary-source inspection confirms that Shao v2 Eq. (1) defines its gap
using the supplied block partition and the full spectral diameter. Its
Section 3.2 corollary has the direct angle exponent 1-d. The present
theorem and corollary therefore address that direct conclusion, although
they do not prove the intermediate-factor conjecture used there.
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10144v2

The cited Stepaniants manuscript states a negative result for the exact
canonical intermediate chi_mono chi_coef quantity, already for b=2,d=3.
That negative result does not conflict with the present direct theorem:
the direct bound explicitly pays a power of the cross-block gap, and
does not assert that the intermediate factors are uniformly bounded.
Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/main/randomized-and-low-rank-approximation/KE-05/solution.md

Chen et al. Section 5 conjectures a sliding b-th-order relative gap and
removal of a condition-number parameter in its low-rank approximation
and Krylov-conditioning results. This is a different input parameter
from a supplied equal-size partition gap. The present manuscript must
not be described as resolving that entire conjecture merely from the
stated theorem. Its existing cautious novelty language is appropriate.
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06486v2

Only these directly relevant primary-source relationships were checked;
this audit is not an exhaustive search of orthogonal matrix-polynomial,
block-Lanczos, or random Krylov literature. The terminal identity uses
classical symmetric Krylov structure, and a lack of a located prior
statement is not evidence of priority. Before external significance
claims, retain a precise comparison of hypotheses, gap parameter,
dimension/confidence dependence, and the actual algorithmic consequence.
