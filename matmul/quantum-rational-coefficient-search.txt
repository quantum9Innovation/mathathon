1. THE FINITE RESULT
===================

Write f(x)=x log(x)-x+1 and f_2(x)=(x-1)^2/2 for x>0.
quantum-rational-coefficients.py generates exact positive rational numbers
t_i,c_i, i=1,...,25, and exact rational e,eta, satisfying

  e=1/2,000,000,        0<eta<1/1,000,000,
  R_0(x)=sum_i c_i/(x+t_i),
  f^-(x)=(x-1)^2 R_0(x)/(1+e),
  f^+(x)=f^-(x)+eta f_2(x),

  0<=f^-(x)<=f(x)<=f^+(x)       FOR EVERY x>0.       (1.1)

It exports the rational terms as both (t_i,c_i) and

  T_i=1/(1+t_i),       u_i=c_i/(1+t_i),

so (x-1)^2 R_0(x)=sum_i u_i (x-1)^2/[1+T_i(x-1)].
Thus ALL scalar data in the 50-block lower / 51-block upper entropy
SDPs can be rational, apart from the separate supplied matrix inputs.
The block order is 2N. Pointwise bracket width is eta*chi_P^2/2,
not an input-independent absolute entropy error. The parent paper's

The output is quantum-entropy-rational-rule.json. Numerator/denominator

3. A SYMMETRY REDUCTION: TWELVE ROOTS IN (0,1)
=============================================

The ideal construction has modified measure

  dnu(t)=dt/[(1+t)^3 P(t)],
  P(t)=prod_(j=1)^12 (t+a_j)^2 (t+1/a_j)^2,
  Omega(t)=t(t+1)P(t).                              (3.1)

P has degree48, P(t)>0 for t>0 and t^48 P(1/t)=P(t).
The measure has finite moments through degree49. Let q be its monic
degree25 orthogonal polynomial. Its roots are distinct and positive
by the parent paper's finite-moment Gaussian lemma. Substitution t=1/u
gives

  dnu(1/u)/u^2 = u^49 dnu(u).

Consequently t^25 q(1/t) is orthogonal to all degrees0,...,24:
its integral against t^k becomes the integral of q(u)u^(24-k).
Uniqueness of the orthogonal polynomial shows that its root set is
closed under t ->1/t. As there are25 positive distinct roots, exactly
one equals1 and the others are twelve reciprocal pairs.

If A_i are the ideal Gaussian weights, inversion transforms the rule
to another positive25-node rule exact through degree49, with weights
A_i t_i^49 at nodes1/t_i. Uniqueness therefore gives
A_(1/t)=A_t t^49. Since Omega(1/t)=t^-51 Omega(t), the rescaled
Stieltjes residues c(t)=A_t Omega(t) obey

  c(1/t)=c(t)/t^2.                                 (3.2)

Thus, with w_0>0 and w_j>0, the ideal interpolant is

  R(x)=w_0/(x+1)+sum_(j=1)^12 (w_j/2)
                         [t_j/(x+t_j)+(1/t_j)/(x+1/t_j)],
  0<t_j<1,             w_0+sum_j w_j=1.            (3.3)

The last identity is exact interpolation R(0)=H(0)=1,
where H(x)=f(x)/(x-1)^2. Interpolation at zero uses Gaussian exactness
on Omega(t)/t, a degree49 polynomial.

Set u=(x-1)/(x+1), z_j=(1-t_j)/(1+t_j), v_j=z_j^2.
Define the positive rational function

  A_*(y)=w_0+sum_(j=1)^12 w_j/(1-v_j y),
  0<v_j<1.

Direct substitution in (3.3) yields

  R(x)/(1-u)=[(1+u)A_*(u^2)-1]/(2u).             (3.4)

The removable u=0 limit is used. For the exact entropy kernel,

  H(x)/(1-u)=[(1+u)A(u^2)-1]/(2u),
  A(y)=atanh(sqrt(y))/sqrt(y),       A(0)=1.       (3.5)

The parent Gaussian rule interpolates H at a_j and1/a_j with their
first derivatives, and at0,1. Equations(3.4)--(3.5) show that A_* is
the type[12/12] Hermite interpolant to A at

  y_j=((1-a_j)/(1+a_j))^2, with multiplicity2,
  y=0, with multiplicity1.                         (3.6)

It is unique. Indeed its numerator P_*, monic denominator Q_*, both
degree<=12, are coprime since all twelve poles1/v_j are distinct
and their residues are nonzero. For any two solutions, the cross
polynomial has degree<=24 and25 zeros counted with multiplicity;
it vanishes identically. Coprimality then gives proportional coefficient
pairs, and normalization of the degree12 denominator makes them equal.
This also rules out spurious linear solutions having numerator and
denominator both zero at an interpolation point: compare such a
solution to the exact positive coprime one before canceling factors.

The25 unknown coefficients (13 numerator,12 denominator) are determined
by a25-by25 real linear system. It uses only rational y_j and

  z_j=(1-a_j)/(1+a_j),
  A(y_j)=-log(a_j)/(2 z_j),
  A'(y_j)=[1/(1-y_j)-A(y_j)]/(2 y_j).              (3.7)

The notation z_j in (3.7) denotes interpolation coordinates, whereas
the z_j in (3.4) denotes unknown pole coordinates; the program keeps
them in separate functions. No square root is needed for (3.7).

5. ROUNDING DOWN THE WHOLE STIELTJES FUNCTION
============================================

Let [t_i^-,t_i^+] and[c_i^-,c_i^+] be the certified positive intervals.
Round t_i^+ UP to60 significant decimal digits, obtaining rational
bar(t_i), and round c_i^- DOWN to60 significant digits, obtaining
rational under(c_i)>0. Define

  kappa=min_i under(c_i)/c_i^+ * t_i^-/bar(t_i).

Every quantity on the right is rational. For every x>0, term by term,

  kappa*c_i/(x+t_i)
    <=under(c_i)/(x+bar(t_i))<=c_i/(x+t_i).

Indeed (x+t_i)/(x+bar(t_i))>=t_i^-/bar(t_i), and the other
factors have the indicated signs. Summation proves

  kappa R(x)<=R_0(x)<=R(x),       0<kappa<=1.       (5.1)

Set eta=eta_*+2(1-kappa), EXACTLY. From (2.2),(5.1),

  f^-<=f_*^-<=f,
  f-f^- <= (f-f_*^-)+(1-kappa)f_*^-
         <=[eta_*+2(1-kappa)] f_2=eta f_2,

since f_*^-<=f<=2f_2 globally. This proves (1.1).
The verified coefficient loss obeys 1-kappa<5.499e-55, so exact
rational comparison certifies eta<1e-6. No original interpolation
equality is presumed to survive rational rounding; (5.1) replaces it.

The parent Petz spectral formula, with nonnegative scalar weights,
transfers (1.1) to the stated entropy brackets. Rationality and
