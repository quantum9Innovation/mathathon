Raw-factor algebraic search: exact-scalar reductions and the inertia escape
2026-11-14. Independent continuation; no shared manuscript/state was edited.

STATUS

No new algorithm meeting the unrestricted ~I*r+m^omega construction target
was established. This search gives two concrete results:

  (1) Exact scalar determinant/trace shortcuts are substantially stronger
      than needed: reverse differentiation turns several proposed raw-factor
      scalar oracles into full Gram/rectangular multiplication algorithms.
  (2) A cheap polynomial-ratio bracket for sigma_(r+1)^2 can be obtained
      directly from r+1 random product actions. Thus an inertia-only route
      need not compute the exact scalars in (1).

Standard full block-Wiedemann/Kalman construction, polynomial-matrix
compression, and batched exact updates do not presently yield the target
after raw-factor costs are included. An exact sign/inertia certificate that
avoids a dense Gram remains the substantive missing mechanism. These are
scoped reductions and cost audits, not lower bounds on approximate spectral
algorithms or a new matrix multiplication theorem. No novelty is claimed
for reverse AD, Gaussian compression, Schur complements, or Krylov methods.

Read first: research-direction.typ, current research-state.txt, relevant
approach-ledger.txt entries, ridge-filters.tex, and factor-pca-search.txt.
The completed hierarchy is preserved; its original theta=1/m hard case

1. EXACT SCALAR PRODUCT NORM ALREADY RECOVERS THE FULL GRAM

Proposition (straight-line arithmetic reduction).
Suppose a field-arithmetic program computes exactly

  f(A,B)=||AB||_F^2=tr(A^T A B B^T)

from raw A(n,m),B(m,p), at cost T(n,m,p). Reverse differentiation gives
all entries of its B-gradient with constant-factor arithmetic overhead:

  Y=grad_B f=2(A^T A)B.

Choose B to be a fresh dense Gaussian m-by-p matrix, p>=m. Its first
m columns B0 are invertible almost surely. Therefore the complete Gram is

  A^T A = (1/2)Y[:,1:m] B0^(-1).

The total is O(T+n*m+m*p+m^omega), including random entries, the entire
gradient output, and the small inverse/product. No tall transform, exact
Gram, factor norm, or input-conditioned solve is hidden. This works for
arbitrary fixed A, including rank deficiency, because only B0 is inverted.

The reverse chain rule for +,-,*,/ takes O(1) operations per original gate,
so the statement counts ALL operations, not merely multiplications. The
primary Baur--Strassen theorem proves constant-overhead differentiation in
the nonscalar model and explicitly notes analogous all-operation bounds:
W. Baur,V. Strassen, The complexity of partial derivatives, TCS22(1983),
Theorem1 and introduction, pp317--318:
https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/tropical/Baur-Strassen.pdf .

Scope: the clean statement is for exact arithmetic circuits/straight-line
programs. It also applies to exact algebraic algorithms on a generic smooth
branch, with usual nonzero-pivot guards. For fixed A, a Gaussian B avoids
nontrivial algebraic branch boundaries almost surely. One must NOT blindly
differentiate an arbitrary Monte Carlo approximation, an integer-valued
comparison oracle, or a program known correct only at one isolated input.
Positive-root primitives can be differentiated on positive smooth branches;
the field-only statement suffices for the exact polynomial algorithms under
consideration here. Storage for the reverse pass is allowed by the present
arithmetic model; it is at most proportional to the program size.

Rectangular multiplication consequence. Given X,Y(n,m), apply a Gram
algorithm to [X Y](n,2m). Its off-diagonal block is X^T Y. Thus a scalar
oracle with T=~O(m(n+p)+m^omega) would give exact rectangular multiplication
in ~O(n*m+m^omega) at constant-factor-expanded widths. In the hard shape
n=p=N=m^(9/8), this would replace the checked Gram exponent169/72 in
powers of m by9/4. It is a real matrix multiplication improvement, not a
free scalar-statistic shortcut. This is NOT an unconditional lower bound:
such a multiplication improvement may be possible.

In particular, the exact trace tr(PQ), P=A^T A,Q=BB^T, is not an
obviously cheaper primitive just because it has one scalar output. It is
already f above. Approximate stochastic estimates do not inherit this exact
AD reduction and are not excluded by it.


2. EVEN ONE-FACTOR GRAM MOMENTS AND DETERMINANTS HAVE THIS ISSUE

For G=X^T X and generic full-column-rank X(N,m), define

  f2(X)=tr(G^2),       grad f2=4XG;
  e2(G)=((tr G)^2-tr(G^2))/2,
                    grad_X e2=2X[(tr G)I-G];
  d(X)=det(I+G),      grad d=2X adj(I+G).

The first m rows X0 are invertible for generic X. Multiplying the same
rows of a gradient by X0^(-1) recovers G immediately for f2/e2 (trG is
computable in O(Nm)). For d, recover H=adj(I+G), then

  G=d*H^(-1)-I.

The small postprocessing costs O(m^omega). Thus an exact raw second
spectral moment, second elementary coefficient, or positive-shift Gram
determinant at the proposed target cost also gives the complete Gram.
The determinant is never zero, so no tiny-eigenvalue condition enters.

To turn the generic-X statements into arbitrary-X algorithms, draw a dense
Gaussian R and use the routine on X+R,X-R,R. Each is generic almost surely,
including its first m rows, and polarization gives

  G(X)=[G(X+R)+G(X-R)]/2-G(R).

This costs three evaluations, O(Nm) extra arithmetic/random samples, and
small-core algebra. This genericization also avoids pivot-branch derivative
issues for otherwise exact rational programs. It does not justify AD of a
mere approximate determinant value; derivative accuracy needs its own proof.

Computing the full exact characteristic polynomial includes these harder
coefficients/determinant evaluations. A direct raw-factor charpoly routine
with ~I+m^omega cost would therefore carry an exact rectangular-MM advance.
For a product routine, one may set B=[I_m 0] so that its nonzero squared
singular values are those of X. The two-factor trace reduction in Section1
is cleaner when the oracle is valid at generic B rather than a fixed
special sparse B.

The determinant/adjoint equivalence is classical, not new. Villard's
explicit differentiation of the Kaltofen determinant algorithm makes this
connection concrete for Krylov/baby-step algorithms:
https://arxiv.org/html/0804.1021v1 , Sections1--2.
The raw rectangular recovery above is an elementary consequence whose
literature priority was not established in this search.


3. CURRENT EXACT CHARPOLY/KRYLOV PAPERS DO NOT GIVE A RAW-FACTOR ORACLE

Neiger--Pernet2021 Theorem1.1 takes an explicitly supplied square matrix
and computes its characteristic polynomial in O(m^omega) field operations,
with zero tests. Its polynomial-matrix algorithms do not make the entries
of a composed raw Gram free:
https://arxiv.org/html/2010.04662v2 .

Neiger--Pernet--Villard2024 compute maximal Krylov bases from a supplied
square A and initial U in O(m^omega loglogm), and obtain the smaller-block
case in O(m^omega). Their polynomial fractions represent (I-xA)^(-1)U;
they do not state the same bound given only a tall Gram factor or separate
A,B factors. Replacing dense multiplication by an implicit operator at
every polynomial-matrix step requires a new cost proof:
https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07345v2 , introduction and Theorem4.1.

Important new exact-algebra update: Villard, On computing the determinant
over a ring, posted Nov5,2026, proves O(m^omega) division-free and zero-test-
free determinant/charpoly circuits for a GIVEN square matrix. Its compression
and homotopy are genuine strong tools, but still do not supply the input
entries of P,Q or PQ. Applying it to a large raw bordered matrix has that
large dimension, not m. The straight-line nature also makes Section1's
AD warning especially direct:
https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07786v1 , Theorem5.1 and introduction.

No result in these inspected statements bypasses raw Gram formation.
Conversely, this is not evidence that a structure-exploiting extension is
impossible; it identifies the new theorem such an extension would prove.


4. FULL BLOCK-WIEDEMANN/KALMAN COST, WITH FAST BATCHING

For a dimension-m implicit core Gram/product operator, standard generic
block Wiedemann uses approximately2m/b consecutive b-column moments.
The arithmetic for each operator action is at least CHARGED through the
raw factors, rather than m^2b. A primary description gives the sequence
length2n/b+2 and generator degree<=ceil(n/b): Kaltofen's survey exposition,
https://users.cs.duke.edu/~elk27/bibliography/96/Ka96_stratagem.pdf .
The modern certificate paper describes the block sequence and qL~n scalar
applications; a cheap VERIFIER is not a cheap constructor:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00810 .

Use the strongest rectangular bound already adopted in the project. In the
hard case N=m^a,a=9/8,b=m^c, one factor action has exponent

  f(c)=a+1,                            c<=a/(a+1)=9/17;
  f(c)=(a+(a+1)c)^2/(4ac),             9/17<=c<=1.

The consecutive moment-generation term has exponent f(c)+1-c. In the
first branch it decreases with c. In the second branch it equals

  a/(4c)+(a+1)/2+1+[(a-1)^2/(4a)]c.

Its derivative is negative for c<=1 when a=9/8, so this checked cost is
minimized at c=1:169/72, precisely the exact Gram bound. At the largest
input-optimal block it is353/136=2.595588..., and at b=sqrt(m) it is
21/8=2.625, both worse than Gram. Adding generator/core algebra only
increases these costs. The target is9/4.

This is an upper-bound optimization for the STANDARD consecutive-moment
construction, not a black-box lower bound for all determinant algorithms.
Generic maximal Krylov basis construction also needs m total basis columns;
Keller-Gehrig doubling avoids scalar passes only when suitable square
powers/compressed polynomial operators are actually supplied. Materializing
those powers from raw factors is the omitted operation. A low true minimal
polynomial or known structured invariant space may help, but is an additional
promise not valid for every input.

Baby-step/giant-step determinant algorithms explicitly compute a dense
power A^s before their giant steps (see Villard2008 AlgorithmDet, step2).
Replacing that step by raw-factor action does not magically square an
operator. No valid O(I+m^omega) construction resulted from that substitution.


5. RAW BORDERED PENCILS AND WHY ELIMINATION MERELY HIDES THE GRAMS

Even det(I+X^T X) has a raw sparse bordered representation

  det[ I_N  X ; -X^T  I_m ] = det(I_m+X^T X).

Its dimension is N+m with O(Nm) nonzeros. The dense exact algorithms cost
(N+m)^omega. Eliminating I_N explicitly produces the Gram. A putative
specialized near-input-plus-core exact determinant algorithm for this
arrow structure would be a new rectangular-MM algorithm by Section2.

For the general singular threshold z>0, use the raw symmetric pencil

  L_z = [ zI_n    0       A       0
            0    zI_p     0      B^T
           A^T    0       0      -I_m
            0     B      -I_m     0 ].

The inner block D0=[0 -I;-I 0] is its own inverse and has m positive,
m negative eigenvalues. Its Schur complement is

  S_z=[zI_n AB;(AB)^T zI_p].

Therefore negative_inertia(L_z)=m+#{sigma_i(AB)>z}. Conversely eliminating
the positive outer diagonals produces the2m core

  [ -(A^T A)/z   -I ; -I  -(BB^T)/z ],

so ordinary elimination pays exactly for P and Q. The bordered expression
is a useful correct linearization, not a cost saving by itself.

An uncompressed rank-one Woodbury update to a dense m-core requires its
current inverse applied to the new raw row; batching b rows costs dense
core/block products and small inversions. A merge tree that constructs exact
core sums ultimately forms the same full Gram. Fast factor batching or
polynomial elimination must be charged at every stage; no free transformed
tall factors or uncharged Schur complements were found in this search.


6. CHEAP TARGET-SCALE INITIALIZATION THAT DOES ESCAPE THE EXACT-SCALAR ISSUE

There is no need to compute a full exact spectral moment merely to obtain
a polynomial-ratio initial bracket for sigma_j^2,j=r+1. This elementary
Gaussian-compression lemma is useful if an inertia-only primitive is found.

Draw Omega(p,j) standard Gaussian and orthonormalize it to Q. Compute
Y=M Q through raw factors and the SMALL compression Hs=Y^T Y(j,j).
For j<=m, even conventional costs are O(I*j)+O(m^omega): p*j^2 and
n*j^2 are bounded by I*j, and all j-core algebra fits m^omega.

Let lambda_j=sigma_j(M)^2>0 and let beta_j=lambda_min(Hs). Compression
min--max gives beta_j<=lambda_j. In the top-j true right singular space,

  Hs >=lambda_j*(U_j^T Q)^T(U_j^T Q),

so beta_j>=lambda_j*sigma_min(U_j^T Q)^2. A Gaussian square-head
distance bound gives sigma_min(U_j^T Omega)>=delta/(C*j^(3/2)), and
Markov gives ||Omega||_F^2<=C*p*j/delta, jointly with probability1-delta
after constant budget changes. Thus

  beta_j >=lambda_j*delta^3/(C*p*j^4).

For rho=e_j(Hs)/e_(j-1)(Hs), the exact small coefficient lemma gives
beta_j/j<=rho<=beta_j. Consequently

  rho<=lambda_j<=[C*p*j^5/delta^3]*rho

on this event. Only dimension/confidence logarithms enter subsequent
bisection, irrespective of lambda_1/lambda_j or input conditioning.

If rank(Hs)<j, the Gaussian product sketch has rank=rank(M)<j almost
surely, so its range gives an exact width<=r support projection of M.
Compute M^T times that basis through the factors and expand. This also
handles zero optimal tails without full Gram formation. Rank failure
checks have fixed costs; there is no unbounded restart.

This Gaussian-compression scale lemma is standard in spirit and not
claimed new. Its role here is to show that avoiding exact full scalars
does not force a spectral-ratio search. The genuinely missing step is
the threshold decision/certificate, not target-scale initialization.


7. WHAT AN INERTIA-ONLY BREAKTHROUGH STILL HAS TO SUPPLY

Exact inertia is integer-valued and locally constant away from roots;
its derivative is zero. Sections1--2 therefore do NOT turn a sign-only
raw-factor algorithm into a full Gram algorithm. This is a real possible
escape from the exact-scalar reductions, not a proof that such an algorithm
exists. Near-input construction still needs more than a count: it must
produce an adequate physical subspace without paying for a dense congruence.

A precise certificate target for the symmetric S_z above is a width<=r
matrix W=[U;V] such that S_z is PSD on W-perp. Such a dual negative-pivot
certificate is sufficient. For x perpendicular to

  U-(1/z)M V,

the graph vector [x;-(1/z)M^T x] belongs to W-perp. Its S_z quadratic
equals z||x||^2-||M^T x||^2/z, so the physical left space
range(U-MV/z) has residual<=z. Mapping the certificate costs O(I*r),
and exact range algebra/output have already been accounted. This is the
proper orientation; merely taking W's left block need not work.

At z just above sigma_(r+1), S_z has at most r negative eigenvalues.
Full Gaussian-congruence LDL supplies this certificate but currently pays
for dense product/Gram work. A raw arrow-pivot algorithm exploiting only
the EXCESS negative index beyond the known m of D0 could be a different
mechanism. It must provide both the count/threshold logic and these dual
columns within ~I*r+m^omega. No such construction is proved here.

Oblivious partial-principal compression is not enough in general. For

  K=I-(1+gamma)uu^T, ||u||=1, gamma=1/m,

there is one negative eigenvalue. If u is uniform, every proper coordinate
principal submatrix is PSD: negativity needs subset size>m/(1+gamma)>m-1.
For a Gaussian b-dimensional subspace, negativity instead requires
||Proj(u)||^2>1/(1+gamma). Its probability is at most(1+gamma)*b/m by
Markov, since the expected projection energy is b/m. Thus a small number
of independent sublinear random compressions misses the negative direction
with high probability. This is NOT an adaptive lower bound: a single
K-action reveals u in this specially structured example. It only rejects
the proposed oblivious small-principal-subproblem shortcut.


8. CONCRETE NEXT PROOF OBLIGATIONS AND HONEST OUTCOME

The algebraic route would become substantial if it proved ONE of:

  A. A raw symmetric bordered-pencil inertia algorithm with a small dual
     negative-index certificate, at ~I*r+m^omega cost, avoiding the dense
     Schur frontier. Counts alone do not establish returned low-rank factors.
  B. A new exact rectangular-MM algorithm. The determinant/trace circuits
     above would be legitimate ways to obtain one, but cannot be invoked
     as known cheap scalar primitives.
  C. An adaptive approximate sign/certificate algorithm whose AD does not
     imply an exact Gram and whose error control does not restore the
     isotropic-sketch cap or theta^(-1/2) raw passes.

Any candidate must count raw transformations, inner products of wide
iterates, generator construction, degree-dependent polynomial work, and
the output. Its initial scale can use Section6, so no true singular-value
ratio logarithm is necessary. A favorable scalar low-minimal-polynomial
promise is not the unrestricted theorem requested.

No candidate surviving all those obligations was found in this search.
The exact-scalar reductions and the raw-pencil/dual-certificate formulation
are the concrete mathematical progress. They do not settle the research
goal or prove it impossible. The hard target remains N=m^(9/8),r=1,
theta=1/m,total N^(2+o(1)); the completed shape-limited hierarchy remains
valid and is not being replaced by this unsuccessful algebraic search.


9. PRIORITY CONTINUATION: ORDER-SENSITIVE RAW PARTIAL KRYLOV

The parent subsequently proposed a significantly better targeted primitive:
given raw X(N,m),v(m), compute ONLY

  K_q(X^T X,v)=[v,Hv,...,H^(q-1)v]

in ~O(I_X*q^(omega-2)+m*q^2+m^omega), where I_X=N*m. This is an
OPEN CANDIDATE, not disproved by Sections1--4 and not established by the
sources inspected here. The earlier full-moment audit must not be applied
to it as if q=m. Its order-dependent input overhead is materially different
from a hypothetical full exact scalar oracle at input-plus-core cost.

At the original shape I=m^(17/8),q=~sqrt(m),omega=9/4, its input term is
m^(17/8+1/8)=m^(9/4), its m*q^2 term is m^2 times logarithms, and its
core is m^(9/4). Thus it would be a major general algorithmic improvement
with a decisive solution of the original hard total-output instance.
It retains polynomial dependence on accuracy in the general tradeoff;
it is not yet the stronger logarithmic-accuracy target for every geometry.

9.1 Precise truncated polynomial problem

For a supplied constant small coupling J, define

  F(z)=[ I_N      -X
        -z J X^T  I_m ].

The lower part of F(z)^(-1)[0;v] is

  w(z)=(I-z J X^T X)^(-1)v mod z^q.

The coefficients are the requested partial Krylov sequence. Identity J
gives the one-Gram problem. The signs here are chosen to give I-zH;
the alternative plus sign merely alternates coefficients.

The promising precise obligation is an ORDER-q approximant/kernel or
fraction computation on this raw bordered matrix, whose cost depends on
q^(omega-2), rather than computing its full minimal denominator first.
One may normalize the truncated right denominator to one: the desired
power-series vector is unique modulo z^q. Full generic denominator degree
can still be m, regardless of q, so computing the full exact rational
fraction is stronger than needed.

NPV2024 Lemma3.2 gives an arbitrary prescribed-order Krylov expansion,
but still pays an O(n^omega) FULL minimal-kernel-basis setup before its
truncated inverse/product steps. Its improved high-order lifting is not a
proof of the raw, low-order bound above. No order-sensitive raw kernel
bound of the proposed form was located. The target is therefore a concrete
new polynomial-matrix theorem, not a substitution already licensed by NPV.

9.2 Required extension from covariance to arbitrary factor products

Computing short Krylov for ONE Gram is not automatically enough for AB:
forming X=A*(BB^T)^(1/2) would hide a true Gram and a full tall transform.
There is, however, a clean constant-factor raw formulation. Set

  Xraw=diag(A,B^T), G=Xraw^T Xraw=diag(P,Q),
  J=[0 I;I 0].

Then (JG)^2=diag(QP,PQ). A short-Krylov routine supporting the fixed
permutation J therefore handles product-of-Grams powers at only twice
the order and dimension, without whitening or forming either Gram.
The raw input size stays O(I), and the saddle matrix in9.1 applies with
this explicit J. A theorem restricted to J=I must not silently claim this
extension; the coefficient matrix is now generally nonsymmetric, although
the algebraic truncated-series task is still well defined.

For physical left Krylov, M M^T(Ax)=A(QP)x. Initialize a suitable raw
coefficient seed through Q A^T times a physical Gaussian vector and then
lift the product-of-Grams iterates by A. This gives exactly the ordinary
physical Krylov space, not an unrelated coefficient-space projection.
Extra seed/operator actions are within O(I) and constant order factors.

9.3 All downstream hard-case costs fit if the primitive is proved

With q=~sqrt(m), the complete m-by-q coefficient block is lifted by A
in T_MM(N,m,q)=m^(17/8+o(1)) in the original shape: q remains inside
the input-optimal range c<=9/17. Even conventional physical QR costs
N*q^2=m^(17/8) times logs. Full-basis M^T actions, compressed Gram
construction, and final physical rank-one projection also fit belowm^(9/4).
The small q-core can be extracted within q^omega plus lower physical
costs by the exact-field Gram/LDL routine already established; even a
cubic q-core arithmetic charge is onlym^(3/2). Full output is
N^2=m^(9/4). Thus no hidden postprocessing obstacle invalidates this
conditional route. The missing theorem is genuinely sequence generation.

9.4 Why obvious batching/Padé recursion does not yet prove it

The unknown Krylov columns cannot simply be passed to one fast rectangular
multiply. A row-update Sherman--Morrison formula shows the closure problem.
For R0(z)=(I-zH0)^(-1), adding xx^T changes the RHS expansion to

  Rnew(z)v=R0(z)v
      +z R0(z)x [x^T R0(z)v]/[1-z x^T R0(z)x].

It requires R0(z)x and x^T R0(z)x for the NEW seed x, not just the
stored expansion R0(z)v. For a concrete PSD example, let v=e1 and
H0=diag(1,a). Its entire old Krylov sequence is independent of a.
After adding x=(1,1)^T,

  (H0+xx^T)^2 v = [5,a+3]^T,

which depends on the previously invisible coordinate. Thus a merge rule
retaining only each child's one-RHS truncated expansion is not sufficient.
The raw input may retain that coordinate, so this is a state-summary
counterexample, not a lower bound on a better global algorithm.

The same issue defeats naive divide-and-conquer over raw rows: powers of
H1+H2 require noncommuting mixed words, not just powers of each child on
v. Keller-Gehrig doubling needs H^(2^j) actions or a suitable polynomial
fraction; counting those as free reintroduces the desired primitive itself.
Low displacement rank of the final Hankel moment matrix only helps AFTER
its moments/generators are available. It does not by itself generate them
from the raw factors.

Batched negative-shift solves are a different possible construction of a
rational space, not exact coefficients of this nilpotent/order-q expansion.
Confluent interpolation near zero can require exponentially accurate solves;
the existing coefficient-growth audit prevents hiding that refinement work
in termwise error control. This does not preclude a new stable simultaneous
extraction, but it is not currently a proof of exact partial Krylov.

9.5 Current assessment of the targeted candidate

No decisive algebraic obstruction was found for the proposed q-sensitive
bound, and the exact-scalar AD reductions do not exclude it. No inspected
primary theorem supplies it either. The useful precise next lemma is:

  Solve the rank-one-RHS raw saddle expansion F(z)^(-1)[0;v] moduloz^q
  in ~O(I*q^(omega-2)+m*q^2+m^omega), uniformly for a fixed coordinate
  permutation J, without a full degree-m kernel/fraction setup.

A proof should exhibit its actual truncated representation, show closure
under whatever recursion is used, and charge every raw block multiplication.
It must not assume that a full square operator, unknown future iterates,
or all transformed tall factors are already present. If proved, its physical
Krylov consequence and hard-case total bound above are ready to use.
