Independent coordinator audit: signed positive-part candidate
============================================================
2026-11-14. Working proof audit, not a priority certificate.

The active broad N^2/seminal-result goal is NOT achieved by this candidate.
The result concerns S=X J X^T with small positive index, at threshold zero.
It does not yet apply cheaply to AB's nonzero singular-value threshold.

Candidate statement checked here
-------------------------------
X is N-by-m, N>=m, and J is a fixed symmetric involution. More general
explicit symmetric J can be treated by a small-core congruence, with its
cost and implicit tall-factor application charged. Let q=n_+(S)<=r.
In a real-arithmetic model with comparisons, positive square roots and
Gaussian draws, return PSD factors of width <=q with

  ||C-S_+||_2 <= eps ||S_+||_2

with probability >=1-delta, using conventional arithmetic

  ~O(N m (r+1)+m^3),

plus O(N^2 r) if the full dense result is explicitly requested. Logarithms
may involve dimensions, 1/eps and 1/delta, but no ratio between the positive
and negative spectral scales. This is an exact-arithmetic claim; the
characteristic-polynomial normalization below is not a finite-word claim.
The signed-resolvent lemma from raw-factor-fresh-search.txt is an input to
this audit; its entire proof was not re-audited in this file.

1. Support and positive norm scale
---------------------------------
A constant-distortion fast OSE gives G0=L^T L with ker(L)=ker(X). With
R=L^T(LL^T)^(-1), Z=XR and K=L J L^T, one has S=Z K Z^T and

  g_lo I <= Z^T Z <= g_hi I,

for absolute positive constants. Neither Z nor a full tall transformed
factor is formed. All thin applications cost O(Nm*width) conventionally.
The positive index is that of the explicit supported core K, by inertia.
If it is zero, output zero. If a=lambda_max(K)>0, generalized Rayleigh
quotients give

  g_lo a <= ||S_+|| <= g_hi a.

The worker supplied a magnitude-independent approximation of a. For an
even power of two d>=log_2(k), k=order(K), form K^d. Its nonzero eigenvalues
are mu_j^d, with mu_j=|lambda_j(K)| decreasing. Elementary coefficients
e_j of its characteristic polynomial obey

  mu_j^d/j <= e_j/e_(j-1) <= (k-j+1)mu_j^d.

Lower bound: every (j-1)-subset can be extended by a missing top-j index,
whose eigenvalue is >=mu_j^d; a j-subset is counted at most j times.
Upper bound: remove a largest-index element from each j-subset; its value
is <=mu_j^d and each (j-1)-subset has at most k-j+1 extensions.

Successive square roots give a_j=(e_j/e_(j-1))^(1/d) in [mu_j/2,2mu_j].
Newton log-concavity makes a_j nonincreasing. Binary-search the first j
with n_+(K-a_j I/4)>0. If mu_l=a, this predicate is true by l, hence

  a/2 <= a_j <4a.

Thus v=4 g_hi a_j satisfies 2||S_+||<=v<=64||S_+|| for a coarse ratio
g_hi/g_lo<=4. Matrix powers, a cubic characteristic-polynomial routine,
and O(log k) inertia calls cost ~O(m^3). The root argument is related to
classical Graeffe/root-radius methods; no novelty is claimed for it.

2. A bounded filter independent of the negative scale
---------------------------------------------------
Put T=S/v, f(x)=x_+/(1+x^2), and F=f(T). Positive eigenvalues of T are
at most 1/2, so 0<=F<=2I/5 and rank(F)=q. The scalar identity

 f(x) = (1/2) x/(1+x^2)
       +(1/pi) integral_R x^2 exp(y)
                         /[(x^2+exp(2y))(1+x^2)] dy

holds for every real x. The log-integrand is bounded by
min(exp(y),exp(-y)) uniformly in x. On each closed strip
|Im(y)|<=d0<pi/2, the denominator is bounded below by a constant times
(x^2+|exp(y)|^2)(1+x^2). This gives an integrable uniform boundary bound.
Poisson summation / the strip trapezoidal estimate therefore gives
error O(exp(-c/h)) at step h. Truncating |y|<=O(log(1/eta)) gives error
O(eta). Taking h=Theta(1/log(1/eta)) yields O(log^2(1/eta)) nodes.
All poles in x are imaginary; there is no shift near a nonzero real
threshold. Half-shifted logarithmic nodes avoid t=exp(y)=1 collisions.

The quadrature has f_hat(0)=0 exactly. Its terms are rational functions
of x with factors (x^2+t^2)^(-1), (1+x^2)^(-1). Partial fractions or
sequential resolvent actions use only imaginary shifts. All coefficients,
nodes and inverse pole separations are at worst polynomial in 1/eta;
coarse-refinement accuracy therefore costs logarithms, not eta^(-c).

Each node can simply factor its m-by-m coarse shifted core conventionally.
There are only polylogarithmically many nodes. The nongalactic theorem
does not require Frobenius normal form or fast matrix multiplication.

3. Two actions and the small inverse function
--------------------------------------------
Draw a Gaussian Omega with width O(q+log(1/delta)), capped at supported
dimension if necessary. In the supported eigenbasis, standard Gaussian
events give ||Omega||<=L, sigma_min(Omega_head)>=beta with L/beta
polynomial in m and 1/delta. The exact ambient kernel is irrelevant
because the filter vanishes at zero and its implementation can cancel
identity terms analytically, returning vectors in range(X).

For Y=f_hat(T)Omega and Q=orth(Y), put chi=L/beta. The direct rank-q
identity F=F Omega Omega_head^dagger U_head^T gives

  ||(I-QQ^T)F|| <= eta chi.

Apply f_hat(T) a SECOND time to Q. Symmetrizing its compressed matrix
B does not increase its error from B0=Q^T F Q, which is PSD, rank<=q,
and bounded by 2I/5. Clip B's eigenvalues to [0,2/5] and keep at most q.
Weyl's inequality shows this changes B by at most eta. Consequently

  F_hat=Q B_hat Q^T >=0, rank(F_hat)<=q,
  ||F_hat-F|| <=2 eta(chi+1).

For z in [0,2/5], define psi(z)=(1-sqrt(1-4z^2))/(2z), psi(0)=0.
Its positive Catalan series z+z^3+2z^5+5z^7+... proves, by telescoping
matrix powers, the OPERATOR Lipschitz bound

  ||psi(A)-psi(B)|| <=(25/12)||A-B||

for PSD A,B of norm<=2/5. This is stronger than merely observing scalar
Lipschitz continuity, which alone would not justify the matrix claim.
Since psi(f(x))=x for 0<=x<=1/2, and f vanishes on negative x,

  ||v psi(F_hat)-S_+|| <=(25/6)v eta(chi+1).

Choose eta=eps/[1000(chi+1)] to leave room for numerical function-action
and small-core errors. All new tolerances have only logarithmic cost.
Apply psi only to the small PSD core and lift its square-root factors.
An unfiltered Q^T S Q Ritz computation is deliberately not used: an
arbitrarily large negative bulk would make that argument invalid.

4. What this audit does and does not establish
--------------------------------------------
The algebra, scale normalization, Gaussian range identity, second-action
reconstruction and operator Lipschitz step pass this independent review.
Core and thin-factor costs fit the claimed conventional envelope, assuming
the existing raw imaginary-resolvent lemma and a fast OSE implementation.
No large hardware benchmark or fabricated numerical experiment is needed
to justify these analytic steps. A finite-word stability theorem, complete
constants for the strip quadrature, and independent review of every backend
lemma are separate obligations if a formal manuscript is pursued.

Priority remains OPEN. In particular, for full-column-rank X, Sylvester's
law gives n_+(S)=n_+(J). Small positive inertia then amounts to a rank-q
positive update of a negative PSD matrix after a core congruence. Classical
secular, Birman--Schwinger and low-rank update methods are serious possible
antecedents. Comparing only to randomized PSD-projection papers does not
settle this issue. Nor does this theorem prove a broad N^2 algorithm.
