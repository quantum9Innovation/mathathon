This note proves an extension of the candidate manuscript
\texttt{relative-fractional-response.tex}. The extension is a mathematical
statement, not a claim of historical novelty or a fast construction algorithm.
The path lower bound below uses the candidate manuscript's proved one-edge
positive-update path realization. Its transfer steps are independently
checked in Section~\ref{sec:audit}.

\section{Two rank-one edits: full closure with cancellation}

Fix $0<\alpha<1$, $n\ge1$, and $D_+,D_-\ge1$. Consider triples
\[
 A\succ0,\qquad H=A+uu^T=B+vv^T,\qquad B\succ0,
 \quad
 \frac{\det H}{\det A}=D_+,\quad
 \frac{\det H}{\det B}=D_-.
\]
Equivalently, $u^TA^{-1}u=D_+-1$ and $v^TB^{-1}v=D_--1$.
The positive and negative edits can cancel. They need not be orthogonal.
Put $Q=A^{-\alpha/2}B^\alpha A^{-\alpha/2}$, and write $x_+=\max(x,0)$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete signed relative spectral closure]
\label{thm:signed}
The closure of the ordered spectra of $Q$ is precisely the set of
$q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_n>0$ such that
\begin{align}
 \sum_i\log q_i&=\alpha\log(D_+/D_-),\label{eq:det}\\
 \sum_i(\log q_i)_+&\le\alpha\log D_+,\label{eq:pos}\\
 \sum_i(-\log q_i)_+&\le\alpha\log D_-.\label{eq:neg}
\end{align}
In particular two rank-one signed edits can spread their surviving positive
and negative logarithmic budgets arbitrarily among the relative eigenvalues.
\end{theorem}

The last two inequalities have the same slack, by \eqref{eq:det}. A
nonzero common slack measures cancellation invisible to the final determinant.
For $n=1$ the sole eigenvalue is $(D_+/D_-)^\alpha$; the theorem includes
this case. No assertion of finite-condition-number attainment is made.

\paragraph{Precise classical subsumption.}
For $n\ge2$, conditions \eqref{eq:det}--\eqref{eq:neg} are exactly
\[
 \log q\prec\alpha\log(D_+,1,\ldots,1,D_-^{-1}).
\]
Indeed all the nonfinal prefix bounds on the right have the same value
$\alpha\log D_+$; their maximum on the left is controlled by the
positive log sum and the final determinant constraint. The companion audit
\texttt{araki-horn-relative-range.tex} proves that for any fixed ordinary
relative input spectrum $r$, the fractional relative spectral closure is
$\log q\prec\alpha\log r$. Its proof combines Araki log-majorization,
the real Weyl--Horn triangular realization theorem, and classical
separated-scale Schur asymptotics. Therefore Theorem~\ref{thm:signed}
is a corollary of that general classical synthesis. In fact its
sufficiency can be achieved while requiring $u^TA^{-1}v=0$: choose
$S$ with eigenvalues $(D_+,1,\ldots,1,D_-^{-1})$, add its positive
eigenvector correction to $I$, and subtract its negative eigenvector
correction to obtain $S$. Scaling by $A^{1/2}$ preserves that
$A^{-1}$ orthogonality and both exact budgets. The explicit proof below
is useful for checking cancellation, but is not independent novelty.

\begin{lemma}[General separated-scale power limit]\label{lem:scale}
Let $M\succ0$ be fixed, $a_i=\delta^{n-i}$,
$A_\delta=\diag(a_i)$, and
$X_\delta=A_\delta^{1/2}MA_\delta^{1/2}$.
Let $d_i$ be the reverse Schur pivots of $M$:
\[
 d_i=M_{ii}-M_{i,>i}M_{>i,>i}^{-1}M_{>i,i},\quad d_n=M_{nn}.
\]
Then
\[
 A_\delta^{-\alpha/2}X_\delta^\alpha A_\delta^{-\alpha/2}
       \longrightarrow\diag(d_i^\alpha)
 \quad\hbox{in norm as }\delta\downarrow0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $c_\alpha=\sin(\pi\alpha)/\pi$. On a diagonal entry, use
$X^\alpha=c_\alpha\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}X(X+tI)^{-1}\,dt$
and substitute $t=a_i s$. Since
\[
 a_i(X_\delta+a_i sI)^{-1}_{ii}
  =\bigl[M+s a_iA_\delta^{-1}\bigr]^{-1}_{ii},
\]
eliminating indices $j<i$ whose added diagonal tends to infinity, and then
the $>i$ block, gives pointwise convergence of the diagonal integrand to
$s^{\alpha-1}d_i/(d_i+s)$. The integrand is bounded by
$s^{\alpha-1}\min(1,M_{ii}/s)$ because
$0\preceq X(X+tI)^{-1}\preceq I$ and $X(X+tI)^{-1}\preceq X/t$.
This is integrable at both endpoints. Dominated convergence and the scalar
fractional-power integral prove the diagonal assertion.

For off-diagonal entries choose $m>0$ with $M\succeq mI$ and put
$C=M-mI\succeq0$. The Woodbury identity around $mA_\delta+tI$ gives,
for $i\ne j$,
\[
 \left|(X_\delta+tI)^{-1}_{ij}\right|
 \le \norm C\frac{\sqrt{a_i a_j}}{(ma_i+t)(ma_j+t)}.
\]
Indeed the middle Woodbury inverse is
$[I+C^{1/2}A_\delta^{1/2}(mA_\delta+tI)^{-1}
A_\delta^{1/2}C^{1/2}]^{-1}\preceq I$.
The off-diagonal fractional integral therefore yields, for $i<j$ and
$x=a_i/a_j$,
\[
 \left|a_i^{-\alpha/2}(X_\delta^\alpha)_{ij}a_j^{-\alpha/2}\right|
 \le \norm C\,m^{\alpha-1}
 x^{(1-\alpha)/2}\frac{1-x^\alpha}{1-x}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here we used the scalar divided-difference integral. Symmetry handles the
other entries, and the dimension is fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:signed}]
Since $B^\alpha\preceq H^\alpha$, the $i$th ordered generalized eigenvalue
of $(B^\alpha,A^\alpha)$ is bounded by that of $(H^\alpha,A^\alpha)$.
The latter are all at least one. Consequently
\[
 \sum_i(\log q_i)_+
 \le\log\det(A^{-\alpha/2}H^\alpha A^{-\alpha/2})
 =\alpha\log D_+.
\]
Interchanging $A,B$ proves the negative budget because the generalized
eigenvalues are reciprocated. Determinants give \eqref{eq:det}. These
conditions define a closed set bounded away from zero, since
$q_i\ge D_-^{-\alpha}$.

For sufficiency put $r_i=q_i^{1/\alpha}$. Choose
\[
 h_i\ge\max(1,r_i),\qquad \prod_i h_i=D_+.
\]
This is possible because $\prod_i\max(1,r_i)\le D_+$; for example
multiply $h_1=\max(1,r_1)$ by the remaining product slack. Set
\[
 x_i^2=(h_i-1)\prod_{j>i}h_j,\qquad H_0=I+xx^T.
\]
The reverse pivots of $H_0$ are exactly $h_i$, by the rank-one Schur
complement formula. Thus its reverse $LDL^T$ factorization is
$H_0=U\diag(h_i)U^T$ with $U$ unit upper triangular.

Let $l_i=r_i/h_i\in(0,1]$, and put
\[
 z_i^2=(1-l_i)\prod_{j>i}l_j,\quad
 y=U\diag(\sqrt{h_i})z,\quad
 M=H_0-yy^T
   =U\diag(\sqrt{h_i})(I-zz^T)\diag(\sqrt{h_i})U^T.
\]
Telescoping gives $\norm z^2=1-\prod_i l_i=1-D_-^{-1}<1$, so
$M\succ0$. The reverse pivots of $I-zz^T$ are $l_i$, hence those of
$M$ are $h_i l_i=r_i$. To see the latter factorization statement
directly, a unit upper triangular congruence preserves every trailing
principal determinant, and a diagonal congruence multiplies its $i$th
reverse pivot by $h_i$.

Now take
\[
 A_\delta=\diag(\delta^{n-i}),\quad
 u_\delta=A_\delta^{1/2}x,\quad
 v_\delta=A_\delta^{1/2}y,\quad
 H_\delta=A_\delta^{1/2}H_0A_\delta^{1/2},\quad
 B_\delta=A_\delta^{1/2}MA_\delta^{1/2}.
\]
Both rank-one identities hold exactly. Also
$\det H_\delta/\det A_\delta=\prod h_i=D_+$ and
$\det H_\delta/\det B_\delta=\prod l_i^{-1}=D_-$ at every
$\delta$. Lemma~\ref{lem:scale} gives the prescribed limiting spectrum.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact rank of a signed correction preconditioner}

\begin{lemma}[Minimum preconditioner correction rank]\label{lem:rank}
Let $S,T\succ0$ and $0<\ell<1<u$. Write $q_i$ for the generalized
eigenvalues of $(T,S)$. The minimum rank of a symmetric $F$ for which
$P=S+F\succ0$ and
\[
                  \ell P\preceq T\preceq uP
\]
is
\[
       \#\{i:q_i>u\}+\#\{i:q_i<\ell\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The upper inequality gives $T-uS\preceq uF$, hence $F$ has at least
$\#\{q_i>u\}$ positive eigenvalues by eigenvalue monotonicity under
Loewner order and Sylvester inertia. The lower inequality gives
$\ell F\preceq T-\ell S$, so $F$ has at least
$\#\{q_i<\ell\}$ negative eigenvalues. Their sum bounds its rank.
For equality diagonalize $S^{-1/2}TS^{-1/2}=V\diag(q_i)V^T$ and retain
$q_i-1$ only outside $[\ell,u]$ in
$F=S^{1/2}V\diag(f_i)V^TS^{1/2}$. The retained ratios $T/P$ are one;
the others equal $q_i$. Thus $P$ is positive and satisfies the bounds.
\end{proof}

For $x\ge0$, write
\[
       J(x)=\max\{0,\lceil x\rceil-1\}.
\]
The strict endpoint matters: a budget ratio equal to an integer does not
permit that many eigenvalues strictly outside the interval.

\begin{corollary}[Sharp two-budget rank law]\label{cor:twobudget}
Over all finite dimensions and triples in Theorem~\ref{thm:signed}, the
exact supremum of the minimum rank needed for
\[
 \ell(A^\alpha+F)\preceq B^\alpha\preceq u(A^\alpha+F),
 \qquad A^\alpha+F\succ0,
\]
is
\[
 J\!\left(\frac{\alpha\log D_+}{\log u}\right)
 +J\!\left(\frac{\alpha\log D_-}{-\log\ell}\right).
\]
Both extreme counts can be attained simultaneously. The assertion is over
unrestricted dimensions; the formula $\min(n,\hbox{displayed sum})$ is
\emph{not} generally valid in a fixed dimension. For example $n=1$ and
$D_+=D_-$ force $Q=1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply the two logarithmic budgets to the strict thresholds, then
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank}. For joint sharpness allocate the entire positive
budget among $m_+=J(\alpha\log D_+/\log u)$ equal logarithms and the
entire negative budget among $m_-=J(\alpha\log D_-/-\log\ell)$ equal
negative logarithms. Each counted direction lies strictly outside the
interval. If a count is zero but its budget is nonzero, allocate that
budget to one additional direction; its eigenvalue can lie on or within
the appropriate interval. These lists obey the determinant identity and
both budgets with equality. Theorem~\ref{thm:signed} approximates them
closely enough to preserve the counted strict inequalities. The universal
upper bound excludes any excess count.
\end{proof}

With $\ell=1-\varepsilon$, $u=1+\varepsilon$, this gives a positive
preconditioner with condition-number guarantee $(1+\varepsilon)/(1-\varepsilon)$.
The rank is of order $\alpha(\log D_++\log D_-)/\varepsilon$ for
fixed nontrivial budgets. No algorithm or finite-precision complexity
claim is being made.

\section{Two signed edge changes on one ordinary weighted path}

Let an original connected unit-mass weighted path have one edge increased
by factor $D_+$ and a different edge decreased by factor $D_-^{-1}$,
where $D_+,D_->1$. Let $L,L'$ be its original and final Laplacians.
Let $H$ be the envelope path Laplacian taking the larger conductance at
each edited edge. Restrict all matrices to $\mathbf1^\perp$. The matrix
tree theorem, or a direct path incidence calculation, gives exactly
\[
      \det H/\det L=D_+,\qquad \det H/\det L'=D_-.
\]
Thus the two-budget upper bound holds at every finite path length,
independently of the conductances of its unedited edges.

\begin{theorem}[Joint path sharpness]\label{thm:path}
For the class of paths just described, the exact supremal minimum rank of
a symmetric correction $F$ such that $P=L^\alpha+F$ is positive on
$\mathbf1^\perp$ and
\[
                   \ell P\preceq L'^\alpha\preceq uP
\]
is the same two-budget expression in Corollary~\ref{cor:twobudget}.
The path length and conductance ratio are unrestricted. The theorem
concerns exactly two edited edges when both budgets are nontrivial.
\end{theorem}

The proof needs a continuity statement because joining two witnesses
introduces a small positive eigenvalue and inverse powers then diverge.
Ordinary continuity of the pseudoinverse is insufficient.

\begin{lemma}[Weak path concatenation]\label{lem:glue}
Take two fixed pairs of connected unit-mass path Laplacians
$(L_1,L_1')$, $(L_2,L_2')$, each pair having the same vertex set. Join
an end of the first path to an end of the second by an unedited edge of
conductance $s>0$, in both original and updated graphs. Write their
Laplacians as $L_s,L_s'$. Then for $0<\alpha<1$ the relative correction
\[
 (L_s^\dagger)^{\alpha/2}(L_s'^\alpha-L_s^\alpha)
                  (L_s^\dagger)^{\alpha/2}
\]
converges in norm, as $s\downarrow0$, to the direct sum of the two
component relative corrections and zero on their two-dimensional space
of componentwise constants.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The global constant vector is a common exact kernel and can be removed.
In its orthogonal complement use the fixed decomposition consisting of
the one-dimensional componentwise constant direction and the sum of the
two component mean-zero spaces. The matrices have the form
\[
 A_s=\begin{pmatrix}s\mu&sa^T\\sa&A_0+sC\end{pmatrix},\quad
 B_s=\begin{pmatrix}s\mu&sa^T\\sa&B_0+sC\end{pmatrix},
 \quad A_0,B_0\succ0,\quad \mu>0.
\]
The coefficients $\mu,a,C$ are the same in both matrices because only
the joining edge couples componentwise constants. In fact
$\mu=|V_1|^{-1}+|V_2|^{-1}$.

Spectral separation at $s=0$ gives an analytic orthogonal
block diagonalization with the orthogonal matrix $I+O(s)$. The single
small eigenvalues are both $s\mu+O(s^2)$; the high blocks are
$A_0+O(s)$ and $B_0+O(s)$. Functional calculus in this diagonalization
therefore gives, in the fixed decomposition,
\[
 B_s^\alpha-A_s^\alpha=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 O(s^{\alpha+1}+s^2)&O(s)\\
 O(s)&B_0^\alpha-A_0^\alpha+O(s)
 \end{pmatrix}
\]
and
\[
 A_s^{-\alpha/2}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 O(s^{-\alpha/2})&O(s^{1-\alpha/2})\\
 O(s^{1-\alpha/2})&A_0^{-\alpha/2}+o(1)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For the first display, the low eigenvalue powers differ by
$O(s^{\alpha+1})$ because their first-order coefficient is the same;
the high spectral subspace contributes $O(s^2)$ to the low diagonal.
Multiplying these block estimates shows that the low diagonal of the
relative correction is $O(s+s^{2-\alpha})$, its cross blocks tend to
zero, and the high block tends to
$A_0^{-\alpha/2}(B_0^\alpha-A_0^\alpha)A_0^{-\alpha/2}$.
Restoring the common exact kernel proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:path}]
The upper bound follows from the envelope budgets. For the lower bound,
the candidate manuscript's one-edge path theorem supplies a positive
update witness using factor $D_+$ with the maximal positive count above
$u$. If that count is zero, take any single-edge path with this factor.
It also supplies a positive update witness with factor $D_-$ with the
maximal count above $1/\ell$; reversing its original and updated graphs
gives a negative-update path with that many relative eigenvalues below
$\ell$. The reciprocal-spectrum identity holds exactly for the reversed
pair. Again use any single-edge witness if its count is zero.

Join their ends by a sufficiently weak unedited edge. The result is one
ordinary unit-mass path with exactly the two desired edited edges.
Lemma~\ref{lem:glue} preserves both strict counts for sufficiently small
positive joining conductance. Each edited edge is still a bridge, so
both determinant budgets are exact for every joining conductance; no
limiting renormalization is needed. The universal upper bound excludes
additional out-of-interval eigenvalues. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:rank}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No compression bound from net tree-volume change]
\label{cor:volume}
Fix $D>1$, and increase one edge of a unit-mass weighted path by factor
$D$ while decreasing another by factor $D^{-1}$. The product of the
nonzero Laplacian eigenvalues is unchanged, and therefore
$\det Q_\alpha=1$ exactly. Nevertheless the exact worst-case minimum
correction rank for a $(1\pm\varepsilon)$ preconditioner, $0<\varepsilon<1$,
is
\[
 J\!\left(\frac{\alpha\log D}{\log(1+\varepsilon)}\right)
 +J\!\left(\frac{\alpha\log D}{-\log(1-\varepsilon)}\right)
 =\frac{2\alpha\log D}{\varepsilon}+O_{\alpha,D}(1).
\]
Thus the net determinant change gives no dimension-independent bound on
the correction rank in a signed dynamic update: for any fixed
$\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ this rank is unbounded as $D\to\infty$, although
the net determinant remains exactly one. The separate positive
and negative budgets retain the required information.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On a tree the nonzero Laplacian eigenvalue product is the vertex count
times the product of its conductances. The reciprocal edge factors cancel.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:path} with $D_+=D_-=D$ and use the scalar Taylor
expansions of the two denominators.
\end{proof}

\section{Independent audit of the one-edge path transfer}\label{sec:audit}

The candidate proof first uses cyclic Lanczos to obtain an SPD Jacobi
matrix $T$ and a boundary update $\kappa e_1e_1^T$. Alternating signs
make the off-diagonal entries negative. Then $d=T^{-1}e_1>0$ and
$D T D\mathbf1=d_1e_1$ with $D=\diag(d)$, giving a grounded weighted
path. A new ground vertex of mass $R^2$ produces
\[
 H_R=\begin{pmatrix}d_1/R^2&-e_1^T/R\\-e_1/R&T\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 H_R'=H_R+\kappa(-d_1/R,e_1)(-d_1/R,e_1)^T.
\]
These formulas, the common kernel $(R,d)^T$, and the bridge leverage
$\kappa d_1$ are correct. As $R\to\infty$, their sole kernel persists
and every other eigenvalue stays away from zero, so the relative
fractional calculus converges to the SPD witness with one extra zero.

To remove masses, replacing each massive vertex by an integer block of
unit vertices linked with conductance $T$ preserves the path topology.
At a fixed block size the internal high eigenvalues diverge as $T$ grows,
while the low compression is the appropriate mass-normalized quotient.
For the fractional \emph{difference}, the bound
\[
 \norm{(L_T+tI)^{-1}-(L_T'+tI)^{-1}}
       \le\min(2/t,\norm{L_T'-L_T}/t^2)
\]
dominates the $t^\alpha$ resolvent integral at zero and infinity.
Thus no false convergence of $L_T^\alpha$ itself is used. Negative
fractional powers converge by the persistent one-dimensional kernel,
the positive low spectral gap, and suppression of the divergent high
modes. Rounding masses and taking strong-edge limits sequentially is
legitimate. Every edited edge is a bridge, so the leverage remains exactly
its conductance-increase ratio throughout. No gap was found in this
transfer under unrestricted dimension and conductance ratios.

\section{Priority limits and source comparison}

Signed low-rank matrix-function updates, Krylov approximation, and graph
applications predate this note. The original paper of Beckermann,
Kressner and Schweitzer explicitly treats $f(A+D)-f(A)$ for low-rank
$D$ and graph insertion/removal applications
\cite{BKS}; its abstract and introduction do not provide the present
complete energy-relative spectral closure. Shmueli, Drineas and Avron
also treat square-root updates and downdates, including repeated
downdate/update applications \cite{SDA}. These are comparator papers,
not evidence of novelty by omission. The general scale lemma and signed
closure are consequences of classical multiplicative majorization,
triangular-factor realizations, and separated-scale asymptotics, as made
precise after Theorem~\ref{thm:signed}. The effective-resistance Gram
matrix of distinct bridge edges on a tree is diagonal. Thus the ordinary
relative spectrum for the signed two-edge path update is already exactly
$(D_+,1,\ldots,1,D_-^{-1})$, consistent with that subsumption.
The two-budget upper bound and the rank/inertia lemma are elementary.
The potentially substantive graph conclusion is their simultaneous exact
sharpness on unit-mass paths with only two signed edge edits.
