# Uniform phase of the cubic two-block moment: the coefficient is exactly p

Derived 2026-11-15. This note proves, for every fixed odd prime p and every sufficiently small fixed delta_p>0, that the contribution described below from uniformly distributed directed-edge tables is p+o_p(1). It uses the degree-two configuration model, with two distinguishable stubs at each vertex. All constants and limits in intermediate estimates may depend on the fixed prime; the final coefficient p is exact. There is no restriction on N modulo p or modulo 2.

The only probabilistic analytic input is an elementary finite-support lattice local central limit lemma, proved in Section 6 by Fourier inversion and exponential tilting. No theorem about independent matrix entries or graph contiguity is used.

## 1. An exact iid representation

Gauge the planted vector to the all-one vector. The kernel equations then read

    C1 x = x+z,        C2 z = x+z.

For each coordinate choose independent uniform A,D,B,E in F_p, and put

    C=A+D-B,           F=A+D-E.

Let the local random pair of p-by-p integer matrices be

    W1[a,b] = 1_{A=a}(1_{B=b}+1_{C=b}),
    W2[d,e] = 1_{D=d}(1_{E=e}+1_{F=e}).

Use N independent copies, and write Mj=sum_i Wj. Each table has total sum 2N. For a nonnegative integer table m, define

    P(m)=1_{m=m^T, all m_aa even}
         product_{a<b} m_ab! product_a (m_aa-1)!!.

The convention (-1)!!=1 is understood. Exact enumeration of the two independent stub pairings gives

    E |ker_Fp M| = p^(4N)/( (2N-1)!! )^2 E[P(M1)P(M2)].       (1)

Indeed A,D specify the kernel vector at a coordinate; B,C and E,F specify its ordered stub-neighbor labels. Every ordered neighbor assignment satisfying the two sums is counted once by the p^4 choices. Pairing stubs with those labels is possible exactly when the relevant directed-edge table is symmetric with even diagonal, and the number of compatible pairings is P(m).

Put q=p and t=2N/q^2. The uniform contribution Z_unif is the right side of (1), restricted to

    ||Mj-t J|| <= delta_p N,       j=1,2,                    (2)

where the norm is Euclidean/Frobenius. For small enough delta_p this entails that every entry is positive and of order N. We prove

    Z_unif = p+o_p(1).                                      (3)

## 2. Covariance and the weighted Gaussian

The mean of Wj is (2/p^2)J. Let H be the real vector space of p-by-p tables with total zero. Let R denote the orthogonal projection onto tables constant within each row. On H, the covariance of each Wj is

    Sigma = (2/p^2)(I+R),                                  (4)

and the cross-covariance of W1 and W2 is zero. To check this, for a test table f with total zero,

    Var(f(A,B)+f(A,C))
       = (2/p^2)||f||^2 +(2/p^3)sum_a (sum_b f_ab)^2.

Here B,C are independent uniform conditional on A, since D is uniform. Each one of (A,B),(A,C) is independent of each one of (D,E),(D,F): the corresponding four linear forms in A,D,B,E are linearly independent. This proves the asserted zero cross-covariance.

Write

    d=p(p-1)/2,        s=p(p+1)/2-1=d+p-1,
    r0=p^2-1=d+s.

The eigenvalues of Sigma on H are 4/p^2 with multiplicity p-1, and 2/p^2 with multiplicity p(p-1)=2d. In particular

    det Sigma=(4/p^2)^(p-1)(2/p^2)^(2d).                    (5)

Let S be the space of symmetric real tables with total zero. On S, the compression of R has eigenvalue 1/2 on

    {f_ab=v_a+v_b: sum_a v_a=0},

and eigenvalue zero on symmetric tables whose row sums vanish. Their dimensions are p-1 and d respectively.

For m=tJ+sqrt(N)X in the moderate range ||X||<=log N, Stirling's formula uniformly gives

    P(m)/(2N-1)!!
      =p^(-2N)(4 pi N/p^2)^(d/2) 2^((p-1)/2)
       exp[(p^2/8)||X||^2](1+o_p(1)),                    (6)

provided m is symmetric with even diagonal. The error is uniform: the cubic Taylor remainder is O_p(log^3 N/sqrt(N)), and all Stirling errors tend to zero. The first-order entropy term vanishes because the total table sum is fixed.

Combining the Gaussian density with (6), the exponent on S for one block is

    -1/2 <X,Sigma^(-1)X> +(p^2/8)||X||^2
      =-(p^2/8)(||X||^2-||RX||^2).                       (7)

Thus the corresponding Gaussian precision on S has eigenvalues p^2/8 with multiplicity p-1, and p^2/4 with multiplicity d. Its integral is

    I=(2 pi)^(s/2)(8/p^2)^((p-1)/2)(4/p^2)^(d/2).         (8)

All these determinants use Euclidean volume on the specified subspaces.

## 3. The full lattice has index 2^(2p-2) p^3

Let L0 be the integer lattice in H x H, and let L be generated by all differences W(a,d,b,e)-W(0,0,0,0). The Euclidean covolume of L0 is p^2.

The following is an exact description of the affine lattice N W(0,0,0,0)+L. Both table totals are 2N, all row sums are even, and three displayed congruences hold modulo p. For row sums R1_a,R2_d write n1_a=R1_a/2 and n2_d=R2_d/2. The congruences are

    L1 = sum_ab b m1_ab -sum_a a n1_a-sum_d d n2_d =0,
    L2 = sum_de e m2_de -sum_a a n1_a-sum_d d n2_d =0,
    Q  = sum_ab ab m1_ab -sum_de de m2_de
         -sum_a a^2 n1_a+sum_d d^2 n2_d =0.                (9)

Every local W satisfies them. The row parities have index 2^(2p-2) on fixed-total integer tables. The three congruences are independent modulo p on the row-even lattice, so the displayed conditions have total index 2^(2p-2)p^3.

For completeness, there are no further lattice restrictions. A character of H x H which is constant on the local support has, modulo additive constants on each table, the following form. In units modulo 1,

    theta1_ab=alpha_a+(t a+k)b/p,
    theta2_de=gamma_d+(-t d+l)e/p,
    2 alpha_a=R-[t a^2+(k+l)a]/p,
    2 gamma_d=S+[t d^2-(k+l)d]/p,                         (10)

where t,k,l run through F_p and the choices of halves supply one row-sign character per row, modulo the two global table signs. To derive (10), first vary B at fixed A,D. The relation theta1_a,b+theta1_a,A+D-b independent of b forces the row function to be a constant plus an additive F_p character; the same holds in the other table. Taking mixed differences in a,d then forces the two character slopes to be t a+k and -t d+l. The remaining equation is precisely the last two lines of (10). Conversely each such character is constant on the support. These give exactly 2^(2p-2)p^3 characters, proving equality of the generated lattice with the displayed congruence lattice by duality of finite-index integer lattices.

One can also check independence of L1,L2,Q directly. Their coefficient matrices, working modulo p and using 1/2, are

    L1: (b-a/2, -d/2),
    L2: (-a/2, e-d/2),
    Q : (ab-a^2/2, -de+d^2/2).

Any linear combination constant within both full tables first has zero coefficient of b for every a, forcing the Q and L1 coefficients to vanish; the remaining coefficient then forces that of L2 to vanish. Odd p is used here and in (10).

It follows that

    covol(L)=p^5 2^(2p-2).                                (11)

## 4. The lattice after symmetry has only two modular restrictions

Let K0 be the lattice of symmetric integer p-by-p matrices with total zero. Its covolume in S is

    covol(K0)=p 2^(d/2).                                  (12)

Indeed the full symmetric integer lattice has covolume 2^(d/2); the total-sum functional is primitive, and its Euclidean gradient in the symmetric subspace is J, of norm p.

Inside K0, requiring all diagonal entries even has index 2^(p-1): their parities have even total because the full table has total zero. Then requiring all row sums even imposes another p-1 independent parity conditions: on the off-diagonal entries these are the vertex-degree parities of the complete graph, whose incidence matrix over F_2 has rank p-1. Hence the two parity requirements have index 2^(2p-2) per block.

On symmetric tables, L1 and L2 become negatives of one another:

    L1=sum_a a n1_a-sum_d d n2_d=:T,       L2=-T.

The Q congruence remains. The two congruences T,Q are independent on the symmetric, diagonal-even, row-even lattice. To see this, in the first table vary the diagonal by +2 at (a,a) and -2 at (0,0), keeping the second table zero. This changes (T,Q) by (a,a^2). These vectors span F_p^2 for odd p (take a=1,-1). Thus the total mod-p index is p^2.

Consequently the lattice K consisting of pairs in L intersect (S x S) whose diagonal entries are all even has covolume

    covol(K)
      =(p 2^(d/2))^2 2^(4p-4) p^2
      =p^4 2^(d+4p-4),                                   (13)

and

    covol(L)/covol(K)=p 2^(-d-2p+2).                       (14)

There is no hidden subsequence condition. The affine symmetric feasible set is nonempty for every N: put both tables' only nonzero entry, 2N, at (0,0). Its differences form exactly K. Since K has full rank in S x S, every affine translate has a point within O_p(1) of t(J,J). Thus its central mesh converges to Lebesgue measure with density N^s/covol(K), regardless of N modulo p or 2.

## 5. Gaussian summation and all prefactors

The joint covariance on H x H is Sigma direct-sum Sigma. The relative local central limit estimate in Section 6, uniformly for ||Mj-tJ||<=sqrt(N)log N, is

    P((M1,M2)=(m1,m2))
      =(1+o_p(1)) covol(L) (2 pi N)^(-r0)(det Sigma)^(-1)
       exp[-1/2 sum_j <Xj,Sigma^(-1)Xj>].                 (15)

This statement is for lattice points in the affine support; other points have probability zero.

Using (6), the sum over the symmetric affine lattice becomes a Riemann sum on S x S. Equations (8) and (15) yield the coefficient

    (4 pi/p^2)^d 2^(p-1) (2 pi)^(-r0)(det Sigma)^(-1)
     [covol(L)/covol(K)] I^2.                             (16)

The powers of N cancel because d-r0+s=0. From (5),(8),

    I^2/(det Sigma)=(2 pi)^s 2^(p-1) p^(2d).

Thus the analytic part of (16), excluding the covolume ratio, is

    2^(d+2p-2).

Multiplication by (14) proves that (16) is exactly p.

The logarithmic window may be extended to the whole fixed neighborhood (2), without changing the limit. Here is a direct bound that does not assume a global entropy theorem. The finite-support rate function J for the joint iid table increments is analytic near their mean and has quadratic term one half of the inverse covariance form. On symmetric tables, subtracting the quadratic term supplied by the pairing weights gives exactly the positive precision in (7). The least precision eigenvalue is p^2/8. Shrinking delta_p makes the cubic remainders at most half the positive quadratic form. Exponential tilting or the Chernoff bound then gives, throughout (2),

    p^(4N) P((M1,M2)=m) P(m1)P(m2)/( (2N-1)!! )^2
      <= C_p N^d exp[-c_p ||m-t(J,J)||^2/N].              (17)

The polynomial factor is harmless. There are at most (2N+1)^(2p^2) possible tables. Outside the sqrt(N)log N window, the sum in (17) tends to zero. This proves (3) for a fixed neighborhood, with all contributions included and no informal truncation of type prefactors.

## 6. Relative moderate-deviation lattice estimate used in (15)

The needed lemma is as follows. Let V have finite support in an affine translate of a full-rank lattice L in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, and suppose the differences of its support generate exactly L. Assume covariance Sigma is positive definite. If V_i are iid and m belongs to their N-fold affine lattice, then uniformly for ||m-N E V||<=sqrt(N)log N,

    P(sum_i V_i=m)
      =(1+o(1)) covol(L)(2 pi N)^(-r/2)(det Sigma)^(-1/2)
       exp[-<m-N E V,Sigma^(-1)(m-N E V)>/(2N)].          (18)

This is a relative estimate; an ordinary absolute-error CLT would not suffice after multiplication by (6).

A short proof: exponential tilt the finite law by the unique small parameter eta for which its mean is m/N. Existence and uniqueness near the original mean follow from the inverse function theorem, since the derivative of the mean is Sigma. Uniformly in the displayed window, eta=O(log N/sqrt(N)); the tilted covariance is Sigma+O(log N/sqrt(N)). Under the tilted law evaluate the probability at its exact mean by Fourier inversion on the dual lattice torus. The maximality of L means the only points of modulus-one characteristic function are dual-lattice points, giving a single maximum on that torus. Outside a fixed neighborhood the integrand is exponentially small, uniformly for small tilts. Within it, Taylor expansion and Gaussian domination give the value covol(L)(2 pi N)^(-r/2)(det Sigma_eta)^(-1/2)(1+o(1)), uniformly in the tilt. Undoing the tilt introduces the rate factor exp[-N J(m/N)]. Its Taylor remainder, after multiplication by N, is O(log^3 N/sqrt(N)), and its quadratic term is the exponent in (18). This proves (18), including the required relative error and the exact lattice prefactor.

The same tilt argument in a fixed small neighborhood supplies a uniform point-probability upper bound by a polynomial times exp[-N J(m/N)], as used in (17).

## Scope

This note supplies the uniform-phase contribution p+o_p(1), including its lattice factors. A separate argument is required for vectors near the p constant planted solutions, and a strict entropy inequality/compactness argument is required for the remaining type region. Once those contributions are p+o_p(1) and o_p(1) respectively, the degree-two configuration-model expectation is 2p+o_p(1). Conditioning both internal configurations on simplicity then gives a bound with an absolute coefficient times p, since the joint simplicity probability tends to exp(-3/2).

### Fourier details for the local estimate

Identify the real span of the lattice with R^r and use Euclidean volume. Let `phi_eta(xi)` denote the characteristic function after a small real exponential tilt eta. Fourier inversion on any fundamental region of the reciprocal lattice has prefactor `covol(L)/(2 pi)^r`. The centered characteristic function has, uniformly for eta in a fixed small compact neighborhood,

    log[exp(-i xi.mu_eta) phi_eta(xi)]
       =-xi^T Sigma_eta xi/2+O(||xi||^3),

and `Sigma_eta>=c I`. Consequently its modulus is at most `exp(-c'||xi||^2)` near zero. On the complement of that neighborhood in the reciprocal torus its modulus is at most a constant rho<1: equality in the triangle inequality would say that `exp(i xi.v)` is constant on the support, contradicting maximality of the lattice. Compactness makes both bounds uniform for small tilts, since all support probabilities remain positive and continuous in eta.

For inversion at `m=N mu_eta`, split the integral at `||xi||=N^(-2/5)`. Between this radius and the fixed small neighborhood the contribution is `O(exp(-c N^(1/5)))`; outside the neighborhood it is `O(rho^N)`. In the remaining integral set `u=sqrt(N) xi`. On bounded u the cubic remainder is `O(||u||^3/sqrt(N))`; the preceding Gaussian modulus bound supplies an integrable dominating function, uniformly in eta. Gaussian integration gives

    P_eta(sum V_i=m)
       =(1+o(1)) covol(L)(2 pi N)^(-r/2)
         (det Sigma_eta)^(-1/2).

The result is uniform for every small tilt under discussion. Finally the centered log moment-generating function has expansion

    Lambda(eta)=eta^T Sigma eta/2+O(||eta||^3),
    eta=Sigma^(-1)q+O(||q||^2),
    J(q)=q^T Sigma^(-1)q/2+O(||q||^3),

where `q=m/N-EV`. For `||q||<=log(N)/sqrt(N)` this gives a rate-exponent error `O(log^3(N)/sqrt(N))=o(1)` and covariance error `O(log(N)/sqrt(N))=o(1)`. Undoing the tilt proves (18) with a uniform relative error. These arguments also show directly that no semigroup holes remain in this central window: every point in the stated affine lattice has the asserted positive asymptotic mass.
