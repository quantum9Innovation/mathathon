1. THE RESULT AND ITS NORMALIZATION
----------------------------------
For 0<alpha<1 and 0<s<=1, define

  H(alpha,s) = sup_{A,B PSD, A!=B}
     Tr|B^alpha-A^alpha|^s / Tr|B-A|^(alpha*s),

where the supremum is over all finite dimensions. Put beta=1-alpha.
The fixed-alpha companion theorem proves

  s^2 H(alpha,s) -> 1/[2*pi^2*alpha*beta].

The following JOINT limit has a different feature:

THEOREM A (relative joint endpoint law).
For EVERY joint sequence s->0 and beta->0,

  s^2 beta^(1-2*s) H(1-beta,s) -> 1/(2*pi^2).      (A0)

Thus H(1-beta,s) ~ beta^(2*s-1)/(2*pi^2*s^2), including when beta
is superexponentially small in 1/s. Consequently, if additionally
u=-s*log(beta)->u0 in [0,infinity], then

  beta*s^2 H(1-beta,s) -> exp(-2*u0)/(2*pi^2).       (A1)

Here exp(-infinity)=0. In particular, along every such joint sequence,

  beta*s^2 H(1-beta,s) - beta^(2*s)/(2*pi^2) -> 0.  (A2)

The crossover (A1)--(A2) is a consequence of the stronger relative law
(A0). The RELATIVE law continues to hold when u0=infinity.
In particular one cannot substitute alpha(s) blindly in the fixed-alpha
theorem: beta=exp(-u0/s) changes its normalized limit by exp(-2*u0).
At alpha=1 itself H(1,s)=1. No runtime theorem follows from Theorem A.

A separate endpoint phenomenon can also be proved:

THEOREM B (fixed Schatten exponent, quantitative scope below).
As beta->0, for fixed 0<s<1/2,

  c_s beta^(2*s-1) <= H(1-beta,s)
       <= C_s beta^(2*s-1).                        (B1)

At s=1/2,

  c log(e/beta) <= H(1-beta,1/2)
                    <= C log(e/beta).             (B2)

For each fixed 1/2<s<1, H(1-beta,s) stays bounded as beta->0, but

  liminf_{beta->0} H(1-beta,s) > 1 = H(1,s).       (B3)

Constants in B1 depend on FIXED s. No matching multiplicative asymptotic
at fixed s is asserted. These fixed-s conclusions
serve also as independent sanity checks for the joint theorem.

2. REDUCTION AND TWO POSITIVE COMPRESSIONS
-----------------------------------------
The rank-one reduction from the companion paper is exact for 0<s<=1:

  H(alpha,s) = sup_{A>=0, ||v||=1}
               Tr[((A+vv*)^alpha-A^alpha)^s].     (2.1)

Proof reminder: telescope the signed spectral decomposition of any
Hermitian perturbation through A+(B-A)_+; all endpoints are PSD.
The power triangle and homogeneity give the upper, and rank-one
pairs give the reverse. The positive increment has norm one.

Write E=vv*, B=A+E and Delta=B^alpha-A^alpha>=0. Then ||Delta||<=1.
Let P=1_[0,1](A), Q=I-P, v0=Pv, v1=Qv. Positive pinching gives

  Tr Delta^s <= Tr(P Delta P)^s + Tr(Q Delta Q)^s,  0<s<=1. (2.2)

This follows from concavity of t^s / compression Jensen, or from
pinching as the average of Delta and a unitary conjugate. No indefinite
pinching lower bound is used.

Put c_alpha=sin(pi*alpha)/pi. Woodbury gives the exact positive integral

 Delta = c_alpha int_0^infinity
   t^alpha (A+t)^(-1) vv* (A+t)^(-1) / (1+m(t)) dt,
 m(t)=v*(A+t)^(-1)v.                              (2.3)

If A0=PAP on ran(P), then

  P Delta P <= (A0+v0v0*)^alpha-A0^alpha =: Delta0. (2.4)

Indeed m(t)>=v0*(A0+t)^(-1)v0 and the compressed numerator is unchanged.
Thus the low spectral block can be compared to a genuine PSD pair
with 0<=A0<=I, ||v0||<=1 and 0<=A0+v0v0*<=2I.

On the high spectral block A1=QAQ>=I, deleting the denominator yields

  Q Delta Q <= L := D(x^alpha)_{A1}[v1v1*].        (2.5)

In a spectral basis A1=diag(lambda_i), L has entries

  L_ij = (v1)_i conjugate((v1)_j)
            (lambda_i^alpha-lambda_j^alpha)/(lambda_i-lambda_j),

with the diagonal quotient alpha*lambda_i^(alpha-1). This is PSD,
both by its integral formula and by operator monotonicity.

3. THE LOW BLOCK HAS A UNIFORM O(s^-2) MOMENT
--------------------------------------------
For 1/2<=alpha<1, all pairs in (2.4) satisfy

  Tr Delta0^s <= C/s^2,                 0<s<=1.    (3.1)

Here C is ABSOLUTE, independent of alpha, dimension, and the pair.
It is enough to give an explicit uniform rank approximability proof.
Let E0=v0v0*. Subtract the rank-one term

  c_alpha int_0^infinity t^alpha E0/(1+t)^2 dt
       = alpha E0.                                (3.2)

This is the beta integral Gamma(alpha+1)Gamma(1-alpha). The residual
logarithmic integrand, with t=e^z, is

 F(z)=c_alpha t^(alpha+1)
  [(A0+t)^(-1)-(A0+E0+t)^(-1)-E0/(1+t)^2].        (3.3)

It is analytic in |Im z|<pi. For fixed d<pi, uniformly in
alpha in [1/2,1) and |Im z|<=d,

  ||F(x+i eta)|| <= C_d min(e^(alpha*x),e^(-(2-alpha)*x))
                   <= C_d min(e^(x/2),e^(-x))       (3.4)

with the second display understood separately on x<=0 and x>=0.
For |t|<=1, bound the resolvent difference by C_d/|t| and the
subtracted term by C_d. For |t|>=1, expand each resolvent once:
R0 E0 R1=E0/t^2+O_d(|t|^-3), since both endpoints have norm <=2;
E0/(1+t)^2=E0/t^2+O_d(|t|^-3). This proves (3.4).
Each residual node has rank at most two.

The Gaussian quadrature lemma of the companion, now with the COMMON
slopes 1/2 and 1 and a fixed strip, supplies a rank C(L+1)^2
approximant to Delta0 of operator error e^-L, including (3.2).
Its constants are uniform because the envelope just proved is uniform.
The norm bound ||Delta0||<=1 and layer cake then give (3.1).

4. A SHARP GAUSSIAN UPPER THAT IS UNIFORM NEAR ALPHA=1
-----------------------------------------------------
We will use the following estimate only in the regime beta>=s^3.
For every fixed d<pi, there is C_d independent of beta in (0,1/2]
such that, for all L>=L_d and every rank-one normalized PSD pair,

 N_Delta(L) <= L^2/(4*pi*d*alpha*beta)
                       + C_d beta^-1 (L+1)^(7/4).  (4.1)

Consequently

 beta*s^2 H(alpha,s) <= 1/(2*pi*d*alpha) + C_d s^(1/4),
                  0<s<=1/4, 0<beta<=1/2.           (4.2)

To check all hidden constants, use the PSD log-integrand from (2.3)
BEFORE Woodbury, i.e. c_alpha*t^(alpha+1)(R_A-R_B). On the fixed
strip it has envelope

  M_d min(e^(alpha*x),e^(-beta*x)),  M_d<=C_d beta. (4.3)

The gain M_d=O(beta), from sin(pi*beta), is essential.
In the Gaussian proof set w=L^(3/4). The retained-window number is
J=O(L/(beta*w)+1), their boundary integral prefactor is C_d beta*w,
and the omitted-window tail has prefactor
M_d(1/alpha+1/beta)=O_d(1). Therefore the TOTAL quadrature errors are
bounded by C_d L exp(-L-3w), C_d L exp(-2L), and C_d exp(-L-w),
all with constants independent of beta. The large-L threshold is uniform.
The triangular integral is L^2/(2*alpha*beta). Riemann and window
overheads are O_d(beta^-1 L*w); lattice enlargement contributes
O_d(beta^-1 L^(5/2)/w). With w=L^(3/4) these give (4.1).
The finite L range is bounded at a fixed uniform threshold, also
by O_d(beta^-1). Integrating layer cake gives (4.2). The strip d
is fixed before limits; no strip-boundary constants are assumed uniform.

5. THE HIGH BLOCK'S EXACT LOEWNER KERNEL
----------------------------------------
For lambda_i>=1 put x_i=log(lambda_i)>=0 and z_i=(v1)_i. The divided
difference in (2.5) factors as

  L_ij = z_i conjugate(z_j) exp[-beta*(x_i+x_j)/2] k_beta(x_i-x_j),
  k_beta(x)=sinh((1-beta)*x/2)/sinh(x/2),
  k_beta(0)=1-beta.                                (5.1)

With Fourier transform hat(k)(xi)=int k(x) exp(-i*xi*x) dx,

  hat(k_beta)(xi)=2*pi*sin(pi*beta)
                     /[cosh(2*pi*xi)-cos(pi*beta)]. (5.2)

This is the classical Fourier transform of a hyperbolic-sine ratio.
It follows, for example, by integrating the two exponential expansions
on x>0 and x<0 and summing the partial fractions of cotangent; it is
also the Poisson kernel of a strip after rescaling. Its positivity is
the usual positive definiteness of sinh(alpha*x)/sinh(x), already
classical in matrix-mean theory. No new transform or positive-definite
function is claimed.

An explicit exponential decomposition on x>=0 is

 k_beta(x)=exp(-beta*x/2)+r_beta(x),
 r_beta(z)=-exp[-(1-beta/2)*z]*(1-exp(-beta*z))/(1-exp(-z)), (5.3)

with the removable value at zero. For 0<beta<=1/2, Re z>=-1/4,
|Im z|<=1/4,

 |r_beta(z)| <= C beta (1+(Re z)_+) exp[-3*(Re z)_+/4]. (5.4)

To prove this, write (1-exp(-beta*z))/beta=z int_0^1 exp(-t*beta*z)dt.
The ratio (1-exp(-z))/z is bounded away from zero on the bounded
part of this half-strip; on its unbounded part |1-exp(-z)| is bounded
away from zero. These facts and 1-beta/2>=3/4 give (5.4).

6. A UNIFORM HANKEL-CORNER LEMMA
--------------------------------
The following elementary approximation fact keeps the image errors
uniform even if beta is superexponentially small.

LEMMA 6.1.
Suppose h is holomorphic on Re z>=-1/4, |Im z|<=1/4 (on an open
neighborhood) and

  |h(z)| <= C beta (1+(Re z)_+) exp[-c*(Re z)_+].   (6.1)

For arbitrary finite nonnegative nodes a_i,b_j and vectors p_i,q_j,
let T_ij=p_i h(a_i+b_j) conjugate(q_j). Then

  Tr|T|^s <= C' beta^s (||p||_2||q||_2)^s / s^2,
                                                 0<s<=1. (6.2)

Here the two norms are ordinary Euclidean vector norms.
In the same-vector application the factor is beta^s ||p||_2^(2*s).
The constants depend only on C,c and the fixed strip, not on nodes,
dimension, beta, or the lengths of the node intervals.

PROOF.
For each L>=1 set R=C0(L+1), with C0 fixed sufficiently large.
The kernel is at most beta*e^-L whenever a>R or b>R. On [0,2R],
approximate h by a polynomial of degree N<=C1(L+1)^2 with uniform
error beta*e^-L. Here is a fully quantitative reason. A Bernstein
ellipse after scaling [0,2R] can be chosen with rho=1+c0/R, c0 small;
it stays inside the displayed half-strip, and |h|<=C2 beta there.
The Chebyshev error is <=C3 beta R exp(-c1 N/R). Taking
N=C4 R(L+log(R+1))=O((L+1)^2) proves the claim.

Restrict the polynomial kernel p_N(a+b) to a,b<=R and set it zero
outside. It has rank at most N+1, since collecting powers of a gives
p_N(a+b)=sum_{k=0}^N a^k q_k(b). The residual has operator norm
<= beta*e^-L ||p||_2||q||_2, by its Frobenius norm. The original
operator norm is <=C beta ||p||_2||q||_2. Counting and layer cake
give (6.2).
END PROOF.

For later use let ell>=1 and

  h_{beta,ell}(z)=sum_{n>=0} r_beta(z+n*ell).       (6.3)

Uniform geometric convergence on the half-strip and (5.4) imply (6.1)
for h_{beta,ell}, with ABSOLUTE constants independent of ell,beta.
This covers the entire image tail, not merely one nearest image.

7. PERIODIC CELL UPPER AND ITS SHARP FOURIER SUM
------------------------------------------------
Partition x>=0 into cells I_j=[j*ell,(j+1)*ell), j>=0, where ell>=1.
In one cell use local coordinates y in [0,ell), let

  p_i = z_i exp(-beta*x_i/2),
  m_j = sum_{x_i in I_j} |p_i|^2,
  mu_j = sum_{x_i in I_j} |z_i|^2.

Thus m_j<=exp(-beta*j*ell)mu_j and sum mu_j<=1.
Let L_j be the compressed Loewner matrix (5.1) on this cell.
Periodize k_beta with period ell:

  p_beta,ell(z)=sum_{n in Z} k_beta(z+n*ell)
               =sum_{k in Z} d_k exp(2*pi*i*k*z/ell),
  d_k = 2*pi*sin(pi*beta)
       /{ell*[cosh(4*pi^2*k/ell)-cos(pi*beta)]}>0. (7.1)

The series are absolutely convergent for each beta>0. The positive
Fourier decomposition and the Schatten power triangle give

  Tr P_j^s <= m_j^s sum_{k in Z} d_k^s,           (7.2)

for the matrix P_j obtained by replacing k by its periodization.
Orthogonality of the Fourier vectors in the arbitrary spectral measure
is NOT assumed; only their common squared norm m_j is used.

For the zero mode,

  d_0=2*pi*cot(pi*beta/2)/ell <=4/(beta*ell).       (7.3)

For 0<s<1/2 the remaining modes obey the exact upper

 sum_{k!=0}d_k^s
 <= beta^s ell^(1-s) (pi^2)^s I_s/pi^2,
 I_s=int_0^infinity (sinh u)^(-2*s) du
     =2^(2*s-1) B(s,1-2*s),  s*I_s->1/2.         (7.4)

Indeed sin(pi*beta)<=pi*beta and
cosh q-cos(pi*beta)>=2*sinh^2(q/2) give
d_k<=(pi^2 beta/ell)csch^2(2*pi^2*k/ell).
The summand is decreasing. For s<1/2 its integral from zero is finite,
so sum_{k>=1} <= integral_0^infinity. Substitution yields (7.4).

We now charge the difference between the periodization and the true
kernel. For |z|<=ell, the two image sums in (5.3) consist of:

(i) two separable long-range kernels, whose combined rank is <=2 and
whose weighted matrix norm is <=C m_j/(beta*ell), if beta*ell<=1;

(ii) the two corner kernels h_{beta,ell}(a+b), after reflecting one
cell coordinate, with a,b>=0. Lemma 6.1 gives total moment
<=C beta^s m_j^s/s^2.

For (i), sum exp[-beta*(z+n*ell)/2] over n>=1; its coefficient is
exp(-beta*ell/2)/(1-exp(-beta*ell/2))<=2/(beta*ell).
The two local multipliers exp(+/-beta*y/2) have norm bounded by e^(1/2)
on the cell when beta*ell<=1. For (ii), for n>=1 set the corner sum
a+b=z+ell or -z+ell and use (6.3). Reflection only changes the node
labels and preserves the Euclidean norms of the two weighted vectors.

Thus for EVERY 0<s<1/2 and beta*ell<=1,

 Tr L_j^s <= m_j^s [
    beta^s ell^(1-s) (pi^2)^s I_s/pi^2
      + C1 (C2/(beta*ell))^s + C3 beta^s/s^2 ].    (7.5)

All C_i are absolute. The two possibly indefinite image operators
are controlled by the power triangle; no Loewner deletion is made.

8. OPTIMIZE THE SPECTRAL CELL MASSES
------------------------------------
Positive pinching of the PSD matrix L over its finitely many occupied
cells gives Tr L^s <= sum_j Tr L_j^s. For 0<s<1, ordinary Holder with
exponents 1/s and 1/(1-s) gives

 sum_j m_j^s <= sum_j mu_j^s exp(-s*beta*j*ell)
 <= [sum_{j>=0} exp(-s*beta*j*ell/(1-s))]^(1-s)
 = [1-exp(-s*beta*ell/(1-s))]^(s-1) =: T.        (8.1)

This is valid uniformly for every finite spectral measure, atomic or
otherwise; it is the only optimization over the background needed.

Take ell=1/beta. Then beta*ell=1 exactly, so every preceding bound
applies literally. The spatial factor is now independent of beta:

  T_s=[1-exp(-s/(1-s))]^(s-1), s*T_s->1.          (8.2)

Define A_s=(pi^2)^s I_s/pi^2, so s*A_s->1/(2*pi^2). Combining
(2.2), (3.1), (7.5), (8.1) proves the uniform NONASYMPTOTIC upper

 H(1-beta,s) <= C/s^2
    + T_s [A_s beta^(2*s-1)+C+C beta^s/s^2],
             0<beta<=1/2, 0<s<1/2.                (8.3)

For fixed s<1/2 this immediately gives the pure-power upper in (B1),
without the logarithmic loss in the original version of this note.

For the joint theorem multiply (8.3) by s^2 beta^(1-2*s):

 s^2 beta^(1-2*s) H(1-beta,s)
 <= s^2 T_s A_s
    + C beta^(1-2*s)
    + C s^2 T_s beta^(1-2*s)
    + C T_s beta^(1-s).                           (8.4)

The main coefficient tends to 1/(2*pi^2). For beta<=s^3 and
0<s<=1/4, the three errors are respectively
O(s^(3-6*s)), O(s^(4-6*s)), O(s^(2-3*s)), using T_s=O(1/s).
They tend to zero UNIFORMLY in this entire regime, including arbitrarily
superexponentially small beta. No beta^-C times an uncontrolled o(1)
is suppressed. This is the precise reason the relative law is valid.

9. A UNIFORM MATCHING NONLINEAR LOWER
-------------------------------------
Use the PSD Cauchy lower bound already PROVED for every alpha in (0,1)
and 0<s<=1/2 in sharp-fractional-holder.tex, equations for the positive
input lower bound. Its finite-interval transfer and ALT/Jensen proof
do not require alpha fixed. We recall enough to expose all constants.

Choose eta=1/2, delta=s*beta/(1-s), c0=(eta^-1+delta^-1)^-1,
and rho(x)=c0 exp(eta*x) for x<=0, c0 exp(-delta*x) for x>=0.
Let m(t)=int rho(x)/(exp(x)+t) dx and

  q(x)=c_alpha rho(x) exp(-beta*x)/(1+m(exp x)).

The exact nonlinear Woodbury/Cauchy factorization and ALT give

  H(alpha,s) >= J_s/(2*pi) int_R q(x)^s dx,
  J_s=int_R [pi*sech(pi*xi)]^(2*s) dxi,
  s*J_s -> 1/pi.                                 (9.1)

This is a statement about the supremum of FINITE matrices: finite
intervals and their finite-matrix step approximations are taken before
any limits. There is no unbounded-operator functional calculus shortcut.

For x>=0, m(exp x)<=exp(-x)<=1. Consequently

 int_R q(x)^s dx >= (c_alpha*c0/2)^s/[s*(beta+delta)]. (9.2)

For 0<s<=1/4, beta<=1/2, delta<=1/6, and

 c_alpha*c0/2 >= beta^2 * 3*s/[4*pi*(1-s)].

Therefore

  beta*s^2 H(1-beta,s) >= b_s beta^(2*s),
  b_s=[s*J_s/(2*pi)](1-s)
                 [3*s/(4*pi*(1-s))]^s
       ->1/(2*pi^2).                              (9.3)

This lower is uniform over ALL beta in (0,1/2], not merely an
e^-O(1/s) range. Crucially it retains the actual nonlinear denominator.

10. FINISH THE JOINT LIMIT
---------------------------
For beta<=s^3, (8.4) and (9.3) prove (A0) directly, with uniform
relative normalized error tending to zero. For beta>=s^3 along a joint sequence
beta->0, beta^(2*s)->1. The lower (9.3) tends to 1/(2*pi^2).
The uniform Gaussian upper (4.2) gives limsup <=1/(2*pi*d) for
every fixed d<pi. Taking d upward to pi proves the matching upper.
Dividing by beta^(2*s), which tends to one in this second regime,
gives (A0). An arbitrary sequence splits into these two regimes.
Equations (A1)--(A2) follow by beta^(2*s)=exp(-2*u).

11. THE BROWNIAN ENDPOINT MODEL
--------------------------------
Fix finitely many u_i>0, a unit vector v, and

  A_beta=diag(exp(u_i/beta)), B_beta=A_beta+vv*.

Let L_beta=D(x^(1-beta))_{A_beta}[vv*]. From (2.3),

  (1+a_min^-1)^-1 L_beta <= Delta_beta <= L_beta,
  a_min=min_i exp(u_i/beta).                       (11.1)

The quotient entries tend to exp(-max(u_i,u_j)), including the
diagonal. Also ||L_beta||<=alpha*a_min^(-beta)<=1. Thus

  Delta_beta -> K, K_ij=v_i conjugate(v_j) min(t_i,t_j), t_i=e^-u_i. (11.2)

This is a weighted BROWNIAN COVARIANCE Gram matrix, a classical kernel.
It explains a different endpoint structure from the exact rank-one
map at alpha=1. By finite discretization, all fixed finite-rank pieces
of the continuum probability-measure version are realizable limits.

For uniform probability on t in [0,tau], its eigenvalues are

  tau/[pi^2*(j-1/2)^2], j>=1.                      (11.3)

They follow by differentiating the min(t,u) integral equation twice,
with Dirichlet condition at0 and Neumann at tau. Alternatively the
n-node matrices tau*min(i,j)/n^2 have eigenvalues
tau/[4*n^2*sin^2((2*j-1)*pi/(4*n+2))], which converge to (11.3).
Any finite node set has strictly positive t_i and can be scaled to
max t_i<1 before applying (11.2). This avoids a zero/infinite base.

It follows already that H(1-beta,s)->infinity for s<=1/2. For any
1/2<s<1, take t=(1,1-d) and v=(1,1)/sqrt2 in the limiting model.
The larger eigenvalue is >=1-d and the smaller is >=d/8 for d<=1/2.
Thus its trace-s moment is at least 1-d+(d/8)^s>1 if d is sufficiently
small depending on s. A common scaling tau<1 sufficiently near1
preserves the strict inequality and makes both u_i positive.
This proves the strict lower discontinuity in (B3).

12. UNIVERSAL BROWNIAN DOMINATION AND ITS EIGENVALUE BOUND
--------------------------------------------------------
For the high block, use u_i=beta*log(lambda_i) and t_i=e^-u_i.
The kernel k_beta(u/beta) has Fourier transform

  2*pi*beta*sin(pi*beta)
       /[cosh(2*pi*beta*xi)-cos(pi*beta)].           (12.1)

It is dominated pointwise by 1/(xi^2+1/4), the transform of exp(-|u|/2).
Indeed cosh(2*pi*beta*xi)-1>=2*pi^2*beta^2*xi^2
>=2*pi*beta*sin(pi*beta)*xi^2, and
1-cos(pi*beta)>=(pi*beta/2)*sin(pi*beta), since tan(x/2)>=x/2.
Fourier positivity therefore proves the Loewner domination

  0<=L_beta<=K, K_ij=z_i conjugate(z_j) min(t_i,t_j). (12.2)

Here sum |z_i|^2<=1 and 0<t_i<=1. Such Brownian Gram matrices satisfy

  lambda_j(K)<=C/j^2                              (12.3)

with an absolute C, uniformly over all atomic measures and dimensions.
Proof: let mu=sum |z_i|^2 delta_(t_i), and T:L2(mu)->L2([0,1],dx)
be Tf(x)=int_[x,1] f(t)dmu(t). Its T*T is the Brownian Gram operator.
Partition [0,1] using quantiles of Lebesgue+mu, whose total mass<=2.
Use the n equal mass levels 2*k/n; repeated quantiles at atoms are
deduplicated. Every open interval between successive distinct cuts
has both length and interior mu mass <=2/n. Boundary atoms are kept
exactly in the step function approximant S which, on an interval
(a,b), takes the constant integral over [b,1]. Its rank is at most
n+1. The residual is the direct sum of local Volterra maps, each
of norm <=sqrt(length*interior mass)<=2/n. Thus
a_(n+2)(T)<=2/n and lambda_(n+2)(K)<=4/n^2. Enlarging the absolute
constant covers the first few eigenvalues.

Together with (3.1), this proves the bounded upper in (B3).

13. QUANTITATIVE LOWER AT FIXED s AND THE CRITICAL LOGARITHM
----------------------------------------------------------
Take probability mu uniform on t in [a,b], 0<a<b<1 fixed, and set
u=-log(t). The derivative kernels L_beta in (11.1) and K in (12.2)
act on L2(mu). Their difference is PSD and has operator norm O(beta^2).
To see the norm estimate, its unweighted convolution difference is

 d_beta(h)=exp(-|h|/2)-k_beta(h/beta)
 =exp(-|h|/2)*(exp(|h|)-1)/(exp(|h|/beta)-1)>=0.

For beta<=1/2,

 int_R d_beta(h)dh <= pi^2 beta^2.                 (13.1)

Indeed exp(h)-1<=h exp(h), then put h=beta*z and bound
exp(beta*z/2)/(exp z-1) <= exp(-z/2)/(1-exp(-z)); the integral
of z exp(-z/2)/(1-exp(-z)) is sum_(n>=0)(n+1/2)^-2=pi^2/2.
The density of u is <=b/(b-a), and the exterior weight
exp[-(u+v)/2] is <=b. Schur's test proves

  ||K-L_beta|| <= [pi^2*b^2/(b-a)] beta^2.         (13.2)

Dropping the positive rank-one constant a from min(t,u) gives
lambda_j(K)>=(b-a)/[pi^2*(j-1/2)^2]. Hence, for all j<=c/beta,

  lambda_j(L_beta)>=c'/j^2.                        (13.3)

The nonlinear denominator comparison (11.1) changes this only by
a bounded factor, since a_min=b^(-1/beta). All operators for fixed
beta have bounded base spectrum, so the same finite transfer used
in the companion proves these lower bounds for finite-matrix suprema.
Summing (13.3) proves the lower in (B1) and the lower logarithm in (B2).

For the upper, a simple uniform sinc rule for the envelope (4.3) gives

 N_Delta(L)<=C beta^-1 (L+1)^2, L>=0,              (13.4)

with ABSOLUTE C. Choose mesh h=c/(L+C0). Boundary L1 norms are
O(c_alpha*(1/alpha+1/beta))=O(1); truncate at log t between
-(L+C0)/alpha and (L+C0)/beta. Both discarded positive lattice tails
are O(e^-L) because c_alpha/beta<=1. This explicitly proves the
uniform threshold and coefficient size in (13.4).
Inverting it gives lambda_j(Delta)<=C exp(-c sqrt(beta*j)).
The high compression also satisfies this bound and, by (12.3),
lambda_j(Q Delta Q)<=C/j^2. Split its trace-s sum at

  J=C0 beta^-1 log^2(e/beta),

with C0 fixed sufficiently large. At s=1/2 the polynomial part gives
O(log(e/beta)). The exponential tail is bounded by the same order by
the substitution y=sqrt(beta*j), enlarging C0 if needed. The low block
has the uniform bound (3.1). For s<1/2 use the sharper periodic upper
(8.3) instead of this split. This completes Theorem B with the matching
pure power in (B1).
