SHARP LEADING WIDTH FOR NEARLY-LINEAR FRACTIONAL GRAPH UPDATES
===========================================================

This note upgrades the two-endpoint graph construction using the
completed constructive quadrature in sharp-matrix-function-updates.tex.
The graph-path realization then supplies its matching worst-case width
coefficient. The algorithmic inputs, scalar quadrature and graph lower
bound are separate results; their synthesis is proved explicitly here.
No new historical-priority certificate is asserted.

1. AUTHORITATIVE INPUT AND SCOPE
===============================
The exact imported source checked for this deduction is

  docs/sharp-matrix-function-updates.tex
  SHA256 334b439ea2dd72c09e8d6024582e74862590c6cf192e5d48a9e7a43f1f63b3e6.

Its Lemma "Constructive moving strip" (label lem:constructive) and
equation eq:movingCount are the scalar input below. That source includes
an explicit O_alpha(L^3) positive scalar realization, with no exponential,
Gaussian-integral or error-function oracle. The lemma's proof and
actual-source audit remain the source of that scalar construction.

Fix 0<alpha<1, beta=1-alpha. Let K,K' be combinatorial Laplacians of
weighted undirected graphs on the same N vertices, and let H>0 be a
common positive diagonal vertex-mass matrix, held fixed across the
update. Set

  A=H^-1/2 K H^-1/2, B=H^-1/2 K' H^-1/2.

Masses are arbitrary; H need not equal a degree matrix. Suppose K' is
obtained from K by increasing k>=1 distinct non-loop edge conductances
by amounts d_e>0. Let M count edges in the union of the endpoint graphs.
There is a randomized algorithm returning general real factors P,Q with

  ||PQ^T-(B^alpha-A^alpha)|| <=epsilon||B-A||^alpha       (1)

with probability >=1-delta, in expected

  ~O_alpha((M+N)k)                                     (2)

arithmetic operations. Suppressed factors are logarithmic in dimensions,
k,1/epsilon,1/delta. No mass-ratio, spectral-gap, endpoint norm, or
condition-number logarithm is suppressed. Endpoints may be singular
or disconnected, and additions may change their connected components.

The factor width is O_alpha(k log^2(2k/epsilon)). For each fixed k,
uniformly over all endpoint graphs, dimensions, conductances and masses,
as epsilon decreases to zero it is at most

  [k/(4 pi^2 alpha(1-alpha))+o_k(1)] log^2(1/epsilon).   (3)

The output is a correction and requires no base matrix function.
Dense expansion adds O(N^2 times width). The approximation PQ^T need
not be exactly symmetric. An exactly symmetric signed factor follows
by symmetrization with at most twice the width and the same work order;
no sharp leading coefficient for that symmetric implementation is
claimed here. The lower bound in Section 7 applies to ALL approximating
matrices, so the leading constant in (3) is sharp for general factors.

At alpha=1/2 the algorithm uses real arithmetic, comparisons, scalar
square roots and discrete randomness. General fixed alpha needs its
node-dependent scalar power evaluations charged as in the imported
source. This is an exact-arithmetic theorem with expected work, not a
finite-word stability theorem or deterministic runtime cap.

2. THE POSITIVE UPDATE'S INTRINSIC SCALE IS MASS-INDEPENDENT
=========================================================
For e={u,v}, write b_e=e_u-e_v and

  U_e=sqrt(d_e) H^-1/2 b_e, E=B-A=UU^T,
  a=||E||_F, b=||U||_F^2=tr E, chi=b/a.

All are computable sparsely after merging parallel changes. Explicitly,
if h_v is the mass at v and c_v=sum_(e incident v)d_e, then

  a^2=sum_v(c_v/h_v)^2+2 sum_e d_e^2/(h_u h_v),
  b=sum_e d_e(1/h_u+1/h_v).

Because E>=0 and rank E<=k, its nonnegative eigenvalues give

  ||E||<=a<=sqrt(k)||E||,  1<=chi<=sqrt(k).             (4)

This bound holds for arbitrarily unequal masses. It relies on positivity;
the corresponding bound for arbitrary signed mass-weighted updates
cannot be inferred from the unweighted-vertex-mass case.

3. EXACT AND INEXACT TWO-ENDPOINT NODES
=====================================
For t>0 set R_t=(A+tI)^-1, S_t=(B+tI)^-1,
V_t=R_t U, W_t=S_t U. The exact resolvent identity gives

  D_t=R_t-S_t=V_t W_t^T,
  ||D_t||<=min(1/t,a/t^2).                             (5)

For the normalized fractional difference,

  B^alpha-A^alpha=c_alpha int_0^infinity t^alpha D_t dt,
  c_alpha=sin(pi alpha)/pi.

With t=a exp(y), divide its logarithmic integrand by a^alpha. It has
the PSD strip envelope of the imported lemma, analytic for |Im y|<pi.
Indeed |lambda+t exp(i theta)|>=cos(d/2)(lambda+t) for
lambda,t>=0 and |theta|<=d<pi. Both endpoint resolvent estimates and
the difference identity give envelope

  ||F(x+i theta)||<=C_(alpha,d) min(exp(alpha x),exp(-beta x)),

uniformly in all graph data. The real-line value and derivative bounds
used by the constructive scalar lemma transfer for the same reason.

Suppose each column solve producing Vhat_t,What_t has relative energy
error <=eta at its respective shifted endpoint. Then

  ||Vhat-V||_F,||What-W||_F <=eta sqrt(b)/t,
  ||V||,||W||<=sqrt(b)/t.

This follows because for C>=tI and x=C^-1u,

  ||xhat-x||_2<=t^-1/2||xhat-x||_C
             <=eta t^-1/2 sqrt(u^TC^-1u)<=eta||u||/t.

No common, linear or symmetric approximate inverse map is assumed.
For eta<=1, the cross node obeys

  ||Vhat_t What_t^T-D_t||<=(2eta+eta^2)b/t^2
                        <=3eta b/t^2.                (6)

The inner SDD system implementing a mass-normalized shifted action is

  (A+tI)^-1u=H^1/2 (K+tH)^-1 H^1/2u.                (7)

The same formula holds at the second endpoint. The matrix K+tH is
SDD positive definite. Energy norms transport exactly: if xhat,x are
inner solutions, then

  ||H^1/2(xhat-x)||_(A+tI)=||xhat-x||_(K+tH).

Thus a relative energy solver for the sparse inner system supplies
exactly the physical error statement used in (6). Sparse matvecs and
the two diagonal scalings cost O(M+N). No polynomial or logarithmic
factor in mass ratios enters this exact-arithmetic transfer.

4. ACTUAL SHARP NODES AND THE SOLVE-ACCURACY BUDGET
=================================================
Apply the imported constructive lemma with d_0=pi. After its fixed
constant budget adjustments, the actual nodes s_j>0 and nonnegative
coefficients a_j satisfy

  q<=L^2/(4 pi^2 alpha beta)+O_alpha(L^(7/4)),
  |log s_j|<=B_0=C_alpha(L+1),
  sum_j a_j<=C_alpha(L+1),                             (8)

and the exact-resolvent quadrature error is O_alpha(a^alpha exp(-L)).
Here B_0 denotes a computable bound from the retained pre-rounded
log-node locations and their rounding allowance, as in the imported
source; no logarithm of an input scale is used to compute it.

Set t_j=a s_j and positive physical weights

  omega_j=a_j c_alpha t_j^(alpha+1).

Equation (6), with the SAME eta for every node, gives

  sum_j omega_j ||Vhat_j What_j^T-D_(t_j)||
   <=3c_alpha eta b sum_j a_j t_j^(alpha-1)
   = 3c_alpha eta chi a^alpha sum_j a_j s_j^(-beta)
   <=C_alpha eta chi a^alpha(L+1)exp(beta B_0).         (9)

Consequently it suffices to choose

  eta<=c_alpha' exp(-L-beta B_0)/[chi(L+1)].            (10)

Its logarithmic precision is

  log(1/eta)=O_alpha(L+log chi)=O_alpha(L+log(2k)).      (11)

This is an analysis-only logarithm and exponential. For an actual
scalar realization, choose an integer m>=2(L+B_0+1), produce 2^-m by
halving, and take eta=2^-m/[C_alpha chi(L+1)]. Since 2 log2>1 and
beta<1, a large enough fixed C_alpha makes this at most (10).
Integer comparisons/halving cost O_alpha(L+1) operations.

Choose L by repeated halving as in the imported source, so that

  exp(-L)<=epsilon/[C_alpha(k+1)],
  L=log(1/epsilon)+O_alpha(log(2k)).                    (12)

Combining the quadrature error, (9),(10), and
a^alpha<=k^(alpha/2)||E||^alpha proves (1), with slack for the fixed
constant and scalar weight errors. As in the imported lemma, fixed
c_alpha and node-dependent powers are evaluated to the error budget;
its positive actual scalar construction already includes the relevant
rounding/constant estimates. The resulting q from (8),(12) proves (3).

In particular for fixed k the O(L^(7/4)) node-count remainder is o(L^2),
and the O(log(2k)) shift in L does not change the leading coefficient.
No free Gaussian quadrature or exact transcendental node oracle is
being invoked.

5. FACTORS, WORK, AND CONFIDENCE
==============================
Concatenate P_j=sqrt(omega_j)Vhat_j and
Q_j=sqrt(omega_j)What_j. Each has k columns. There are 2kq column
solves, O(Nkq) factor entries, O(N+k) sparse normalization arithmetic
apart from deterministic sorting, and O_alpha(L^3) scalar construction
work. Equation (11) and the standard nearly-linear SDD energy solver
give expected total work

  ~O_alpha((M+N)kq log(1/eta)+L^3)
   =~O_alpha((M+N)k).                                 (13)

The suppressed logs include only the parameters specified in Section 1.
For arbitrary desired confidence delta, use the constant-success energy
solvers and median pairwise energy-distance amplification in
graph-fractional-solver-audit.txt, Section 8. With
s=O(log(2kq/delta)) independent candidates at eta/3, choose the one
minimizing its median pairwise inner-system energy distance. A strict
majority of good candidates implies selected energy error <=eta times
the true solution energy norm. Pairwise distances use sparse K+tH and
cost O((M+N)s^2) per column, only logarithmic overhead. A union bound
then gives simultaneous accuracy of all 2kq solves. This does not
assume a common linear solver map, and it preserves expected work.

Primary solver: Koutis--Miller--Peng, arXiv:1102.4842v4,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.4842
Theorem 4.6 (expected energy-accuracy runtime), Lemma 4.3 (nonnegative
diagonal shifts), and Lemma 4.5 (constant-success preconditioning chain)
are the same inputs audited in graph-fractional-solver-audit.txt.

For an exactly symmetric output, replace each cross node by its symmetric
part. The factor identity

  (VW^T+WV^T)/2
   =[V+W,V-W] diag(I_k/4,-I_k/4)[V+W,V-W]^T

gives a signed factor with width at most 2kq and O(Nkq) construction
arithmetic. Symmetrization cannot increase error to the symmetric target.
The sharpness assertion (3) concerns the original general factors only.

6. OTHER GRAPH UPDATE CLASSES COVERED BY THE SAME UPGRADE
======================================================
For unit vertex masses, the same sharp quadrature and solve budget work
for arbitrary SIGNED changes, using U_e=sqrt(|d_e|)b_e and signature J.
Then b/a<=sqrt(2k), by the off-diagonal cancellation bound proved in
graph-fractional-solver-audit.txt. Replace cross nodes by Vhat J What^T.
The upper coefficient (3) is still valid, with no gap or connectivity
promise. A matching lower coefficient restricted to this ordinary
unit-mass graph class has not been established here.

For arbitrary common masses and signed edits, the upper algorithm still
works with chi=||U||_F^2/||E||_F, but the work includes log chi through
the solve precision. One may not silently suppress that parameter.
For changes of a single sign, chi<=sqrt(k) as in Section 2; the same
parameter-free construction therefore also covers all-negative edits
with both endpoints nonnegative-weight graph Laplacians. Swap the two
endpoints if necessary to reduce to the positive case.

7. MATCHING LOWER BOUND FROM CONNECTED MASS-WEIGHTED PATHS
=======================================================
The separate graph realization in graph-path-realization.txt proves
that every positive rank-one fractional matrix correction is approximable,
in the strict singular-value-count sense, by increasing one existing
edge on a connected weighted path with fixed positive vertex masses.
It normalizes the update norm EXACTLY to one. Connecting k such path
witnesses by arbitrarily weak unchanged edges yields a connected path
with k increased edges, positive rank-k perturbation of norm one, and
the same strict counts in the limit. The statement concerns suprema
over all finite dimensions, masses and conductances; these may depend
on epsilon. It is not a lower bound on a fixed graph as epsilon varies.

The companion positive-matrix count theorem therefore gives

  sup N_epsilon(B^alpha-A^alpha)
   =[k/(4 pi^2 alpha beta)+o_k(1)]log^2(1/epsilon),      (14)

where the supremum ranges already over those connected mass-weighted
paths and norm-one positive k-edge updates. N_epsilon counts singular
values STRICTLY greater than epsilon. The same expression is a generic
PSD upper bound, so there is no gap between the two classes at leading
order. The matrix theorem's matching lower uses positive rank-one
witnesses and direct sums, exactly the sign class realized by the paths.

For any matrix T, a rank-l approximation with operator error <=epsilon
requires l>=N_epsilon(T), by the singular-value min--max principle.
Thus (14) forces the coefficient in (3) for every algorithm, including
randomized algorithms that succeed with any positive probability on
each fixed input. The realized paths are within the mass-positive-update
algorithm's class, and (4) prevents any extra mass-ratio precision cost.
Consequently (3),(13),(14) give an actual near-linear thin-construction
algorithm with SHARP leading worst-case width on this graph class.

This is width optimality, not a runtime lower bound. It makes no
statement that a fixed graph, prescribed mass range, ordinary degree
normalization, or unit-mass paths attain the same coefficient.

8. CONTRIBUTION AND PRIORITY BOUNDARY
===================================
The scalar quadrature, universal matrix compression constants, graph
path realization, sparse shifted solves, and resolvent identity all have
their own sources and audits. This note proves that their actual
implementations fit together without losing the leading width, invoking
an uncharged scalar oracle, or introducing a hidden mass/condition
parameter in the positive graph class. It yields both (13) and the
sharp coefficient (3), with (14) providing the graph-restricted lower.
