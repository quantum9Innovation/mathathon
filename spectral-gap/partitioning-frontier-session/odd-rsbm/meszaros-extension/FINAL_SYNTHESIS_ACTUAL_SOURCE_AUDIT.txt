1. Mixing reference and parity

The all-ones row functional has rank one over F_p for every n>=1, even
when p divides n. Thus its level set has p^(n-1) vectors. The row-sum
identity C1=d1 holds for the configuration adjacency convention including
loops counted twice, also when p divides d. Constants are mapped
deterministically to da1, so the separate constant reference is correct.
Nothing in the input partition or companion estimates imposes n even;
the condition used is dn even. The central lattice proof requires p odd
and d>=3; it does not require p coprime to d or n.

2. Assembly of the unscaled strong-mixing sum

Shrinking eta preserves the nearconstant bound. The selected entropy
inequality makes the number of nearconstant inputs exponentially smaller
than p^n, so their reference contribution vanishes. Their actual atom
sum is O(n^(1-d/2))=o(1), including d=3.

For sufficiently large n the central and nearconstant sets are disjoint.
Every remaining nonconstant input outside the central window has maximum
profile mass below 1-eta and satisfies the strict profile-distance
condition of the entropy tail. Thus the actual intermediate atom sum is
O(n^-2). The reference contribution there is bounded by p times a
fixed-alphabet multinomial profile tail, which tends to zero for every
fixed positive B. A fixed K>sqrt(B) covers the log(n+1)/log n adjustment
for the central count bound. Its uniform atom error is o(p^(-n+1));
multiplication by at most p^n inputs gives p*o(1)=o(1) for fixed p.
No error rate uniform in a growing prime is used.

3. Planted shift and uniform affine bound

With delta=d1-d2, the reference condition for Cx=delta*x+b is exactly
d2*s(x)=s(b). The proof correctly chooses only p not dividing d2.
For nonconstant inputs the affine functional admits p^(N-1) solutions,
and each reference atom has mass p^(-N+1), giving at most one. For
constant x=a1 the condition is b=d2*a1, giving at most one constant.
The upper bound two is therefore valid even if p divides N or d1.

4. Product first moment and reference evaluation

Independence of the internal configurations gives the displayed kernel
moment. Replacing the first factor costs at most (2+e_N)e_N by the
uniform affine bound on the second block. Replacing the second costs
at most 2e_N using the reference affine bound. Both hold for every B,
so the total 4e_N+e_N^2 is uniform in B.

When both inputs are nonconstant, both reference conditions reduce to
s(x)+s(y)=0; this is one nonzero linear equation on F_p^(2N), hence at
most p^(2N-1) pairs. Their mass gives p. Constant-constant pairs require
a+b=0 and contribute p. If only x=a1 is constant, the exact first
equation is By=-d2*a1, giving at most p^(N-rank B) vectors for each a.
The remaining nonconstant atom is at most p^(-N+1), giving p^(2-rank B).
The reversed orientation is the same. No sign of the planted shift is
reversed in these steps.

The deterministic rank bound uses disjoint row supports. Greedy row
selection discards at most 1+d2(d2-1) rows per selection because the
cross matrix is zero-one with both row and column sums d2. Selected
rows are nonzero and independent over every field. Thus the mixed
terms vanish uniformly over B along all admissible sizes.

5. Real nullity, prime limits and simplicity conditioning

Real nullity at least two annihilates all integer minors of order 2N-1.
These minors remain zero modulo p, giving an F_p kernel of size at least
p^2. Markov therefore bounds the limsup failure probability by 2/p.
The argument takes the n-limit separately for each fixed odd prime not
dividing d2; arbitrarily large such primes make the configuration-model
limsup zero. It does not substitute a prime depending on N.

Only after that uniform configuration-model failure probability is zero
does the proof condition both independent blocks on simplicity. For a
fixed degree the simplicity probability is bounded below away from zero
on every admissible size sequence. Conditioning has a fixed bounded cost
and gives precisely uniform labelled simple regular blocks. The bound is
already uniform in deterministic B, so neither conditioning nor sampling
an independent cross graph weakens it. No moment is transferred merely
by qualitative contiguity.

6. Exact recovery and scope

The deterministic planted eigenvector is nonzero, and on the simple
eigenvalue event spans the rational kernel. Its first coordinate is
nonzero, so normalizing recovers its signs up to global exchange. For
fixed degrees, bounded row norms give O(N)-bit minors by Hadamard, and
standard fraction-free elimination has polynomial bit complexity.
