Exact small-block lifts for positive rational Petz divergences
============================================================
Working research record, 2026-11-15 UTC.

STATUS AND RESULT

The requested small-block construction is proved below from first
principles. It represents the STANDARD/PETZ rational divergence, not
the trace of the maximal matrix perspective. Each rational node needs
exactly TWO complex-Hermitian PSD blocks of order 2n. There is no n^2
Kronecker matrix in the representation or its construction.

The idea is the elementary infimal convolution identity for the inverse
of a sum of positive operators. It is a classical quadratic identity;
no claim is made that this lift is new. It reconstructs the kind of
small-block result asserted without proof in Faust--Fawzi (2023),
Theorem 5, without assuming its unpublished reference [Faw23].

Combining the lift with the positive rational bounds proved in
positive-rational-approximation.tex gives, for trace-one rho,sigma > 0,

 |D(rho||sigma)-D_m(rho||sigma)|
   <= (C/2) exp[-pi sqrt(2m)] chi^2_P(rho||sigma),

 chi^2_P(rho||sigma) = Tr[rho^2 sigma^{-1}]-1,

using 2m blocks of order 2n and 4mn^2 real auxiliary dimensions.
Thus scalar error epsilon requires at most

 m <= log^2(C/epsilon)/(2pi^2)+3

rational nodes, hence at most log^2(C/epsilon)/pi^2+6 such PSD blocks.
Constants are fixed and independent of m, n, rho and sigma. C is the
fixed a=1,b=2 constant in the already proved scalar theorem, not a new
explicit numerical constant. Arithmetic preparation/root extraction
and numerical conditioning retain the limitations of that theorem.

An additional sign argument gives positive LOWER rational approximants
at the same leading rate. Their Petz divergences are globally below D.
Adding one endpoint kernel gives a convex UPPER SDP as well. Full
statements and error/optimization certificates appear in Sections 4--6.

This is an exact representation/approximation theorem. It is not a
general O(n^2)-time solver theorem and does not settle the broader
matrix-multiplication objective. No novelty, priority, external review,
or formal-verification certificate is asserted.

1. THE QUADRATIC IDENTITY

Let H be any finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, with positive
self-adjoint operators P,Q and vector c. Then

 <c,(P+Q)^{-1}c>
   = min_{x+y=c} [<x,P^{-1}x>+<y,Q^{-1}y>].                 (1)

Proof. Put z=(P+Q)^{-1}c. For any x+y=c, expansion gives

 <x,P^{-1}x>+<y,Q^{-1}y>-<c,z>
   = <x-Pz,P^{-1}(x-Pz)>+<y-Qz,Q^{-1}(y-Qz)> >= 0.

The expansion uses real parts in the two cross terms, so is equally
valid over complex H. Equality holds at x=Pz, y=Qz. The minimum is
attained, and is unique because P and Q are positive definite. QED.

An equivalent proof is the Lagrange multiplier condition
P^{-1}x=Q^{-1}y=z, followed by (P+Q)z=c. Neither proof forms the
matrix of P+Q in a basis.

2. EXACT TWO-BLOCK LIFT FOR A SYLVESTER QUADRATIC

Let A,B > 0 be n by n Hermitian matrices and C any COMPLEX n by n
matrix. Equip the matrix space with <X,Y>_HS=Tr[X*Y]. Set

 K_{A,B}(C)=<C,(L_A+R_B)^{-1}C>_HS,
 L_A(Z)=AZ, R_B(Z)=ZB.

Left and right multiplication are positive self-adjoint operators.
Applying (1) gives

 K_{A,B}(C)
   = min_{X+Y=C} {Tr[X* A^{-1} X]+Tr[Y B^{-1} Y*]}.        (2)

Consequently tau >= K_{A,B}(C) if and only if there exist complex
X,Y and Hermitian U,V satisfying

 X+Y=C,

 [ A   X ] >= 0,       [ B   Y* ] >= 0,                   (3)
 [ X*  U ]             [ Y   V  ]

 tau >= Tr U+Tr V.

Each displayed PSD block has order 2n. The first Schur complement is
U >= X* A^{-1}X. The second is V >= Y B^{-1}Y*, with the order of
Y and Y* ESSENTIAL. Minimizing the sum of traces proves sufficiency
and necessity via (2). Every matrix entry is affine in A,B,C,X,Y,U,V.

One can eliminate Y=C-X. X has 2n^2 real coordinates and U,V have
n^2 each, hence exactly 4n^2 real auxiliary coordinates. No scalar
quadratic-form auxiliary is required. The optimal X,Y need NOT be
Hermitian even when C is Hermitian; imposing this restriction is not
authorized by the proof and generally changes the value.

If Z solves AZ+ZB=C, the equality certificate is

 X=AZ, Y=ZB, U=X* A^{-1}X, V=Y B^{-1}Y*.

This is only a proof/verification certificate. The SDP formulation (3)
does not solve a Sylvester equation or construct an n^2 by n^2 matrix.

Rectangular generalization: for A of order p, B of order q, and C of
shape p by q, the same construction uses two blocks of order p+q.
After eliminating Y there are 2pq+p^2+q^2=(p+q)^2 real auxiliary
coordinates. This is a general Sylvester quadratic identity, not a
special property of density matrices.

3. SPECIALIZATION TO THE STANDARD/PETZ KERNEL

For 0<t<1 let f_t(x)=(x-1)^2/[1+t(x-1)]. Let rho,sigma > 0 and
Delta=rho-sigma. Define the standard/Petz divergence

 S_f(rho||sigma)
 = <sigma^{1/2}, f(L_rho R_sigma^{-1}) sigma^{1/2}>_HS.

In eigenbases rho u_i=r_i u_i and sigma v_j=s_j v_j,

 S_f = sum_{i,j} s_j f(r_i/s_j) |<u_i,v_j>|^2.

For f=f_t this becomes

 S_{f_t}
 = sum_{i,j} (r_i-s_j)^2 |<u_i,v_j>|^2/[t r_i+(1-t)s_j]
 = <Delta,(t L_rho+(1-t)R_sigma)^{-1}Delta>_HS.             (4)

The last identity follows because
<u_i,Delta v_j>=(r_i-s_j)<u_i,v_j>. This also proves that the
lift represents exactly the Petz kernel used in the scalar-to-quantum
error transfer; there is no substitution of a maximal divergence.

Use (3) with A=t rho, B=(1-t)sigma and C=Delta:

 X+Y=rho-sigma,

 [ t rho   X ] >= 0,    [ (1-t)sigma   Y* ] >= 0,         (5)
 [ X*      U ]          [ Y            V  ]

 tau >= Tr U+Tr V.

This is the promised exact two-block representation.

An equivalent scaling avoids small prefactors on the diagonal:

 sqrt(t) X+sqrt(1-t)Y=Delta,

 [ rho   X ] >= 0,      [ sigma   Y* ] >= 0,              (6)
 [ X*    U ]            [ Y       V  ]

 tau >= Tr U+Tr V.

The extra square roots are scalar coefficients. Formula (6) also makes
the endpoints transparent. At t=0 use one block
[sigma,Delta;Delta,U]>=0 and tau>=Tr U; at t=1 use
[rho,Delta;Delta,U]>=0 and tau>=Tr U. All constructed interior nodes
in the existing scalar theorem satisfy 0<t<1, so (5) suffices there.

For fhat=sum_{i=1}^m u_i f_{t_i}, u_i>0, one takes separate X_i,Y_i,
U_i,V_i for each node in (5) and the single affine inequality

 tau >= sum_i u_i (Tr U_i+Tr V_i).                        (7)

There are exactly 2m complex-Hermitian PSD blocks of order 2n, and
4mn^2 real auxiliary coordinates after eliminating every Y_i.
The blocks imply rho,sigma >= 0; the stated theorem restricts to the
positive definite domain where inverses are unambiguous.

For completeness, the same blocks give the usual PSD extension.
For general A,B>=0, Schur complements mean
U>=X* A^+ X, range(X) subset range(A), and
V>=Y B^+ Y*, range(Y*) subset range(B), where + denotes pseudoinverse.
The infimum identity still holds with (L_A+R_B)^+ and the condition
C in range(L_A+R_B); it is infinite if that condition fails.
To prove it, take Z=(L_A+R_B)^+ C, X=AZ, Y=ZB and use the same
completion of squares with pseudoinverses on their ranges. For
A=t rho, B=(1-t)sigma and C=Delta, the range condition always holds:
if rho Z=0 and Z sigma=0, then <Z,rho>=<Z,sigma>=0, so
<Z,Delta>=0. Thus Delta is perpendicular to the kernel of the
self-adjoint Sylvester operator. Its value equals the limit obtained
from rho+epsilon I,sigma+epsilon I, because Delta stays fixed and the
Sylvester operator increases by epsilon times the identity. This
also supplies a direct boundary proof without assuming continuity of
an unspecified matrix functional.

Joint convexity is immediate: the epigraph is a projection of the
convex affine-LMI feasible set. Positive homogeneity follows by scaling
all matrix variables. For a completely positive trace-preserving map
Phi, apply id_2 tensor Phi to each PSD block and Phi to the splitting
constraint. Trace preservation keeps the objective unchanged, proving

 S_fhat(Phi(rho)||Phi(sigma)) <= S_fhat(rho||sigma),

where images are positive definite, or with the PSD extension
when they are singular. Thus the lift itself also proves data
processing under quantum channels. This property is not needed to
prove the approximation error.

4. SHARP GLOBAL QUANTUM APPROXIMATION THEOREM

Assume a scalar positive rational approximant obeys

 |f_a(x)-fhat_m(x)| <= epsilon f_b(x), x>0.                (8)

All spectral weights s_j |<u_i,v_j>|^2 are nonnegative. Termwise
application of (8) to the spectral formula in Section 3 yields

 |S_{f_a}(rho||sigma)-S_fhat_m(rho||sigma)|
    <= epsilon S_{f_b}(rho||sigma).                      (9)

This proof uses only ordinary diagonalization; it needs neither a
matrix perspective identity nor a new monotonicity theorem.

The proved scalar result in positive-rational-approximation.tex is

 epsilon_m <= C_{a,b} exp[-2pi sqrt(a(b-a)m/b)],

for 1<=a<b<=2, with positive distinct interior nodes. Hence (9), with
the exact 2m-block lift, holds for ALL those power quasi-entropies.

For a=1,b=2 and trace-one rho,sigma,

 S_f1 = D(rho||sigma)=Tr[rho(log rho-log sigma)],
 S_f2 = (Tr[rho^2 sigma^{-1}]-1)/2 = chi_P^2/2.

Therefore there exist jointly convex, nonnegative, channel-monotone
functions D_m, with 2m blocks of order 2n, such that

 |D-D_m| <= (C/2) exp[-pi sqrt(2m)] chi_P^2.              (10)

Given epsilon>0 with L=log(C/epsilon)>0, the explicit scalar
construction gives error <= epsilon chi_P^2/2 using

 m <= L^2/(2pi^2)+3,       #PSD blocks=2m.                (11)

There is no hidden n factor in the number of blocks. The dependence
on n consists of block order 2n and O(mn^2) real variable dimensions.

For positive matrices with unequal traces, use generalized relative
entropy D_gen=Tr[rho(log rho-log sigma)]-Tr rho+Tr sigma,
and the same proof gives error <=epsilon Tr[Delta sigma^{-1}Delta]/2.

5. POSITIVE ONE-SIDED APPROXIMANTS AT THE SAME RATE

This section uses the SIGN of the actual scalar construction, not just
its absolute-error theorem. The construction uses the multipoint
polynomial Omega(t)=t(t+1) times products of squared real linear
factors. Its exact Stieltjes error has sign sign(Omega(-x)), hence

 f_a(x)-r_m(x) has sign sign(x-1), 1<=a<b<=2,             (12)

apart from interpolation zeros; r_m is the positive rational sum.
This sign is explicitly proved in the existing manuscript.

General proposition. Suppose |f_a-r_m|<=e f_b, e>0, for the actual
positive construction with 1<=a<b<=2. Define r_m^-=r_m/(1+e),
simply scaling all its positive weights. Then

 0 <= f_a-r_m^- <= [2e/(1+e)] f_b  for EVERY x>0.        (13)

Proof on 0<x<=1. Taylor's formula and f_a''(x)=x^{a-2} imply
f_b<=f_a. In addition, a f_a(x)<=b f_b(x). To prove the latter
including a=1, put y=-log x and use the identity

 a f_a(x)=1-x-x integral_0^y exp[-(a-1)t]dt.

Its right-hand side is increasing in a, proving the claim. Thus
f_b<=f_a<=(b/a)f_b<=2f_b on this interval. The sign (12) and the
error bound now give

 f_a <= r_m <= f_a+e f_b <= (1+e)f_a.

Thus r_m^-<=f_a and
0<=f_a-r_m^-<=[e/(1+e)]f_a<=[2e/(1+e)]f_b.

Proof on x>=1. Here f_a<=f_b by f_a''(x)<=f_b''(x), while (12)
gives r_m<=f_a. Hence r_m^-<=f_a and

 f_a-r_m^-=[f_a-r_m+e f_a]/(1+e)<=2e f_b/(1+e).

Both sides vanish at x=1 with the continuous relative convention.
This proves (13), preserving the number and location of poles and
strict positivity of all weights. QED.

Consequently every power-divergence construction in the full positive
C1 range admits a positive one-sided lower approximation with the
same leading exponent and rational-node budget. For the negative
range -1<=b<a<=0, transport the corresponding positive-parameter
inequality by f_a(x)=x f_{1-a}(1/x), which replaces every node t by
1-t and preserves positive weights. This transfers one-sidedness
and the relative error without another scaling.

The same positive spectral weights transfer (13) to the corresponding
Petz divergences. For any fixed a in [1,2) and b=2 the lower Petz
approximation has a convex SDP epigraph with 2m blocks of order 2n.
Adding eta S_f2 gives an upper SDP with just one additional block,
with the same error scale. The remainder specializes to a=1,b=2.

Take e=eta/2, eta>0, and define for relative entropy

 D_m^-=S_{r_m^-},
 D_m^+=D_m^-+eta S_f2.

Applying the same positive spectral weights as in Section 4 gives

 D_m^- <= D <= D_m^+,
 D-D_m^- <= eta chi_P^2/2,
 D_m^+-D <= eta chi_P^2/2,                               (14)

using

 m <= log^2(2C/eta)/(2pi^2)+3.                            (15)

Both functions are jointly convex and have explicit affine SDP
epigraphs. D_m^- uses 2m blocks of order 2n. The additional term in
D_m^+ needs ONE endpoint block

 [ sigma   Delta ] >= 0,   cost +(eta/2)Tr W.             (16)
 [ Delta   W     ]

Thus D_m^+ uses 2m+1 blocks of order 2n. The factor 2 in log(2C/eta)
only changes the fixed prefactor and lower-order node terms; the
sharp leading coefficient 1/(2pi^2) in the rational-node count stays
the same. In m language the lower approximation has error bounded by
a fixed constant times exp[-pi sqrt(2m)] chi_P^2.

The extra endpoint pole in the upper approximant is natural. A finite
positive sum with all nodes strictly inside (0,1) grows only linearly
as x->infinity, whereas f1(x) grows as x log x. Such a sum cannot be
a global upper bound on f1. The t=0 kernel grows quadratically and
provides a valid positive global upper approximant.

6. OPTIMIZATION CERTIFICATES AND SOLVER SCOPE

Let F be any feasible set defined by affine constraints and LMIs in
rho,sigma, restricted to trace-one positive definite states. Define

 p=inf_F D, p^-=inf_F D_m^-, p^+=inf_F D_m^+.

Since both approximants have SDP epigraphs,

 p^- <= p <= p^+.                                       (17)

Whenever the optima are attained and chi_P^2<=R throughout F,

 p-p^- <= eta R/2,       p^+-p <= eta R/2.                (18)

The same statements hold for infima by an approximating sequence.
Thus the pair of SDPs gives a certified objective bracket of width
at most eta R. For target width delta and R>0, take eta=delta/R;
then the common rational node count is at most

 log^2(2CR/delta)/(2pi^2)+3.                              (19)

Even without a known uniform R, a feasible state returned by either
program gives a true upper bound by evaluating D_m^+ at that state,
and the lower SDP gives a true lower bound. Their numerical primal/
dual objective bounds give an a posteriori optimization bracket.
In particular, if x_-=(rho_-,sigma_-) minimizes the LOWER program,
then [p^-,D_m^+(x_-)] brackets p, with its EXACT width equal to

 D_m^+(x_-)-p^-=eta chi_P^2(x_-)/2.                       (20)

Thus no supplied global chi^2 cap is needed for this a posteriori
certificate: evaluate chi_P^2 at the returned lower optimizer. For an
approximately optimal lower point x with D_m^-(x)<=p^-+zeta, the same
width is at most zeta+eta chi_P^2(x)/2. More generally, replacing p^-
by a certified SDP dual bound ell gives the directly checkable width
[D_m^-(x)-ell]+eta chi_P^2(x)/2. This requires a feasible x and a
valid dual bound, not merely a small solver-reported residual.
Solver feasibility/rounding tolerances must of course be included in
a certified numerical implementation; none are hidden in (17).

A convenient sufficient bound is rho<=kappa sigma, kappa>=1. Set
C0=sigma^{-1/2}rho sigma^{-1/2}<=kappa I. Then

 Tr[rho^2 sigma^{-1}]=Tr[sigma C0^2]
    <=kappa Tr[sigma C0]=kappa,

so one may take R=kappa-1. At R=0 the feasible states have rho=sigma
and the divergence is zero, so no approximation is needed.

Cone storage versus the direct Kronecker epigraph:

  Direct: m blocks of order n^2+1, cone dimension ~mn^4,
          logdet barrier parameter m(n^2+1).
  Here:   2m blocks of order 2n, cone dimension 8mn^2,
          logdet barrier parameter 4mn.

The cone dimensions here count real coordinates of complex-Hermitian
cones. The trace inequality contributes one nonnegative scalar cone
and adds 1 to the barrier parameter. Other problem constraints add
their own cones and variables. The upper program adds one block of
order 2n and n^2 real auxiliary coordinates.

Under the usual assumptions for a short-step interior-point theorem,
the PSD-cone contribution to the iteration count changes from
O(n sqrt(m) log(1/tol)) to O(sqrt(mn) log(1/tol)). This comparison is
ONLY an iteration/barrier-parameter comparison. The per-iteration
linear solve depends on all affine constraints and variables and can
still be expensive. Even assembling all auxiliary matrices costs
Omega(mn^2), and generic dense Newton elimination is not quadratic in
n. No end-to-end O(n^2), bit-complexity, numerical-conditioning,
machine-stability or unconditional practical-speedup claim follows.

Real-only solvers can use the standard realification of a Hermitian
PSD matrix, doubling its order from 2n to 4n. This changes constants,
not the O(mn^2) storage scaling. For genuinely real symmetric rho,
sigma, all optimal variables may be real; X is still unrestricted
real n by n and U,V are symmetric, with 2n^2+n real auxiliary
coordinates per node.

7. COMPUTABLE SCALAR CERTIFICATE AND CHECKS

The unspecified asymptotic constant C is not needed to certify a
particular constructed approximation. Here is an explicit certificate
specialized from the two comparison bounds in the existing manuscript
to a=1,b=2. For its two node products B_-,B_+, compute

 d_- = max_{0<=s<=1} sqrt(s) B_-(s)^2,
 d_+ = max_{0<=s<=1} sqrt(s) B_+(s)^2.

Then a rigorous scalar relative error bound is

 e_m=2pi max(d_-^2,d_+^2)+4d_-d_+.                       (21)

Indeed its left-hand comparison bound is 2pi d_-^2+4d_-d_+, and
its right-hand comparison bound is 4d_-d_++2pi d_+^2. Thus
|f1-r_m|<=e_m f2 globally. The maxima are taken over endpoints and
positive roots of the explicit critical polynomial in the existing
manuscript with r=1/4. If the products use the same n Ganelius nodes,
then m=2n+1, d_-=d_+=d, and e_m=(2pi+4)d^2.

For actual coefficients and any rigorously evaluated upper bound
e>=e_m, use the positive rational lower approximation r_m/(1+e)
and its upper correction eta_m f2, where eta_m=2e/(1+e). Then the
pointwise and a posteriori brackets above hold with eta=eta_m. This
is a finite certificate using explicit scalar products and root
problems, without knowing a numerical value for the Ganelius constant.
Polynomial root extraction and verified evaluations remain separately
charged; this is not a new finite-precision/bit-complexity theorem.

Random genuinely complex examples at n=2,3,5,9, twenty each, with
strictly positive trace-one rho,sigma and t uniformly between .001
and .999 were checked with scipy.linalg.solve_sylvester. Setting
Z to the Sylvester solution and X=t rho Z, Y=(1-t)Z sigma yielded:

 n   max ||X+Y-Delta||_F    max |split cost-<Delta,Z>|
 2       4.11e-16                    8.88e-16
 3       1.31e-15                    2.66e-15
 5       3.80e-15                    2.13e-14
 9       3.38e-15                    2.49e-14

Random perturbations (X+H,Y-H) always increased the objective, as
predicted by the exact completion-of-squares proof. These floating
point tests are orientation checks, not substitutes for the proof.
No SDP solver was used for these checks.

8. PRIMARY-SOURCE SCREEN AND THE ABANDONED FRENKEL ROUTE

Primary source for the claimed old small-block theorem:
  Oisin Faust and Hamza Fawzi, Rational approximations of operator
  monotone and operator convex functions, arXiv:2305.12405v1 (2023),
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12405
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.12405

Theorem 5 states O(m) blocks of order 2n, with a global Petz-chi^2
weighted relative-entropy error. The preceding sentence explicitly
offers it without proof, based on forthcoming [Faw23]. The reference
title is Smaller semidefinite approximations of quantum f-divergences.
The paper's stated conjectural exponent becomes the proved scalar
exponent used here. The present lift is independently derived and
does not resolve whether it is identical to [Faw23]'s intended proof.

Targeted title/author and Sylvester/infimal-convolution searches did
not locate a separately available current primary proof of [Faw23].
Faust's December 2023 thesis lists it as in preparation:
  https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/95b542c8-984c-45ca-bfee-e90bcad55a73/content

This bounded search does not certify novelty or exhaust the literature.
The infimal-convolution identity itself is elementary and classical.
Parallel-sum background is explicitly credited to W. N. Anderson Jr.
and R. J. Duffin, Series and parallel addition of matrices, J. Math.
Anal. Appl. 26(3),576--594 (1969),
  https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-247X(69)90200-5
For positive P,Q, equation (1) is the quadratic-form variational
identity for P^{-1}:Q^{-1}=(P+Q)^{-1}. The proof in Section 1 is
self-contained and does not require extracting a theorem from that
reference.

A route considered before (1) was found uses Frenkel's exact integral
of trace-positive affine pencils. These need only n-sized PSD blocks.
Relevant primary papers:
  Peter E. Frenkel, Integral formula for quantum relative entropy
  implies data processing inequality, Quantum 7, 1102 (2023),
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12194
  Christoph Hirche and Marco Tomamichel, Quantum Renyi and
  f-divergences from integral representations, CMP 405,208 (2024),
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12343
  Gereon Kossmann and Rene Schwonnek, Optimising the relative entropy
  under semidefinite constraints, npj Quantum Information 12,23 (2026),
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17016
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-026-01184-4

The last work obtains sublinear discretization convergence with small
matrix dimensions. A fixed positive quadrature of the trace-positive
pencils reduces to a piecewise-linear hinge sum even on commuting
scalar inputs. It cannot inherit root-exponential rational convergence
just by substituting the new rational coefficients. For example, on
[1,2] the scalar f1 has curvature f1''>=1/2. If a continuous
piecewise-linear function with at most m+1 pieces approximates f1 in
uniform absolute error E, one segment has length at least 1/(m+1).
The second midpoint difference of f1 on that segment is at least
h^2/8 while an affine function's is zero. The three evaluation errors
bound that difference by 4E, so E>=1/[32(m+1)^2]. A relative f2
bound on this interval implies absolute error <=epsilon/2, giving
epsilon>=1/[16(m+1)^2]. A sharper constant is not needed: fixed hinge
quadrature is polynomial, not root-exponential, in this model.

Moreover their general integral-based f-divergence is distinct from
Petz for rational generators. Its chi^2 is the BKM metric, not
Tr[rho^2 sigma^{-1}]-1. The exact Sylvester infimal convolution above
avoids both issues by retaining the original Petz rational kernels.
