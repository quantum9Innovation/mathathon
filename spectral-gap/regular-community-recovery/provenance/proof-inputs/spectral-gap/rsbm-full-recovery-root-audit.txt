2. EXACT GENERAL TWO-STAR COUNT
------------------------------
Put d=a+b, lambda=a-b. In each block take aN distinguished internal
stubs and a uniform perfect pairing; independently biject the bN plus
cross stubs with the bN minus cross stubs. This model exists whenever aN
is even, exactly the relevant parity requirement. A loop contributes two
to the diagonal adjacency entry and to the local neighbor sum.

For a fixed finite label alphabet F_p, a plus profile consists of its
root x and the labels at its ordered a internal and b cross partners,
subject to their sum being lambda*x in F_p. One internal label is uniquely
determined by the root and the other d-1 labels, so there are p^d profiles.
The analogous statement holds in the minus block. Let the two profile
count arrays be n^+,n^-, with total N each.

For a fixed assignment of profiles to labeled vertices, classify each
internal stub by (its root label u, its requested partner label v).
Let the counts be m_uv. Pairing is possible precisely when m is symmetric
and its diagonal entries are even. Its number of compatible pairings is

  W(m) = product_{u<v} m_uv! * product_u (m_uu-1)!!.

Off-diagonal classes are paired by a bijection to their reverse classes;
each diagonal class is paired internally. This includes loops and parallel
edges without an orientation or multiplicity correction. Cross-stub
classes h^+_uv and h^-_vu must agree, and then the compatible cross
bijections number product_{u,v} h_uv!.

Every configuration and kernel vector gives exactly one ordered profile
at each vertex, and conversely every compatible configuration counted
under a profile assignment satisfies all its equations. Multiplying by
the two multinomial vertex-assignment counts proves EXACTLY

 E |ker_Fp(A-lambda I)|
 = sum_valid [N!/product n_s^+!] [N!/product n_t^-!]
     * W(m^+)W(m^-)/((aN-1)!!)^2
     * product h_uv!/(bN)!.

This is the general source's equation (1). In particular, no hidden
factor of N!, a!, b!, graph-factorization count, or edge orientation is
missing. Internal and cross pairing consistency impose all needed
marginal identities; the local equations do not impose any further
global restriction beyond the compatible pairings being counted.

3. ENTROPY AND UNIFORM POLYNOMIAL REMAINDERS
------------------------------------------
Write S_+,S_- for the two profile laws, F_+,F_- for the internal tables
normalized by aN, and G for the cross table normalized by bN. The entropy
exponent per N is exactly

 Phi = H(S_+)+H(S_-) - (a/2)(H(F_+)+H(F_-)) - b H(G).

To check it without a Stirling approximation, use zero-safe factorial
bounds r log r-r <= log(r!) <= r log r-r+1+log(r+1). With
q=p(p+1)/2, symmetric tables of total T=aN satisfy

 W(m)/(T-1)!! <= e^(q+1)(T+1)^(q+1) exp[-(T/2)H(m/T)].

Indeed the off-diagonal/diagonal leading terms combine to
(1/2) sum_uv m_uv log m_uv -T/2. The q individual upper errors
and one denominator lower error give the displayed bound, also when
some classes are empty. The cross ratio is bounded by

 product h_uv!/(bN)! <= e^(p^2)(bN+1)^(p^2) exp[-bN H(G)].

The multinomial upper bound exp[NH(S)] has no polynomial loss. There
are at most (N+1)^(2p^d) pairs of profile count arrays. Thus all errors
are fixed powers of N, uniformly even at the boundary of type space.
There is no saddle-point or nondegenerate-Hessian assumption here.

Independently average the two star laws over permutations of their a
internal slots and b cross slots. All pairing tables and root laws are
unchanged, constraints are preserved, and star entropies increase. It
suffices to prove the upper bound for these symmetrized laws. Put

 P = law(X) = law(A_i) = law(D_j),
 Q = law(Y) = law(C_i) = law(B_j).

Internal table symmetry gives the first equalities within a block;
the two cross tables give the equalities across blocks. An internal
neighbor is determined by the root and the retained neighbors, so

 H(S_+) <= H(X)+(a-1)H(A|X)+bH(B|X),
 H(S_-) <= H(Y)+(a-1)H(C|Y)+bH(D|Y).

Writing I_+,I_- for the two internal mutual informations and I_c for
the cross mutual information, the retained terms sum to

 d(H(P)+H(Q))-(a-1)(I_++I_-)-2bI_c.

The table entropies being subtracted are
a(H(P)+H(Q))-(a/2)(I_++I_-) and
b(H(P)+H(Q))-bI_c. Consequently

 Phi <= -(a/2-1)(I_++I_-)-bI_c <= 0,  for all a>=2.

This proves the explicit source bound

 E |ker_Fp(A-lambda I)|
 <= e^[2(q+1)+p^2] (aN+1)^[2(q+1)] (bN+1)^(p^2)
       * (N+1)^(2p^d).

At p=2 its power is C_d=2^(d+1)+12. No field characteristic or block
parity is used in this entropy inequality. It bounds the whole kernel,
including all nonplanted modular vectors; no real-to-modular converse
is being assumed.

4. CONDITIONING ON EXACTLY THE REQUESTED SIMPLE LAW
-------------------------------------------------
A simple labeled internal a-regular graph has (a!)^N distinguished-stub
realizations. A simple labeled b-biregular cross graph has (b!)^(2N)
cross-bijection realizations. Therefore independent conditioning of the
three independent factors gives precisely the three independent uniform
simple graph laws in the question.

For fixed degrees their joint simplicity probability is bounded away
from zero along every admissible size sequence. I checked the primary
source Janson, "The probability that a random multigraph is simple, II,"
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.6344 : actual Theorem 1.1 for ordinary
configurations, and the bounded-degree specialization of Theorem 5.1
(equivalently Theorem 5.3) for bipartite configurations. Their hypotheses
hold here. Thus nonnegative moments increase by at most a fixed factor
on conditioning, and high-probability configuration statements transfer.
No contiguity result between different regular-graph constructions is
being used.

In particular E_simple 2^k_2=O_{a,b}(N^C_d), where k_2 is the binary
nullity. Integer-minor reduction gives rational nullity <= k_2. This
does not imply rational nullity one, and no such implication is needed.

5. GENERAL SOURCE'S ZERO-PHASE ARGUMENT, a>=3
--------------------------------------------
Choose any fixed odd prime p not dividing b. If a valid type has Phi=0,
equality must hold both in symmetrization and in every entropy step.
The positive coefficient a/2-1 forces I_+=I_-=I_c=0, while equality
in conditional subadditivity forces the retained neighbors to be
conditionally independent given their root. As each single-neighbor
law is root-independent, the retained joint law is the corresponding
product law and independent of the root.

Hence with independent X,U_1,...,U_(a-1) having law P and V_1,...,V_b
having law Q, the variable lambda X-sum U_i-sum V_j has law P. Fourier
transformation on F_p gives

 P_hat(t)=P_hat(lambda t) P_hat(-t)^(a-1) Q_hat(-t)^b.

For r=|P_hat(t)| this implies r<=r^(a-1), so r is 0 or 1. A nontrivial
character of the additive group F_p is injective; if P is not a point
mass then every such modulus is strictly below 1, and hence all are 0.
Thus P is either a point mass or uniform. The same applies to Q.

If P is a point mass, the omitted-internal-label constraint makes a
sum of b independent Q variables deterministic. Holding the others
fixed proves Q is a point mass as well, not merely that bQ is constant.
Writing their values c,e, the constraints give b(c+e)=0, so e=-c.
If P is uniform and Q were a point mass the reversed argument would
contradict P uniform. Thus both are uniform. Independence identifies
the whole zero-phase star laws, exactly as stated in the source.

For Boolean alternatives the roots are supported on the two distinct
field values +1,-1. They cannot be uniform on F_p. A fixed macroscopic
flip fraction is also bounded away from the constant planted phases.
The feasible star-law space is compact and Phi is continuous, including
at zero entries. It therefore has a strictly negative maximum on this
closed Boolean root-law set. Polynomially many types and the uniform
factorial bounds yield exponentially few macroscopic alternatives.
Real Boolean equations imply the modular equations, so this modular
first moment is a valid upper bound. No growing-prime limit is involved.

6. UNIFORM SMALL-SUPPORT EXPOSURE: FULL RECONSTRUCTION
----------------------------------------------------
For a real eigenvector v with eigenvalue lambda>0, v is orthogonal to
1 because A*1=d*1 and lambda!=d. It has a positive maximum and a negative
minimum. The positive maximum requires at least lambda positive
neighbors (each is at most that maximum); the analogous fact holds
for the negative minimum. In a simple graph these show

 |supp(v)| >= 2(lambda+1).

Let S be its support, |S|=s, and T its exterior neighbor set. Every
vertex of T must have at least two incident edges to S, because its
eigenvector equation is a vanishing sum of nonzero support coordinates.
If e edges lie inside S and b_S leave S, then ds=2e+b_S and
|T|<=b_S/2. This is a necessary condition even when cancellations among
more than two neighbors are present; no sign assumption on those
cancellations is being made.

For a fixed S, expose partners of the ds stubs rooted in S, skipping
already paired stubs. Each relevant internal-pairing or cross-bijection
pool retains Theta(N) stubs for s<=epsilon N and sufficiently small
degree-dependent epsilon. Call an exposure a collision if its partner
is rooted in S or in an exterior vertex already seen. At each selected
step, conditional collision probability is <=C s/N; its candidate
targets occupy only O_d(s) stubs even with the three typed pools.

There are e+b_S actual exposures, exactly |T| of which first touch an
exterior vertex. Thus collisions number

 e+b_S-|T| >= e+b_S/2 = ds/2.

This accounts correctly for an internal edge, which consumes two
S-stubs but is exposed only once. Pad to ds steps by deterministic
noncollisions. The conditional probability bound and a union bound
over selected collision indices give

 Pr[necessary exterior condition for this S]
 <= 2^(ds)(Cs/N)^(ds/2).

Union bound over S gives [K(s/N)^h]^s with h=(d-2)/2>0. For
f_s=[K(s/N)^h]^s one has
f_(s+1)/f_s <= K e^h ((s+1)/N)^h. Choose epsilon so this is <1/2
through the desired range. Summing from s=2(lambda+1) gives

 O_{a,b}(N^[-(d-2)(lambda+1)]).

Conditioning on simplicity costs only a constant. The minimum-support
claim is invoked only for the simple graph event; the configuration
union bound need not have that minimum-support property itself.
The argument is valid for all admissible N and all three graph factors,
including b=1, and gives the exact rate quoted in the general source.

For two balanced sign eigenvectors sigma,tau, their flip counts in the
two planted blocks agree, because both have coordinate sum zero.
After replacing tau by -tau their common flip count k is <=N/2. A
small k gives an eigenvector tau-sigma of support 2k; the exposure
bound excludes it. A macroscopic k is excluded by Section 5. This
proves the general source's Boolean uniqueness claim for a>=3.

7. SEPARATE CUBIC SOURCES: MOMENT AND BOOLEAN RIGIDITY
----------------------------------------------------
Both complete cubic source files and both actual Boolean-rigidity
derivations were read. The following details reconstruct their proofs.

Gauge the random cross matching to the identity. Relabeling invariance
and independence leave two independent internal configuration matrices.
After the sign conjugation the kernel equations are C_+x=x+z and
C_-z=x+z. At coordinate i the ordered profile is determined by
(a,d,b,e), with other internal neighbors c=a+d-b and f=a+d-e.
Thus the exact p^4-profile count in the cubic source has two W(m)
factors and denominator ((2N-1)!!)^2; its joint-profile multinomial
is appropriate because the matching has identified the coordinates.

The commuting b<->c and e<->f involutions leave both pairing tables
unchanged. Averaging under them increases entropy. Under the averaged
law the tables are the laws of (a,b) and (d,e), so
H(a,d,b,e)-H(a,b)-H(d,e)<=0. This is valid also in characteristic two.
The explicit factorial factors give E 2^k=O(N^24), as stated. The
general two-star proof would also work at (2,1), with a looser N^28
moment; the improved N^24 exponent is consistent with gauging away
the cross-matching count and is not required for the theorem.

For Boolean rigidity, the flip condition at each vertex is exactly
"number of internal mark-boundary edges equals the matching mark-
boundary indicator." Let k flips occur on each side and t be the
even internal boundary size. Independently counting the selected
boundary stubs and the compatible matching gives

 E_(k,t)=binom(N,k) binom(k,t) binom(N-k,t)
   * [4^t t! (2k-t-1)!! (2(N-k)-t-1)!!/(2N-1)!!]^2.

This agrees algebraically with the alternative Pt^2 Qt formula in
low-degree-identifiability.md. For k<=epsilon N the displayed
double-factorial and factorial estimates give
E_(k,t)<=(Ck/N)^k uniformly in t, and summing t and k>=2 gives
O(N^-2). k=1 is impossible in the simple model, so it is correctly
omitted before conditioning; its loop-based configuration realizations
must not be included in this bound.

For alpha=k/N,beta=t/N the leading entropy is

 F=-s(alpha-beta)-s(1-alpha-beta)+s(2alpha-beta)
     +s(2-2alpha-beta)+(4beta-2)log2,
 s(x)=x log x, s(0)=0.

Differentiation gives the claimed strictly decreasing beta derivative
and unique optimizer
beta_*=(7-sqrt(49-180alpha(1-alpha)))/15. With
u=alpha-beta_*,v=1-alpha-beta_*,x=2u+beta_*,y=2v+beta_*, its stationarity
is xy=16uv. The envelope derivative has the sign of

 x^2 v-y^2 u=(u-v)(4uv-beta_*^2),
 4uv-beta_*^2=2beta_*(1/3-beta_*).

It is strictly negative for 0<alpha<1/2. The optimal value tends to
zero as alpha decreases to zero; hence it is strictly negative on
every compact macroscopic range. Uniform zero-safe Stirling errors
give the exponential bound claimed. Simplicity has limiting probability
exp(-3/2) for the two internal blocks: its displayed coefficient-ratio
derivation is correct. Therefore Boolean uniqueness is 1-O(N^-2),
along odd as well as even N. I do not infer cubic real corank one.

The finite localized counterexample and finite extra-eigenspace example
in the rigidity note are not needed for recovery. No inference from
their uniqueness of signs to real corank one has been used here.

 KL(P_+ || R_+) = J_+ + b I_c <= L.

E_P (W-m(X))^2=E v(X). Under R_+, its conditional variance is

 E_R (W-m(X))^2-E_P (W-m(X))^2
 >=(a-2)E v(X)+b Var(Y) >=b Var(Y).

f=(W-m(X))^2 takes values in [0,4a^2] on BOTH distributions. Pinsker's

 b Var(Y) <= 4a^2 TV(P_+,R_+)
             <=4a^2 sqrt(KL(P_+||R_+)/2)
             <=4a^2 sqrt(L/2).

For common flip fraction r, Var(Y)=4r(1-r), hence

 Phi <= -(2b^2/a^4) r^2(1-r)^2.

Every constant and inequality direction checks. This proof retains the

9. EXACT RECOVERY AND BIT COMPLEXITY
-----------------------------------
Given a sign assignment tau_i=(-1)^z_i, the parity of its number of
opposite-sign neighbors is ((A+dI)z)_i over F_2. Thus every equitable
(a,b) assignment satisfies

 (A+dI)z=(b mod 2)1.

Since d-lambda=2b, the homogeneous matrix is A-lambda I over F_2.
The planted assignment ensures the affine system is consistent; its
solution set has precisely 2^k_2 points. The moment bound therefore
controls the number actually enumerated, even though the homogeneous
binary equation itself cannot distinguish the two real signs.

Solve by exact binary elimination. If 2^k_2>n^(C_d+3), abstain;
otherwise enumerate the affine space and verify exactly b opposite
neighbors per vertex over the integers. Markov bounds abstention by
O(N^-3). Each verification and Gray-code update costs polynomial time
(indeed O(n) at fixed d), and the explicit cap gives deterministic
polynomial worst-case bit complexity on every input.

The parity system is only a necessary condition. The exact integer
verification is essential and is present in both reviewed algorithms.
Every passing sign vector obeys A tau=lambda tau and is automatically
balanced: d<1,tau>=<1,A tau>=lambda<1,tau>, with d-lambda=2b>0.
On the Boolean-rigidity event, the only passing vectors are +/-sigma;
so returning the first passing vector recovers the planted unordered
partition. No approximate eigenvector rounding or analytic gap is used.

Without the cap, elimination plus enumeration lists all equitable
partitions with expected polynomial time under the model, by the same
moment. The cutoff supplies worst-case polynomial time with a vanishing
abstention probability. This is sufficient for the theorem; it does
not promise success on every graph in the model's support.

The rational spectral alternative is also valid. Rational nullity is
at most modular nullity by integer minors. A basis and invertible set
of pivot rows allow enumeration of every possible sign choice on those
rows, hence every Boolean eigenvector. Hadamard bounds all integer
minors of the fixed-degree, fixed-diagonal matrix by exp(O_{a,b}(n));
fraction-free elimination and reconstruction have polynomial bit
complexity. This alternative likewise needs only logarithmic real
nullity, not real nullity one.

11a. Exact grouping and the uniform-fiber entropy bound

For fixed directed tables (m^+,m^-,h), Omega is a finite set of ALL
ordered arrays of N plus stars and N minus stars obeying their local
equations and these tables. It includes their ROOT LABELS, not merely
the partner-slot arrays. Each configuration and kernel vector gives
one such array. For every array its number of compatible pairings is
exactly W(m^+)W(m^-) product h_uv!, independent of its detailed local
types. Thus the source's grouped exact identity is simply the identity
of Section 2, grouped before summing local-type multinomials.

Choose the full array uniformly from a nonempty Omega. Independently
permuting the N vertex positions in either block is a bijection of
Omega: it permutes entire stars including their roots and requested
neighbor labels, preserving all table sums and local equations. At
each individual vertex, arbitrary permutations of its a internal or b
cross slots are also bijections. They preserve local equations because
only neighbor sums enter, and table entries because the neighbor
multisets are unchanged. These facts do not presume a specific graph
pairing is fixed inside the fiber.

Uniform measure is invariant under all those bijections. Thus every
plus vertex has one common slot-symmetric law T_+, and every minus
vertex one common law T_-. Each array's deterministic total internal
table is m^+. Averaging its aN contributions and using the common
vertex/slot laws proves EXACTLY law(X,A_1)=m^+/(aN), not an asymptotic
or an arbitrary mixture. The other internal law is m^-/(aN), and
law(X,B_1)=law(D_1,Y)=h/(bN). Internal table symmetry and cross-table
consistency give all marginal identities used in Section 3.

The entropy of the full uniform array is log|Omega|. Subadditivity over
its 2N vertex profiles therefore gives

 log|Omega| <= N[H(T_+)+H(T_-)]
 <= N[(a/2)(H(F_+)+H(F_-))+bH(G)].

No independence between vertices, blocks, roots, or slots was asserted.
The second inequality is exactly the already proved star entropy
bound. This proves the desired exponential cardinality bound with
NO extra prefactor. In particular every multinomial choice of root
labelings and local types is already inside log|Omega|. Multiplying
by an additional binomial or local-type count would only weaken this
valid estimate and is not needed to account for an omitted choice.

11b. All polynomial factors and the universal exponent

 E |ker_Fp(A-lambda I)|
