THE SHARP JOINT BOUNDARY LAW FOR A FRACTIONAL RANK-ONE UPDATE
===========================================================

Research derivation, 2026-11-15 UTC. Independent proof review: PASS.
See fractional-boundary-crossover-independent-audit.txt and the proof
verification record for reviewed hashes, dependencies and scope.
This note treats positive rank-one perturbations whose normalized endpoint
bound approaches its critical value at the same time as the tolerance
decreases. It does not extrapolate a fixed-R asymptotic uniformly in R.
The proof combines a projected resolvent envelope with the Gaussian
localization and weighted-Cauchy tools already proved in the companion.
No external priority claim or arithmetic-runtime theorem is made.

1. PRECISE STATEMENT
===================
Fix 0<alpha<1 and put beta=1-alpha. For L>0 and a fixed tau>=0 set

  epsilon_L=exp(-L), h_L=exp(-tau L).

Consider all finite real symmetric pairs

  A>=0, B=A+ww^T<=(1+h_L)I, ||w||=1.                 (1)

The dimension may depend on L. Let D=B^alpha-A^alpha and let

  N_tau(L)=sup_(pairs(1)) #{j: lambda_j(D)>exp(-L)}.   (2)

The eigenvalues are nonnegative by operator monotonicity, so these are
also the strict singular-value counts. Then

  lim_(L->infinity) N_tau(L)/L^2 = C_alpha(tau),       (3)

where

  C_alpha(tau)=(1/alpha-tau^2)/(4pi^2),     0<=tau<=1,
             =(1-alpha*tau)^2/(4pi^2 alpha beta),
                                           1<=tau<=1/alpha,
             =0,                           tau>=1/alpha. (4)

The formulas agree at both transition points. At tau=0 this recovers
the fixed bounded-endpoint constant 1/(4pi^2 alpha). At the upper
transition there is the stronger exact assertion

  N_tau(L)=1 for every L>0 if tau>=1/alpha.            (5)

The same joint limit holds if the supremum is restricted to increasing
ONE existing edge conductance on a connected weighted path with arbitrary
positive vertex masses held fixed across the update, in symmetric
mass-normalized coordinates. Both the update norm and endpoint bound
are as in (1). This graph class is explained in Section 7.

For general h>=0, independently of any limiting regime, there is an
explicit positive rank-one matrix F satisfying

  0<=D-F,  ||D-F||_*<=h^alpha                         (6)

whenever A>=0, B=A+ww^T<=(1+h)I and ||w||=1. Here ||.||_* is nuclear
norm. In particular sum_(j>=2)lambda_j(D)<=h^alpha. Section 2 proves
the bound with constant one. No claim that this constant is optimal is
needed for (3).

2. A NUCLEAR TAIL BOUND AND THE EXACT ONE-RANK REGION
==================================================
Let a=w^TAw. From w^TBw=1+a<=1+h we have a<=h. For t>0 write

  R_t=(A+tI)^-1, m(t)=w^TR_t w, Q=I-ww^T.

The rank-one resolvent difference is

  D_t=(A+tI)^-1-(B+tI)^-1
     =R_t ww^T R_t/(1+m(t)).                          (7)

Set c_alpha=sin(pi alpha)/pi. Functional calculus gives the positive
norm-convergent integral D=c_alpha int_0^infinity t^alpha D_t dt.
The trace of the projected rank-one integrand is exactly

  tr(Q D_t Q)=[-m'(t)-m(t)^2]/[1+m(t)].               (8)

Since R_t^2<=R_t/t, its numerator is at most m(t)/t-m(t)^2. Thus

  tr(QD_tQ)
   <=[m(t)/(1+m(t))][1/t-m(t)]<=1/t-m(t).             (9)

The last bracket is nonnegative because m(t)<=1/t. Integrating (9),
using the scalar fractional-power integral and the spectral measure of
A at w, gives

  tr(Q D Q)<=w^T A^alpha w<=(w^TAw)^alpha<=h^alpha.    (10)

Scalar Jensen proves the middle inequality. In a finite dimension no
trace-limit exchange issue arises; Tonelli also applies to the positive
integrand.

The scalar d_0=w^TDw is strictly positive, as is immediate from (7).
Define

  F=(D w)(D w)^T/d_0.                                (11)

In the decomposition span(w) direct-sum w-perp, this removes the first
row and column of D by its Schur complement. Positivity of D implies
D-F>=0, and its trace is at most tr(QDQ). Therefore (6) follows.
The best positive rank-one truncation has no larger trace error, proving
the stated eigenvalue-tail bound. If h^alpha<=epsilon, no second
eigenvalue can be strictly above epsilon. Conversely A=0,B=ww^T has
exactly one eigenvalue one in its correction. Applying this with
h=h_L proves (5), including equality tau=1/alpha.

3. A PROJECTED COMPLEX RESOLVENT ENVELOPE
=======================================
For fixed integer p>=1, let K_p be the span of

  w, Aw, ..., A^(p-1)w,

and let Q_p be its orthogonal-complement projection. Its codimension is
at most p. Fix a strip angle 0<d<pi. For z=t exp(i theta), t>0 and
|theta|<=d, define R_z=(A+zI)^-1 and m(z)=w^TR_z w. The exact node
is D_z=R_z ww^T R_z/(1+m(z)). Transposes, rather than adjoints, are
intentional in this analytic formula.

For lambda>=0,

  |lambda+z|>=cos(d/2)(lambda+t).

After rotation by theta/2, every scalar (lambda+z)^-1 has real part
at least cos(d/2)/(lambda+t). The same applies to the added scalar 1.
Consequently

  |1+m(z)|>=cos(d/2)[1+m(t)],
  m(t)>=1/(a+t),                                    (12)

where the second inequality is scalar Jensen for the unit spectral
measure of w. All constants below depend only on p,d,alpha, never h,
dimension, A or w.

First, the two endpoint resolvents give ||D_z||<=C_d/t. Also
Q R_z w=-z^-1 Q A R_z w, so

  ||Q R_z w||^2
    <=C_d t^-2 int lambda^2/(lambda+t)^2 dmu(lambda)
    <=C_d a/t^3.                                    (13)

Here lambda/(lambda+t)^2<=1/(4t). Combining (12),(13) shows that for
t>=a, ||Q D_z Q||<=C_d a/t^2. For t<a the first bound C_d/t is already
at most C_d a/t^2. Hence globally

  ||Q_p D_z Q_p||<=C_d min(1/t,h/t^2).               (14)

For the large-t improvement, Q_p kills the first p resolvent moments:

  Q_p R_z w=(-1)^p z^-p Q_p R_z A^p w.

Because ||A||<=1+h<=2 and a<=h,
||A^p w||^2<=2^(2p-1)h. The norm of R_z is at most C_d/t, while
the denominator in (12) is at least a fixed positive constant. Thus

  ||Q_p D_z Q_p||<=C_(p,d) h/t^(2p+2).              (15)

Define the logarithmic analytic integrand

  F_p(y)=c_alpha exp((alpha+1)y) Q_p D_(exp(y)) Q_p.

Its integral on the real line is Q_p D Q_p, and every real value is PSD
of rank at most one. Equations (14),(15) prove, on |Im y|<=d,

  ||F_p(x+i theta)||<=C_(p,d,alpha) exp(-g_L(x)),

  g_L(x)=max{-alpha*x, tau*L+beta*x,
                         tau*L+(2p+beta)*x}.         (16)

The piecewise-linear function is convex, nonnegative, and has uniformly
bounded slopes for fixed p. Its minimum alpha*tau*L is at x=-tau*L.
The growing high-energy slope in (16) is obtained by removing only a
fixed number of Krylov directions, not by assigning p ranks to each
quadrature node.

4. GAUSSIAN LOCALIZATION WITH A MOVING THREE-LINE ENVELOPE
========================================================
We need the following precise extension of lem:gaussian in
sharp-fractional-holder.tex. Its proof is included because the original
statement has only two fixed slopes.

Lemma. Fix p,d,alpha,tau and v>0. Suppose F is analytic on the closed
d-strip, satisfies (16) with the same fixed prefactor C, and has rank
at most one at real arguments. There is a positive-weight quadrature
approximating int F to error at most exp(-vL), with at most

  [1/(2pi d)] int_R (vL-g_L(x))_+ dx+O(L^(7/4))       (17)

nodes. The bound is uniform over all such F and all matrix dimensions.

Proof. Put T=vL, ell=T^(3/4), and use entire Gaussian windows
phi_j(z)=pi^-1/2 int_(j ell)^((j+1)ell) exp(-(z-u)^2)du.
These sum to one and are positive on the real line. The slopes of g_L
are bounded by the fixed number kappa=max(alpha,2p+beta). Thus the two
strip boundary integrals of F*phi_j are bounded by
C' ell exp(-m_j), m_j=min_(I_j)g_L, with C' independent of L: apply
g_L(x)>=g_L(u)-kappa|x-u| and integrate the Gaussian.

Retain windows meeting {x:g_L(x)<=T+ell}. If that sublevel set is empty,
the same integral bound gives total error O(exp(-T-ell)), so return
zero. Otherwise it is one interval of length O(T), since the left tail
slope is alpha and every right tail slope is at least beta. There are
O(T/ell) retained windows. Omitting all other windows contributes
O(exp(-T-ell)); this is the Gaussian convolution of the complement
of the retained union, with the same Lipschitz estimate.

For each retained window take trapezoid spacing

  h_j=2pi*d/max(ell,T-m_j+3ell).

The ordinary strip trapezoid error is bounded by its two boundary L1
norms divided by exp(2pi*d/h_j)-1. Summing therefore gives
O(T exp(-T-3ell)). Retain only lattice nodes within distance 2sqrt(T)
of I_j. Their omitted Gaussian tails give O(T exp(-2T)), because
g_L>=0 bounds the real-line integrand by C. These errors are below
exp(-T) for all sufficiently large L.

The node count is at most the sum over retained windows of
  [(ell+4sqrt(T))/(2pi*d)]max(ell,T-m_j+3ell)+2.
The kappa-Lipschitz Riemann-sum error replaces the principal term by
int(T-g_L)_+ with error O(T ell). The 3ell spacing buffer has the
same order, window enlargement costs O(T^(5/2)/ell), and integer
rounding costs O(T/ell). All are O(T^(7/4)). This proves (17), with
positive weights h_j phi_j at real lattice nodes. The proof invokes
no dimension-dependent bound or hidden h-dependent prefactor.

Apply the lemma to (16). A q-node approximation has rank at most q.
The difference D-Q_p D Q_p has rank at most 2p, so rank perturbation
and the strict-threshold best-rank theorem give

  N_D(vL)<=2p+[1/(2pi*d)]int_R(vL-g_L(x))_+dx
                                  +O(L^(7/4)).        (18)

For fixed v,tau, elementary integration gives

  L^-2 int(vL-g_L)_+ =
    0,                             v<=alpha*tau,
    (v-alpha*tau)^2/(2alpha*beta),  alpha*tau<=v<=tau,
    v^2/(2alpha)-tau^2/2
      +(v-tau)^2/[2(2p+beta)],      v>=tau.            (19)

First let L tend to infinity, then d increase to pi, then p tend to
infinity. All these are upper bounds for the same matrix count.
For every sequence of pairs obeying (1),

  limsup N_D(vL)/L^2<=C(v,tau),                       (20)

  C(v,tau)=0,                              v<=alpha*tau,
          =(v-alpha*tau)^2/(4pi^2 alpha beta),
                                            alpha*tau<=v<=tau,
          =(v^2/alpha-tau^2)/(4pi^2),        v>=tau.

This is continuous and nondecreasing in v. In particular (20) proves
the required upper at v=1. Equation (10) of the bounded-spectrum note
also gives a uniform simpler upper N_D(vL)<=C_alpha[(vL)^2+vL+1],
since the endpoints always lie in [0,2]. This global bound will justify
dominated integral arguments below.

5. A SMALL-MEAN SPECTRAL DENSITY GIVES THE MATCHING LOWER
======================================================
Fix L>=1. Put eta=1/2, delta_L=1+1/L, c=1/4, and abbreviate delta=delta_L.
Define

  c_delta=(eta^-1+delta^-1)^-1,
  M_delta=c_delta[(eta+1)^-1+(delta-1)^-1],
  kappa_delta=min(c/4,1/[128(1+M_delta)]),
  h_0=kappa_delta*h_L.                               (21)

On the entire log line the probability density

  psi(x)=c_delta*(exp(x)/h_0)^eta,      x<=log h_0,
        =c_delta*(exp(x)/h_0)^(-delta),x>=log h_0

has mean int exp(x)psi(x)dx=M_delta*h_0<=h_L/128.
Restrict it to x<=log c and renormalize to a probability density rho_L.
The removed mass is (c_delta/delta)(h_0/c)^delta<=1/4, so its normalizer
Z_L lies in [3/4,1]. Therefore

  int exp(x)rho_L(x)dx<=h_L/96.                       (22)

All constants in the comparisons below can be chosen uniformly for
delta in [1,2]. Also kappa_delta is between fixed positive multiples
of 1/(L+1). In particular

  log h_0=-tau L+O(log(L+1)).                         (23)

Define m_L(t)=int rho_L(x)/(exp(x)+t)dx and the Cauchy weight

  q_L(x)=c_alpha rho_L(x)exp(-beta*x)/(1+m_L(exp(x))). (24)

For 0<t<=h_0, direct integration of the lower density gives
m_L(t) between positive constant multiples of h_0^(-eta)t^(eta-1).
For the upper estimate, the positive-density part contributes at most
C/h_0, which is no larger than that quantity. For h_0<=t<=c, the mass
below h_0 is bounded below by a fixed constant, so m_L(t) is between
positive constant multiples of 1/t. These facts yield the uniform bounds

  q_L(log t) is comparable to t^alpha,                0<t<=h_0,
  q_L(log t) is comparable to h_0^delta*t^(alpha-delta),
                                                      h_0<=t<=c. (25)

The constants depend on alpha but not L, h_L or matrix dimension.
For example c_delta stays in [1/3,2/5], and Z_L in [3/4,1], throughout
the stated delta range. The same comparisons apply to 1+m_L, since
t<=c and its lower bounds are already bounded below by constants.

The one-tail Cauchy argument in the bounded-spectrum note and the
companion convolution lemma give finite-matrix lower examples. The
endpoint constraint deserves an explicit check in this varying regime.
Restrict rho_L to I=[log h_0-T,log c], with mass t_I>=1/2, take A_I as
multiplication by exp(x), and w_I=sqrt(rho_L) on I. Step multiplication
models with eigenvalues no larger than the original exp(x) preserve
the mean upper (22). Normalize both finite endpoints by t_I. The update
then has norm one, while

  ||A||<=c/t_I<=1/2,
  w^TAw<=h_L/(96 t_I^2)<=h_L/24.                     (26)

For R=1+h_L, the rank-one order test gives B=A+ww^T<=R I whenever
w^T(RI-A)^-1w<=1. In view of (26),

  w^T(RI-A)^-1w
    <=1/R+(w^TAw)/[R(R-1/2)]<1.                     (27)

Indeed w^TAw<=h_L/24<h_L(R-1/2). Thus all these finite normalized
models obey (1), with strict upper slack, even when h_L is exponentially
small. This check uses no fixed-R uniformity assumption.

For 0<s<=1/2 the positive Woodbury/Cauchy domination gives

  sup_(pairs(1)) tr D^s
   >=[J_(1/2,s)/(2pi)]int_(-infinity)^(log c)q_L(x)^s dx. (28)

Here the integral is first restricted to the fixed bounded I, finite
matrix transfer is applied there, and only then T tends to infinity.
Normalization multiplies the correction by t_I^(-alpha)>=1. This is
the same justified finite transfer as in the already audited companion.
Also s J_(1/2,s) tends to 1/pi as s decreases to zero.

Set s=1/L, with L>=2 so s<=1/2. By (25), comparison constants disappear after taking the
1/L power. Elementary integration and (23) give

  (1/L)int q_L(x)^(1/L)dx
   -> I_alpha(tau)
    := exp(-alpha*tau)/alpha
           +[exp(-alpha*tau)-exp(-tau)]/beta.          (29)

More explicitly the two model integrals divided by L equal

  h_0^(alpha/L)/alpha
   +[h_0^(alpha/L)-h_0^(delta/L)c^((alpha-delta)/L)]/(delta-alpha).

Since delta=1+1/L and log h_0/L tends to -tau, this proves (29) without
an unproved uniform tail asymptotic. Equations (28),(29) imply that for
each L there is a genuine finite-dimensional witness D_L in (1) with

  liminf_(L->infinity) L^-2 tr D_L^(1/L)
       >=I_alpha(tau)/(2pi^2).                        (30)

One may take a finite model whose trace is within one of the lower
right side in (28). Finite dimension may grow arbitrarily quickly;
this is an extremal compression theorem, not a bit-complexity claim.

6. A TWO-PARAMETER COUNT DEFICIT ARGUMENT
======================================
For the witnesses D_L in (30), put n_L(v)=L^-2 N_(D_L)(vL). Their
norms are at most one by the PSD operator-Holder bound already checked
in the bounded-spectrum note. Layer cake gives exactly

  L^-2 tr D_L^(1/L)=int_0^infinity exp(-v)n_L(v)dv.    (31)

The pointwise limsup bound (20) applies to the same witnesses for every
fixed v>0. The global [0,2] rational upper stated after (20) is an
integrable polynomial dominator for exp(-v)n_L(v), uniformly in L>=1.
Moreover direct integration of (20), or integration of its phase-area
form, gives

  int_0^infinity exp(-v)C(v,tau)dv
       =I_alpha(tau)/(2pi^2).                         (32)

For verification, write phi(u)=alpha*u for u>=tau and
phi(u)=tau-beta*u for 0<=u<=tau. Then
C(v,tau)=(2pi^2)^-1 int_0^infinity(v-phi(u))_+du.
Tonelli reduces (32) to int exp(-phi(u))du, which is (29).

The lower (30), reverse Fatou with the global dominator, and (31),(32)
show convergence of the integrals in (31) to (32). If C(1,tau)>0 but
n_L(1) had a fixed positive deficit along a subsequence, monotonicity of
n_L and continuity of C would retain a positive deficit on an interval
immediately below v=1. On its complement, the pointwise limsup upper
and the same dominator permit no positive limiting integral excess.
That contradicts convergence of the full integrals. Hence

  liminf n_L(1)>=C(1,tau).

The universal upper (20) gives the reverse bound, establishing (3),(4).
When C(1,tau)=0 the upper alone suffices, and Section 2 additionally
proves the exact finite-L statement (5). Thus the proof never infers
counts from a small-s moment for a fixed operator or from a fixed-R
asymptotic outside its hypotheses.

7. GRAPH TRANSFER, DEPENDENCIES, AND LIMITS
========================================
For every fixed L, R=1+h_L is strictly above one. Section 6 of
bounded-spectrum-fractional-counts.txt proves equality at every strict
threshold between the positive rank-one matrix class with endpoint
bound R and one-edge updates on connected fixed-mass weighted paths.
Its proof creates strict norm slack before passing to finite ground mass.
Apply that equality separately at each L. It transports (3) to the
connected-path class without requiring any uniform bound on the masses,
dimension or finite-ground parameter. Those data may depend on L.
