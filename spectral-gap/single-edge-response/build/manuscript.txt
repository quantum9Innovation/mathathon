                One edge and a fractional graph Laplacian:
    relative spectra, degree constraints, and constructive compression

                                                   Abstract
          How many relative spectral directions can change when one graph edge is inserted or its
      conductance is increased? For 0P   < α < 1 we study C = (L† )α/2 (L′α − Lα )(L† )α/2 . The edge
      leverage τ fixes the exact budget j log(1+λj (C)) = α log(1+τ ). Every finite nonnegative spec-
      tral list exhausting this budget is a limit on weighted paths with unit vertex masses. The same
      universality at τ = 1 holds for one actual edge insertion between simple unweighted connected
      bipartite graphs, with resistance exactly one in every host. This gives the exact unrestricted
      worst-case rank above any strict tolerance. With growing dimension, the complete finite-target
      uniform closure permits a weak budget inequality; this differs from fixed-dimensional matrix
      closure and from exact finite attainment. Among simple unweighted hosts, for doubling one
      existing unit edge, maximum degree two has sharp worst-case rank log2 (1/ε)/(2π 2 ) to leading
      order, whereas every fixed maximum degree d ≥ 3 has sharp leading rank α log(2d/(d + 2))/ε,
      even on bipartite bridge examples. The upper bound subtracts the entire rooted local response
      using a free/wired resistance comparison. Local constraints prevent full spectral universality
      at fixed degree. Finally, an explicit factor can be constructed with two-sided relative error in
      expected nearly linear arithmetic work. All solves, feasible flows, small-Gram computation, and
      factor entries are charged; the hidden logarithms contain no graph spectral gap or conductance
      ratio. For this simple unweighted existing-edge doubling problem at fixed degree d ≥ 2, the
      construction attains the sharp uniform leading rank constant. The arithmetic model and the
      separately charged baseline inverse are specified. Classical matrix inequalities, rational approx-
      imation, integral-operator asymptotics, graph coarse graining, and electrical-flow solvers are
      credited throughout. Historical priority is not certified.

1.1   Classical inputs and organization
The necessary matrix inequalities are classical. Araki supplies log-majorization; Weyl–Horn and
Kostant supply prescribed triangular or Cholesky diagonals; successive Schur asymptotics are es-
tablished in matrix perturbation theory [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7]. Appendix A gives their complete
relative-range combination, including signed pairs and batch envelopes. The main text first gives a
direct rank-one proof and then realizes its limits by graphs. Series and parallel resistor laws, Schur
complements, Lanczos reduction, and Ritz min–max are classical; strongly coupled graph coarse
graining also has prior work [11].
    The degree-two proof uses Laptev’s weighted-Cauchy spectral asymptotic [13], a classical Fourier
transform [14], and a uniform finite-grid comparison with one fixed continuum operator. Stahl’s
rational approximation gives a separate nonsharp order bound [12]. The sharp higher-degree proof
uses standard expander existence, trace-class determinants, Kron reduction, and free/wired network
principles [15, 16, 25, 26, 28, 27]. Rational matrix-function updates are established [9, 10]. The
algorithm uses classical logarithmic quadrature and nearly linear graph solvers [17, 18, 19]. These
credits do not certify priority for the graph-relative specializations or their combination.

6.5   Preconditioner representation and application
Let M = Lα + XX T . Since M ⪰ Lα , the two-sided guarantee implies

                                     (1 − ε)M ⪯ L′α ⪯ (1 + ε)M.

Thus M is a relative spectral preconditioner. This statement concerns its representation and
quality. Applying M −1 by Woodbury still requires a base L−α action. If that action costs Tbase ,
the additional core setup is rTbase + O(nr2 + r3 ) and each application costs Tbase + O(nr + r2 ). No
base inverse action is free here.
    For fixed leverage the factor construction itself is nearly linear up to logarithms and has optimal-
order worst-case rank B0 /ε. The logarithmic raw-width bound is consistent with the sharp relative
lower examples: realizing many separated relative modes with a single unit-mass edge can require
extremely large graph dimension. There is no arbitrary-SPD analogue of the dipole maximum-
principle estimate. The proof uses the explicit graph and feasible flow access. It does not assert
the same guarantee for an arbitrary SPD input accessed only through noisy resolvent products.

7.3   What the rank laws determine
The exact count Mτ (ε) is an existence and obstruction law. It does not bound the size or con-
ductance ratios of a graph approximating a prescribed extremal spectrum. The degree-three con-
struction gives finite examples but can use very large reservoirs and widely separated sizes. The


                                                        38
centered-path coefficient 1/(2π 2 ) also equals the uniform worst-case leading coefficient over all fi-
nite unit paths and cycles. The matching upper uses a fixed continuum operator dominating every
finite low-frequency grid, rather than inferring uniformity from fixed-index spectral convergence.
    The constructive theorem gives explicit two-sided relative factors from graph primitives. Its
near-sharp rank variant can approach the structural leading rank constant, but it does not compute
the exact one-sided spectral truncation. A generalized Nyström alternative can also be analyzed
using ridge effective dimension and classical Gaussian estimates [20]; its fractional sampling and
matrix-action oracles must be fully charged. The flow-based construction avoids treating those
fractional vector operations as free.

7.4   Relation to matrix-function approximation
The rational rank identity used in an alternative degree-two order bound belongs to classical rational
matrix-function updating; Beckermann, Cortinovis, Kressner and Schweitzer discuss its connection
to Bernstein and Van Loan’s rank-one identities [9, Section 3.3]. Their Theorem 4.5 bounds an
ordinary operator-norm update error using uniform scalar rational approximation. Converting such
an error E to the present metric by

                                  (L† )α/2 E(L† )α/2 ≤ λ2 (L)−α ∥E∥

can reintroduce a graph spectral gap. The square-root Riccati framework of Shmueli, Drineas
and Avron supplies update decay and error analysis, with costs that explicitly charge the supplied
square-root and inverse square-root actions [10, Section 5.5]. Those are established update methods
with different error or access assumptions.
    Contour and sinc quadrature for matrix fractional powers precede the present construction [21,
22, 17]. Bonito, Lei and Pasciak also analyze fractional operator-domain norms [22, Theorem 3.2].
Benzi and Simunec study rational Krylov computation of fractional diffusion vectors on graphs [23].
Persson, Meyer and Musco transfer low-rank approximations to an operator-monotone function of
a single matrix [24]. Applying a low-rank method directly to the present C still requires a charged
representation or action for that matrix. The additional graph-specific steps here are the dipole
moment bounds, feasible-flow conversion to fractional relative accuracy, and the computable relative
Gram. These comparisons distinguish statements; they are not an exhaustive historical priority
assessment.
    Perturbation determinants and their trace formulas have extensive classical theory [26, 25].
Determinants, local graph limits, and resistance also meet in Lyons’s work on tree entropy [29].
That normalized one-operator entropy differs from the unnormalized relative fractional determinant
for a distinguished edge used here. Kron reduction and resistance preservation are established
circuit principles [28], and regular-tree capacities and free/wired resistance conventions are classical
network theory [27]. The sharp graph count combines these inputs with a multiport realization;
none of these underlying mechanisms is claimed as a new general theory.
    The fixed-degree outlier and the arithmetic theorem address separate constraints. The former
limits possible spectra. The latter bounds the cost of approximating a given graph response in
a stated arithmetic model. Sharp leading constants are proved at every fixed degree d ≥ 2. No
effective rate is supplied for the compactness-based higher-degree asymptotic. Finite-word stability
and historical priority remain unresolved here.

Corollary B.2 (Exact rank and simplicity for one graph edge). For a positive edge-conductance
edit of a connected graph, every nonzero eigenvalue of the relative fractional correction is positive
and simple. Its rank is
                                #{λ > 0 : λ ∈ spec(L), Pλ b ̸= 0},

Proof. Restrict to 1⊥ and apply the cyclic-subspace argument above. A weighted path Laplacian
is irreducible symmetric tridiagonal, so its eigenvalues are simple. If an eigenvector v with positive
eigenvalue is orthogonal to an endpoint incidence, then v1 = v2 . The endpoint eigenvector equation
gives λv1 = 0, hence v1 = v2 = 0. Recurrence along the path forces v = 0, a contradiction. Every
positive path eigenspace is therefore seen by that incidence.
