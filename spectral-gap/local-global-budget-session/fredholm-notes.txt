Gap-zero Fredholm identity and rooted generalized Ritz transfer
==============================================================
Research note by the Fredholm subagent, 2026-11-15.
This note proves the analytic obligations. It does not assert sharpness
of the resulting extremal graph-count coefficient.

1. A bounded positive operator with no spectral gap
------------------------------------------------
Let H be a separable Hilbert space, A a bounded nonnegative injective
self-adjoint operator, b in H, and

  tau = ||A^(-1/2)b||^2 < infinity.

Fix 0<alpha<1, put B=A+bb*, and c_alpha=sin(pi alpha)/pi. All negative
powers below initially have their spectral-calculus domains. For t>0 set

  m(t) = <b,(A+t)^(-1)b>,
  w(t) = A^(-alpha/2)(A+t)^(-1)b.

The vector w(t) exists. Indeed, if mu is b's spectral measure for A,
its squared norm is the integral of lambda^(-alpha)/(lambda+t)^2
against mu; lambda^(-alpha) is bounded by a fixed multiple of
lambda^(-1) on (0,||A||]. The zero-operator case is vacuous under
injectivity unless H={0}.

The elementary beta integral gives

  c_alpha integral_0^infinity t^alpha ||w(t)||^2 dt
    = alpha integral lambda^(-1) dmu(lambda) = alpha tau.       (1)

Tonelli applies to its nonnegative integrand. Consequently

  C = c_alpha integral_0^infinity
          [t^alpha/(1+m(t))] w(t)w(t)* dt                     (2)

exists as a Bochner integral in trace norm. It is positive trace class
and tr C <= alpha tau. Multiplication on both sides by the bounded
operator A^(alpha/2), followed by the fractional resolvent identity and
the rank-one inverse identity, yields

  B^alpha-A^alpha = A^(alpha/2) C A^(alpha/2).                  (3)

One can also obtain (3) from the regularized identity below, using
operator-norm continuity of positive fractional powers. Thus (2) is
precisely the bounded form extension of the formal relative correction
A^(-alpha/2)(B^alpha-A^alpha)A^(-alpha/2). Uniqueness follows because
ran A^(alpha/2) is dense. In particular there is no undeclared bounded
inverse assumption in this definition.

2. Exact determinant, including gap zero
---------------------------------------
The identity is

  log det_F(I+C) = alpha log(1+tau).                            (4)

Here det_F is the ordinary trace-class Fredholm determinant. To prove
it without manipulating unbounded inverse products, fix delta>0 and
write A_delta=A+delta I and B_delta=A_delta+bb*. Define C_delta from
the corresponding bounded relative product. Its resolvent formula has

  w_delta(t)=(A+delta)^(-alpha/2)(A+delta+t)^(-1)b,
  m_delta(t)=<b,(A+delta+t)^(-1)b>.

Spectral calculus gives w_delta(t)->w(t), m_delta(t)->m(t), and
||w_delta(t)||<=||w(t)|| for each t>0. Rank-one trace norm continuity
and the integrable majorant in (1) therefore give

  ||C_delta-C||_1 -> 0.                                      (5)

For clarity, ||xx*-yy*||_1 <= (||x||+||y||)||x-y||. The norm of
the difference of the two integrands, including their denominators,
is at most 2 t^alpha ||w(t)||^2, up to c_alpha. This suffices for
dominated convergence.

It remains to compute the determinant with delta fixed. Let

  X_s=A_delta+sbb*,
  R_s=A_delta^(-alpha/2) X_s^alpha A_delta^(-alpha/2),
  0<=s<=1.

The map s -> R_s-I is continuously differentiable in trace norm,
R_s>=I, and its derivative is obtained from

  dX_s^alpha/ds = c_alpha integral_0^infinity
       t^alpha (X_s+t)^(-1)bb*(X_s+t)^(-1) dt.                 (6)

The common positive lower bound delta and bounded b justify trace-norm
differentiation and the integral uniformly in s. Applying the usual
Fredholm logarithmic derivative rule and cyclicity of the trace gives

  d/ds log det_F R_s
    = tr[X_s^(-alpha) dX_s^alpha/ds]
    = c_alpha integral_0^infinity t^alpha
         <b,X_s^(-alpha)(X_s+t)^(-2)b> dt
    = alpha <b,X_s^(-1)b>
    = alpha tau_delta/(1+s tau_delta),                         (7)

where tau_delta=<b,A_delta^(-1)b>. All operators occurring in this
calculation are bounded; each traced product is trace class. The
second-to-last equality uses the same scalar beta integral as (1).
Integrating (7) from zero to one yields

  log det_F(I+C_delta) = alpha log(1+tau_delta).

Now tau_delta increases to tau by monotone convergence, and (5) permits
passing to the Fredholm determinant. The determinants are positive,
so their real logarithms converge. This proves (4).

Standard determinant facts used here can be checked in the author-hosted
Dyatlov--Zworski text Mathematical Theory of Scattering Resonances,
Appendix B.5.2: equation (B.5.20) bounds determinant differences by a
trace-norm difference; equation (B.5.23) is the logarithmic derivative
for holomorphic trace-class families. For fixed delta our path extends
holomorphically to a neighborhood of [0,1], since its positive operators
stay uniformly away from zero. Alternatively finite-rank compression
proves the same C^1 path formula from ordinary Jacobi's identity.
Primary source: https://math.mit.edu/~dyatlov/res/res_final.pdf
The needed formulas were inspected through source-specific indexed
excerpts; direct full-PDF opening exceeded the browser size limit.

3. Transfer under rooted local convergence, without inverse powers
----------------------------------------------------------------
Let (G_n,u_n,v_n) be finite connected rooted-edge graphs of maximum
degree at most Delta converging locally to an infinite connected rooted
graph (G,u,v). Set A_n=L(G_n), B_n=A_n+b_nb_n*, A=L(G), B=A+bb*,
with b=e_u-e_v and b_n=e_{u_n}-e_{v_n}. In particular all spectra of
the endpoint Laplacians lie in [0,2(Delta+1)].

For fixed finitely supported vectors f_1,...,f_k on G, transport their
coordinates to G_n for all sufficiently large n. Polynomial locality
and uniform polynomial approximation to x^alpha on the displayed compact
interval imply convergence of the two k-by-k Gram matrices

  D_n(i,j)=<f_i,n,(B_n^alpha-A_n^alpha)f_j,n>,
  E_n(i,j)=<f_i,n,A_n^alpha f_j,n>

to the corresponding D,E on G. The constant kernel of the finite graph
causes no difficulty: replacing f_i,n by its orthogonal projection off
constants changes neither matrix, because both powers annihilate that
kernel. For linearly independent f_i, E is positive definite: the
infinite connected graph Laplacian is injective on l2, since a vector
of zero energy is constant and an l2 constant is zero. Hence E_n>0 for
large n and

  E_n^(-1/2) D_n E_n^(-1/2) -> E^(-1/2) D E^(-1/2).            (8)

These are ordinary Ritz matrices for the finite relative C_n and the
infinite relative C on the images under A_n^(alpha/2) and A^(alpha/2),
respectively. Min--max therefore lower-bounds each finite full-graph
ordered eigenvalue by the corresponding Ritz eigenvalue.

Moreover A^(alpha/2)c00(G) is dense in l2(G). Indeed c00 is dense,
A^(alpha/2) is bounded, and injectivity makes its range dense. Thus
any fixed finite family of top eigenvectors of the compact positive C
can be approximated arbitrarily well by these finite-support images.
Combining this density with (8) proves, for every fixed j,

  lambda_j(C) <= liminf_n lambda_j(C_n),                       (9)

with ordered eigenvalues padded by zeros. A finite-dimensional
approximation of the top eigenspace supplies the same conclusion when
there are multiplicities; if the j-th eigenvalue is zero, it is trivial.

For graph edges, the leverage hypothesis required in Sections 1--2
is automatic. The variational identity

  ||A^(-1/2)b||^2
    = sup_{f in c00, <f,Af>>0} |<b,f>|^2/<f,Af> <= 1          (10)

follows from the spectral theorem and from the edited edge's one energy
term. This variational quantity is the wired resistance between u,v,
because c00 imposes zero boundary values outside finite exhaustions.

4. Exact compulsory local budget, with a caution about sharpness
--------------------------------------------------------------
For finite bridge edits, sum_j log(1+lambda_j(C_n))=alpha log 2.
Equations (4) and (9) imply that every fixed local partial sum must
survive in the finite graph. More explicitly, if eps_n decreases to zero
and q_n=N_{C_n}(eps_n), then for every fixed k,

  (q_n-k)_+ log(1+eps_n)
    <= alpha log 2 - sum_{j=1}^k log(1+lambda_j(C_n)).         (11)

To check (11) also when q_n<k, its left side is zero and its right side
is nonnegative. It follows that

  limsup_n eps_n q_n
    <= alpha log 2 - sum_{j=1}^k log(1+lambda_j(C)).

Letting k increase and using (4) gives the local/global bound

  limsup_n eps_n N_{C_n}(eps_n)
    <= alpha log[2/(1+tau_wired(G,u,v))].                      (12)

No assertion of attainable equality in (12) is included. It is a
one-sided budget statement: spectral budget escaping to small global
modes could still have a nonflat distribution and yield a strictly
smaller leading strict count. The finite-support Ritz proof is exactly
what permits charging the entire determinant of the local response,
rather than only its largest eigenvalue.
