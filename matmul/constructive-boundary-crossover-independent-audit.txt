1. NORMALIZED INPUT AND THE h-DEPENDENT WORK CLAIM
------------------------------------------------
The theorem explicitly assumes E=ww^T and ||w||=1. It does not silently
restore an arbitrary physical factor ||E||^alpha using a power oracle.
The changed-edge vector is sparse and computed from conductance change
and the common positive diagonal masses by allowed square roots.
Every normalized sparse A or B action costs O(M+N), even with arbitrary
mass ratios in the exact-real-arithmetic model.

The halving count m_e gives S=log(1/epsilon)+O_alpha(1). Certified bounds
on log(2), computed to exponentially small absolute error, contribute
only a bounded change to that expression. The h-halving loop is capped
at J=ceil(16(S+1)/alpha), so its work is O_alpha(S+1), uniformly for
arbitrarily tiny supplied h. At every stage h<=2^(-j_h)<=exp(-b).
Without clipping, b differs from log(1/h) by O_alpha(1). When clipping
occurs, both the true and clipped phase-area expressions vanish.
No log(1/h) or computation of epsilon^tau is hidden in the algorithm.

2. KRYLOV PROJECTION AND UNIFORM ANALYTIC ENVELOPE
------------------------------------------------
Exact Gram--Schmidt on w,Aw,...,A^(p-1)w uses O((M+N)p+Np^2) work.
If a residual vanishes exactly, the existing Krylov span is invariant
under A, so all subsequent powers remain in it. Discarding such a
residual therefore preserves the required moment annihilation. A lower
bound on nonzero residual norms is unnecessary in this arithmetic model.

For |arg z|<=d, rotated scalar resolvents give the denominator bound
|1+m(z)|>=cos(d/2)(1+m(|z|)). Jensen gives m(t)>=1/(w^TAw+t), and
w^TAw<=h. The three projected resolvent bounds follow independently:
the endpoint difference is O(c_d^-1/t); annihilating w gives the
O(c_d^-3 h/t^2) bound; annihilating the first p moments and using
||A^p w||^2<=2^(2p-1)h gives O(4^p c_d^-3 h/t^(2p+2)).

Thus the exact logarithmic integrand is bounded by
C_alpha 4^p c_d^-3 exp(-g(x)), with the stated three-line convex g.
Its breakpoints, minimum alpha*b and slope bound 2p+1 are correct.
On the real axis the additional bound is genuinely uniform in p:
D_t'=-R_A D_t-D_t R_B implies t||D_t'||<=2||D_t||;
t^(alpha+1)||D_t||<=min(t^alpha,t^(alpha-1))<=1.
Projection has norm one and is fixed while differentiating.

3. GROWING-p QUADRATURE AND THE SHARP PHASE AREA
------------------------------------------------
For p between S^(1/8) and 2S^(1/8), ell=S^(3/4) and
d=pi_- - S^(-1/4), the complete Gaussian-strip prefactor has logarithm
O_alpha(p+p^2+log(S+1))=o(ell). This explicitly includes the
Gaussian convolution factor from the growing envelope slope. It is
not an application of a fixed-p error estimate with an uncontrolled
constant. The lower approximation pi_- only makes the actual trapezoid
decay exponent at least the chosen H_j.

The retained sublevel set and its endpoints are correct in both possible
right-hand branches. Omitted windows, strip trapezoid error and finite
lattice tails have the stated exponential slack, uniformly in b.
If the sublevel set is empty, the omitted-window argument controls the
whole projected integral, so no special division or solve is required.

The interval-minimum count error is O_alpha(S*ell+p*ell^2); spacing
buffers, enlargement and rounding fit O_alpha(S^(7/4)). Direct
integration gives exactly the three cases in source equation (11).
After multiplication by 1/(2*pi_-*d), the limiting phase area is
Phi_alpha(S,b); the last high-energy triangle is O_alpha(S^2/p),
which is O_alpha(S^(15/8)). The moving-strip loss is O(S^(7/4)).
No factor p multiplies the projected rank-one node count.

Because S=log(1/epsilon)+O_alpha(1), and b is either within O_alpha(1)
of log(1/h) or in the zero-area clipped range, the phase-area Lipschitz
bound proves the stated UNIFORM supplied-h width bound. The final
2p restoration width is smaller than its remainder.

4. SCALAR REALIZATION AND SHIFTED-SDD ACCURACY
---------------------------------------------
The scalar rule uses nonnegative integrals of polynomial squares,
not an assumption that the integrating polynomial has nonnegative
coefficients. Degree O_alpha(S+1) Taylor polynomials and scaled
exponentiation evaluate the normalized nodes and exp((alpha+1)x)
weights with explicitly budgeted error and O_alpha((S+1)^3) total work.
Only the normalized update theorem is claimed; its ordinary auxiliary
correction has intrinsic scale one and fixed endpoint scale T=2.
At alpha=1/2 every required noninteger algebraic operation is an allowed
positive square root. The general-alpha normalized exponentials are
computed by the displayed Taylor routines rather than a free power call.

The initial source incorrectly suggested that increasing Taylor degree
could arbitrarily improve the fixed clipping tail. This was repaired:
the fixed B0=4sqrt(S+1) gives O(exp(-16(S+1))) clipping error, while
only Taylor error has an adjustable exponential constant. Uniform
real normalized-sample bounds make the fixed clipping tail sufficient
after summing polynomially many nodes. Node sensitivity uses the
separately verified uniform derivative bound.

Each projected node is the cross product of two separately computed
shifted actions. Their energy-relative errors imply Euclidean action
errors <=eta/t, so projected node error <=3eta/t^2. The positive weight
mass O_alpha(S+1) and |log t|=O_alpha(S+1) then give source (14).
Dyadic eta with logarithm O_alpha(S+1) suffices. No common, symmetric
or linear approximate-solver map is assumed.

The systems K+tH and K'+tH are SPD SDD for every positive shift,
including disconnected host graphs. The H^(1/2) coordinate transform
preserves system energy exactly. For the positive rank-one update,
||w||^2=||E||=1; thus the factor norm used in solver error bounds has
no hidden mass or cancellation parameter.

5. RESTORATION, TERMINAL BRANCH AND COMPLETE COST
------------------------------------------------
For any nonsymmetric D_tilde, the displayed restoration factors give

  [V,Q D_tilde V][D_tilde^T V,V]^T
     =P D_tilde+Q D_tilde P=D_tilde-Q D_tilde Q.

Their width is at most 2r<=2p. The error map is E->E-QEQ, whose
norm is at most 2||E||; the source's looser 3||E|| budget also holds.
Computing both thin D_tilde actions from its O(S^2)-width factors
costs O(N S^2 p). There is no dense matrix expansion.

In the terminal region, operator monotonicity gives
B^alpha>=(ww^T)^alpha=ww^T, while Jensen gives
w^TA^alpha w<=h^alpha. Hence d0=w^TDw>=1-h^alpha>=3/4.
The Schur rank-one F=(Dw)(Dw)^T/d0 has tail norm <=h^alpha, and
the correction norm is <=1. With action error u=epsilon/128,
d_hat>1/2 on the success event. Direct numerator/denominator
perturbation gives ||F_hat-F||<7u, leaving the claimed error slack.
The d_hat<=0 guard makes the routine defined also on failure events.
At alpha=1/2, h<=epsilon^2/4 is the exact sufficient comparison.
The equality h^alpha=epsilon is correctly not assigned a constructive
width-one guarantee here.

All projected factor entries, O(Npq) projections, O(Np^2) basis work,
ordinary correction, restoration and polynomial scalar operations fit
expected ~O_alpha(M+N). The existing median-energy-distance amplification
adds only logarithmic confidence factors. The stated failure allocation
has total at most delta. The expected qualifier is necessary and retained.
No mass ratio, endpoint norm ratio, spectral gap or condition number is
hidden. Finite-word conditioning is excluded explicitly.

The matching lower is the separately reviewed joint-boundary strict-count
theorem on connected common-mass paths. It proves optimal leading output
width, without supplying a runtime lower bound or ordinary-unit-mass
sharpness.
