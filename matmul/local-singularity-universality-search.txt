SHARP LOCALIZATION LAWS FOR SEVERAL AND INFINITELY MANY FRACTIONAL CUSPS
====================================================================

Owner: factor_four_local_universality. Date: 2026-11-15 UTC.
Status: complete mathematical derivation below; independent review pending.
No external novelty, human peer review, formal proof, runtime optimality,
or general quadratic-time claim is made. All prior single-cusp constants
are inputs from the already audited companion manuscripts, not reproved
or claimed anew here. No commits or external communications were made.

Main finding: one rank-one perturbation can simultaneously realize the
leading compression cost of every isolated interior fractional cusp.
It need not spend a separate perturbation-rank direction on each cusp.
This yields an additive law for exact small-Schatten-exponent constants,
and for infinitely many decaying cusps gives arbitrary powers beyond
the root-exponential/log-squared compression regime.

The normalization matters. Endpoint spectra lie in a fixed compact
interval, but the perturbation norm is allowed to approach zero, and
error is measured against ||B-A||^alpha. Therefore the unrestricted
single-cusp factor alpha*(1-alpha) remains. The separate checked note
bounded-spectrum-fractional-counts.txt fixes a bound on endpoint norm
relative to perturbation norm and has the different PSD factor alpha.
That theorem is used as a distinction, not contradicted or superseded.

1. DEFINITIONS AND THE COMPANION INPUT
=====================================

Fix 0<alpha<1 and beta=1-alpha. Let I=[a,b], a<b. All matrices are
finite Hermitian, dimension is unrestricted, and functional-calculus
outputs may be complex. For a scalar f on a neighborhood of I define

 H_s^I(f) = sup_{sigma(A),sigma(B) subset I, A!=B}
              tr|f(B)-f(A)|^s / tr|B-A|^(alpha*s),     0<s<=1.       (1.1)

For each fixed integer r>=1 let N_{f,r}^I(L) be the supremum of

 #{k: sigma_k(f(B)-f(A)) > exp(-L)||B-A||^alpha}                       (1.2)

over the same pairs with rank(B-A)<=r. Strict thresholds are intentional.
The perturbation norm is the operator norm. Write H_s(F) and N_{F,r}
for the dimension-free, whole-real-line versions for homogeneous F.

Put g_+(x)=(-ix)^alpha and g_-(x)=(ix)^alpha, principal branches.
For F=kappa*g_+ + lambda*g_- let

 m(F)=1_{kappa!=0}+1_{lambda!=0},
 c(F)=m(F)/(2*pi^2*alpha*beta).                                      (1.3)

The audited fractional-channel-classification.tex supplies:

 lim_{s->0} s^2 H_s(F) = 2*c(F),
 lim_{L->infinity} N_{F,r}(L)/L^2 = r*c(F),                           (1.4)

and the coefficient-uniform statement

 sup_{(kappa,lambda)!=0}
 | s^2 H_s(F)/(|kappa|^s+|lambda|^s)
                   -1/(pi^2*alpha*beta) | ->0.                       (1.5)

It also supplies rank-one reduction: H_s(F) is the supremum of
tr|F(B)-F(A)|^s for positive rank-one unit-norm updates B-A=vv*,
||v||=1. Positive rank-one examples attain the leading count in (1.4).
Homogeneity makes the intrinsic-scale formulation (1.2) equivalent to
the unit-update formulation for F. The same companion integral bounds
give ||F(B)-F(A)|| <= C_F ||B-A||^alpha.

Every nonzero real homogeneous fractional map
F(x)=u*x_+^alpha+v*(-x)_+^alpha has m(F)=2. The two exceptional complex
analytic branches have m(F)=1. For the real even map F_0(x)=|x|^alpha,

 c_0 = 1/(pi^2*alpha*beta),       H_s(F_0) ~ 2*c_0/s^2.               (1.6)

2. ANALYTIC REMAINDERS COST ONLY O(L) RANK AND O(1/s) POWER CONSTANT
=================================================================

Lemma 2.1. Let h be holomorphic on some complex neighborhood of I.
There are constants C,c>0 such that for every integer q>=0 one can
choose a scalar polynomial p_q of degree at most q with

 ||[h(B)-h(A)]-[p_q(B)-p_q(A)]||
                         <= C*exp(-c*q)||B-A||                     (2.1)

whenever the spectra of A,B belong to a fixed slightly enlarged real
interval I_delta. Constants are independent of matrix size.

Proof. Choose three nested Bernstein ellipses (after affine scaling
of I_delta), all lying in the holomorphy domain, with the middle ellipse
Gamma a positive distance from I_delta. Chebyshev truncation gives
sup_Gamma |h-p_q| <= C exp(-c q). Cauchy's formula and the resolvent
identity give

 [h-p_q](B)-[h-p_q](A)
   = (1/(2*pi*i)) int_Gamma [h(z)-p_q(z)]
                    (zI-B)^(-1)(B-A)(zI-A)^(-1) dz.

Both resolvents are uniformly bounded on Gamma. This proves (2.1).
The nesting is fixed before q or the input matrices are chosen.

For any polynomial p_q, rank(p_q(B)-p_q(A))<=q*rank(B-A). Indeed
B^k-A^k=sum_{ell=0}^{k-1} B^ell(B-A)A^(k-1-ell), so the full column
range lies in sum_{ell=0}^{q-1} range(B^ell(B-A)).

For rank-one increments of norm t, (2.1) gives
sigma_{q+1}(h(B)-h(A)) <= C t exp(-c q), including q=0. Therefore

 tr|h(B)-h(A)|^s <= (C t)^s/(1-exp(-c s)).                           (2.2)

For general A,B with spectra in I, let D=b-a, E=B-A, and choose a fixed
integer K with D/K<delta. The straight-line points A+jE/K remain in I.
Within each of its K segments telescope the signed spectral rank-one
decomposition of E/K. Every partial increment has operator norm at most
||E||/K<=D/K, since it is a partial spectral sum of E/K. Thus all
intermediate endpoints remain in I_delta. Apply (2.2) and the Schatten
power triangle to all segments and rank-one increments. This yields

 tr|h(B)-h(A)|^s
  <= [C^s K^(1-s)/(1-exp(-c s))] tr|E|^s
  <= [C^s K^(1-s)D^((1-alpha)s)/(1-exp(-c s))]
                                                   tr|E|^(alpha*s).

Consequently

                         H_s^I(h)=O_h,I(1/s).                       (2.3)

Also, since ||E||<=D, the polynomial correction in (2.1) has error at
most exp(-L)||E||^alpha at degree O_h,I(L+1). Thus analytic h contributes
only O_h,I(r(L+1)) rank in (1.2). This is a relative-to-update derivative
bound; an absolute scalar uniform approximation alone would not prove it.

3. THE FINITE-DIMENSIONAL BLOW-UP / RANK-ONE GLUING LEMMA
=======================================================

Let c_1,...,c_M be distinct points in the INTERIOR of I, and let

 f(x)=h(x)+sum_{j=1}^M F_j(x-c_j),                                   (3.1)

where h is as in Lemma 2.1 and each F_j is homogeneous of degree alpha
and of the form in Section 1. Terms identically zero can be omitted.
Near c_j, all terms other than F_j(x-c_j) are analytic. Their sum is
denoted h_j(x). Hence

 f(c_j+x)=f(c_j)+F_j(x)+O_j(|x|) as x->0.                            (3.2)

Lemma 3.1 (first-order block blow-up). For fixed finite Hermitian
T_+,T_- in the same block decomposition and C=diag(c_j I_{n_j}),

 t^(-alpha)[f(C+tT_+)-f(C+tT_-)]
   -> direct_sum_j [F_j((T_+)_{jj})-F_j((T_-)_{jj})]                (3.3)

in operator norm as t decreases to zero. All involved endpoint spectra
are in I for sufficiently small t. The finite matrices and t may depend
on the requested accuracy. No uniform gap or word-size claim is made.

Proof. For one fixed Hermitian T, define an anti-Hermitian S by

 S_{ij}=-T_{ij}/(c_i-c_j) for i!=j,    S_{ii}=0.

Then U_t=exp(tS) is unitary, U_t=I+O(t), and direct expansion gives

 U_t^*(C+tT)U_t = C+t*diag(T_{jj})+O(t^2),

because T+[C,S] is block diagonal. On a fixed compact neighborhood of I,
f in (3.1) is operator alpha-Hoelder: each F_j has the companion integral
bound, and h is operator Lipschitz by Cauchy's formula. Thus functional
calculus changes the O(t^2) remainder by O(t^(2alpha)). Undoing U_t
changes the bounded f-output by O(t). It follows that

 f(C+tT)=direct_sum_j f(c_j I+t T_{jj})+O(t^(2alpha)+t).

The local expansion (3.2), by finite spectral calculus, gives
f(c_j I+t T_{jj})=f(c_j)I+t^alpha F_j(T_{jj})+O(t).
The constant blocks cancel between T_+ and T_-. Since both 2alpha>alpha
and 1>alpha, division by t^alpha proves (3.3).

Now take ANY finite positive rank-one unit-update pairs
B_j-A_j=v_jv_j*, ||v_j||=1, and masses p_j>0 with sum p_j=1. Put

 H_j=(A_j+B_j)/2,       v=direct_sum_j sqrt(p_j)*v_j,
 T_+=diag(p_j H_j)+vv*/2,       T_-=diag(p_j H_j)-vv*/2.

Then ||v||=1 and the two global endpoints

 A_t=C+tT_-,            B_t=C+tT_+

obey B_t-A_t=t vv*: one POSITIVE rank-one increment of norm exactly t.
The diagonal blocks of T_-,T_+ are p_j A_j,p_j B_j respectively.
Equation (3.3) consequently becomes

 t^(-alpha)[f(B_t)-f(A_t)]
       -> direct_sum_j p_j^alpha [F_j(B_j)-F_j(A_j)].                (3.4)

This is the simultaneous nonlinear lower mechanism. It is not the
direct sum of M independent rank-one perturbations. The actual global
perturbation remains rank ONE throughout; off-block couplings vanish
only after a proved first-order change of basis and the blow-up limit.

4. EXACT FINITE-CUSP ADDITIVITY AND THE FIXED-s MASS SANDWICH
==========================================================

Theorem 4.1. For f in (3.1), write c_j^*=c(F_j) and
Q=sum_j m(F_j). Then

 lim_{s->0} s^2 H_s^I(f) = Q/(pi^2*alpha*beta),                     (4.1)

 lim_{L->infinity} N_{f,r}^I(L)/L^2
                         = r*Q/(2*pi^2*alpha*beta).                 (4.2)

The lower constants in both statements already hold for positive
rank-one increments and their r-fold direct sums. Cusp positions and
nonzero fixed amplitudes do not enter these leading coefficients.

More precisely, for every 0<s<=1 and any masses p_j>0 summing to one,

 sum_j p_j^(alpha*s) H_s(F_j) <= H_s^I(f)
                      <= sum_j H_s(F_j)+H_s^I(h).                    (4.3)

Proof of (4.3),(4.1). The upper is the power triangle applied in function
space and (2.3). For the lower choose independent finite rank-one
near-maximizers for every H_s(F_j), which exist by the companion
rank-one reduction. In (3.4) the numerator's power trace converges,
since its dimension is finite at this point, whereas its input power
denominator is exactly t^(alpha*s). This proves the lower in (4.3)
after letting the near-maximizer errors vanish. No supremum is inter-
changed with an integral. Fix any positive masses, multiply by s^2,
and use (1.4), p_j^(alpha*s)->1, and s^2 H_s^I(h)->0. This proves (4.1).

The best lower bound from these masses is also explicit:

 H_s^I(f) >= [sum_j H_s(F_j)^(1/(1-alpha*s))]^(1-alpha*s).           (4.4)

This follows by concave maximization of sum_j H_j*p_j^(alpha*s).
It is a lower bound, not an exact fixed-s additivity assertion.

Count upper. Approximate each F_j correction at error
exp(-L)||E||^alpha/(M+1) using (1.4)'s uniformly bounded-rank
approximants; use Lemma 2.1 for h. Their ranks add. Fixed allocations
shift L only by constants. Thus limsup in (4.2) is at most r sum c_j^*.

Count lower for r=1. Choose fixed masses and a fixed C_0 such that
p_j^alpha exp(C_0)>2 for every j. At a given large L choose independent
finite rank-one unit-update witnesses for F_j having at least
(c_j^*-o(1))(L-C_0)^2 singular values above exp(-(L-C_0)). Formula
(3.4) has all these singular values above 2exp(-L), so for sufficiently
small t the actual normalized output has them above exp(-L).
Summing yields the lower sum c_j^*. For general fixed r take r direct
copies of this ONE-rank-one construction. The total increment has rank
r and still norm t. This proves (4.2).

For a concrete real example,

 f(x)=p(x)+sum_{j=1}^M a_j*(x-c_j)_+^alpha,   a_j real and nonzero,

with polynomial p and distinct interior c_j, has Q=2M. Its moment
coefficient is 2M/(pi^2 alpha beta), and its count coefficient is
rM/(pi^2 alpha beta). Relative to the companion PSD power coefficient,
the small-s power-constant multiplier is 4M. This is a statement about
power constants; ordinary Schatten norm constants are their 1/s powers.

5. UNIFORMITY IN THE NUMBER, AMPLITUDES, AND POSITIONS OF CUSPS
============================================================

For this paragraph set h=0. Let M=M_s be any positive finite integer,
allow c_j=c_{j,s} to be any distinct interior points, and allow all
nonzero coefficient pairs (kappa_j,lambda_j) to vary arbitrarily with s.
Put

 W_s=sum_j (|kappa_j|^s+|lambda_j|^s).

Equal masses in (4.3) give

 M_s^(-alpha*s) sum_j H_s(F_j) <= H_s^I(f_s)
                                         <= sum_j H_s(F_j).         (5.1)

If s log M_s->0, (1.5) and (5.1) prove the uniform law

 s^2 H_s^I(f_s)/W_s -> 1/(pi^2 alpha beta).                          (5.2)

No minimum cusp separation, lower amplitude, or lower update scale is
needed. Exact distinctness suffices because the supremum in (1.1)
permits arbitrarily small t after the finite local witnesses are chosen.
The endpoint interval can also vary while remaining nondegenerate and
containing all cusps in its interior; no uniform t is asserted.

For fixed M this includes arbitrarily tiny, even superexponentially
small cusp coefficients. If positions coalesce EXACTLY, coefficient
cancellation can remove channels, so the conclusion changes. There is
no contradiction: no positive lower bound on ||E|| was imposed.
An analytic remainder can be added with its quantified O(1/s) bound;
for a normalized uniform relative statement its size must be controlled
relative to W_s. Unrestricted varying h is not covered by (5.2).

6. AN INFINITE-CUSP LAW WITH ARBITRARY POLYNOMIAL BLOW-UP ORDER
===========================================================

Choose distinct c_j in the interior of I, with no accumulation point
inside I (for example I=[0,1], c_j=2^(-j)). Let b_j>=1 tend to infinity,
and suppose their amplitude counting function satisfies, for constants
C>0 and nu>0,

 A(u)=#{j: b_j<=u} ~ C*u^nu as u->infinity.                          (6.1)

Define the globally alpha-Hoelder scalar function

 f(x)=sum_{j>=1} exp(-b_j)*|x-c_j|^alpha.                            (6.2)

The coefficients are summable by (6.1), so (6.2) converges locally
uniformly on R and has scalar and operator alpha-Hoelder constants at
most their respective F_0 constants times sum exp(-b_j). Every c_j is
isolated. Near that point all other terms form a holomorphic function:
choose a disk avoiding every other c_k; branches can be chosen there,
are bounded uniformly in k, and their weighted series converges normally.
Thus (3.2) holds for each finite selected set, with local cusp
F_j=exp(-b_j)*F_0 and analytic local remainder. The proof of the blow-up
lemma uses only these local expansions and the stated global operator
Hoelder bound, so it applies to every finite selection of these cusps.

Theorem 6.1. For (6.2), H_s^I(f) is finite for every 0<s<=1 and

 H_s^I(f) ~ [2*C*Gamma(nu+1)/(pi^2*alpha*beta)] s^(-(nu+2))          (6.3)

as s->0. For every fixed r>=1,

 N_{f,r}^I(L)
  ~ [2*r*C/(pi^2*alpha*beta*(nu+1)*(nu+2))] L^(nu+2).               (6.4)

Positive rank-one increments already attain the leading r=1 lower
bound. The statement concerns suprema over finite input matrices,
depending on s or L, not an infinite-matrix input or a fixed pair's
singular-value asymptotic. No implementation cost or bit-complexity
theorem follows from this existence/compression statement.

Moment proof. Put D(s)=sum_j exp(-s*b_j), and H_0(s)=H_s(F_0). Infinite
power-triangle passage is justified by first using finite partial sums,
then taking their norm limit on each fixed finite matrix pair. It gives

 H_s^I(f) <= H_0(s)*D(s).                                           (6.5)

For every finite selected set, the blow-up lemma and optimized masses
give the lower bound (4.4). Letting the number of selected cusps increase
yields

 H_s^I(f) >= H_0(s)*D(s/(1-alpha*s))^(1-alpha*s).                    (6.6)

Here alpha*s<1 because alpha<1 and s<=1. All sums in (6.5)-(6.6) are
finite: (6.1) gives A(u)<=C'(1+u^nu), and integration by parts yields

 D(s)=s int_0^infinity exp(-su) A(u)du
             ~ C*Gamma(nu+1)*s^(-nu).                              (6.7)

The last limit follows after v=su by dominated convergence, using the
polynomial bound on A. With q_s=s/(1-alpha*s), q_s/s->1 and

 D(q_s)^(1-alpha*s)/D(s) ->1,                                       (6.8)

because log D(s)=nu log(1/s)+O(1), so s log D(s)->0. Equations
(6.5)-(6.8) and (1.6) prove (6.3). This argument is a squeeze between
actual finite-matrix lower examples and a valid infinite-sum upper.

Count proof: upper. From A(u)<=C'(1+u^nu), Stieltjes integration gives

 sum_{b_j>T} exp(-b_j) <= C'' exp(-T)(1+T^(nu+1)).                   (6.9)

Take T=L+(nu+4)log(L+2), enlarging by a fixed constant as needed, and
retain the J=A(T)=O(L^nu) terms with b_j<=T. The discarded correction
has operator norm at most exp(-L)||E||^alpha/2 by the F_0 operator
Hoelder bound and (6.9). Allocate error exp(-L)||E||^alpha/(2J) to
each retained weighted term, returning zero whenever that tolerance
exceeds its uniform norm bound.

For any eta>0, the single-cusp count upper (1.4) implies that these
terms have total approximating rank at most

 (r*c_0+eta) sum_j [L+O(log L)-b_j]_+^2 + O_eta(J),                 (6.10)

uniformly over all input pairs of rank at most r. Terms whose depth is
bounded or slightly negative can be absorbed into O_eta(J), by their
uniform norm bound and a fixed finite-threshold single-cusp count bound.
The implied constants can depend on alpha,r,eta,C,nu, not the inputs.

For any V->infinity,

 sum_j (V-b_j)_+^2
       =2 int_0^V (V-u) A(u)du
       ~ [2*C/((nu+1)(nu+2))] V^(nu+2).                            (6.11)

Replacing V=L by L+O(log L) changes this leading term by o(L^(nu+2)).
Combine (6.10)-(6.11), then let eta->0, to prove the desired limsup.

Count proof: lower for r=1. Fix 0<delta<1 and retain the
J=A((1-delta)L) cusps with b_j<=(1-delta)L. Give each mass p_j=1/J.
For these terms the effective local depth

 u_j=L-b_j-alpha log J-log 4

is at least delta L-O(log L) and hence tends to infinity uniformly.
Choose independent finite unit rank-one witnesses for F_0 at every u_j.
For all large L their counts exceed (c_0-eta)u_j^2. Their weighted,
mass-scaled blocks have all these singular values above 4exp(-L).
The finite-selection blow-up lemma makes the actual normalized global
output differ by less than exp(-L), with an arbitrarily small but
strictly positive update norm t. Thus its count at exp(-L) is at least

 (c_0-eta) sum_{b_j<=(1-delta)L}
                      [L-b_j-alpha log J-log 4]^2.                  (6.12)

The O(log L) shifts are lower order. Stieltjes integration and (6.1)
give the normalized limit

 L^(-(nu+2)) sum_{b_j<=(1-delta)L}(L-b_j)^2
     -> C*nu int_0^(1-delta) (1-v)^2 v^(nu-1)dv.                   (6.13)

For completeness this follows by integration by parts on [0,1-delta],
including the endpoint mass term, followed by dominated convergence
of L^(-nu)A(Lv) to C*v^nu. Let delta->0 and eta->0; the integral
becomes 2*C/((nu+1)(nu+2)). This proves the r=1 liminf. Taking r direct
copies preserves the common update norm and gives the factor r in
(6.4). No moment-to-count Tauberian interchange is needed here.

Example. Let b_j=j^gamma for any gamma>0 (changing finitely many b_j
to enforce b_j>=1 has no effect). Then C=1 and nu=1/gamma, giving

 H_s^I(f_gamma)
  ~ [2*Gamma(1+1/gamma)/(pi^2*alpha*beta)] s^(-2-1/gamma),

 N_{f_gamma,r}^I(L)
  ~ [r/(pi^2*alpha*beta)]
                  [2*gamma^2/((gamma+1)(2*gamma+1))] L^(2+1/gamma).

Thus any prescribed exponent p>2 occurs by gamma=1/(p-2), even though
f_gamma is globally alpha-Hoelder and H_s^I(f_gamma)<infinity for every
positive s. For gamma=1 the exact count coefficient is r*c_0/3 at L^3,
and the moment coefficient is 2*c_0 at s^(-3). At r=1, the Gamma(4)=6
layer-cake factor checks these two constants: (c_0/3)*6=2*c_0.

7. WHAT IS NOT PROVED / WHICH DIRECTIONS WERE REJECTED
=====================================================

* A scalar o(|x-c|^alpha) remainder alone does not give the sharp upper.
  The finite theorem explicitly requires the global decomposition (3.1)
  with analytic h. Its lower only needs the stated local expansions.
  Higher-order branch terms, analytic amplitudes multiplying cusps, and
  arbitrary logarithmic/slowly varying local multipliers would need a
  further sharp rational/localization upper; they are not silently covered.

* Different local exponents are not covered by the intrinsic-scale
  theorem with a single alpha. At a shrinking update scale t, a cusp
  exponent larger than alpha contributes t^(alpha_j-alpha), which may
  affect the accuracy law. Fixed-unit-update compact-spectrum constants
  are a separate possible theorem and were not assumed here.

* No sharp universal count for spectra constrained to I AND a lower
  bound on ||E|| is claimed. The arbitrarily small update norm is
  essential to the simultaneous block embedding and to (5.2)'s lack
  of a cusp-separation condition. Fixed ||E||=1 does not follow.

* This is not a new root-exponential scalar approximation theorem or
  a new Cauchy/Hankel localization method. It is a finite-matrix
  rank-one gluing principle applied to the already proved sharp
  noncommutative constants and compression laws.

* The count theorem is a best-rank existence theorem. It supplies no
  charged algorithm for resolving arbitrarily small update scales or
  closely spaced cusps, no stable coefficient preparation, and no
  finite-word or actual-hardware theorem.
