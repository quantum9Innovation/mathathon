VERDICT
-------
Both core counterexample proofs reconstruct correctly. They demonstrate
maximal hard-projector and unique cardinality-bisection-label change under
a vanishing relative energy perturbation, in simple connected WEIGHTED
combinatorial graphs with ordinary UNIT vertex masses and maximum
COMBINATORIAL degree AT MOST four. They do not make an unweighted-host,
bounded weighted-degree, normalized-Laplacian, or volume-bisection claim.

The two notes use the same C4 x path mechanism, but their explicitly
specified parameter families are NOT identical. The first note edits an
edge of conductance EXACTLY ONE. The second edits an edge of conductance
a=n/(n+1)<1; its increment is a, so it is an edge doubling but is not
literally a unit-edge doubling until the entire graph is rescaled by 1/a.
That rescaling is harmless for leverage, eigenvectors and minimizing cuts,
but it changes all numerical conductances and cut objective values.

No substantive mathematical defect was found. One minor wording issue:
at r=m=2 the underlying graph is the cube, whose maximum degree is three,
so sentences saying 'maximum degree four' should say 'at most four' if
they are intended as literal equalities for every parameter. All degree-
upper-bound conclusions in the proposed claim are correct as written.

This is a mathematical/scope audit, not a historical-novelty or research-
significance certificate. Neither frozen proof source was edited.

1. EXACT RELATION BETWEEN THE TWO CONSTRUCTIONS
----------------------------------------------
Use r=m and n=4r, and permute the square labels if needed.

First note, physical graph:
  edited square rails: conductance 1;
  other square rails: 1+1/n;
  each longitudinal path edge: 2nr^2=8r^3;
  edited increment: 1.

Second note, physical graph:
  edited square rails: a=n/(n+1);
  other square rails: 1;
  longitudinal path edges: 2r^3;
  edited increment: a.

Multiply the second pair of endpoint Laplacians by 1/a. Its rails
become exactly 1 and 1+1/n, and its edited increment becomes one, as
in the first note. Its longitudinal conductance becomes 2r^3/a,
however, which differs from the first note's 8r^3. Thus these are
the same architectural family with different sufficiently large
rigidity constants, not the very same specified graphs up to scaling.

Their flat low spectra and sign-cut values after this scaling coincide.
The first proof separately verifies its own stronger path rigidity,
so no transplantation of an unproved parameter is needed.

2. FIRST NOTE: PRODUCT SPECTRUM AND THE UNIQUE SWITCH
---------------------------------------------------
Analyzing A=widehat L/2 is legitimate: scalar multiplication preserves
the usual Euclidean Fiedler eigenspaces and all cut minimizers.
The square eigenvalues on the three unit Hadamard vectors are exactly
1,1+delta,2+delta, with delta=1/n. Their tensor products with the
normalized constant path vector have entries +/-1/sqrt(n).

For every positive path mode,
  nu_1=4sin^2(pi/(2r))>=4/r^2,
and M=nr^2 therefore gives high product eigenvalues at least 4n.
The old Fiedler vector x is simple.

For the edited incidence b, its exact decomposition is
  b=beta x+beta z+h, beta=2/sqrt(n), ||h||^2=2-8/n,
with b^T y=0 and h in the high subspace. The unit physical edit
becomes B=A+(1/2)bb^T. On {1,y}^perp the secular function

 f(s)=1+(1/2)[beta^2/(1-s)+beta^2/(2+delta-s)
                            +h^T(A-sI)^-1 h]

is strictly increasing on (1,2+delta), from -infinity to +infinity.
Rank-one interlacing leaves every other eigenvalue of that restriction
at least 2+delta. At t=1+delta, the source's estimate

 f(t)<=-1+2/n+1/(4n-1-1/n)<0, n>=8,

is valid. Hence its unique secular root is above t; y remains at t
and is the unique new Fiedler vector. Physical Fiedler eigenvalues
are 2 and 2+2/n. Both endpoint gaps are positive, but the relevant
low separation shrinks with n; no fixed eigengap promise is being met.

3. UNIQUE GLOBAL CARDINALITY BISECTIONS
--------------------------------------
For a balanced cut S, its normalized sign vector f_S is orthogonal
to constants, has norm one, and satisfies
  f_S^T widehat L f_S=4 w(partial S)/n.
The flat old eigenvector x is itself such a sign vector, so it attains
the spectral lower bound for every cardinality bisection. Simplicity
of lambda_2 makes equality possible only at +/-x. The same reasoning
applies to the new flat simple eigenvector y. This proves uniqueness
of the GLOBAL minimum bisections, rather than merely of their spectral
relaxations or of an algorithm's output.

The four sign intersections each have r vertices. Thus the two unordered
bisections have disagreement n/2 even after choosing the better global
sign/complement. The old optimum has physical weight n/2. The competing
y-cut has weight (n/2)(1+1/n)=n/2+1/2. The edit crosses the x-cut,
adding one, and does not cross the y-cut. All stated objective values
therefore agree with the Fiedler lower-bound calculation.

This large label change does not assert a large multiplicative loss
in objective value: retaining the old cut costs only another half unit
over a new optimum of size n/2+1/2.

4. VANISHING LEVERAGE, FRACTIONAL RESPONSE, AND PROJECTORS
--------------------------------------------------------
The first note's scale conversion is correct:
  tau=(1/2)b^T A^dagger b.
Its exact low contribution is
  (2/n)[1+1/(2+delta)],
and its high contribution is at most 1/(4n). This is strictly below
13/(4n)<4/n and is positive. The stated path-mode scalar formula has
the correct square-mode coefficients and the same factor one-half.

On the common positive support, rank-one determinant and fractional
calculus give
  product_j(1+lambda_j(C_alpha))=(1+tau)^alpha,
with every lambda_j(C_alpha)>=0. It follows that
  ||C_alpha||<=(1+tau)^alpha-1<=alpha tau ->0.
There is no hidden mass change: the metric uses the actual ordinary
combinatorial Laplacian on unit vertices.

The flat Hadamard vectors are orthogonal, so their rank-one orthogonal
projector difference has eigenvalues +1,-1 and zeros. The claimed
operator norm one and squared Frobenius norm two are exact.
Positive fractional powers preserve eigenvectors and eigenvalue order;
the claimed Fiedler-projector failure transfers to L^alpha. This does
not by itself assert any normalized-Laplacian partition conclusion.

5. SECOND NOTE: INDEPENDENT SCHUR CHECK
--------------------------------------
Here a=4m/(4m+1), M=2m^3, and the high longitudinal gap is at least
Lambda=8m. The low square eigenvalues are exactly 0,2a,2,2a+2.
The edited incidence has x,z projections 1/sqrt(m), and zero y projection.

On W=1^perp intersect y^perp, the old L-2I has x block
-a/(2m) and positive complementary block D. The z component has
D-eigenvalue 2a; all other active components have eigenvalue at least
Lambda-2. Hence the stated bound
  a s0<=1/(2m)+2a/(Lambda-2)<1
holds for every m>=2. The Schur complement after the update is exactly

  (a/m)[1/(1+a s0)-1/2]>0.

Thus y is uniquely the new Fiedler vector. The cut values and uniqueness
then follow exactly as in Section3. Its leverage estimate

 tau<=1/(2m)+a/[2(a+1)m]+2a/Lambda<1/m

is correct. Energy Cauchy--Schwarz gives a rr^T<=tau L, and Loewner--
Heinz supplies the fractional relative order bound. The exact logdet
identity is also correct. The graph has 4m+4(m-1)=2n-4 edges and is
planar (nested squares with corresponding corners joined), bipartite,
and simple. Ordinary unit vertex masses are implicit in these
combinatorial tensor Laplacians and the Euclidean eigenvectors.

6. THE INTEGER UNWEIGHTED CAVEAT IS NECESSARY AND CORRECT
-------------------------------------------------------
If an unweighted host has a unique minimum bisection of integer cost k,
every different admissible cut has old cost at least k+1. Under one
unit-edge addition or doubling, every cost increases by either zero
or one. The old optimum has new cost at most k+1, so no different cut
can become a STRICTLY better unique optimum. A tie can appear.
This argument applies to any fixed admissible cut family with integer
old costs, not only bisections. It does not protect Fiedler vectors,
whose Rayleigh objectives are not integral.

The weighted construction evades exactly this obstruction: the two
competing old cut costs differ by half the edited increment. Rescaling
to integer weights would also rescale the increment, so it would not
produce an integer-host counterexample with a UNIT increment.

7. OTHER STATEMENTS IN THE TWO NOTES
------------------------------------
The second note's extremal-distance proposition is correct: every
unordered cut distance is at most n/2 and every difference of rank-one
orthogonal projectors has norm at most one. Choosing m large makes
tau<epsilon and attains both upper bounds at finite dimension.

Its necessary-parameter-escape bound is also correct. For an existing
edge incidence r, spectral Cauchy--Schwarz gives
  (r^T L^dagger r)(r^T Lr)>=4,
while r^TLr=d_w(u)+d_w(v)+2a<=2(d+1)C. Multiplying by a>=c gives
tau>=2c/[(d+1)C]. Hence at fixed combinatorial degree, vanishing leverage
under edge doubling really requires unbounded conductance ratio. The
large longitudinal weights are a substantive scope condition.

The two-affine-envelope observation is valid: an edge increment t
adds t to every separating cut and zero to every nonseparating cut.
The minimizing families within those classes never change; only their
single possible crossing can change the global optimizer set.

The four-vertex small-additive-edit variant is valid and distinct from
edge doubling. Its updated x,z block is
  [[2+delta/2,delta],[delta,4+delta/2]],
with eigenvalues 3+delta/2 +/-sqrt(1+delta^2). The smaller is above two
for 0<delta<4/3. Thus its claimed flat unique-Fiedler and exact-bisection
switch holds, but its increment delta is small, not the whole old edge
conductance a. It does not eliminate the dynamic-range requirement for
DOUBLING a unit edge at bounded combinatorial degree.

Finally the first note's ridge-response proposition checks directly:
Sherman--Morrison gives a positive rank-one D_s with
tr D_s=-s d/ds log(1+d r(s)); its logarithmic-scale integral is
log(1+tau). The bound tr D_s<=tau/(1+tau) gives the claimed Frobenius
integral and Markov bound. Its stated classical status is appropriate;
this audit certifies the algebra, not novelty.
