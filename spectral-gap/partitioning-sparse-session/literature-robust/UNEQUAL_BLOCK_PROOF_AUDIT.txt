1. Quotient algebra and prime selection
--------------------------------------
Since lambda is an integer eigenvalue of Q, c1=a1-lambda and
c2=a2-lambda are integers and c1*c2=b1*b2. The biadjacency matrix
really has B*1=b1*1 and B^T*1=b2*1, since n1*b1=n2*b2.
For every odd prime p not dividing b1*b2, neither ci vanishes modulo p.
No condition p not dividing ai, lambda, or either block size is needed.
The quotient discriminant (a1-a2)^2+4*b1*b2 is strictly positive, so its
two real eigenvalues are distinct. Its smaller eigenvalue is strictly
below both a1 and a2, and the larger is strictly above both; this follows
immediately from the quadratic formula and b1*b2>0.

2. Affine one-block estimate
----------------------------
For x=alpha*1 the reference equation U_x=lambda*x+t requires
t=c*alpha*1. Since c is nonzero in F_p, at most one alpha contributes,
with probability1. For nonconstant x, the affine support condition is
c*sum(x)=sum(t), a single nonzero linear equation on all n coordinates.
It has p^(n-1) solutions before excluding constants, and every admitted
nonconstant x contributes p^(-n+1). Thus the reference sum is <=2,
uniformly in arbitrary t. Summing pointwise errors gives <=2+epsilon
for the actual law. All signs in this calculation are correct.

3. Error summation for unequal block sizes
------------------------------------------
Conditioning on deterministic B, independence of internal blocks gives
the exact product sum in(6). With e1_x the pointwise discrepancy for
block1, replacing P1 costs at most
 sum_x e1_x sum_y P2(y,lambda*y-B^T*x)
 <=epsilon1*(2+epsilon2).
The inner sum is an affine expectation with a fixed right-hand side,
regardless of rectangular B. After this replacement, replacing P2 costs
at most
 sum_y e2_y sum_x R1(x,lambda*x-B*y) <=2*epsilon2.
Therefore(7) is correct, uniformly over B, without a missing exponential
number of summands. Both ni tend to infinity, so both fixed-prime
epsilon terms tend to zero independently.

4. Reference pair counting and the rectangular coefficient positions
--------------------------------------------------------------------
For nonconstant x and y, the two sum constraints are
 c1*s_x+b2*s_y=0, b1*s_x+c2*s_y=0.
The b2 in the first equation comes from summing B*y using B's column
sums; the b1 in the second comes from summing B^T*x using its column
sums. Thus the source's matrix [[c1,b2],[b1,c2]] is correct.
Its determinant is zero and its entries are nonzero over the chosen
field, so its rank is exactly one. Composing this rank-one equation
with the surjective maps x->s_x and y->s_y gives at most
p^(n1+n2-1) pairs. Their individual reference probabilities multiply
to p^(-n1-n2+2), hence their contribution is <=p.

For constant x=alpha*1,y=beta*1, the equations instead use row sums:
 c1*alpha+b1*beta=0, b2*alpha+c2*beta=0.
This second2x2 matrix also has rank exactly one, yielding precisely p
pairs, each with probability1. The source correctly distinguishes these
two coefficient matrices; interchanging b1,b2 in only one equation
would have been erroneous.

For x constant and y nonconstant, B*y=-c1*alpha*1 has either no
solutions or p^(n2-rank_p B) solutions for each alpha. Its second-block
reference probability is at most p^(-n2+1). Multiplying by p choices
of alpha gives p^(2-rank_p B). With y constant instead, the factors are
p^(n1-rank_p B^T) and p^(-n1+1), producing the same bound despite
unequal sizes. These four disjoint cases prove(9).

5. Deterministic rank growth of every allowed B
-----------------------------------------------
The support of one row has b1 columns. Each such column occurs in at
most b2-1 other rows. Greedy selection therefore removes at most
1+b1*(b2-1) available rows per selection. The chosen nonzero rows have
pairwise disjoint supports and are independent over every field. This
gives the stated lower bound on rank_p B. It also covers b1=1 or b2=1.
The bound is compatible with rank<=min(n1,n2) under n1*b1=n2*b2 and
diverges linearly for fixed positive b1,b2. It is a field-independent
support argument, not an inference from large real rank.

6. Planted vector and real nullity
---------------------------------
For v=(b1*1,-c1*1), the first block of (A-lambda I)v is
c1*b1*1-c1*b1*1=0; the second is b2*b1*1-c2*c1*1=0.
Thus v is an exact nonzero integer vector, and real nullity>=1.
If real nullity>=2, every(Ntotal-1)-minor of A-lambda I vanishes as an
integer, so modular nullity>=2 for every prime. Modular kernel size is
then at least p^2. The moment bound yields(11), with the supremum over
B legitimate because its error and rank bounds were uniform. Fixing p,
taking the size limit, and only then taking admissible primes to infinity
proves matching-model real nullity1 with probability tending to one.
There is no hidden growing-prime uniformity assumption.

7. Contiguity, uniformity and conditioning
------------------------------------------
Finite independent products preserve mutual contiguity, including factors
whose degrees differ and whose even sizes run along proportional
subsequences. This follows by successively applying the bounded-function
criterion E_P f->0 implies E_Q f->0 for0<=f<=1. Each corresponding
loopless configuration graph is simple with probability bounded below
for fixed degree. Conditioning on both simplicity events therefore
preserves a probability1-o(1) property and gives independent uniform
simple internal graphs.

The source's worst-case deterministic-B argument is valid: there are
finitely many allowed B at each size, so a maximizing B exists. If the
simple-model supremum failed to vanish, choose such a deterministic
sequence along a bad subsequence. The matching-model failure probability
still vanishes uniformly, and product contiguity plus bounded-probability
conditioning would force the chosen simple-model probabilities to vanish,
contradicting their choice. Averaging then covers any independent law
of B. Choosing B adaptively from realized internals is correctly excluded.

8. Recovery and bit complexity
-------------------------------
At the smaller eigenvalue, c1>0 while b1>0, so the planted vector has
opposite nonzero signs on the two blocks. A one-dimensional rational
kernel recovers these two sign classes up to an overall sign. Its
entries need not have equal magnitudes, and the source does not assume
they do. Matrix entries and eigenvalue are bounded in terms of fixed
degree parameters. Original rows have bounded Euclidean norm, so the
stated Hadamard determinant bit bound O(Ntotal*log(sum degrees+1)) is
valid. Fraction-free rational elimination is polynomial in bit complexity.
The source correctly makes no sparse-runtime or numerical-conditioning
claim.

9. Equal-total-degree corollary and explicit embedded example
------------------------------------------------------------
When ai=d-bi, the quotient eigenvalues are d and lambda=d-b1-b2.
Then c1=b2,c2=b1, and v=(b1*1,-b2*1). Its coordinate sum vanishes
because n1*b1=n2*b2. Thus the displayed corollary is an exact
specialization, including when lambda is negative or embedded in the
d-regular-tree bulk.

For d=7,b1=2,b2=4, the ratio n1=2*n2 is correct, internal degrees
are5 and3, Q has trace8 and determinant7, and its eigenvalues are7,1.
The planted vector(2,-4) scales to(1,-2). The full graph is7-regular,
so degrees alone do not identify its blocks. The Bethe-Hessian curve
on v is r^2-r+6=(r-1/2)^2+23/4, strictly positive for all real r;
orthogonality to every negative eigenspace follows by symmetry. This
does not conflict with exact kernel recovery or independent-edge SBM
threshold results.

Separate optional observation sent to the parent
------------------------------------------------
The source expressly leaves noninteger quotient eigenvalues untreated.
That is a scope restriction rather than a false assertion. However, a
short deterministic Galois observation handles them on connected graphs:
if Q's discriminant is nonsquare, its two eigenvalues are conjugate roots
of an irreducible monic quadratic over Q. Their algebraic multiplicities
in the integer characteristic polynomial of A are equal. The larger
quotient eigenvalue has a strictly positive block-constant eigenvector,
so on a connected graph it is the simple Perron eigenvalue. Hence the
smaller conjugate eigenvalue is simple too. Symmetry turns algebraic
multiplicity into kernel dimension. This observation is not incorporated
in the audited source or needed for its proof, and no novelty is claimed
for this elementary conjugacy argument. An author may add it separately
with an explicit connectedness argument for the random internal model.
