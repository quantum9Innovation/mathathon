Independent product-AMM literature audit -- 2026-11-14

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
This is a targeted primary-source audit, not a completeness or novelty
certificate. No new total-runtime improvement for the general spectral-norm
product problem has been established by this audit. In particular, none of the
sources below proves the desired gap-free rank-r bound with logarithmic
dependence on inverse accuracy and construction time ~O(I r + m^omega).
All explicit-output comparisons must still add T_MM(n,s,p) for width s, or
the relevant full-matrix reconstruction cost.

1. Joint leverage score sampling (October 2026)
------------------------------------------------
Neophytos Charalambides, Mert Pilanci, Alfred O. Hero III,
Approximate Matrix Multiplication through Joint Leverage Score Sampling.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29466
https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29466v1
Primary PDF cached transiently at /tmp/mathathon-joint-leverage.pdf;
layout text at /tmp/mathathon-joint-leverage.txt.

The paper uses A in R^{N x L}, B in R^{N x M}, and approximates A^T B
by sampling s pairs of rows with replacement. Let U_A,U_B be reduced left
orthonormal bases, bar_l_i=||U_A(i,:)||^2, tilde_l_i=||U_B(i,:)||^2,
tau=sum_i sqrt(bar_l_i tilde_l_i), mu=sqrt(L M), and
pi_i=sqrt(bar_l_i tilde_l_i)/tau. Approximate probabilities satisfy
hat_pi_i >= beta pi_i for all i, with beta in (0,1].

Lemma 5, equation (2.11), proves only the factor-norm implication

  ||A^T B - (S A)^T(S B)||_2 <= e ||A||_2 ||B||_2

when ||U_A^T U_B-(S U_A)^T(S U_B)||_2 <= e. This is not a bound
in terms of sigma_{r+1}(A^T B) or product-tail energy.

Theorem 11 as printed assumes the leverage comparability condition (2.13):

  max{ max_i sqrt(bar_l_i/tilde_l_i) * max_i tilde_l_i,
       max_i sqrt(tilde_l_i/bar_l_i) * max_i bar_l_i } <= mu/tau.

It claims s=Theta(mu log(2 mu/d)/(beta e^2)) and success probability at
least 1-d*max{sqrt(L/M),sqrt(M/L)}. For the absolute error eps in
Lemma 5 one must set e=eps/(||A||_2||B||_2). Proposition 12 states a
slightly looser beta dependence. Setup must compute or approximate the
leverage-score distribution; output costs O(L M s) conventionally, as
stated immediately before Algorithm 1, or the corresponding fast MM cost.

Translation to the current product uses the paper's N=m,L=n,M=p, so the
paper's tall-matrix notation N>max(L,M) does not hold directly. Using thin
bases with the actual ranks does not improve the hard case: when both
input factors have full inner rank m, both left bases in R^m span all of
R^m. Every latent leverage score is then exactly 1, tau=m, pi_i=1/m,
and the effective rank dimensions give mu=m. The advertised sufficient
sampling width is ~m/e^2, at least the original inner width for e<1.
Keeping all m terms is already exact. Thus this is not a smaller-width
algorithm for the hard dense-factor regime, even before accuracy and
explicit-output costs are counted.

Directly checkable defects in the printed argument:

(a) Lemma 10 claims

  lambda_max(tilde_Phi) <= max_i alpha_i * max_i tilde_l_i,
  tilde_Phi=sum_i alpha_i tilde_u_i^T tilde_u_i,
  alpha_i=sqrt(bar_l_i/tilde_l_i).

Take N=2,L=M=1 and U_A=U_B=(1,1)^T/sqrt(2). Then alpha_i=1,
tilde_l_i=1/2 and tilde_Phi=1. The claimed inequality is 1<=1/2.
More generally, U_A=U_B=U with orthonormal columns gives Phi=I while
the maximum leverage can be strictly below 1. The proof's equality
replacing the sum of squared row inner products by their maximum is
false. A valid immediate bound is lambda_max(tilde_Phi)<=max_i alpha_i,
since sum_i tilde_u_i^T tilde_u_i=I; it lacks the claimed leverage factor.
Consequently the variance bound used with (2.13) is not established by
the printed proof. This audit does not claim that no corrected theorem
for this sampling distribution is possible.

(b) Theorem 14 as printed gives embedding width
Theta(log(2/d)/(beta e^2)), independent of rank d_A. For any e<1 a
subspace embedding of a d_A-dimensional subspace must have at least
d_A rows, by rank. Taking d_A larger than the displayed width contradicts
the statement. The standard rank factor is absent as printed.

These defects are primary-text observations, not an appeal to citations
or an assertion about author intent. They are enough to preclude using
the printed improved claims as established comparison bounds.

2. A purported quadratic-time arbitrary-absolute-error algorithm (2014)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Shiva Manne, Manjish Pal,
Fast Approximate Matrix Multiplication by Solving Linear Systems.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.4230
https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/117/download/
Primary PDF cached transiently at /tmp/mathathon-manne-pal.pdf;
layout text at /tmp/mathathon-manne-pal.txt.

The advertised ~O(N^2) arbitrary absolute Frobenius-error guarantee is not
valid for arbitrary real inputs under the supplied construction. Section 2
reduces the unknown product C to C v=u=A(B v), for an all-equal vector v,
then Section 4 solves a regularized version of V^T V vec(C)=V^T u.
This loses all components of C orthogonal to v in each row.

For a concrete 2x2 input let

  A=[[1,-1],[0,0]], B=I_2, C=A,
  v=(1/8,1/8)^T (the paper sets v_i=N^{-3}).

Then u=C v=0. The unique solution of every positively regularized system
with this right-hand side is zero; steepest descent initialized at zero
is already at that solution. Its error from C is sqrt(2) in Frobenius
norm and sqrt(2) in spectral norm, independent of the requested accuracy.
Scaling A makes the error arbitrarily large while u remains zero.

Lemma 4 in Section 4.3 drops cross terms as though
(sum_i c_{ji})^2 >= sum_i c_{ji}^2. This is false for the first row above.
The underdetermination persists independently of this algebraic error:
regularization selects one of many possible row vectors with the same
inner product against v. Thus the claim is not an established quadratic
upper bound against which a new algorithm needs to compete.

3. Classical random-walk AMM (MFCS 2026)
---------------------------------------
Simon Apers, Arjan Cornelissen, Samson Wang,
Randomized and Quantum Approximate Matrix Multiplication.
https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2026.77
https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol386-mfcs2026/LIPIcs.MFCS.2026.77/LIPIcs.MFCS.2026.77.pdf
Full version: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08509

Definition 1 and the following paragraph restrict the study to maximum
entry error and Frobenius error. Table 1 on PDF page 3 gives the new
classical random-walk runtime, in square dimensions, as

  ~O(|| |A||B| ||_{1,1}^2 / eps^2)

for Frobenius error, plus O(N^2) preprocessing/output. Here |A| and |B|
denote entrywise absolute values and ||.||_{1,1} is the sum of absolute
entries. This is a norm of the product of the absolute-valued factors,
not ||AB||_F, and can be arbitrarily larger under cancellation. The
paper's dominance claim explicitly excludes using exact fast MM as a
subroutine. Its quantum bounds use a different computational model.
This is useful related work, but is not a product-tail spectral theorem
or a solution of the high-accuracy example in research-direction.typ.

4. Output-Frobenius sparse recovery (February 2026)
--------------------------------------------------
Yahel Uffenheimer, Omri Weinstein,
Improved Sparse Recovery for Approximate Matrix Multiplication.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04386

The abstract states that for square N x N matrices and parameter t<=N,
runtime is O(N^2(t+log N)), with a biased estimate satisfying

  E C=(t/N)AB,
  E ||C-AB||_F^2 <= (1-t/N)||AB||_F^2.

An unbiased variant has expected squared Frobenius error bounded by
(N/t)||AB||_F^2. This does depend on the output rather than factor norms,
but does not provide near-optimal rank-r spectral error. The biased
bound is not small for t<<N; for flat rank-m tails the product Frobenius
norm can be sqrt(m) times the desired absolute spectral tolerance.
Using ||E||_2<=||E||_F does not fix this dependence. This entry is an
abstract-level audit only; no claim about all stronger possible analyses
of the algorithm is made here.

5. Gram balancing and the limitations of that route
--------------------------------------------------
Piyush Sao et al., A Nuclear-Norm Lower Bound for Dithered Scalar
Quantization of Matrix Products (September 2026).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05641
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05641v1

Section 5 explicitly gives Gram-matrix balancing, and the paper states
that the infimum of ||AT||_F^2 ||T^{-1}B||_F^2 over invertible T is
||AB||_*^2 (including the appropriate infimum treatment for singular
inputs). Therefore exact inner balancing is already in this literature;
it must not be called a new AMM result.

A simple approximate-balancing observation, not claimed novel:
if (1-eta)P<=Ptilde<=(1+eta)P and likewise for Q, where P=A^T A,
Q=B B^T are positive definite, choose W=T T^T to minimize
tr(Ptilde W)+tr(Qtilde W^{-1}). Then

  ||AT||_F ||T^{-1}B||_F
    <= [(1+eta)/(1-eta)] ||AB||_*.

Indeed the minimized sketched sum is twice the nuclear norm of the
two-sided sketched product; true factor energies are at most 1/(1-eta)
times sketched energies; and embedding distortion bounds that nuclear
norm by (1+eta)||AB||_*. Constant-distortion sketches and this dense
core can be built in ~O(I+m^omega), but materializing AT,T^{-1}B costs
T_MM(n,m,m)+T_MM(m,m,p), comparable to the Gram route. Generating only
s Gaussian compressed directions avoids materialization at ~O(I s),
but standard AMM accuracy then depends on nuclear/factor energy and
needs s of order m on the flat-tail regime. This has not produced an
improvement over direct product range finding, Krylov, or fast Gram.

6. Output-aware positive-semidefinite shifts
------------------------------------------
If H is PSD and a computable Nyström underapproximation L satisfies
0<=H-L<=2 eps I, then C=L+eps I has ||H-C||_2<=eps. This is an
elementary Loewner-interval centering argument, and shifted/modified
Nyström methods are established tools; it is not claimed novel.

For an arbitrary nonsymmetric product the Hermitian dilation
[[0,AB],[B^T A^T,0]] does not automatically transfer this benefit:
an identity shift changes only its diagonal blocks. A generalization
works if a cheap known isometry J makes AB=H J with H PSD: output
(L+eps I)J. Without such a known orientation, finding the required
polar/isometric factor is an additional substantial task. No general
total-runtime gain is proved by this observation.

7. Residual deflation plus COD/sampling: a comparison that survives checking
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let U in R^{n x k}, V in R^{p x l} have orthonormal columns, set h=k+l,
and let M=AB. The familiar bilateral/tangent-space approximation is

  C0=UU^T M + M VV^T - UU^T M VV^T,
  M-C0=(I-UU^T) A B (I-VV^T), rank(C0)<=h.

Writing a_i,b_i for the original paired columns, define

  a_i^res=(I-UU^T)a_i, b_i^res=(I-VV^T)b_i,
  S=sum_i ||a_i^res||_2 ||b_i^res||_2.

The exact scalar S is computable in O(I h): compute U^T A and B V,
then use ||a_i^res||^2=||a_i||^2-||U^T a_i||^2 and the analogous
formula for b_i. No dense m x m transform needs to be materialized.
One may diagonally balance each residual pair, so the two residual
factors both have squared Frobenius norm S, then run COD at width
O(S/eps). This is a valid construction/certificate route.

It is not, by itself, a new variable-rank spectral AMM improvement.
The nuclear triangle inequality and Eckart--Young--Mirsky imply

  sum_{j>h} sigma_j(M) <= ||M-C0||_* <= S.

An ordinary Gaussian product range finder can already exploit exactly
this promise using width O((h+S/eps+1) log(1/delta)). Here is one
explicit derivation. Set q=ceil(64 S/eps)+1 and kappa=h+q, truncated
at rank(M) if necessary. Then

  sigma_{kappa+1}(M) <= eps/64,
  ||M-M_kappa||_F^2 <= eps*S/64.

For a Gaussian range finder with target kappa and oversampling
p=kappa+2, HMT (2011), Theorem 10.6, gives

  E ||(I-QQ^T)M||_2
   <= (1+sqrt(kappa/(p-1))) sigma_{kappa+1}
       + e sqrt(kappa+p)/p ||M-M_kappa||_F
   <= [(2+e sqrt(2))/64] eps < eps/10.

Markov gives failure probability below 1/10 for error eps. Concatenating
O(log(1/delta)) independent sketch blocks makes failure at most delta:
the concatenated range contains every successful block's range, and
orthogonal projection error is monotone under range enlargement. If
the target reaches the actual rank, a sufficiently wide Gaussian
sketch recovers the range exactly with probability one instead.

HMT primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.4061 . The resulting C is
QQ^T M and its width is O((h+S/eps+1)log(1/delta)); applications of M
and M^T use the supplied factors. Conventional construction is
~O(I s + (n+p)s^2) and output expansion is T_MM(n,s,p); fast blocked
products should be used when comparing exponents. Thus a linear
S/eps residual-COD width is not better than the standard range-finder
width after this nuclear-tail argument. The residual computation can
still supply an adaptive upper bound on an unknown tail parameter,
but this is not a proved novel SOTA gain.

8. Partial Gram recovery on diagonal-plus-low-rank input classes
---------------------------------------------------------------
A rigorous restricted construction is possible, but has substantial
structured-matrix prior art. Suppose P=A^T A=D+UU^T with U in R^{m x k}
and three disjoint size-k row sets J1,J2,J3 on which U has full rank.
Only 3k Gram columns plus diag(P) suffice to reconstruct all of P:

  C12=P[J1,J2]=U1 U2^T,
  C32=P[J3,J2]=U3 U2^T,
  C31=P[J3,J1]=U3 U1^T,
  H11=C12 C32^{-1} C31=U1 U1^T.

For indices outside J1, P[:,J1] equals the low-rank part L[:,J1];
inside J1, fill the block with H11. Call the resulting m x k matrix W.
Then L=W H11^{-1} W^T and D=diag(P)-diag(L). The 3k Gram columns
cost O(n m k) and the full dense reconstruction O(m^2 k+k^3).
Apply the same construction to Q=B B^T. This gives exact Gram
matrices in O(I k+m^2 k+k^3), so fast core algebra and lifting yield
optimal rank-r product error with conventional total

  ~O(I(k+r)+m^2 k+k^3+m^omega) + T_MM(n,r,p).

For constant k this meets the proposed construction scale on this
restricted class, including arbitrary close product singular values.
The pivot assumption holds for any fixed three disjoint size-k sets
when U is full spark. This is an exact-real-arithmetic observation;
no stability or conditioning guarantee is asserted.

It is not currently a novel AMM theorem. Relevant primary sources:

  P.-G. Martinsson (2011), A Fast Randomized Algorithm for Computing a
  Hierarchically Semiseparable Representation of a Matrix.
  https://doi.org/10.1137/100786617
  https://amath.colorado.edu/faculty/martinss/Pubs/2011_randomhudson.pdf

  P.-G. Martinsson (2016), Compressing Rank-Structured Matrices via
  Randomized Sampling.
  https://users.oden.utexas.edu/~pgm/Pubs/2016_martinsson_randomhudson2.pdf

  D. Halikias, A. Townsend (2024), Structured matrix recovery from
  matrix-vector products.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09841
  https://doi.org/10.1002/nla.2531

  A. Fernandez, F. Dangel, P. Hennig, F. Schneider (2025), Sketching
  Low-Rank Plus Diagonal Matrices.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23587

Diagonal-plus-low-rank Grams are special cases of hierarchical matrices
with low-rank off-diagonal blocks. Existing randomized reconstruction
therefore already gives inexpensive structured-core recovery with
matrix-vector and, for some methods, selected-entry access. Entries of
these Grams can be evaluated from the input factors and matvecs cost
O(I). The simple pivot formula is useful for accounting but does not
establish a novel improvement over all applicable methods.

9. Sample reuse in variance-reduced eigensolvers (ALT 2026)
----------------------------------------------------------
Y. Jin, I. Karmarkar, A. Sidford, J. Wang,
Reusing Samples in Variance Reduction.
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v313/jin26a.html
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v313/main/assets/jin26a/jin26a.pdf
Primary PDF/text cached transiently at /tmp/mathathon-reuse-samples.*.

Table 2 improves the full-batch/sample-query tradeoff for top eigenvector
computation of a covariance to, up to logarithms,

  sqrt(alpha/gap) full batch queries,
  sr(A) alpha^{-2} reused row samples, alpha>Theta(gap).

This is an actual recent refinement and should not be missed when
auditing stochastic alternatives. On the flat-spectrum covariance
subclass with a dense N x m factor, sr(A)=Theta(m), gap=Theta(1/m),
and I=Theta(Nm), conventional arithmetic gives

  T(alpha)=~O(I sqrt(alpha*m)+m^2 alpha^{-2}).

At N=m^{9/8}, the optimum is alpha=m^{-1/4} and T=~m^{5/2}
=~N^{20/9}, above the fast Gram bound N^{169/81}. Reusing sampled
rows may support further blocking, but no reduction below the Gram
frontier has been proved here; the sample counts cannot be silently
charged as free core operations. These are covariance-eigenvector
bounds, not yet a general two-factor product theorem.

10. Rayleigh-to-Nystrom deflation and adaptive sketch reuse
----------------------------------------------------------
Independent mathematical audit of the rank-r extension (2026-11-14):
the proposed all-eigenvalue inequality is valid. For H>=0, unit z,
s=z^T H z>0, define

  R=H-H z z^T H/s.

Then for every j<dim(H),

  lambda_j(R) <= [lambda_1(H)/s] lambda_{j+1}(H).

In particular s>=(1-e)lambda_1(H) gives the proposed factor (1-e)^-1.
There is a short proof avoiding secular-equation pole bookkeeping.
First suppose H>0. In an orthonormal basis starting with z, write

  H=[[s,b^T],[b,C]],   S=C-b b^T/s.

The residual is diag(0,S). Rank-one interlacing gives
lambda_j(S)>=lambda_{j+1}(H). Schur's determinant identity gives

  product_j lambda_j(S)/lambda_{j+1}(H)=lambda_1(H)/s.

Every factor on the left is at least one, so each factor is at most
their product. For singular H, apply the result to H+eta I and let
eta decrease to zero. The s>0 condition makes the residual continuous.
A numerical falsifier sweep over 3000 random diagonal spectra spanning
six orders of magnitude and random z found no violation; the determinant
argument supplies the proof, not this numerical check.

For M_i=M(I-V_i V_i^T), H_i=M_i M_i^T, choose such a z_i and set

  v_i=M_i^T z_i/sqrt(s_i).

Then v_i is unit and orthogonal to V_i, and right deflation produces
exactly the preceding Nystrom residual. Iterating the all-j bound gives

  ||M(I-V_r V_r^T)||^2 <= (1-e)^(-r) lambda_{r+1}(M M^T).

Taking e=1-(1+theta)^(-2/r), or a suitable constant times theta/r,
therefore gives the relative spectral rank-r guarantee. This is an
elementary extension of standard Nystrom/Schur-complement deflation;
the operation or elementary matrix lemma should not be presented as
independently certified novel mathematics.

Adaptive reuse of the initial B-side sketch is valid as well. Write
Q=B B^T, V_i=B^T C_i, D_i=B V_i=Q C_i, and R_i=I-D_i C_i^T. Then

  C_i^T Q C_i=I, R_i^2=R_i,
  B_i=R_i B, Q_i=R_i Q R_i^T=Q-D_i D_i^T.

The original Loewner event (1-h)Q<=Qtilde<=(1+h)Q implies the same
relative event for R_i Qtilde R_i^T versus Q_i for EVERY R_i, including
all adaptively selected ones; no independence assumption is needed.
For the next vector take c=R_i^T A^T z/sqrt(s), d=Q c, v=B^T c.
Orthogonality gives C_i^T Q c=0. Consequently

  R_{i+1}=R_i-d c^T=(I-d c^T)R_i,
  Qtilde_{i+1}=(I-d c^T)Qtilde_i(I-c d^T).

This is a rank-two symmetric core update and costs O(m^2). Actual B
actions compute d and v; they remain charged to input work. Whitening
of A can be fixed across all stages. The predicted rank-r cost thus is

  ~O(I+G_h+r*m^omega+r*m^2/sqrt(h)
                  +I*r*sqrt(h*r/theta)+T_MM(n,r,p)),

including the r-stage coarse eigenvalue estimates, repeated threshold
core factorizations, and explicit output. For fixed r, the previously
verified output-optimal exponent regime survives.

Related primary source checked for novelty context:
Y. Nakatsukasa, Z. Tang (May 2026), Finding accurate eigenvalues and
eigenvectors of positive semi-definite matrices given a subspace.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05043
https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05043v1
Its Theorem 1 orders Nystrom, SVD-extract and Rayleigh--Ritz eigenvalue
estimates; its setup and Section 2 explicitly treat Nystrom as a standard
single-pass extraction. It does not state the present general-factor
sketch-preconditioned total-time theorem. Failure to find the exact
all-j formula by keyword search is not evidence that this elementary
Schur-complement consequence is new.

Remaining work
--------------
The general absolute-epsilon goal remains open. Candidate algorithms
must specify the input class, rank/output form, confidence, norm and
tail parameters, setup and lifting work, precision/iteration costs,
and explicit output expansion. The counterexamples above eliminate
specific misleading frontier claims; they are not lower bounds against
the overall problem or against arbitrary factor-aware algorithms.
