Broad factor-access spectral research: reductions to major primitives
====================================================================
Independent research note, 2026-11-14. Only this dedicated file is edited.
The goal is a broad ~O(I*r+m^omega) construction with logarithmic accuracy
dependence, including the original N=m^(9/8), r=1, theta=1/m case.
This note gives precise positive conditional reductions and isolates an
actionable partial-Krylov route. It does not claim the missing primitives
have been solved or that spectral LRA automatically implies exact MM.

Main findings
-------------
1. Fast EXACT tall-Gram determinant or full raw-factor Krylov preprocessing
   would imply a genuine improvement to square matrix multiplication.
   At all tall aspects, input+core with core exponent9/4 implies11/5.
   At just the original N=m^(9/8) aspect it already implies20/9.
2. A weaker PARTIAL raw-factor Krylov theorem with cost
      ~O(I*q^(omega-2)+m*q^2+m^omega)
   could solve the original hard case at q=sqrt(m) without forcing those
   full-Krylov/MM consequences. No inspected published theorem supplies
   this raw-factor extension. Its needed polynomial linearization is explicit.
3. A rank-sensitive truncated INERTIA DECOMPOSITION is an alternate broad
   target. Any maximal negative-definite basis, not an accurate eigenbasis,
   supplies the correct dual projection. A sign-only determinant oracle
   escapes the differentiation reduction, but efficient witness extraction
   is an additional obligation.
4. Ordinary high-accuracy tall least squares and zero-tail low-rank product
   recovery already admit sketch-preconditioned input+core approaches.
   They are useful components, not by themselves the missing breakthrough.

1. Exact Gram determinant -> exact Gram -> faster square MM
----------------------------------------------------------
Let A be an arbitrary N-by-m real matrix, N>=m. Suppose a differentiation-
compatible exact field algorithm computes det(X^T X) from raw tall X in

  T(N,m)=~O(Nm+m^w), 2<w<3.

The basic assumption is a rational straight-line computation, or a
branching computation on a generic nonsingular branch where reverse
automatic differentiation applies. This is an exact magnitude computation,
not a sign test or a relative-error approximation.

Take a fresh invertible Gaussian R in R^(m by m), set X=[R;A], and put

  P=A^T A, H=R^T R+P, F=det(H)>0.

Jacobi differentiation gives the m-by-m block

  grad_R F = 2F R H^(-1).

Reverse differentiation computes all partials at constant-factor field
overhead, plus initialization/output of at most O(Nm) entries. Hence

  H^(-1)=(2F)^(-1) R^(-1) grad_R F,
  P=(H^(-1))^(-1)-R^T R

are recovered with an additional O(m^w) field operations. No tall matrix
product is performed in this recovery. R ensures full column rank even
when A is rank deficient. It also avoids exceptional branch boundaries:
for fixed A, a nonidentically-zero rational equality test in R vanishes
with probability zero. A test identically zero in R is constant on this
restricted input family. A straight-line version needs no such qualification.
For randomized branching algorithms, correct generic branches and their
derivatives must be justified; one cannot simply differentiate a failure
path. The clean arithmetic-circuit implication is already sufficient.

This is an application of Baur--Strassen, not a new differentiation
principle. It yields a useful specialized consequence for raw tall Grams.
Appending identity rows would simplify the algebra at R=I, but random R
avoids a branch-specific specialization that could have an incorrect
derivative with respect to the appended rows.

Cross-products follow by applying this Gram algorithm to the raw tall
matrix [U V]: its off-diagonal block is U^T V. Thus an m-by-N times
N-by-m product costs ~O(Nm+m^w), with no symmetry promise on that product.

Now multiply two D-by-D matrices. Split output rows and columns into
b-by-b blocks, so each block is a b-by-D times D-by-b multiplication.
The preceding algorithm gives total

  ~O((D/b)^2*(Db+b^w))
    =~O(D^3/b+D^2*b^(w-2)).

At b=D^(1/(w-1)), the exponent is

  w_new=3-1/(w-1).

For w=9/4 this equals11/5=2.2, strictly below9/4. If a primitive is
proved only for aspect N=m^kappa, the same blocking gives

  w_new(kappa)=max(3-1/kappa, 2+(w-2)/kappa).

At kappa=9/8 and w=9/4 the bound is20/9=2.2222.... Thus even that
single original tall aspect has a nontrivial MM consequence.

If the hypothetical algorithm is a MODULAR reduction to input work plus
square-MM calls, so every improved square-MM exponent can be substituted
back into it, this can be bootstrapped. For w=2+e,

  e_new=e/(1+e), so e_j=e_0/(1+j*e_0) -> 0.

It would imply omega=2 as an infimum. A black box with a permanently
hardcoded m^(9/4) term only gives the one-step11/5 consequence, not this
bootstrap. Do not conflate the two assumptions.

The same implication applies to an exact raw tall-Gram characteristic
polynomial, since it gives a determinant after appending R. It does NOT
follow from approximate determinant values: a uniformly small function
error does not control derivatives. Nor does it follow from determinant
SIGN, which is locally constant away from zero.

2. Exact full raw-factor Krylov preprocessing has the same consequence
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose an algorithm returns the exact monomial Krylov matrix

  K=[v,Hv,...,H^(m-1)v], H=X^T X,

from raw tall X in ~O(Nm+m^w), without first forming H. Use X=[R;A]
again. H has simple spectrum almost surely: its eigenvalue-discriminant
polynomial in R is not identically zero. For existence, choose a large
diagonal D with distinct entries and D-P positive definite; a real R
with R^T R=D-P gives H=D. A fresh Gaussian v then has nonzero overlap
with every eigendirection almost surely, so K is invertible.

One additional raw H action computes H^m v in O(Nm), giving

  K_shift=[Hv,...,H^m v], H=K_shift*K^(-1).

Recover P=H-R^T R in O(m^w) further work. Therefore full exact raw
Krylov at input+core implies the same rectangular and square-MM bounds
as Section1. This reduction needs no automatic differentiation and
works in the field/square-root/comparison/Gaussian model already used
by the manuscripts. An exact invariant basis together with its action
matrix has the same implication. A mere unspecified span without the
monomial iterates/action representation is a different output promise.

Neiger--Pernet--Villard2024 Theorem4.1 takes an EXPLICIT coefficient
matrix. Its fast polynomial-kernel/Frobenius preprocessing does not
automatically take raw X as that input. The reduction explains exactly
why that extension would be a major algorithmic advance.

3. Partial raw Krylov: the credible weaker target
------------------------------------------------
The parent's proposed partial target is materially weaker:

  T_partial(I,m,q)
    =~O(I*q^(w-2)+m*q^2+m^w), q<=m.

At the original input I=m^(17/8), q=sqrt(m), w=9/4, its three
exponents are9/4,2,9/4. Hence it fits the original core budget.
At q=m it instead has I*m^(1/4) and the conservative m^3 basis term;
it does not assert the input+core full-Krylov primitive of Section2.
The improvement therefore need not imply a new MM exponent.

This is a proposed theorem, not a bound established by the inspected
sources. Exact monomial vectors, maximal independent columns or a
certified enough partial range must be specified; a numerically unstable
span is not an automatically valid finite-word implementation. Raw
factor applications, coefficient preparation, rank revelation and final
physical lifting all still need charges.

Why the proposed q dependence is plausible as a research target:
once the q Krylov vectors are KNOWN, all their raw-factor products can
be batched. At the original q=sqrt(m), both N-by-m-by-q products and
their tensor permutations are input optimal under the adopted rectangular
bound. The unresolved operation is obtaining this ADAPTIVE sequence
without q sequential input passes. A rank-sensitive polynomial-kernel or
elimination method could conceivably pay q^(w-2) instead. Simply batching
already known right-hand sides does not prove that saving.

Single-covariance exact linearization
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
For H=X^T X, solve the polynomial system

  [[I_N, X],[-z X^T,I_m]] [y(z);x(z)]=[0;v].

Its Schur complement is I_m+zH, so
x(z)=(I+zH)^(-1)v=sum_j(-z)^j H^j v. Computing only its first q
coefficients is exactly the desired partial-Krylov task. Input size is
O(Nm), degree is one, the RHS has one column, and identity diagonal
blocks are explicit. Its determinant degree is at most m, despite the
larger matrix dimension N+m. A useful theorem must exploit these facts
from the raw factors; forming the Schur coefficient H costs the Gram
bound and loses the intended gain.

General product linearization: do not materialize AB
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
A raw-covariance theorem alone does not directly cover arbitrary AB.
Let P=A^T A, Q=B B^T. Ordinary physical normal-operator iterates satisfy

  (M M^T)^j A u = A(QP)^j u.

Use variables (x,w,h,y) of dimensions(m,p,m,n) and equations

  x-z B w=u,  w-B^T h=0,  h-A^T y=0,  y-Ax=0.

Equivalently, the polynomial matrix is

  [[I_m,-zB,0,0],
   [0,I_p,-B^T,0],
   [0,0,I_m,-A^T],
   [-A,0,0,I_n]].

It has O(I) raw-factor entries, degree one, and a rank-one RHS. Its
Schur complement is I-z QP. The desired Taylor coefficients are
(QP)^j u; starting with u=B*g produces the usual M-started Krylov
vectors after the physical A lift. A field-algebra proof for this sparse
identity-block linearization would directly handle the actual input model.
Another form uses X=diag(A,B^T) and the fixed exchange matrix
J=[[0,I],[I,0]]: (J X^T X)^2=diag(QP,PQ). A theorem must justify
this fixed-indefinite-mass extension rather than silently replacing X
with the unmaterialized product M.

4. Truncated inertia decomposition would imply the BROAD target
--------------------------------------------------------------
Use the established constant-sketch product support and whitening:
M=Z Bcal, G=Z^T Z~I, Q=Bcal Bcal^T. Fix a true-mass Neumann
polynomial Bmass~G^(-1) to relative accuracy O(theta); its degree is
O(log(1/theta)) and each block action uses the original factors.
The represented pencil K_t=t Bmass-Q has one fixed generalized spectrum.

Consider this precise missing primitive for such raw-factor pencils:
given t,r, in ~O(I*r+m^w) operations return either

  (a) V with r+1 columns and V^T K_t V negative definite; or
  (b) a basis V with s<=r columns, V^T K_t V negative definite,
      and K_t positive semidefinite on (K_t V)^perp.

The empty V is permitted in(b) when K_t is PSD. This is a truncated
inertia decomposition, not just a determinant value. It need only
resolve the first r+1 negative directions; it need not expose all
positive pivots or all eigenvalues. Shared mass and the original
constant Gram sketches can be reused over thresholds.

Case(a) certifies t<lambda_(r+1) of the represented product spectrum.
Case(b) certifies at most r negative eigenvalues, hence the opposite
inequality. The plain coarse coefficient ratio supplies an initial
bracket of polynomial dimension ratio. Exact bisection to relative
O(theta), with O(log(m/theta)) calls, therefore finds an upper t at
the desired tail scale without any input-condition logarithm.

The physical output in case(b) is the span Z*K_t*V. For x orthogonal
to that span, y=Z^T x satisfies V^T K_t y=0 and y^T K_t y>=0.
Consequently

  x^T M M^T x=y^T Qy
    <=t*y^T Bmass*y<=(1+O(theta))*t*||x||^2.

Combining the threshold and represented/true mass comparisons yields
the desired1+theta spectral factor after choosing the internal constant
accuracy budget. Computing K_t V and the physical lift costs ~O(I*r)
and core work. This gives the broad target plus the explicit output
charge. No eigenvector accuracy or final high-degree projection filter
is needed once this primitive exists.

Any maximal negative-definite basis is enough
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
For nonsingular symmetric K of negative index s, if V has s columns
and V^T K V<0, its K-orthogonal complement is PSD. In a basis[V X]
with V^T KX=0, the quadratic form is block diagonal; Sylvester inertia
puts all s negative directions into the V block. Thus its Euclidean
normals are K V. For s=1, ANY v with v^T K v<0 suffices, and the
one physical normal is Z K v. This is why accurate PCA eigenvectors
are an unnecessarily strong intermediate output.

Exact rank-one negative-witness extraction can also be expressed as
search for a negative LDL pivot and its transformed preimage. Full
Gaussian-congruence LDL gives v=S L^(-T)e_j and K v proportional to
S^(-T)L e_j, recovering the already audited exact-Gram baseline.
The new algorithmic burden is finding that witness from raw factors
without forming every Gram or paying for all positive pivots.

5. Sign-only determinant/inertia can escape the AD implication
-------------------------------------------------------------
Under the promise negative_index(K)<=1 and det(K)!=0,

  sign(det K)>0 iff K is positive definite,
  sign(det K)<0 iff K has one negative eigenvalue.

Thus sign of a threshold determinant is exactly a decision primitive
in that promised regime. Its local derivative is zero, so Section1's
value-to-Gram differentiation reduction does not apply. Without the
index promise, determinant sign gives only parity, not the needed count.

Decision does not yet provide a cheap negative v. Principal-block
self-reductions can use sign tests to decide where negative inertia lies,
but the Schur-complement step needs solves with positive restrictions.
Those restrictions can be arbitrarily ill-conditioned; the fact that
they are SPD does not provide the required uniform input+core solve.
Alternatively reconstructing many principal data with many sign queries
can reintroduce an input factor. A sign/inertia algorithm which returns
a negative pivot/witness at essentially its decision cost is therefore
a meaningful positive primitive to pursue. No lower bound rules it out
here, and no such raw-factor cost theorem was established by this audit.

The inference 'theta->0 spectral LRA gives exact eigendata' is invalid.
For H=diag(L,1,0), L>1, take
u=sqrt(1-1/L)*e1+L^(-1/2)*e3. Then
||(I-u u^T)H^(1/2)||=1, the optimal rank-one spectral residual, although
u is not the leading eigenvector. This holds even at zero slack. It
does not obstruct fast spectral LRA; it explains why an automatic exact
determinant/MM implication has not been justified for that weaker output.

6. Already accessible positive components
-----------------------------------------
Tall least squares with logarithmic precision
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
For raw tall X, a constant OSE plus a small-core whitening makes
G=R^(-T)X^T X R^(-1) have constant condition. Polynomial/Chebyshev
iteration then solves a positive normal system in O(log(1/epsilon))
true-factor passes in the energy norm. Setup is input+square-MM, and
r right-hand sides cost ~O(I*r+m^w) with the normal logarithms. This
is the familiar sketch-preconditioning mechanism, already reflected in
Clarkson--Woodruff's high-accuracy regression results. It does not solve
indefinite threshold systems or give all m inverse columns at rank-free
input cost. Confusing those tasks would hide the hard part.

Exact low-rank products
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The zero-tail case is likewise not the key barrier. If rank(AB)=r,
two constant OSEs produce small SA and BT^T, and the small product
C0=SABT^T has the same rank with high probability. Choose an invertible
r-by-r cross C0[I,J]. Exact CUR gives

  M=(M T_J^T)*C0[I,J]^(-1)*(S_I M).

The two lifted factors require width-r original-factor products, the
core costs m^w, and expansion is charged. This gives the target shape
at zero tail. Making such a core robust relative to a tiny nonzero
spectral tail without an accurate Gram is the unresolved problem.

7. Bounded primary-source audit and possible mechanisms
------------------------------------------------------
Baur--Strassen1983
  Walter Baur, Volker Strassen. The complexity of partial derivatives.
  Theoretical Computer Science22(3),317--330. Theorem1 and the explicit
  matrix-inversion/determinant reduction justify constant-overhead
  differentiation; the paper also discusses counting all operations.
  https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(83)90110-X
  https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/tropical/Baur-Strassen.pdf
  The tall-Gram augmentation/gradient and exponent consequences in
  Sections1--2 are this note's derivations, not statements attributed
  to that paper.

Neiger--Pernet--Villard2024
  Vincent Neiger, Clement Pernet, Gilles Villard. Computing Krylov
  iterates in the time of matrix multiplication. ISSAC2024,419--428.
  Theorem4.1 preprocesses an EXPLICIT n-by-n coefficient matrix into
  a maximal Krylov basis in O(n^w loglog(n)), or O(n^w) with sufficiently
  few starting vectors. Its polynomial-kernel input explicitly contains
  xI-A. It does not supply an implicit-Gram access theorem. Sections1--2
  show why a full input+core extension is much stronger; Section3 asks
  for only a truncated raw identity-block extension.
  https://doi.org/10.1145/3666000.3669715
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07345

Berthomieu--Neiger--Passe2026
  Jeremy Berthomieu, Vincent Neiger, Hugo Passe. Computing submatrices
  of the Hermite normal form of a structured polynomial matrix.
  ISSAC2026, DOI10.1145/3815436.3815457. Theorem1.1 takes supplied
  displacement generators, determinant/adjugate degree bounds and an
  output-row parameter. Its cost retains displacement rank and those
  degree bounds; selected inverse rows are reconstructed by structured
  evaluation/interpolation. The raw saddle identity blocks are promising
  structure, but a generator/output-sensitive q theorem is still needed.
  No constant displacement rank for arbitrary raw X is established here.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08027

Jeannerod--Pernet--Storjohann2013
  Proposition6 gives CUP cost O(N*m*s^(w-2)) for explicit rank-s input.
  This motivates a rank-sensitive rather than full-dense elimination
  target. Here s would need to measure exposed Krylov/negative structure,
  not the full algebraic rank of the raw factors, which can be m.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.5717

Clarkson--Woodruff2013/2017
  Low Rank Approximation and Regression in Input Sparsity Time.
  Their embedding/preconditioning results already reduce regression's
  epsilon dependence to logarithmic. This is evidence that positive
  high-accuracy least squares alone is not the new primitive.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.6365
  https://doi.org/10.1145/3019134

Anand--Chen2026: a useful fresh mechanism, not a subsumption
  Emile Anand, Peter Chen. The Komlos Problem in Matrix-Multiplication
  Time. arXiv2610.20995, October14,2026. Theorem1.1/Remark1.2 gives
  input+square-MM complexity for signing. It replaces a tiny-gap
  top-eigenvalue potential with a resolvent potential and reuses an
  anchor subspace over a batch with rank-one inverse updates. Its
  controlled Gram is PSD AND entrywise nonnegative, with monotonic row
  barriers; arbitrary product pencils lack that promise. Smooth resolvent
  certificates plus cached subspaces are worth examining, but this is
  not a generic factored-product eigensolver theorem.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20995

8. Concrete next proof objectives
---------------------------------
Highest priority: prove a q-sensitive polynomial-kernel or truncated
elimination bound for the explicit raw identity-block linearization in
Section3, initially at q<=sqrt(m) and one RHS. It would resolve the
original exponent target without requiring exact all-spectrum extraction.
Charge raw-factor products rather than pretending the Schur coefficient
is free, and test whether low polynomial output order can replace full
coefficient dimension in the elimination cost.

Parallel longer-term target: a truncated inertia decomposition for the
shared-mass signed pencil, returning arbitrary negative witnesses and
their dual normals. A sign-only determinant scheme with witness-aware
self-reduction could avoid both the exact-value/MM implication and the
unnecessary demand for accurate leading eigenvectors. The raw-access
cost and positive-Schur-solve subproblem are the actual missing theorem.

The exact determinant/full-Krylov consequences are useful ambition
checks: if a proposed method claims those stronger outputs at input+core,
it must either deliver the corresponding MM improvement or identify an
extra promise that invalidates the reduction. The partial-Krylov and
low-index-witness objectives deliberately ask for less and remain
plausible routes to a broad spectral result. None is solved in this note.

9. Actual Anand--Chen mechanism: a transferable lemma and its limits
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Read Proposition2.1 and AppendixB of arXiv2610.20995, rather than only
the abstract. Their general-use ingredient is metric stability of a
cached curvature certificate. The following own lemma isolates a part
which DOES NOT require entrywise nonnegative factors.

Let M0=Mfixed+Gamma*Gamma^T<bI, A0=bI-M0, R0=A0^(-1), with
Mfixed fixed during a batch. For a diagonal weight perturbation Delta,
assume ||Delta||<=a and

  ||R0^(1/2)*Gamma||^2<=L.

Then, without any entrywise-sign condition,

  ||R0^(1/2)*(Gamma*Delta*Gamma^T)*R0^(1/2)||<=aL.

If aL<1 and Mx=M0+Gamma*Delta*Gamma^T, it follows that

  (1-aL)A0 <= bI-Mx <= (1+aL)A0,
  R0/(1+aL) <= (bI-Mx)^(-1) <= R0/(1-aL).

This is an immediate congruence/operator-norm proof, valid for arbitrary
signed Gamma. It is the right generic replacement for an entrywise
monotonicity assertion when all changes are relative PSD-factor weights.

A generic cached-curvature corollary also needs no entrywise signs.
Suppose P0,D0>=0 and a fixed E satisfies P0|E<=(1-rho)D0|E.
If within a window

  Px|E <= (1+eta)P0|E+eta D0|E,
  Dx|E >= (1-eta)D0|E,

then for eta<=rho/6,

  (Px-Dx)|E <= [-rho+(3-rho)eta]D0|E <=-(rho/2)D0|E.

Thus one anchor subspace remains valid throughout that window. The paper
proves analogous stronger drift estimates for its particular weights,
gradients and negative diagonal barrier curvature. A future factor-access
method could reuse this principle if it supplies its OWN leverage cap
and relative drift bounds. It is a positive reusable lemma, not a generic
PCA algorithm extracted from a discrepancy theorem.

What does not transfer for free
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
For Gamma containing all of M0, the metric cap can be as large as
lambda_max(M0)/(b-lambda_max(M0)). Anand--Chen freezes heavy rows and
uses load counting/spread to keep the remaining cap polylogarithmic
while losing few dimensions. Those bounds exploit nonnegative coefficient
vectors, a positive resolvent probe, and the special barrier mass budget.
Arbitrary covariance rows aligned with a near-barrier direction do not
have that polylogarithmic cap. A new leverage/count or relative-curvature
invariant is required; merely replacing entries by absolute values can
destroy the product geometry.

Tiny-row retirement is also task-specific. N identical raw rows v/sqrt(N)
have individual squared norm ||v||^2/N, arbitrarily small, while their
sum Gram is v v^T. Discarding all such rows destroys an order-one spectral
direction. The paper controls separate row discrepancies, where its
retirement error has a different meaning; it does not certify this
covariance deletion.

The Gaussian completion sampler and rank-one row-replacement inverse
maintenance are geometric and can operate in an arbitrary fixed feasible
subspace. They efficiently generate many constrained directions AFTER
such a subspace is known. They do not themselves discover a negative
subspace from raw A,B or remove the cost of computing each true factor
action. Reusing a certificate across a batch is the promising transferable
mechanism; obtaining a large valid trust window at raw input cost is
the exact missing lemma.
