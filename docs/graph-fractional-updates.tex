\begin{abstract}
We combine intrinsic-error matrix-function compression with shifted
Laplacian solvers. After $k$ edge-weight changes in an undirected graph,
the correction to its fractional Laplacian has a width
$O_\alpha(k\log^2(2k/\varepsilon))$ approximation at operator error
$\varepsilon\norm{\Delta L}^\alpha$. Construction takes expected
$\widetilde O_\alpha((m+n)k)$ arithmetic operations, including the
factors, without a spectral-gap assumption. Edge deletions, cancellation,
and changes of connected components are allowed. A separate realization
theorem transfers every positive rank-one fractional matrix correction,
in the limit, to one edge-weight increase on a connected path with
positive vertex masses. Consequently these paths already attain the
exact worst-case leading compression-rank constant from the companion
matrix theorem. The solver, resolvent identity, and Jacobi-to-path
mechanism are established tools; the claims here concern their precise
intrinsic-error synthesis and the transfer of sharp matrix counts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Graph model and algorithmic statement}
An undirected weighted graph has nonnegative conductances and Laplacian
$L=\sum_e w_e b_e b_e^T$, where $b_{uv}=e_u-e_v$.
Let $L'$ be a second graph on the same $n$ vertices, obtained by changing
$k\ge1$ distinct non-loop edge conductances by nonzero amounts $d_e$.
All final conductances must remain nonnegative. Let $m$ count the edges
in the union of the two graphs, and put
\[
 E=L'-L=UJU^T,\qquad
 U_e=\sqrt{|d_e|}b_e,\qquad J=\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{sign}d_e).
\]
Norms without a subscript are operator norms. Fix $0<\alpha<1$.
Fractional powers assign value zero to the zero eigenspace.

\begin{theorem}[Sparse construction]\label{thm:algorithm}
For $0<\varepsilon,\delta<1/2$, there is a randomized algorithm returning
$P,Q\in\R^{n\times\ell}$ such that
\[
 \ell=O_\alpha\bigl(k\log^2(2k/\varepsilon)\bigr),\qquad
 \Pr\{\norm{PQ^T-(L'^\alpha-L^\alpha)}
              \le\varepsilon\norm E^\alpha\}\ge1-\delta.
\]
Its expected construction cost is $\widetilde O_\alpha((m+n)k)$.
Suppressed factors are logarithmic in $n,k,1/\varepsilon,1/\delta$.
It uses shifted SDD solves in the two
endpoint graphs, sparse edge arithmetic, and scalar quadrature.
An exactly symmetric signed factor $Z\Sigma Z^T$ is available with
width at most $2\ell$ and the same asymptotic cost.
\end{theorem}

The output is the \emph{correction}; no initial dense $L^\alpha$ is
needed to construct it. Recovering the full updated fractional Laplacian
also requires its base value. Expanding the correction adds
$O(n^2\ell)$ arithmetic operations. For fixed $k$ and fixed tolerance,
expansion costs $O(n^2)$; for inverse-polynomial tolerance the total
expected cost is $\widetilde O(n^2)$ when $m=O(n^2)$.
These statements concern graph matrix functions, not arbitrary matrix
multiplication.

Costs use exact-real arithmetic and the standard expected-time SDD
solver theorem~\cite{KMP}. For general fixed $\alpha$, scalar power
evaluations at the quadrature nodes are additionally counted as scalar
operations. For $\alpha=1/2$, the nodes and weights can instead be
constructed, to the stated error budget, using arithmetic, comparisons,
positive square roots, and convergent series for fixed constants.
No finite-word stability bound is asserted.

\section{Intrinsic scaling and two endpoint solves}
Merge repeated edits to the same edge before defining $k$ and $U$.
The scale $a=\norm E_F$ and the factor size $b=\norm U_F^2$ are computable
in $O(n+k)$ work from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scale}
 a^2=\sum_v\left(\sum_{e\ni v}d_e\right)^2+2\sum_e d_e^2,
 \qquad b=2\sum_e|d_e|.
\end{equation}
If there are no remaining edits, return zero. Otherwise $a>0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cancellation}
 \norm E\le a\le\sqrt{k}\norm E,\qquad
 1\le\chi:=b/a\le\sqrt{2k}.
\end{equation}
The first upper bound uses $\rank E\le k$; the second follows by
Cauchy--Schwarz and the off-diagonal sum in~\eqref{eq:scale}.
Thus mixed additions and deletions cannot hide arbitrary cancellation
in the supplied edge representation.

For $t>0$, define
\[
 R_t=(L+tI)^{-1},\quad S_t=(L'+tI)^{-1},\quad
 V_t=R_tU,\quad W_t=S_tU.
\]
The resolvent identity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twoendpoint}
 D_t:=R_t-S_t=R_t E S_t=V_t J W_t^T,
 \qquad \norm{D_t}\le\min(2/t,a/t^2).
\end{equation}
No small inverse, Gram matrix, or update QR is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Inexact solves]\label{lem:solve}
Suppose each column solve for $V_t,W_t$ has relative energy error at
most $\eta\le1$. The resulting blocks satisfy
\[
 \norm{\widehat V_tJ\widehat W_t^T-D_t}
          \le 3\eta b/t^2.
\]
The approximate solve maps need not be linear or symmetric.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $A=L+tI$ and a right-hand side $u$, let $x=A^{-1}u$ and assume
$\norm{\widehat x-x}_A\le\eta\norm{x}_A$. Since $A\succeq tI$,
\[
 \norm{\widehat x-x}_2^2
 \le\eta^2t^{-1}u^TA^{-1}u\le\eta^2\norm u_2^2/t^2.
\]
Summing over the columns gives
$\norm{\widehat V-V}_F,\norm{\widehat W-W}_F\le\eta\sqrt b/t$,
while $\norm V_F,\norm W_F\le\sqrt b/t$.
Expand the product difference and use $\norm J=1$ to obtain
$(2\eta+\eta^2)b/t^2\le3\eta b/t^2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Quadrature, error, and full cost}
Put $\beta=1-\alpha$ and $c_\alpha=\sin(\pi\alpha)/\pi$.
Scalar integration followed by functional calculus gives the norm-convergent
identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integral}
 L'^\alpha-L^\alpha=c_\alpha\int_0^\infty t^\alpha D_t\,dt.
\end{equation}
After $t=ae^y$ the integrand is
$F(y)=c_\alpha a^{\alpha+1}e^{(\alpha+1)y}D_{ae^y}$.
For every fixed $d<\pi$, the inequality
$|\lambda+te^{i\theta}|\ge\cos(d/2)(\lambda+t)$ for
$\lambda,t\ge0$, $|\theta|\le d$, proves analyticity and the bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 \norm{F(x+i\theta)}\le C_{\alpha,d}a^\alpha
                 \min(e^{\alpha x},e^{-\beta x}).
\end{equation}
This is the same uniform envelope as in the companion matrix
analysis~\cite{Companion}; in particular no spectral endpoint enters.

Here are sufficient quadrature parameters, with constants depending
only on $\alpha$. Fix a sufficiently small $0<\rho<1/2$.
Use an infinite logarithmic trapezoid rule with
$h=\Theta_\alpha(1/\log(1/\rho))$, small enough, and retain a consecutive
finite interval of nodes $t_j=ae^{jh}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tails}
 s_{\min}:=t_{\min}/a\asymp_\alpha\rho^{1/\alpha},\qquad
 s_{\max}:=t_{\max}/a\asymp_\alpha\rho^{-1/\beta}.
\end{equation}
The constants in the cutoffs are chosen to bound both omitted tails
by $\rho a^\alpha$. The infinite trapezoid error is bounded by
$C_\alpha a^\alpha/(e^{2\pi d/h}-1)$, taking for example $d=\pi/2$.
This follows by shifting the Fourier integral to the two strip edges
in~\eqref{eq:envelope} and summing its nonzero sampled frequencies;
the edge integrals are bounded by
$C_\alpha a^\alpha(1/\alpha+1/\beta)$.
Thus the exact-node quadrature has error $O_\alpha(\rho a^\alpha)$,
positive weights
$\omega_j=c_\alpha h t_j^{\alpha+1}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 q=O_\alpha(\log^2(1/\rho)).
\end{equation}

Lemma~\ref{lem:solve} bounds the accumulated solve error by
\begin{align*}
 \sum_j\omega_j\norm{\widehat V_jJ\widehat W_j^T-D_{t_j}}
 &\le C_\alpha\eta b h\sum_jt_j^{\alpha-1}\\
 &\le C'_\alpha\eta\chi a^\alpha s_{\min}^{-\beta}.
\end{align*}
The last inequality is a geometric-series bound, since $\beta>0$.
Choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budgets}
 \rho=\frac{\varepsilon}{C_\alpha(k+1)},\qquad
 \eta=c_\alpha'\chi^{-1}\rho^{1/\alpha},
\end{equation}
where the first constant is large enough and the second small enough.
Equations~\eqref{eq:tails}--\eqref{eq:budgets} give solve error
$O_\alpha(\rho a^\alpha)$. Together with~\eqref{eq:cancellation}, all
errors are at most $\varepsilon\norm E^\alpha$.
Small relative errors in the positive weights can be included in this
budget: $\sum_j\omega_j\norm{D_{t_j}}=O_\alpha(a^\alpha)$.

The returned factors concatenate
$P_j=\sqrt{\omega_j}\widehat V_jJ$ and
$Q_j=\sqrt{\omega_j}\widehat W_j$. Each block has $k$ columns.
There are $2kq$ column solves and $O(nkq)$ factor arithmetic.
The energy-accuracy SDD theorem~\cite[Theorem 4.6, Lemmas 4.3 and 4.5]{KMP}
applies because $L+tI$ and $L'+tI$ are SDD positive definite, including
disconnected graphs (process their connected components separately;
isolated vertices give scalar diagonal solves). To obtain arbitrary
confidence, run each column solve independently
$s=O(\log(2kq/\delta))$ times at tolerance $\eta/3$, with constant
success probability greater than $1/2$. Choose $s$ odd and select the
candidate with the smallest median pairwise $A$-energy distance
(including the zero self-distance),
where $A$ is that shifted system. On the event that a strict majority
have error at most $r=(\eta/3)\norm{A^{-1}u}_A$, every good candidate
has median distance at most $2r$. The selected candidate is therefore
within $2r$ of a strict majority, which intersects the good majority;
its error is at most $3r$. A binomial tail bounds failure by
$\delta/(2kq)$. All distances are computable by sparse quadratic
forms; their $O((m+n)s^2)$ cost per column is logarithmic overhead.
A union bound now makes all solves accurate simultaneously.
The cited runtime is \emph{expected}, with logarithmic accuracy and
confidence overhead; this proves the runtime stated in
Theorem~\ref{thm:algorithm}, not a deterministic runtime cap.

For an exactly symmetric output replace each approximate node by its
symmetric part, which cannot increase error to the symmetric target.
The identity
\[
 \frac{VJW^T+WJV^T}{2}
 =[V+W,V-W]\begin{pmatrix}J/4&0\\0&-J/4\end{pmatrix}[V+W,V-W]^T
\]
gives a signed factor without dense $k$-by-$k$ factorization.
For $\alpha=1/2$, choose $h=\log(2)/H$ with dyadic integer
$H=\Theta(\log(1/\rho))$; nodes $a2^{j/H}$ and their powers then use
repeated square roots. Integer doubling chooses the cutoffs and precision
budgets. Fixed constants can be evaluated to the needed accuracy by
convergent scalar series. This supplies the stated ordinary-arithmetic
version of the theorem.

\section{Sharp matrix complexity already occurs on paths}
Positive vertex masses are natural for generalized graph eigenproblems.
For conductance Laplacian $K$ and positive diagonal mass matrix $M$, write
$H=M^{-1/2}KM^{-1/2}$. An edge increase of conductance $w$ is the
rank-one update $wM^{-1/2}b_e b_e^TM^{-1/2}$. Masses are held fixed
within each update. This is broader than the combinatorial convention
$M=I$, or the convention $M=\operatorname{diag}(K)$.

Define $\mathcal N_{\rm mat}(\varepsilon)$ as the supremum of
\[
 \#\{j:\sigma_j((A+uu^T)^\alpha-A^\alpha)>\varepsilon\}
\]
over all finite PSD matrices $A$ and unit vectors $u$.
Define $\mathcal N_{\rm path}(\varepsilon)$ similarly over connected
weighted paths with positive masses, one edge conductance increase,
and update operator norm one in mass-normalized coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Exact path transfer]\label{thm:path}
For every $\varepsilon>0$,
$\mathcal N_{\rm path}(\varepsilon)=\mathcal N_{\rm mat}(\varepsilon)$.
Consequently, using the exact PSD count theorem in~\cite{Companion},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \frac{\mathcal N_{\rm path}(\varepsilon)}{\log^2(1/\varepsilon)}
       =\frac1{4\pi^2\alpha(1-\alpha)}.
\end{equation}
For $r$ fixed, connected mass-weighted paths with $r$ edge increases
already attain the universal rank-$r$ leading coefficient
$r/[4\pi^2\alpha(1-\alpha)]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The upper comparison is immediate because every graph pair belongs to
the matrix class. For the reverse inequality first take $A\succ0$.
Restrict to the cyclic subspace generated by $u$; on its orthogonal
complement the correction is zero. Orthogonal Lanczos, with alternating
basis signs, produces a real SPD tridiagonal matrix $T$ with strictly
negative off-diagonal entries and $u=e_1$. Complex input data, if allowed,
give the same real model by diagonalizing $A$ and removing coordinate
phases of $u$ first. Lanczos breakdown simply terminates the cyclic block.

Put $d=T^{-1}e_1$. All entries of $d$ are strictly positive. To check
this, choose $s\ge\lambda_{\max}(T)$ and write $T=sI-B$ with
$B\ge0$, $\norm B<s$; irreducibility makes the geometric inverse series
strictly positive entrywise. With $D=\operatorname{diag}(d)$,
\[
 K_0=DTD,\qquad K_0\mathbf1=d_1 e_1.
\]
Hence $K_0$ is a path conductance Laplacian grounded at vertex $1$,
with ground edge conductance $d_1$. Give its internal vertices masses
$d_i^2$ and the ground vertex mass $R^2$. The full connected path has
mass-normalized Laplacian
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathmatrix}
 H_R=\begin{pmatrix}d_1/R^2&-e_1^T/R\\-e_1/R&T\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Increasing the ground edge conductance by $d_1^2$ adds $v_Rv_R^T$,
where $v_R=(-d_1/R,e_1)^T$ and
$\norm{v_Rv_R^T}=1+d_1^2/R^2$.
Scale \emph{both} endpoint conductance sets by
$c_R=(1+d_1^2/R^2)^{-1}$. The update now has norm exactly one, and
\[
 c_RH_R\longrightarrow0\oplus T,\qquad
 c_R(H_R+v_Rv_R^T)\longrightarrow0\oplus(T+e_1e_1^T).
\]
Continuity of finite-dimensional fractional functional calculus and of
singular values transfers every \emph{strict} threshold count to all
sufficiently large finite $R$.

For singular $A$, first replace it by $A+\tau I$ and let $\tau\downarrow0$;
the same strict-margin argument applies. Thus every finite matrix count
is bounded by the supremal path count. This proves equality, including
thresholds at which some other singular values equal $\varepsilon$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:sharp} now follows from the companion PSD theorem.

For the last assertion, take $r$ disjoint copies of a norm-one update
witness. Their update vectors are orthogonal, so the total update still
has norm one and rank $r$. Concatenate the paths by arbitrarily small
positive endpoint conductances, identically at both endpoints of the
update. Continuity preserves the required strict counts, and the graph
is one connected path. The universal rank-$r$ upper theorem from
\cite{Companion} gives the matching leading coefficient.
\end{proof}

In particular the coefficient for square roots is $1/\pi^2$ per edge.
The exact path theorem is a statement about suprema over all dimensions
and all positive masses; the examples depend on $\varepsilon$. It does
not assert an asymptotic for each fixed graph or establish the same
sharp coefficient for paths with unit masses.

The sparse algorithm extends to fixed positive masses by solving
$(K+tM)x=M^{1/2}u$ and transforming back. Relative energy error is
preserved under this congruence. The intrinsic scale is the Frobenius
norm of the normalized update, while $b$ is the squared Frobenius norm
of its normalized edge factor. The general bound includes logarithmic
dependence on $\chi=b/a$, which need not be bounded by $\sqrt{2k}$
for arbitrary masses. No such additional ratio occurs in
Theorem~\ref{thm:algorithm} for unit masses.

\section{Scope and relation to existing results}
Low-rank rational matrix-function updates, including graph applications,
are established~\cite{BCKS}; fractional graph diffusion and singularity
treatment are studied in~\cite{BS}. Nearly-linear shifted SDD solvers
are supplied by~\cite{KMP}. Jacobi operators and weighted paths with
positive vertex weights have classical correspondences~\cite{CEM}.
The two-endpoint factorization in~\eqref{eq:twoendpoint} is simply the
resolvent identity. These mechanisms are not claimed as new.

The specific deductions proved here are the cancellation-controlled,
intrinsic-error sparse construction for signed edge edits and the exact
supremal fractional-count transfer to one-edge path updates. The sharp
coefficient depends on the companion matrix theorem; its proof is not
repeated here. A bounded literature comparison has not established
priority of these precise conclusions. No general quadratic-time matrix
product algorithm follows. The imported \texttt{spectral-gap} project's
exact mixed permutation moments offer a further sketching direction,
but this manuscript proves no new permutation-sketch concentration bound.
