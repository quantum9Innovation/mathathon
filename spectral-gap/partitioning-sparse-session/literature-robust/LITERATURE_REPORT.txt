Current primary-literature boundary for sparse robust spectral partitioning
Read date: 2026-11-15. Research note, not a novelty certificate.

1. Results that must not be advertised as new targets

Mohanty, Raghavendra, Wu (STOC 2024),
Robust recovery for stochastic block models, simplified and generalized.
https://arxiv.org/html/2402.13921v1
Inspected: introduction, Sections 2, 4, 5 main statements, and Assumption
6.1 / Theorem 6.2 / Algorithm 6.3 and associated localization argument.
Their algorithm handles delta*n arbitrary edge insertions/deletions for
some delta>0 depending on every fixed common-degree SBM above KS, with
any fixed number of communities. The matrix is a boosted Bethe Hessian,
A^(ell) H(t) A^(ell), after constant degree truncation; ell is constant
in n but deteriorates near KS. General sparse robust-PCA Theorem 6.2
assumes the clean symmetric matrix has at most r negative eigenvalues,
row/column absolute sums <=K, and corruption supported on Q x Q with
|Q|<=gamma*n and row/column sums <=K. It outputs an O(rK/v)-dimensional
incoherent subspace preserving a constant projection of every C-flat
unit vector with clean quadratic form <-v. Algorithm 6.3 removes one
coordinate at a time and recomputes a negative spectral projector. The
theorem asserts efficient, not nearly-linear, work. This is not ordinary
low-rank-plus-sparse RPCA: the clean matrix can be sparse and full rank.

Stephan and Zhu (COLT 2025), Community detection with the Bethe-Hessian.
Current version: https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02835v3 (4 Nov 2026).
Inspected: introduction, model definition, main result descriptions and
comparison to MRW. The model has a common expected degree across blocks.
The paper proves exact negative-outlier counting for d>=2, eigenvector
recovery for sufficiently large d, and consistency as d tends to infinity.
Fresh later papers below supersede its two small-degree limitations.

Dong and McKenzie (17 Aug 2026),
The Bethe-Hessian down to the Percolation Threshold.
https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16672v1
Inspected: abstract and main result/model statement.
Exact negative-outlier count is extended to every fixed common expected
degree d>1. Two ingredients are test vectors on the 2-core followed by
controlled extension, and an isotropic Markov-random-field basis adapted
to each informative planted eigenvalue. Proposing d in (1,2) counting as
open would now be wrong.

Dong and McKenzie (30 Oct 2026),
Bethe Hessian Recovery through the Kesten-Stigum Regime.
https://arxiv.org/html/2610.41802v1
Inspected: introduction, Theorems 2.2 and 2.3, Green-function recursion,
and transfer-operator setup, including Proposition 4.8.
For fixed common expected degree d>1, the negative outliers of
H(+/-sqrt(d)) converge to deterministic locations -delta(|mu|), with
delta strictly increasing. Each informative planted eigenspace has
nonvanishing projection on its corresponding Bethe-Hessian eigenspace.
For a simple mu, the normalized overlap converges to positive q_mu.
Randomized polynomial-time rounding achieves weak recovery whenever
some nonconstant |mu|>sqrt(d). This explicitly concerns the raw Hermitian
Bethe Hessians, unlike MRW's boosted robust algorithm. Hence ordinary
small-degree BH recovery at KS is already claimed in current primary
literature. The new ingredient uses Green-function-weighted tree fields
with vanishing eigenvector residual; merely counting inertia was weaker.
This report inspects the theorem, not an independent audit of its proof.

Ding, d'Orsi, Hua, Steurer (COLT 2023),
Reaching Kesten-Stigum Threshold in the Stochastic Block Model under
Node Corruptions.
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v195/hua23a/hua23a.pdf
Inspected: Theorem 4 and nearby scope/necessity discussion.
For symmetric two-community SBM at SNR=1+delta, their polynomial-time
algorithm tolerates a sufficiently small constant fraction mu_delta of
arbitrarily corrupted nodes. The robustness allowance depends on delta,
and this dependence is necessary. This stronger corruption model also
must not be presented as an untouched robust-KS problem.

Banks, Mohanty, Raghavendra (SODA 2021),
Local Statistics, Semidefinite Programming, and Community Detection.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01960
https://sidhanthm.com/pdf/loc-stats.pdf
Inspected: abstract/introduction/main theorem overview, not full proof.
Constant levels of Local Statistics SDP detect SBM and their Degree
Regular Block Model arbitrarily near KS and tolerate a linear number of
adversarial edge edits. For DRBM they also show constant-level failure
below KS. Thus regular-model robust detection near KS is not itself new.

2. Parent-requested neighboring sources and their actual scope

Bhaskara et al. (NeurIPS 2024), On the Robustness of Spectral Algorithms
for Semirandom Stochastic Block Models.
https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14315v1
Inspected: models, Theorems 1--3, open problems, main proof sketch.
Theorem 2 allows arbitrary within-cluster graphs and later within-cluster
edge additions. It needs d_in>=C(nq/2+sqrt(n)) and expected-Laplacian
gap at least sqrt(n)+C*nq+C(sqrt(nq log n)+log n), hence does not settle
constant-degree sparse recovery. Theorem 3 gives a nested-block family
with constant error for normalized Laplacian despite exact recovery for
unnormalized Laplacian. Open questions in Section 2.4 include eliminating
the cap on internal edge probabilities, lowering the degree/gap demand,
and allowing crossing-edge deletions. These are questions as stated in
that paper, not guarantees that no later paper solved them.

Hai-Xiao Wang (22 Sept 2026), Concentration of Regularized Sparse Random
Matrices: Spectral Edge Bounds via Nonbacktracking Operators.
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26615v1
Inspected: Sections 1.1--1.4 main hypotheses and statements, Proposition
1.15, and Section 4 future directions; not the 72-page proof.
This treats regularized inhomogeneous matrices built from independent
entries. The main sparse range includes d>=C and d^6(log N)^3<=N^(1-eps).
NB-radius statements use upper cutoff separation 5sqrt(C0*d log d),
and give additive O(d^(-1/2)) or O(sqrt(log d/d)) errors in normalized
units. Rectangular smallest-singular-value bounds require d>=c log N.
These results handle dependencies induced by regularization, not general
adaptive adversarial edge edits. Their error does not establish a sharp
fixed-degree universal-cover edge. Listed extensions concern non-Hermitian
thresholding and sharp hypergraph norm bounds.

3. A precise substantial target left after this boundary check

Candidate theorem: nearly-linear sparse robust spectral recovery.
This is a proposed research target, not a proved theorem and not a
certified open problem. The inspected papers do not already imply it.

Fix constants C,K,v>0 and r=O(1). Let M be a symmetric n x n matrix with
at most r negative eigenvalues, each row absolute sum <=K. Suppose
M_tilde=M+Delta has O(n) stored nonzeros, Delta supported on Q x Q,
|Q|<=gamma*n, each Delta row absolute sum <=K, gamma sufficiently small
depending only on C,K,v. Given only M_tilde and the constants, output
an orthonormal basis for U in O(nnz(M_tilde)*polylog(n)) arithmetic work
such that dim U=O(rK/v), diag(P_U)<=O(C^2 K^3 r/(v^3 n)), and every
C-flat clean unit witness y with y^T M y<-v has y^T P_U y>=c*v/K.
Use one consistent definition of C-flat (||y||_infinity<=C/sqrt(n));
MRW's prose alternates squared and unsquared C conventions.

With appropriate fixed-parameter constants and parameter knowledge,
this would supply nearly-linear robust community recovery from MRW's
constant-radius boosted sparse matrix. The crucial advance is fast
robust spectral cleanup, not claiming a new KS threshold. An input-size
bound must count the corrupted graph's edges before truncation if the
adversary is allowed to supply many of them.

Why existing ingredients do not prove the candidate: one-coordinate
spectral deletion can take Theta(n) rounds; recomputing a full projector
each time is not near-linear. A fast method would need a batched or
amortized negative spectral-mass filter, with deletion charge O(gamma n),
and must keep the witnesses and incoherence on the same success event.
Chebyshev approximations with spectral slack might estimate negative
mass, but bounding total adaptive work and preserving flat witnesses is
an unresolved step here. Ordinary fast low-rank RPCA assumes a different
clean-signal structure and cannot simply be cited for this problem.

4. A second potentially substantial quantitative question

For two-community SBM at SNR=1+kappa, determine the optimal adversarial
edge-edit robustness budget delta_*(kappa), or even its sharp order as
kappa tends to zero, for nontrivial limiting overlap. Define the order of
limits (n first, then kappa), overlap requirement, fixed degree, and edge
budget convention. Existing results inspected above establish positive
delta(kappa), not a sharp asymptotic formula. A coupling that moves model
parameters to the subcritical region gives a natural upper obstruction,
but matching algorithms and constants are not supplied here. Do not call
this problem new without checking quantitative recent literature. The
2025/2026 'Rate-optimal ... near KS' paper arXiv:2511.16613 concerns a
different regime: C=(sqrt(pn)-sqrt(qn))^2>=K*k^2*log k and minimax small
misclassification, and so its title alone does not settle kappa down to0.

5. Caution on per-eigenvector localization filters

An individual eigenvector's IPR is basis-dependent under repeated or
nearly repeated eigenvalues. Many distant copies of the same finite
tangle yield a degenerate localized eigenspace whose chosen orthonormal
eigenbasis may mix the copies. As the number of copies grows, IPR can
vanish while the entire spectral subspace still lives on o(n) vertices.
A robust theorem should therefore use projector leverage or uniform
subspace delocalization, and specify the n-scale: IPR=o(1) alone is too
weak. This is elementary linear algebra, not a novel obstruction.

No historical-firstness claim, new full theorem, or independent proof
audit is made by this literature note.
