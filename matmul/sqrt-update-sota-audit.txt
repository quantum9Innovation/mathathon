SQUARE-ROOT UPDATE: NOVELTY AND STATE-OF-THE-ART AUDIT
====================================================
Audit date: 2026-11-14/15. Owner: sqrt_update_sota.
Scope: docs/nongalactic-wildcard-search.txt, Sections 1--6.
This file is an internal technical comparison, not a priority certificate.
No external communication, publication, repository commit, or job was made.

VERDICT
-------
The proposed end-to-end theorem is a credible precise algorithmic statement
that is not stated in any primary algorithm paper inspected in this audit.
That bounded finding DOES NOT establish that it is new, nor that it improves
the best algorithm obtainable by composing known results.

Almost all of its mechanisms have clear antecedents: fractional-power
resolvent integrals and root-exponential low-rank approximability; rational
matrix-function updates; positive shifted inverses; Woodbury; and randomized
sketch-and-precondition solvers. An even Neumann truncation giving a
one-sided inverse error is elementary polynomial algebra. The current
evidence supports a useful synthesis and possibly a publishable theorem in
a specialist numerical-linear-algebra/algorithms paper. It does not support
calling the mechanism seminal or a general dense square-root breakthrough.

The plausible contribution to isolate is the complete RAW-FACTOR complexity
and output theorem: an actual PSD factor for the PRINCIPAL correction,
additive error on the update's Holder scale, singular endpoints allowed,
and only dimension/accuracy/failure logarithms in the arithmetic count.
The separate sharp lower bound and exact compression constant may provide
much stronger novelty than this algorithmic assembly. Their priority is
outside this file's scope.

One correction is essential when describing prior work: BCKS2021 Algorithm 3
does NOT algorithmically need a supplied base square root. It computes the
correction using A, the perturbation factors, and shifted solves. The
absence of a base-root input distinguishes this construction from the
Riccati formulation of SDA2023/2024, not from all matrix-function update
algorithms.

Likewise, positivity of the mathematical approximant is not exclusive to
the candidate. In the Hermitian PSD-update specialization, BCKS2021 Eq.(4)
has the core sqrt(Q^THQ+Q^TUU^TQ)-sqrt(Q^THQ), which is already PSD by
operator monotonicity. The direct positive node factors are a convenient
way to realize the candidate in its restricted arithmetic model; they are
not the first positive square-root correction approximation.

1. THE EXACT CLAIM AND THE MODEL
-------------------------------
Input: X in R^(N x m), U in R^(N x r), 1<=r<=m<=N; eps,delta in (0,1).
Set H=XX^T and

  Delta=(H+UU^T)^(1/2)-H^(1/2).

Output: a real N-by-K matrix Z with K=O(r log^2(r/eps)) and

  ||ZZ^T-Delta||_2 <= eps ||U||_2

with probability at least 1-delta. Claimed conventional arithmetic:

  ~O(Nmr+m^3),

where only logarithms of dimensions, 1/eps, 1/delta are suppressed.
There is no dependence on a positive spectral gap, nonzero condition
number, ||H||/||UU^T||, or a precomputed root/eigenbasis. Arithmetic and
positive scalar square roots are exact; no finite-word stability theorem
is asserted. Forming a dense N-by-N answer additionally costs O(N^2 K).

This is a factor of Delta, which is PSD by operator monotonicity. It is
neither a Cholesky factor of H+UU^T nor a low-rank approximation to its
entire principal square root. If the old root is not already available,
computing Z alone does not make application of the entire new root cheap.

The normalization is weaker than eps||Delta||_2. For example, with a very
large scalar base H=cI, ||Delta|| can be far below ||U||. Returning zero
then satisfies the stated tolerance. This does not invalidate the theorem;
it determines which comparisons and applications are relevant.

2. PRIMARY SOURCES AND WHAT WAS ACTUALLY CHECKED
----------------------------------------------
[BKS75] Birman, Koplienko, Solomyak, Estimates of the spectrum of a
difference of fractional powers of selfadjoint operators, 1975, pp.3--10.
Record: https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/ivm6330
Original: https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=ivm&option_lang=eng&paperid=6330&what=fullt

Freshly inspected the original Section 3, Lemma 3, (3.2), (3.9)--(3.13),
and the preceding integral formula (2.3). The rank-m^2 approximant in
(3.10) has error C exp(-c m); constants are independent of A,B and the
lower integration endpoint after scaling. The construction uses spectral
projectors of B. It is not a raw-factor operation-count theorem or the
proposed positive quadrature implementation. Truncating the small-t
piece gives the uniform rank-one root-exponential upper consequence.
Thus logarithm-squared rank existence is old; applying the proof to a
rank-r integrand gives the corresponding block extension. This last
extension is an inference from its proof, not an explicitly named theorem.

[BV00] Daniel S. Bernstein, Charles F. Van Loan, Rational matrix functions
and rank-1 updates, SIMAX 22(1), 145--154 (2000), Theorem 3 and Algorithm 1.
https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/blogs.cornell.edu/dist/c/9924/files/2021/10/fRank1.pdf
https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895479898333636

Read the full ten-page article. Rational degree-q updates have rank at
most q. Its advertised O(qN^2) update count assumes the old rational
matrix function and an appropriate denominator factorization are already
available. Those costs cannot be omitted in a raw-input comparison.

[BKS18] Beckermann, Kressner, Schweitzer, Low-rank updates of matrix
functions, SIMAX 39(1), 539--565 (2018); preprint July 2017.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03045
https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1140108

Inspected algorithms, cost accounting, exactness, and convergence sections,
including Section 2.1 and its explicit O(Ns^2) orthogonalization and O(s^3)
core costs for rank one. It projects the correction using polynomial
Krylov spaces. An earlier quadrature-plus-Sherman--Morrison approach is
already discussed in its Introduction. It offers no printed raw-factor,
update-scale, condition-independent logarithmic-accuracy theorem matching
the candidate. Its bounds should not be read as iteration lower bounds.

[BCKS21] Beckermann, Cortinovis, Kressner, Schweitzer, Low-rank updates
of matrix functions II: Rational Krylov methods, SINUM 59(3),1325--1347
(2021); preprint August 2020.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.11501
https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1362553

Read Algorithms 1--3, Theorems 3.3,3.5,4.5,4.6, and Example 4.8. The
algorithm uses shifted solves, not f(A) as numerical input. Theorem 4.5
bounds the spectral error by four times a rational uniform-approximation
error on a set containing both spectra. Example 4.8 treats square root
through z times an inverse-square-root Markov function and pays a spectral
endpoint factor. Section 3.3 explicitly identifies rational-function
replacement plus generalized Sherman--Morrison as an alternative.

[SDA24] Shmueli, Drineas, Avron, Low-rank updates of matrix square roots,
NLAA 31(1),e2528 (2024), first published August 9,2023.
https://doi.org/10.1002/nla.2528
Final article: https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10443098
Preprint v3: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13156

Read the final article, especially Problem 1, Theorem 1, Corollary 1,
Algorithm 1, Sections 2.2,5.1,5.5,7. It returns a PSD correction factor
and is direct relevant prior art. Theorems assume SPD endpoints; decay is
relative to correction singular values with a conditioning parameter.
The displayed Riccati cost assumes O(1) trust-region iterations at each
rank increase and root/inverse-root actions. Residual-to-forward-error
bounds are supplied; a uniform logarithmic-eps runtime is not proved.
Final Theorem 1 is preprint Theorem 3, and final Corollary 1 is preprint
Corollary 7. The experiments explicitly do not report running times.

[FHL23] Fasi, Higham, Liu, Computing the square root of a low-rank
perturbation of the scaled identity matrix, SIMAX44(1),156--174 (2023).
https://eprints.maths.manchester.ac.uk/2842/1/fhl22.pdf
https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1471559

Read the formula and cost sections. For alpha I+UV*, they obtain an exact
rank-r correction using an r-by-r root, including singular V*U. This is
stronger than approximation in that special base case. It does not solve
the arbitrary-base problem. Retaining its formula as correction factors
gives O(Nr^2+r^3) work, apart from small-root accuracy; this factor-only
cost is our inference. Its Table4.1 charges dense output too, adding
O(N^2r). It must not be misquoted as a printed factor-only runtime.

[NN13] Nelson, Nguyen, OSNAP: Faster numerical linear algebra algorithms
via sparser subspace embeddings, FOCS 2013, Theorem 9.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.1002

Inspected the full embedding statement, including delta. It supplies a
constant-distortion sketch with O(m log^8(m/delta)) rows and
O(log^3(m/delta)) nonzeros per column, for fixed distortion. Applying it
and forming its m-by-m Gram costs ~O(Nm+m^3), using ordinary arithmetic.
There is no new embedding theorem in the candidate.

[MSM14] Meng, Saunders, Mahoney, LSRN, SISC 36(2),C95--C118 (2014).
https://web.stanford.edu/group/SOL/papers/lsrn-sisc-2014.pdf
https://doi.org/10.1137/120866580

Read Algorithms 1--3, Theorems 4.4--4.5, Sections 4.4 and 5. Predictable
logarithmic-accuracy convergence independent of the original conditioning,
including rank deficiency and Tikhonov regularization, is established.
LSRN's dense Gaussian sketch itself costs O(Nm^2), not ~O(Nm).
Its literal preprocessing therefore does not match the candidate for
r<<m. Sparse/fast OSE substitution is the established route to the
candidate's cheaper preprocessing. LSRN alone is not a matching theorem.

[PK23] Persson, Kressner, Randomized low-rank approximation of monotone
matrix functions, SIMAX 44(2),894--918 (2023).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.11023
https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1523923

Read Algorithm 1, Theorem 3.12 and Section 3.4.3. funNystrom applies f
to a Nystrom approximation of one PSD matrix using its actions only;
it does not require f(A) actions. It covers singular matrices and gives
operator-norm and other norm guarantees. The spectral-norm theorem is an
expectation bound with a noted route to deviation bounds. Its target is
the entire f(A), not the difference of two roots.

[PMM25] Persson, Meyer, Musco, Algorithm-agnostic low-rank approximation
of operator monotone matrix functions, SIMAX 46(1),1--21 (2025).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14023
https://doi.org/10.1137/23M1619435

Read Theorems 1.1--1.2, Corollary 1.3, and Theorem 2.7 of the July2024
preprint. Near-optimality for an approximation of A transfers to f(A).
The spectral-norm transfer is condition-free but depends on the best
rank-k error of f(A). It is not a low-rank-update theorem for two large,
nearly equal roots.

[PCM26] Persson, Chen, Musco, Randomized block-Krylov subspace methods
for low-rank approximation of matrix functions, LAA741,32--65,July15,2026.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01888
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2026.03.029

Read the latest arXiv v2 (November26,2025), Algorithms 1--4, Section 3
error theorems and cost discussion. It analyzes Krylov-aware approximation
of f(A) for symmetric A. Algorithm 4 uses d=(s+t)ell matrix-vector
products and O(Nd^2) additional orthogonalization, plus a d-dimensional
matrix-function calculation. Its strongest general statements are
Frobenius-norm bounds involving polynomial approximation and spectral
information. This recent primary source does not state the candidate
principal-correction guarantee. A new scalar function of H alone cannot
represent Delta for arbitrary noncommuting U.

[P20] Pleiss et al., Fast Matrix Square Roots with Applications to
Gaussian Processes and Bayesian Optimization, NeurIPS2020, Theorem 1
and Section 3.4.
https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/fcf55a303b71b84d326fb1d06e332a26-Paper.pdf

Inspected the quadrature, shifted-solve theorem and preconditioning
discussion. This computes root/inverse-root actions. The theorem depends
on kappa. Its preconditioned output is equivalent up to an orthogonal
rotation, which is suitable for sampling but cannot simply be subtracted
from a second principal-root action to obtain Delta. It is not a matching
correction-factor theorem.

4. A PROVABLE CONCRETE BASELINE AND THE ACTUAL SPEEDUP
----------------------------------------------------
The following is our derivation, using exact small-core solves. It gives
a valid comparator under precisely the same model and error target.

1. Form G=X^TX, W=X^TU: O(Nm^2+Nmr).
2. Use the candidate's q=O(log^2(r/eps)) positive quadrature poles/weights.
3. At each t, Cholesky-factor G+tI; compute

     V=t^(-1)[U-X(G+tI)^(-1)W],
     S=I+U^TV,
     D=VS^(-1)V^T.

   Return concatenated sqrt(weight)*V*chol(S)^(-T) factors.
4. The same quadrature proof gives the exact same target and width.

Every resolvent is exact. Per pole the ordinary cost is
O(m^3+m^2r+Nmr+Nr^2+r^3), so the full cost is

  O(Nm^2+q[m^3+Nmr]) = ~O(Nm^2+m^3).

Thus the candidate replaces the long-dimension Gram cost Nm^2 by Nmr.
This improvement is mathematically proved relative to this baseline, with
no claimed lower bound on all previous methods. The nonsuppressed ratio is

  (Nm^2+m^3)/(Nmr+m^3).

Its maximal order is m/r. When Nr >=~ m^2 the candidate approaches the
Nmr-dominated regime and can gain order m/r. When N=Theta(m), both counts
are cubic in m. With m=N the candidate is ~O(N^3), not a subcubic dense
square-root algorithm. With r=m there is no order improvement. These facts
must remain visible in any broad statement of importance.

If an eigendecomposition or a suitable exact thin basis for H has already
been computed, shifted resolvent actions can be applied cheaply without
the candidate's randomized iteration. The candidate's benefit is a
FROM-SCRATCH guarantee for raw factors, not a lower bound on amortized
update work after arbitrary preprocessing.

For a diagonal base, direct diagonal resolvents give the same quadrature
construction in ~O(Nr^2+r^3), without a sketch or m^3 core. This is a useful
corollary, but quadrature-plus-Woodbury is established machinery. No
priority claim for that corollary is justified by this audit.

5. WHY SOME APPARENT COMPETITORS DO NOT IMMEDIATELY MATCH
--------------------------------------------------------
(a) Approximating both roots separately does not preserve the update's
small rank. Take H=I_N and U=u of rank1. Delta has rank1 exactly, while
each root has N singular values at least1. Approximating either root in
spectral norm below1 needs rankN. Therefore a whole-function randomized
LRA theorem cannot simply be invoked twice with K=polylog(1/eps).
This is our elementary example, not a limitation claimed by those papers.

(b) A covariance factor can be updated trivially by concatenating [X,U].
That factor satisfies LL^T=H+UU^T but does not give the principal-root
correction. Cholesky up-/downdates and SVD tracking often concern this
different output and must be labeled accordingly.

(c) The candidate cannot be compared to a correction-relative guarantee
as if the normalizations were interchangeable. A finite rigorous
obstruction to dimension-independent relative-rank bounds is available.
For any n, set d_i=R^i (i=0,...,n-1), P=diag(d_i), H=P^2,
u_i=sqrt(2d_i), and U=sqrt(tau)u. Let L solve PL+LP=uu^T. Then

  L_ij=2sqrt(d_i d_j)/(d_i+d_j), L_ii=1,
  sum_(j!=i) |L_ij| <=4/(sqrt(R)-1).

Choose R>=289, so 3I/4 <= L <=5I/4. For
D_tau=(P^2+tau uu^T)^(1/2)-P, the Riccati identity gives

  tau L-D_tau = integral_0^infinity e^(-sP) D_tau^2 e^(-sP) ds >=0.

Consequently ||D_tau||<=5tau/4 and

  ||tau L-D_tau|| <= ||D_tau||^2/2 <=25tau^2/32.

For tau=1/10, lambda_min(D_tau)>=0.0671875 and
lambda_max(D_tau)<=0.125, hence lambda_min/||D_tau||>=0.5375.
Every rank<n approximation therefore has relative spectral error >1/2.
The update rank is1, the base is SPD, and n is arbitrary. The candidate's
eps||U|| tolerance can be much larger here, so there is no contradiction.
This proves that removing conditioning at a fixed relative correction
error would require a genuinely different qualification.

(d) Existing rational approximation can already tolerate a zero endpoint
when approximation is normalized by a large full spectral scale; the
stronger issue here is uniformity in the ratio between the base and the
update scales. Do not describe every rational method as requiring an
inverse of H or a nonzero gap. Those are properties of specific methods
and bounds, not the entire class.

6. THE STANDARD-COMPOSITION NOVELTY HAZARD
-----------------------------------------
Here is the short route a knowledgeable referee can reconstruct:

* Fractional powers have the Stieltjes resolvent-difference integral.
  Its tails are controlled by min(1/t,||UU^T||/t^2). Analytic trapezoid/sinc
  quadrature yields logarithm-squared nodes independent of the base.
* Each node is a rank-r PSD Woodbury correction.
* A constant-distortion OSE gives a Gram proxy G0. Adding tI preserves
  the same spectral equivalence for EVERY positive shift t.
* A standard preconditioned polynomial approximates (G+tI)^(-1) in
  O(log(1/eta)) iterations independent of the original conditioning.
* Choosing its degree even gives Q* - Q_l >=0. The Woodbury physical
  inverse then has a PSD error with norm at most eta/t.
* A crude node perturbation estimate and eta=poly(eps/r) complete the
  same raw-factor arithmetic count.

No single inspected paper states this exact package. However, the package
is assembled from old tools, and most of the above deductions are short.
The burden is therefore to demonstrate a nonroutine theorem or a genuinely
uncovered application regime, not to assert novelty from a missing search
hit. The rank-order upper bound cannot serve that role because of BKS75.
An elementary one-sided Neumann lemma alone also does not seem sufficient.

The candidate's Section 5 does supply a useful explicit missing detail:
operator-norm propagation through low-rank Woodbury factors with small
quadrature shifts. In combination with the positivity construction it
closes the end-to-end proof. Whether that closure constitutes enough
novelty for a standalone algorithm paper remains a judgment question;
the current evidence does not justify promising that it does.

7. OPEN AUDIT ITEMS AND DEFENSIBLE PRESENTATION
----------------------------------------------
Recommended precise description:

  "A fully costed randomized construction of a PSD factor for a
   square-root correction from raw rectangular factors, with additive
   update-scale spectral error and no spectral conditioning parameter.
   It combines classical resolvent compression with randomized
   preconditioning. The rank dependence has classical upper-bound
   antecedents; the exact raw-factor theorem's priority remains under
   review."

Do not claim "the first low-rank square-root update," "first logarithmic
accuracy," "first condition-free low-rank upper," "faster than SOTA" in
general, "optimal running time," or "stable in floating point."

Remaining substantive questions:

1. Has the same end-to-end raw-factor/update-scale theorem been stated in
   a paper not located here, or is it a short explicit corollary already
   recognized in the matrix-function/randomized-solver literature?
2. Can the sharper worst-case rank lower theorem and exact leading
   compression constant be transferred to an actual constructive factor
   algorithm with controlled raw costs? Current Sections1--6 only prove
   O(log^2), not the optimal leading coefficient.
3. Is there a useful downstream application that specifically needs the
   principal correction at eps||U|| error? Sampling alone often needs only
   a rotated covariance factor, for which cheaper old methods suffice.
4. Can a stable finite-word implementation keep the same practical
   complexity? The expression t^(-1)(U-XQX^TU) can involve cancellation.
   This is outside the current exact-arithmetic claim, not a discovered
   logical defect in that claim.
5. Are the actual polylogarithmic constants modest? The cited OSNAP
   theorem has log^8 row overhead. A full asymptotic conventional count
   does not by itself imply a practical timing advantage.

Searches covered old rational updates, 2017/2018 polynomial updates,
2020/2021 rational updates, 2023/2024 square-root Riccati algorithms,
scaled-identity formulas, randomized matrix-function LRA through a
July2026 journal paper, and standard randomized preconditioning. A
2025 Oxford thesis on Lanczos/quadrature updates and 2026 sketched
matrix-function-action papers also surfaced, but were not used as
evidence for a same-target theorem. No global exhaustive-priority claim
can be supported by this finite audit.
