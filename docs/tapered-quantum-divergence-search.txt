Tapered positive quadrature and the Faust--Fawzi rate question
===========================================================
Coordinator working record, 2026-11-14. Not a novelty certificate.

This is a NEW APPLICATION DIRECTION, separate from the exact Schatten
constant manuscript. It does not settle the original N^2 matrix-product
problem. The full fixed-prefactor Faust--Fawzi conjecture is NOT proved.
The logarithmic-rate consequence below follows from the Gaussian sampling
lemma already independently audited in the Holder work. Whether that
consequence improves the current literature remains to be checked.

Primary starting point
---------------------
Oisin Faust and Hamza Fawzi, Rational approximations of operator monotone
and operator convex functions, arXiv:2305.12405 (2023),
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.12405 .

Use their notation

 f_a(x)=[x^a-a(x-1)-1]/[a(a-1)],

with continuous limits at a=0,1. For 1<=a<b<=2 their epsilon^[m]_(a,b)
is the best relative error |f_a-fhat|/f_b, where

 fhat(x)=sum_(j=1)^m u_j (x-1)^2/[1+t_j(x-1)],
 u_j>=0, 0<=t_j<=1.

Theorem4 proves a bound with leading exponent
pi*sqrt(2a(b-a)m/b) and a polynomial prefactor. Conjecture1 asks for
a fixed-prefactor bound with leading exponent 2pi*sqrt(a(b-a)m/b).
The following gives the conjectured LEADING LOGARITHMIC RATE, with
an unbounded subleading term. These statements must not be conflated.

Derivation of a uniform analytic envelope
----------------------------------------
Their Section4.2 represents

 f_a(x)=(x-1)^2/Z_a * integral_R
       exp((2-a)y)/[(1+exp(y))^2(1+x exp(y))] dy,
 Z_a=a(a-1)pi/sin((a-1)pi),

with Z_1=1 by continuity. Normalize this integrand by f_b(x).
Their Lemma6 gives, for every x,t>0,

 min(1,t^(2-b))/[3(1+xt)] <= f_b(x)/(x-1)^2.

The b=2 endpoint also satisfies this inequality directly because
f_2(x)/(x-1)^2=1/2. At x=1 use the continuous ratio.

For y>=0, this implies the normalized integrand is at most
(3/Z_a)exp(-a y). For y<=0, it is at most
(3/Z_a)exp((b-a)y). For complex z=y+iv, |v|<=d<pi, use

 |1+c exp(z)|>=cos(d/2)[1+c exp(y)], c>0.

Thus the normalized analytic function has the uniform strip envelope

 |F_x(y+iv)|<=C_(a,b,d)
          min(exp((b-a)y),exp(-a y)).

It holds uniformly over EVERY x>0, not on a fixed compact x interval.
It also remains valid at a=1 and b=2.

Apply the positive Gaussian sampling lemma
-----------------------------------------
The audited lemma (nongalactic-wildcard-search.txt Section27;
exact-holder-limit-independent-audit.txt) applies to an analytic strip
with left tail exponent b-a, right tail exponent a, and width pi.
For error exp(-L), it returns positive scalar sampling weights and nodes,
independent of x, with count, for each fixed d<pi,

 m(L)<= b*L^2/[4pi*d*a(b-a)] + O_(a,b,d)(L^(7/4)).

Each sampled term is a positive multiple of
(x-1)^2/[1+t(x-1)], t=exp(y)/(1+exp(y)) in (0,1).
Therefore it is exactly an allowed positive quadrature rule, not an
unconstrained rational approximant outside Faust--Fawzi's feasible class.

Inverting the node count, and then taking d up to pi, gives

 limsup_(m->infty) log(epsilon^[m]_(a,b))/sqrt(m)
       <= -2pi*sqrt[a(b-a)/b].

For every fixed eta>0 this implies an eventual bound

 epsilon^[m]_(a,b)<=C_(a,b,eta)
   exp[-(2pi*sqrt(a(b-a)/b)-eta)*sqrt(m)].

One may keep a fixed strip d<pi to state the explicit subleading bound
exp[-2sqrt(pi*d*a(b-a)/b)*sqrt(m)+O_(a,b,d)(m^(3/8))].
Passing d->pi in an O(m^(3/8)) estimate requires tracking its constants;
do NOT silently write the endpoint exponent plus a uniform O(m^(3/8))
remainder without that additional argument.

In particular, a=1,b=2 reaches every leading rate below pi*sqrt(2),
compared with the pi coefficient in the cited Theorem4. Their Theorem5
relates this approximation to semidefinite representations for quantum
relative entropy. No full complexity claim or fixed-C conjecture is
being asserted from this working note alone.
