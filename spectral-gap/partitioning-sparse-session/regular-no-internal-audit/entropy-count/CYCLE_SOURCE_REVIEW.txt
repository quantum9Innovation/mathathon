1. Precisely audited experiment and target
-----------------------------------------
Fix known k>=3 and N>=1. The base is the named simple cycle C_k. Sample
one independent uniform perfect matching for every base edge, then apply
an independent uniform permutation of all n=kN vertices. Reveal only the
final labeled graph G. The target is the UNORDERED fiber partition Pi.

Equivalently choose a uniform balanced named projection
  f:[n]->Z/kZ, |f^(-1)(i)|=N,
then independent uniform edge matchings. There are n!/(N!)^k such maps
and (N!)^k possible matching tuples conditional on each. A global
shuffle gives exactly this uniform map law. Its remaining within-fiber
coordinate permutations preserve the product of uniform matching laws,
so no gauge-dependent bias is hidden in this equivalence.

The statistical claim is the exact Bayes-optimal success under this
experiment and the optimal randomized minimax success when the unknown
parameter is a balanced NAMED map f, with the unordered fibers as target.
The source does not claim a pointwise impossibility for every estimator
at one specially chosen fixed partition; a hardcoded estimator can of
course succeed at that partition. This distinction is correctly explicit.

2. Monodromy, components and local cover-map count
------------------------------------------------
Transport coordinates along k-1 successive base edges, making their
matchings identity maps. The remaining edge becomes the composition of
the k original edge permutations, with inverses if an orientation is
reversed. This composition is uniform on S_N: conditional on all but
one factor, multiplication by the remaining uniform factor is a
bijection. A monodromy cycle of length ell produces exactly one graph
component C_(k*ell). Thus component count R equals the cycle count of
a uniform permutation. The global vertex shuffle changes none of this.

Every component C_(k*ell) has exactly2k locally bijective maps to the
named base C_k. Choose one vertex image in k ways and which neighbor
maps forward in two ways. Local bijectivity forces that direction to
continue: reversal would map both distinct neighbors to the same base
neighbor. The map closes because k divides its length. Every fiber
appears exactly ell times in that component.

This count is correct for k=3: its two base neighbors are still distinct,
and a reversal still violates local bijectivity. It is also correct for
k=4, where the base's bipartition does not create additional locally
bijective maps. For k>=3 all resulting component cycles are simple;
the excluded k=1,2 models are not silently treated as simple cycles.

3. One GLOBAL automorphism quotient, including repeated components
------------------------------------------------------------------
Independent choices on R observed components give(2k)^R NAMED covering
maps, and every such map is globally balanced: summing their per-component
ell contributions gives N in each fiber.

Two surjective maps f,g give the same unordered partition exactly when
g=sigma o f for one global permutation sigma of the k base labels. Since
every base edge is hit by a lift edge under f, and g is also a cover map,
sigma preserves every base edge. It is an automorphism of C_k. Conversely
every global base automorphism preserves the unordered partition.
Aut(C_k) has order2k, including Aut(C_3)=S_3 of order6. Its action on
surjective maps is free, so every orbit has exactly2k maps. Hence
  M(G)=(2k)^(R-1).

The quotient is ONE global factor2k, not one per component. Independent
phases and orientations on different components usually give genuinely
different fiber partitions of the observed vertex set. Equal component
lengths do not introduce any further quotient: the observed vertices
are labeled, and swapping components can change the target partition.
Graph automorphisms therefore do not divide the posterior's partition
count. The source handles all these distinctions correctly.

4. Exact likelihood and uniform posterior
----------------------------------------
The uniform prior probability of a named balanced f is(N!)^k/n!.
Given compatible(f,G), every base-edge matching is determined uniquely
by the G edges between the two corresponding named fibers. Distinct
base edges are distinct unordered fiber pairs for every simple C_k,
including C_3, so this determination has no multiplicity. Its conditional
likelihood is(N!)^(-k), and therefore
  P(f,G)=1/n!.
As a direct count, the experiment has exactly n! equally likely pairs
(named map,matching tuple), and each such pair determines its(f,G).

For a compatible unordered Pi, exactly2k named maps are compatible with
the particular observed G. Summing their equal joint probabilities gives
  P(Pi,G)=2k/n!,
independent of Pi. Consequently the posterior is uniform on its M(G)
possible partitions and
  P(G)=(2k)^R/n!.
No graph-automorphism division belongs in either of these probabilities.

An unordered balanced partition has k! possible base labelings a priori.
For k>=4, only2k of them need be compatible with this G; the others can
give a different cyclic order of its blocks. The source's named-map
calculation correctly accounts for that latent-order mixture. Replacing
the compatible factor2k by k! would be incorrect, especially at k=4.

5. Exact success, gamma expression and asymptotic
------------------------------------------------
Uniform posterior gives conditional Bayes success
  max_Pi P(Pi|G)=1/M(G)=(2k)^(1-R).
Choosing any compatible partition attains it. For uniform permutation
cycle count R_N, inserting element N either as a singleton or after an
existing element gives the exact recurrence
  F_N(t)=(t+N-1)F_(N-1)(t), F_0(t)=1.
Therefore
  E[t^R_N]=t(t+1)...(t+N-1)/N!
          =Gamma(N+t)/[Gamma(t)Gamma(N+1)].
Setting t=1/(2k) proves
  P_(k,N)=2k Gamma(N+1/(2k)) /
                  [Gamma(1/(2k))Gamma(N+1)].
The fixed-k gamma ratio gives
  P_(k,N)~[2k/Gamma(1/(2k))]N^(-1+1/(2k)),
which tends to zero. There is no computational restriction in this bound.

I also independently checked the graph-probability normalization. If
m_ell counts components of size k*ell, the number of such LABELED graphs
is
  n! / product_ell[(2k*ell)^(m_ell)*m_ell!].
Multiplying by(2k)^R/n! gives
  1/product_ell[ell^(m_ell)*m_ell!],
exactly the uniform-permutation cycle-histogram probability. Here the
cycle symmetry factors count how many labeled graphs have the given
histogram; they are not an extra quotient on partitions of a fixed G.
This separate calculation confirms both the component law and the
absence of a missing repeated-component factor in the posterior.

6. Randomized minimax assertion
-------------------------------
For any estimator, its worst-case success over balanced named maps f
is at most its average under their uniform prior, hence at most the
Bayes optimum P_(k,N). This holds also for randomized estimators.

Conversely sample a compatible unordered partition uniformly given G.
For EVERY fixed true f and every compatible observation, conditional
success over the estimator's own randomization is exactly1/M(G).
For each fixed f the matching monodromy is uniform on S_N, so its
unconditional success equals the same P_(k,N). This is an equalizer
rule and attains the upper bound, proving optimal randomized minimax
success with the exact parameterization stated in the source.

Uniform sampling can fix a canonical component's phase and direction,
then choose each remaining component's phase and direction independently
among2k possibilities. Every global Aut(C_k) orbit has exactly one
representative normalized this way because its action on that first
component's2k maps is free and transitive. Thus the sampling procedure
is genuinely uniform on unordered partitions, not merely on some maps.
For R=1 it produces the unique partition.

7. Connected conditioning and known-base qualification
------------------------------------------------------
The unconditioned lift is connected exactly when monodromy has one cycle.
There are(N-1)! such permutations, so its probability is1/N, including
the N=1 case. At R=1 the posterior support size is1, and the supplied
base C_k determines the unique fiber partition: take residues modulo k
around the observed connected cycle. Changing starting point or direction
only applies a global base automorphism and does not change that unordered
partition. Conditional connected recovery is therefore perfect.

This conditioning changes the model and cannot be dropped from that
positive boundary statement. Likewise knowing k matters: an observed
connected cycle can cover other simple cycle bases whose orders divide
its length. The source explicitly makes no uniqueness or identification
claim across such unknown bases. No scope fix is needed, provided these
qualifications survive in any restatement.

Final assessment
----------------
The frozen actual source proves the claimed exact Bayes and randomized
minimax formula for blind unordered-fiber recovery in the unconditioned,
globally shuffled random N-lift of a supplied simple C_k, fixed k>=3.
Its probability tends to zero, while conditioning connected gives perfect
recovery for known k. The distinction between those statistical models,
and between Bayes/minimax and one hardcoded fixed partition, is already
correctly present. No correction to the frozen source is required.

The comparison with degree>=3 recovery must retain the same blind,
unconditioned, fixed-known-or-bounded-base setting. This review asserts
no result for growing base size, noisy observations, hidden coordinate
recovery, a deck group, arbitrary deterministic covers, or historical
firstness. Historical priority remains a separate question.
