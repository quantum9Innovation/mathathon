Outcome

The claimed bound follows from the displayed argument and the published exact
support inclusion for the first trace correction. I find no missing dependence
on the number of independent permutations, on the coefficient dimension, or
on the base size. The graph consequence applies to every deterministic
connected loopless multigraph, with a numerical constant independent of that
graph. This is an independent mathematical reconstruction, not a formal proof
certificate or a determination of historical novelty.

The result under review is

  P(||P(S_N)|| >= rho+epsilon)
       <= C (D/N) (K/epsilon)^9,

where P is a self-adjoint linear polynomial with D by D matrix coefficients,
S_N consists of any finite number r of independent uniform permutations
restricted to constants orthogonal vectors, rho is the reduced free norm,
and K is the full unitary norm. The same conclusion holds at epsilon=K-rho
by continuity of decreasing events. Thresholds above K have probability zero.
No claim of control for coefficients chosen after observing the permutations
is included.

1. Common denominator and numerator degree

Fix a scalar test polynomial h of degree at most q. Expanding the normalized
matrix coefficient trace of h(P) gives a finite scalar linear combination
of words of length at most q. Its number of terms and the sizes of individual
word coefficients are irrelevant, because interpolation uses the norm bound
on the complete h(P), not a triangle bound on that word expansion.

In one connected graph pattern let e_c be the number of distinct edges of
color c, and let e=sum_c e_c <=q. The multiplicity of 1-jx in its denominator
is #{c:e_c>=j+1}, which is at most floor(q/(j+1)). Thus a common denominator

  g(x)=product_(j=1)^(q-1) (1-jx)^floor(q/(j+1))

works simultaneously for every word, independently of r. Its degree G obeys

  G <= q sum_(k=2)^q 1/k <=q log q.

The numerator of the pattern before clearing this common denominator has
degree (e-v+1)+(v-1)=e. Its original denominator degree is

  sum_c max(e_c-1,0)=e-c_used.

After multiplication by the missing factors the numerator degree is exactly
G+c_used, and hence at most G+q. No bound on r has entered. The removed
invariant vector contributes -xg(x), of degree G+1. The constant word has
normalized trace 1-x and is covered as well. Therefore the numerator and
denominator degrees are at most B=q(1+log q). For q=1 the denominator is
one and the numerator has degree at most one, so there is no small degree
exception.

The functions obtained with different q agree at infinitely many reciprocal
integers N, so they are the same rational function. Consequently their Taylor
coefficients define a consistent linear functional on all scalar polynomials.

2. Interpolation, with explicit constants independent of r and D

Put p=ceil(B), M=ceil(32B^2), and H=||h|| on [-K,K]. Since B>=1,

  p<=2B,  32B^2<=M<=33B^2,  M>=8p^2.

For integer L>=M the exact rational trace formula is valid since M>=q.
The normalized trace is divided by DL, while the actual dimension is
D(L-1), so its absolute value is at most H. As 0<g(1/L)<=1, the numerator
f obeys |f(1/L)|<=H. Its limit at zero obeys the same bound.

The reciprocal grid has gap at most M^(-2). On I=[0,1/M], Markov gives

  ||f'||_I <=2p^2 M ||f||_I.

At a point attaining ||f||, compare with a grid point at distance at most
M^(-2). This gives

  ||f|| <=H+(2p^2/M)||f|| <=H+||f||/4,

and hence ||f||<=2H. This also applies to a complex scalar polynomial, by
applying the real Markov inequality after rotating its value or derivative
to the positive real direction.

On the same interval, every jx<=1/2 and

  -log g(x)<=2x sum_j j floor(q/(j+1))<=2qB/M<=1.

Thus g^(-1)<=e. Writing ell=log g gives

  ||ell'||<=2B^2,   ||ell''||<=4B^3.

The polynomial derivative rate is at most

  2p^2M<=264B^4.

Therefore

  ||f'||<=528B^4 H,
  ||f''||<=139392B^8 H.

For Psi=f exp(-ell), use

  Psi'=exp(-ell)(f'-f ell'),
  Psi''=exp(-ell)[f''-2f'ell'+f((ell')^2-ell'')].

The numerical bounds above imply, conservatively,

  ||Psi'||<=2^11 B^4 H,
  ||Psi''||<=2^19 B^8 H.

For N>=M Taylor's theorem now gives the second order remainder with an
absolute numerical constant. For N<M, the expected normalized trace is
still bounded by H even when the rational formula is unavailable. Also
|nu_0(h)|<=H and |nu_1(h)|<=2^11 B^4H. Hence the remainder multiplied by
N^2 is at most

  2N^2H+2^11 B^4NH
      <=2178B^4H+67584B^6H <=2^17B^8H.

Thus the proof's all N estimate is valid with, for example,

  |E Tr h(X_N)/(DN)-nu_0(h)-nu_1(h)/N|
       <=2^20 B^8 H/N^2.

These constants do not involve r, D, coefficient sums, or base graph data.

3. Smooth extension and Fourier estimate

For f(theta)=h(K cos theta) with cosine coefficients a_j, the polynomial
estimate applied to T_j(x/K) gives a remainder controlled by

  (2^20/N^2) sum_(j>=1) j^8(1+log j)^8 |a_j|.

Cauchy and Schwarz give

  sum_(j>=1) j^8(1+log j)^8 |a_j|
  <= [sum_(j>=1)(1+log j)^16/j^2]^(1/2)
     [sum_(j>=1)j^18|a_j|^2]^(1/2).

The first series is an absolute numerical constant. For an explicit coarse
bound, divide the integers into dyadic blocks. The series is at most

  sum_(k>=0) 2^(-k)(k+2)^16 <2^18 16!.

The second factor is the L2 norm of f's ninth derivative divided by sqrt(pi),
under the stated cosine normalization. This proves the required smooth
remainder with an absolute constant.

There is no hidden continuity assumption in passing from polynomials to
smooth functions. Smooth Chebyshev expansions converge in every finite
derivative norm. The published nu_0 and nu_1 are compactly supported
distributions agreeing with these Taylor coefficients on polynomials, so
their action passes to those expansions. Alternatively the polynomial
bound |nu_1(T_j)|<=C[j(1+log j)]^4 constructs a continuous distribution
on [-K,K] directly through the rapidly convergent Chebyshev series. Compact
distributions with identical polynomial moments agree. Either route suffices.

The constant term is exact:

  E Tr I/(DN)=1-1/N,
  nu_0(1)=1,
  nu_1(1)=-1.

The reviewed proof correctly retains this normalization. The original source
has a contrary sentence in its smooth extension proof, but its rational
definition and first correction moment formulas use the value -1. The new
proof does not inherit that sentence's error.

4. Exact support and growing parameters

The published Corollary9.7 states the exact inclusion

  support(nu_1) subset [-rho,rho].

Its intermediate proof constants may depend on matrix dimensions and on the
polynomial system. That dependence does not affect the use here. For every
finite system, the inclusion itself is exact and hence nu_1(chi)=0 whenever
chi vanishes on a neighborhood of that interval. The same holds for nu_0,
which is the free spectral distribution.

For a sequence P_N with r_N and D_N increasing, apply the exact support
statement to each fixed finite P_N. The auxiliary large matrix dimension
used to define its Taylor coefficient can tend to infinity separately.
There is no interchange of this limit with the growing sequence, and no
rate from that support proof is used in the final estimate.

5. Cutoff and tail probability

Choose one fixed smooth scalar step eta, zero at arguments at most zero
and one at arguments at least one. Define

  chi(x)=eta(2[x-rho-epsilon/2]/epsilon)
         +eta(2[-x-rho-epsilon/2]/epsilon).

The two transitions occur in disjoint half axes, so 0<=chi<=1. The function
vanishes on a neighborhood of [-rho,rho] and equals one for
|x|>=rho+epsilon. Its derivative of order k<=9 is bounded by a universal
constant times epsilon^(-k). The chain rule for chi(K cos theta) involves
only k=1,...,9, and bounds each contribution by C_k(K/epsilon)^k. Since
epsilon<K, these are all bounded by C(K/epsilon)^9. Therefore the L2
norm of the ninth angular derivative is also bounded by that quantity.

Support annihilates both first Taylor terms. The expected nonnegative
unnormalized cutoff trace is thus at most

  C(D/N)(K/epsilon)^9.

If the norm reaches rho+epsilon, an eigenvalue contributes at least one
to that trace. Markov proves the proposed tail bound, including equality
at the spectral threshold. All constants depend only on a fixed ninth
derivative cutoff construction and the convergent numerical series.

6. Graph consequence

For one distinct generator per undirected base edge and coefficient
dimension D=m=|V(G)|, the restricted block matrix is exactly adjacency
on the new fibre subspace. This subspace consists of vectors summing to
zero separately in each base fibre and is reducing for adjacency.

For any tuple of unitaries, a block vector x satisfies

  |<x,P(U)x>| <= y^T A_G y <=||A_G|| ||x||^2,
  where y_v=||x_v||.

Setting all unitaries equal to the identity attains the reverse inequality.
Thus K=||A_G||<=Delta, with no extra generator count.

The graph of P applied to the free regular generators covers G. Any
nonbacktracking closed walk in a component would project to a nonempty
reduced word in the distinct oriented edge generators. Such a word cannot
act trivially on the free group. Hence each component is a tree. Since
G is connected, each component covers every vertex of G and is a copy
of its universal covering tree. Parallel base edges cause no problem,
because their generators are distinct. Thus rho is exactly the universal
cover adjacency norm.

Consequently the graph estimate is

  P(||A_(G_N) on new||>=rho(T_G)+epsilon)
       <= C(m/N)(Delta/epsilon)^9.

For every deterministic bounded degree sequence with m_N=o(N), taking
epsilon_N=(m_N/N)^(1/10) makes the right side tend to zero. If this
epsilon exceeds K-rho, the threshold is above K and the probability is
zero, so the stated example sequence has no domain exception. The trivial
connected base with no edges has K=0 and can be treated separately.

Scope and final assessment

No unresolved mathematical step was found in the proposed generator free
tail bound or its graph consequence. The result uses the published exact
first correction support theorem, rather than a new proof of that support
theorem. The novelty of replacing log r by logarithmic polynomial degree
and absorbing that loss through smoothness still needs a priority search.

The estimate is uniform over deterministic bases, one base at a time. It
does not imply simultaneous control over all coefficient systems sampled
against one permutation tuple, or control for a base selected adaptively
after seeing those permutations. The lower bound necklace construction,
simple graph sharpness claim, and any end to end formalization require
their own reviews and are not established by this file.
