-------

matrix small-s classification by the number of active analytic branches,

the entire result as a recorded corollary. This is a bounded negative

The classical overlap is stronger than merely 'root-exponential decay is
known'. A newly located and fully read paper of Laptev1974 proves EXACT
log-squared Cauchy singular-count constants and uses substantially the
same logarithmic convolution/cell-localization/off-diagonal scheme. Also,
Arazy--Zelenko2006 explicitly represent rank-one nonlinear functional-
calculus differences through two Hankel/Riesz channels. These antecedents
must be credited in any presentation of the new theorem.

The possible new contribution is consequently quite specific:
  (i) the SHARP UNIFORM nonlinear matrix perturbation bound, normalized
      by the perturbation rather than the background spectrum;
  (ii) matching finite-matrix extremizers with that normalization;
  (iii) exact exceptional complex-line classification of the optimal
      coefficient, and the resulting exact joint dimension law.
The weighted Cauchy phase-volume mechanism, positive rational quadrature,
the existence of operator Holder bounds for these functions, and the
connection between rank-one perturbations and Hankel channels are not new.

If the precise matrix statements are new, this is a credible specialist
contribution in quantitative operator functional calculus. The present
evidence does not justify a seminal-concept claim, a broad algorithmic
breakthrough claim, a first discovery of root-exponential geometry, or
a declaration that the original general-quadratic goal is achieved.

1. PRECISE CLAIM AND NORMALIZATION
--------------------------------
The target is docs/complex-branch-classification.txt, reviewed source SHA
ba69cf0fce1538402c20b7a00d84dda2447357ce452d1dfe6e10e90d7754cea7,
and the audited starting theorem sharp-fractional-holder.tex.

For fixed 0<alpha<1 and f(x)=a(x_+)^alpha+b((-x)_+)^alpha, a,b complex,
define the POWER constant

  H_s(f)=sup tr|f(B)-f(A)|^s / tr|B-A|^(alpha*s),

over arbitrary finite Hermitian matrices A!=B. Write uniquely

  f=kappa(-ix)^alpha+lambda(ix)^alpha,
  m=number of nonzero entries of (kappa,lambda).

The proposed result is

  lim s^2 H_s(f)=m/[pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)].

For nonzero f, m=1 exactly on b=a exp(+/- i*pi*alpha), and m=2 otherwise.
The corresponding fixed-rank perturbation count coefficient is half of
the moment coefficient. The PSD-input x^alpha coefficient is half the
one-branch coefficient. These produce the stated 1:2:4 power-constant law.

The ordinary Schatten quasi-norm constant is H_s^(1/s). A factor four
in the power coefficient is not a factor-four ordinary norm estimate.
The limit holds for fixed scalar coefficients; a coefficient tending to
zero with s can have a transition outside the theorem's quantifiers.

For square size n and n*s^2->t>0, the claimed joint law, putting
c=m/[2*pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)], is

  s^2 H_(s,n)(f)->Phi_c(t),
  Phi_c(t)=2c[1-(1+sqrt(t/c))*exp(-sqrt(t/c))].

This is an extremal statement over finite pairs that may depend on s or
the error threshold. A fixed-operator Weyl law is a different assertion.

3. LAPTEV1974: A DIRECT EXACT-CONSTANT ANTECEDENT
------------------------------------------------
A. A. Laptev, Spectral asymptotic behavior of a class of integral
operators, Mathematical Notes16(5)(1974),1038--1043; Russian original
Matematicheskie Zametki16(5)(1974),741--750.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01149794
Primary metadata: https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/mzm7513
Author's English PDF:
  https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~alaptev/Papers/MatZam74.pdf

Read ALL SIX English pages, and visually checked the theorem/parameters
on printed pages1038--1039 because text extraction corrupts the formula.
The actual theorem treats

  (Tu)(x)=int_0^1 x^b y^c/(x+y)^a u(y)dy,
  a>0, b,c>-1/2, rho=b+c-a+1>0,

and proves exactly

  lim_(eps->0) N(eps,T)/log^2(1/eps)=1/(2*pi^2*rho).

Its introduction also permits asymptotically power-like weights in place
of the monomials. It is a singular-value theorem, so it is not confined
to the self-adjoint positive case b=c.

Section3 transforms the operator logarithmically to an exponentially
weighted cosh-convolution. Section4 partitions the log semiaxis into
cells, estimates the diagonal cell spectra through their Fourier decay,
and shows that off-diagonal terms have smaller count. Lemma3 gives an
O(log(1/eps)) count for the relevant off-diagonal kernels. The leading
coefficient comes from summing the same type of phase volume appearing
in the present proof. Laptev explicitly attributes the strategy to Widom.

This materially reduces the novelty that can be claimed for the LOWER
mechanism. Once a nonlinear correction has been factored as a weighted
Cauchy operator or its square, components of its sharp asymptotics can
be applications or extensions of this theorem. Squaring an operator
changes its log^2 count coefficient by a factor1/4, which must be tracked.
The two logarithmic tails and the two complex channels introduce further
bookkeeping; they are not evidence of a new Weyl principle.

This source alone does NOT give the candidate universal upper. Its
theorem concerns a fixed weighted operator and asymptotic thresholds
that can depend on those weights. It does not optimize simultaneously
over all background matrices and impose the matrix input normalization.
It does not state the exceptional scalar complex lines or the joint
finite-dimension extremal law. These are necessary extra implications,
and no exact statement supplying them was located here.

4. MARDVILKO2025: SCALAR PARITY AND MULTIPOINT TOOLS
---------------------------------------------------
T. S. Mardvilko, Uniform rational approximation of the odd and even
Cauchy transforms, Sbornik: Mathematics216(2)(2025),239--256.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.4213/sm10116e
Primary full HTML:
  https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=sm&option_lang=eng&paperid=10116&wshow=paper

Read its full primary HTML, including Theorems1--6, Lemmas2--6 and the
proofs in Sections2--3. The original paper was received May/September2024
and published16April2025. The database crawl date is not its event date.

Theorem1 gives two-sided fixed-factor comparison for the scalar odd
extension |x|^alpha sign(x) on[-1,1], with rate exp(-pi*sqrt(alpha*n)).
The paper compares this to Stahl's strong even-extension asymptotic.
Theorems4 and6 give matching weak rates for odd/even Cauchy transforms
whose measure density behaves like t^alpha near zero.

The central tools are modified-measure multipoint Pade approximants,
orthogonal-polynomial zeros, positive Gauss/Christoffel comparison,
and Blaschke-product estimates. These tools, and the equality of scalar
even/odd root-exponential rates, cannot be claimed as new discoveries.

Its rational function spaces use real polynomial coefficients; the
paper does not classify arbitrary complex amplitudes or analytic branch
lines. It states no universal nonlinear Schatten perturbation constant
and no n*s^2 dimension formula. The scalar compact interval and degree
parameter are different from the normalized finite-matrix supremum.
No direct implication proving the entire candidate was found.

5. ARAZY--ZELENKO2006: THE TWO HANKEL CHANNELS ARE PRIOR
------------------------------------------------------
J. Arazy and L. Zelenko, Directional operator differentiability of
non-smooth functions, Journal of Operator Theory55:1(2006),49--90.
Primary journal PDF:
  https://jot.theta.ro/jot/archive/2006-055-001/2006-055-001-003.pdf
Primary ESI1707 preprint:
  https://www.esi.ac.at/preprints/esi1707.pdf

Screened the actual introduction and the rank-one Hankel representations
in Sections2/4, including the discussion of (2.38) and (4.11). This was
NOT a fresh full reading of all42 journal pages or all36 preprint pages.

The introduction explicitly writes the directional derivative as a
difference of positive/negative Riesz projection pieces, equivalently
Hankel operators. Section4's finite-coupling difference quotient uses
Hankel terms with disjoint ranges and co-kernels, and a nonlinear
perturbation-determinant/Borel-transform weight. It builds on Peller1985.

Thus both the rank-one functional-calculus/Hankel connection and the
presence of two analytically distinguished channels predate this work.
The candidate's exact finite-coupling factorization can be a useful
specialization, but should not be framed as inventing that connection.

No exact small-s universal matrix constant or exceptional-line theorem
was found in the inspected passages. The paper's objective is when
spectral-measure regularity guarantees differentiability for arbitrary
continuous scalar functions; it is not the present normalized Holder
extremal problem. The original Peller1985/1990 sources remain a concrete
priority check to finish before any strong first-discovery claim.

8. WHY THE MATRIX RESULT IS NOT YET A VERIFIED ROUTINE COROLLARY
---------------------------------------------------------------
Laptev plus BKS fixes a sharp coefficient for certain LOWER examples
and a non-sharp UPPER order. That pair of facts does not mathematically
determine the sharp universal coefficient: the BKS upper constant could
be larger. One must separately control the supremum over all backgrounds
with a matching uniform coefficient. A fixed-weight asymptotic theorem
does not supply the required uniformity in its threshold or constants.

Likewise, a divided-difference/derivative lower alone does not settle
the normalized nonlinear problem. For a small increment tE, a linearized
output scales like t while the Holder input scale is t^alpha; the
normalization loses t^(1-alpha). A valid finite-coupling realization or
another normalization-preserving transfer is needed. The candidate
keeps its actual Woodbury denominator for this reason.

The complex exceptional-line rule becomes natural after a two-channel
factorization and a matrix-valued local Fourier law. This is evidence
of a classical structural explanation, not evidence that the final
universal extremal statement has been previously proved. No precise
old theorem covering all needed nonlinear weights, arbitrary complex
coefficients and finite-matrix normalization was checked here.

The dimension formula's upper is the elementary layer-cake integral of
min(n,cL^2). Its MATCHING lower is not a formal consequence of that cap:
it needs compatible near-extremizer profiles and a small-state pair
preserving the needed singular spectrum. Rational Krylov and positive
rational Gaussian quadrature are classical, so this is best presented
as a sharp quantitative consequence/application, not an independent
new state-space or quadrature principle.
