What the direct guarantee says

The fixed-depth input is A in R^(n by m), B in R^(m by p), with
m <= min(n,p), integer depth L >= 1, and integer target rank 1 <= r <= m.
The supplied accuracy schedule satisfies

  0 < theta <= h_L,  h_0 <= 1/64,
  h_j > 0,  h_(j+1)/h_j <= 1/1024,
  0 < delta < 1.

Those ratio conditions imply the strict decreases in the paper's statement.
The output contains factors U,V of a computed width w <= r and an explicitly
formed dense matrix C = UV. Every random outcome has the rank cap and a finite
operation cap. On a measurable event of probability at least 1-delta,

  ||AB-C||_2 <= (1+theta) sigma_(r+1)(AB).

The norm is the spectral/operator norm. Lean's tailSingularValue (A*B) r is
zero indexed: index r is paper sigma_(r+1), rather than sigma_r. Values past
the dimension are zero. Thus r=m includes an exact zero-tail requirement;
the proof handles it through the support/exact branches. Zero products,
dependent factors, repeated singular values, and a zero optimal tail are
included. Full rank, a simple spectrum, a spectral gap, or bounded factor
conditioning are not original-input assumptions. Accuracy is a probability
statement; the rank and operation caps also hold on unsuccessful draws.

The growing-depth family additionally fixes

  1/8 < d < (sqrt(2)-1)/2,
  n=p=N=m^(1+d+o(1)),
  rho=m/N,  theta=rho^(5/2),
  1 <= r <= floor(sqrt(m)),
  m^(-c_delta) <= delta < 1, for fixed c_delta > 0.

The asymptotic claim includes growing rank and vanishing excess theta. It
does not cover arbitrary accuracy/rank/dimension scalings with the same
N^(2+o(1)) conclusion. The finite FamilyProblem caller accepts all m<=N with
the rank/confidence bounds and uses a conservative fallback when necessary.

Mathematical significance

The hierarchy controls the relaxed, noncommuting quadratic invariant

  F - kappa*K*F^(-1)*K <= C*h*D.

The transfer retains the small product alpha*h even after the defect
coefficient is relaxed. A fixed target mass is used across levels and
thresholds. The carried-preimage identity connects the implicit normalized
filter to the physical output without executing the inverse of an implicit
polynomial preconditioner. These are the mechanisms behind the stated
tradeoff; the result is not just a restatement of a fast multiplication bound.

Formal verification adds a check that the mathematical objects are reached by
the named finite arithmetic programs: support reduction, positive even
Neumann mass, threshold signs and near transfer, dependent ranges, conditional
Gaussian events, and output formation. The exact rank and zero-eigenvalue
branches matter because replacing them by full-rank assumptions would weaken
the direct statement. The fast kernels are actual finite circuit witnesses,
with input/output semantics and every arithmetic gate counted.

The growing-depth manuscript targets N^(2+o(1)) time including N^2 dense
output entries. Exponent two is the output-size leading exponent under that
explicit-output convention. The expression still permits unbounded
subpolynomial overhead; it is not a literal O(N^2) bound. If only the two
thin factors are returned, the cost comparison must account for that
different output requirement separately.

At the optimized parameters,

  d* = (3*sqrt(5)-5)/10 = 0.1708203932499369...,
  alpha* = 1/(1+d*) = 0.8541019662496845...,
  s* = (7-3*sqrt(5))/8 = 0.0364745084375789...,

the two stated comparator upper bounds have exponent 2+s*, while the new
family targets exponent two. The permitted rank grows through
N^(alpha*/2+o(1)), with alpha*/2 = 0.4270509831248423.... This compares the
specified available arithmetic upper bounds. It is not a lower-bound
separation from every competing algorithm, a global novelty determination,
or a measured speedup by N^s* at finite N.

When the literal growing schedule first activates

The displayed schedule starts at h_0=1/1024, sets
h_* = h_0*sqrt(rho), and proposes h_1=sqrt(h_0*h_*).
It accepts that first refinement only when h_1 <= h_0/1024. Since h_0>0,

  h_1/h_0 = rho^(1/4),
  rho^(1/4) <= 1/1024
    iff rho <= 1024^(-4) = 2^(-40)
    iff N/m >= 2^40 = 1,099,511,627,776.

This is a necessary and sufficient first-step condition for this particular
literal schedule. It is not a crossover estimate comparing two runtimes.

For the exact illustrative power law N=m^(1+d), it becomes

  log10(m) >= 40*log10(2)/d,
  log10(N) >= (1+d)*40*log10(2)/d.

At d=d*, the cutoffs are

  log10(m) >= 70.49041158066585...,
  log10(N) >= 82.53161140722510...,
  m >= approximately 3.09323*10^70,
  N >= approximately 3.40104*10^82.

Rounded to two decimal places, these are 10^70.49 and 10^82.53.
At the excluded boundary d_max=(sqrt(2)-1)/2=0.2071067811865475...,
the limiting cutoffs would be

  log10(m) >= 58.14005585704779...,
  log10(N) >= 70.18125568360704....

Thus even the limiting core cutoff is about 10^58.14; every admissible fixed
d<d_max has a larger cutoff under the exact power law. The manuscript allows
an o(1) perturbation of that law, so these decimal thresholds describe the
exact-power illustration, not a universal finite-size cutoff for every
permitted sequence. The unconditional finite test is always N/m>=2^40.

Below the first-step threshold, the computed schedule has zero accepted
levels. The formal growing caller supplies a valid one-refinement fixed
schedule with h_0=1/1024, h_1=1/1048576 and internal tolerance
min(theta,1/1048576). It retains width at most r and implies the requested
original tolerance. It does not simply return AB at full width. Absorbing
this finite regime in a family constant proves no useful finite-size speed
advantage for the fallback.

Two concrete shape examples

  m          N             N/m      max floor(sqrt(m))   accepted levels
  1,000      1,000,000     1,000               31                 0
  1,000,000  1,000,000,000 1,000            1,000                 0

Both have rho=0.001 and theta=rho^(5/2)=3.1622776601683793*10^(-8).
For both, the proposed first accuracy is approximately 0.0001736600986366,
whereas the acceptance threshold h_0/1024 is 9.5367431640625*10^(-7).
The proposal fails, so both use the finite fallback. Here theta is already
below the fallback tolerance cap, so the internal tolerance equals theta.
Their effective exact-power d values are 1 and 1/2, outside the asymptotic
family interval; these examples illustrate the finite caller's fallback.

The dense outputs alone contain 10^12 and 10^18 scalar entries, respectively.
This is an entry-count observation, with no assumed storage format or timing
model. These examples are not benchmarks.

An integer family inside the stated exponent interval is

  m=a^6,  N=a^7,  d=1/6,  for integers a>=2.

Here 1/8 < 1/6 < (sqrt(2)-1)/2, the rank cap is r<=a^3, and the requested
tolerance is theta=a^(-5/2). The first-step test reduces to a>=2^40.

For a=3, the dimensions are m=729, N=2187, with r<=27 and
theta=3^(-5/2)=0.0641500299099584.... The dense output has exactly
4,782,969 entries. At an illustrative eight bytes per entry, that is
38,263,752 bytes, or about 38.3 decimal MB. This gives an ordinary-size
example in the permitted integer family. Its ratio N/m=3 rejects the first
refinement, so the caller uses the finite fallback. The internal tolerance
is min(theta,2^(-20))=9.5367431640625*10^(-7), much smaller than the
requested tolerance. The stronger internal accuracy preserves the requested
error guarantee without establishing a finite-size speed advantage. No numerical
benchmark or lower bound on the selected polynomial degree follows from
this example.

For a=10, the same in-interval family has m=1,000,000, N=10,000,000,
r<=1000, and theta=0.0031622776601683793.... The ratio N/m=10 again
forces the fallback with internal tolerance 2^(-20). Its explicit dense
output has 10^14 entries; eight bytes per entry would require 800 decimal
TB. This storage obligation is substantial before any runtime comparison.
The byte estimates in both examples illustrate a hypothetical floating-point
storage format; they are not memory or precision guarantees of the exact-real
model, and no such floating-point implementation is asserted here.

Even one fixed refinement has conservative constants

For a general fixed-depth L=1 instance, the input conditions force

  theta <= h_1 <= h_0/1024 <= 1/(64*1024)
        = 1/65536 = 0.0000152587890625.

The invariant constants are

  C_0=36,  C_1=100*(36+1)=3700,
  A_1=10^6*(C_1+1)=3,701,000,000,
  alpha=theta/(A_1*h_1) <= 1/3,701,000,000
        = 2.7019724398811132*10^(-10).

Consequently the bare inverse-square-root accuracy scale alpha^(-1/2) is
at least 60,835.844697.... This is the scale entering the Chebyshev degree
envelopes before their covariance/failure logarithms. It is not an asserted
lower bound on the program's selected degree.

In the actual query compiler, mainRitzAccuracy=alpha/100 and krylovDegree
uses Chebyshev accuracy mainRitzAccuracy/100=alpha/10000. Its certified bound
has the form

  q <= 2*log(20*R_G/epsilon)/sqrt(epsilon/100)+1,
  epsilon=alpha/100.

Thus the coefficient before that logarithm is 200*alpha^(-1/2), already at
least about 12.17 million at the largest possible alpha for L=1. This is a
large sufficient upper-envelope coefficient, not a necessary degree estimate
or observed iteration count. The search computes the first sufficient
degree by scalar recurrence and counts that search. Smaller theta/h_1 makes
the envelope larger. Growing-depth bounds also display conservative factors
such as 448000 and 44800000 in the strong/query degree estimates; their
eventual absorption into polylogarithms does not make them small numerically.

What a practical speed claim would additionally need

The theorem's model gives unit cost to exact field operations, positive
scalar square roots, comparisons, and independent exact Gaussian samples.
Real representations may grow without bound. The verified arithmetic counts
do not bound the bit lengths, finite-word roundoff, or numerical sensitivity
of the intermediate factors, recurrences, rank tests, and sign decisions.
Gap-free spectral correctness in exact arithmetic does not itself supply a
floating-point stability theorem for those operations.

The literal activation threshold, small alpha, unknown practical constants of
the fast circuit witnesses, and exp(O((log log m)^2)) overhead prevent an
inference of an ordinary-size speed advantage from the leading exponents.
Formal verification establishes the stated mathematical guarantee within
its model; it does not replace a stable implementation, a precision analysis,
or measured comparisons. No wall-clock results, implementations with a proved
word-complexity bound, or benchmark speedups are claimed in this assessment.
