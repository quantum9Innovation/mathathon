Alternate equitable bisections and compact eigenvectors for RSBM(2,1)
Prepared 2026-11-15. Independent derivation; no novelty claim.

MODEL AND SCOPE
There are N vertices in each planted block V+ and V-. Independently choose
a uniform simple 2-regular graph in each block and a uniform perfect matching
between the blocks. Let sigma be +1 on V+ and -1 on V-. The adjacency matrix
A obeys A sigma = sigma. An alternate equitable bisection means a vector
tau in {+1,-1}^{2N}, sum tau=0, A tau=tau, tau != +/- sigma.

RESULT
The expected number of alternate equitable bisections is O(N^{-2}). Hence
the planted bisection is the unique equitable (2,1) bisection, up to global
sign, with probability 1-O(N^{-2}). This is an identifiability statement.
It does NOT establish dim ker(A-I)=1, and does not yield an efficient
recovery algorithm by itself. A real eigenspace may have dimension >1
without containing another balanced sign vector.

DETERMINISTIC FLIP CONDITION
Write tau_v=sigma_v t_v with t_v in {+1,-1}, and let S={v:t_v=-1}.
Subtracting A sigma=sigma from A tau=tau after multiplying coordinates
by sigma gives the following exact condition at every vertex:

  number of original internal edges crossing (S,S^c)
    = number of original matching edges crossing (S,S^c).

Thus the first count is either zero or one. In particular, every vertex
of S has at least one original internal neighbor in S. Balance of tau
requires |S intersect V+|=|S intersect V-|=:k. Replacing tau by -tau
replaces k by N-k, so it suffices to treat 1<=k<=N/2. For simple internal
graphs k=1 is impossible, since its lone flipped vertex in each block
would have no internal neighbor in S.

EXACT CONFIGURATION MODEL FIRST MOMENT
First allow independent degree-2 configuration multigraphs inside blocks,
with two distinguishable stubs at every vertex. The matching is simple.
Fix S+ subset V+ and S- subset V-, each of size k. Let r be the number
of matching edges from S+ to V-\S-. Since both sides have k flipped
vertices, exactly r matching edges also go from V+\S+ to S-.

The probability of this matching intersection is

  P_r = binom(k,r) binom(N-k,r) / binom(N,k).

Within each planted block exactly r vertices of S and r vertices outside
S are active (their cross partner is on the other side of S). Each needs
exactly one internal edge across S. All other vertices need zero.
For any fixed such active sets, the exact probability in one internal
configuration model is

  q(k,r) = 2^(2r) r! (2k-r-1)!! (2(N-k)-r-1)!! / (2N-1)!!,

if r is even; it is zero otherwise. Convention: (-1)!!=1. Indeed choose
one of two stubs at each active vertex, match the r selected S stubs to
the r selected outside stubs (r! choices), and pair all remaining stubs
within their own side. Parity forces r even.

Consequently the exact expected number of valid flip sets with size k
and intersection parameter r is

  T_N(k,r) = binom(N,k) binom(k,r) binom(N-k,r) q(k,r)^2.

This counts oriented sign vectors. Summing only k<=N/2 covers every
unordered alternate bisection at least once; overcounting is harmless.

SMALL k: A UNIFORM BOUND
Fix epsilon=1/1024. Put c=1-3 epsilon/2 and C=8 e^2/c^3. For k<=epsilon N
and 0<=r<=k, r even, the ratio of the second double factorial in q to its
denominator is a product of k+r/2 reciprocals, every denominator at least
2N-2k-r+1 >= 2cN. Therefore

  q(k,r) <= 4^r r! (2k)^(k-r/2) / (2cN)^(k+r/2).

Using binom(N,k)<=(eN/k)^k, binom(k,r)<=2^k,
binom(N-k,r)<=(eN/r)^r, and r!<=r^r gives

  T_N(k,r) <= (C k/N)^k.

The r=0 case uses the standard 0^0=1 convention and the same inequality.
There are at most k+1 possible r values. For
f_k=(k+1)(Ck/N)^k, k>=2,

  f_(k+1)/f_k
     <= (4/3) e C (k+1)/N
     <= (4/3) e C (epsilon+1/N) < 1/2

for all sufficiently large N. Thus

  sum_(2<=k<=epsilon N) sum_r T_N(k,r)
     <= 2 f_2 = 24 C^2/N^2.

Although the unrestricted configuration model also has k=1 candidates,
all of those use internal loops and disappear on conditioning simplicity.
We bound only k>=2 before the conditioning step.

MACROSCOPIC k: STRICT ENTROPY NEGATIVITY
Set a=k/N and b=r/N, where 0<a<=1/2 and 0<=b<=a. With H(x)=-x log x
-(1-x)log(1-x), and 0 log0=0, define

 Q(a,b)=2b log2+b log b
       +(2a-b)log(2a-b)/2
       +(2-2a-b)log(2-2a-b)/2-log2;

 F(a,b)=H(a)+a H(b/a)+(1-a)H(b/(1-a))+2Q(a,b).

Uniform Stirling bounds (including zero factorial arguments) imply

  log T_N(k,r) = N F(a,b)+O(log N)

with an absolute uniform O(log N) on this compact domain.
In the interior,

 F_b = log[16(a-b)(1-a-b)/((2a-b)(2-2a-b))].

F_bb<0, because

 F_bb=-1/(a-b)-1/(1-a-b)+1/(2a-b)+1/(2-2a-b)<0.

At b=0, F_b=log4>0. At b=a it tends to -infinity (including a=1/2).
Thus there is a unique maximizer

  b_*(a) = (7-sqrt(49-180a(1-a)))/15,

which lies in (0,a), and b_*(a)<1/3 for a<1/2, with b_*(1/2)=1/3.

Let M(a)=F(a,b_*(a)). Its derivative is F_a at the optimum:

 F_a=log[(1-a-b)(2a-b)^2/((a-b)(2-2a-b)^2)].

To verify its sign, put x=a-b and y=1-a-b, so 0<x<y for a<1/2.
Then F_a<0 is equivalent to

  (2x+b)/sqrt(x) < (2y+b)/sqrt(y),

which is equivalent to b<2sqrt(xy). The optimum condition is
16xy=(2x+b)(2y+b), equivalently 12xy=2b-3b^2.
Hence b<2sqrt(xy) is equivalent to b<1/3. This holds strictly for a<1/2.
Therefore M is strictly decreasing on (0,1/2). Direct substitution gives

  lim_(a->0) M(a)=0,  M(1/2)=log(2/3)<0.

It follows that F(a,b)<=M(epsilon)<0 whenever epsilon<=a<=1/2.
The macroscopic contribution, having at most N^2 summands and a polynomial
Stirling prefactor, is exponentially small.

TRANSFER TO THE SIMPLE MODEL
For each internal degree-2 configuration model the probability of being
simple tends to exp(-3/4)>0. A self-contained enumeration: the exponential
generating function of simple labeled 2-regular graphs is

  exp(sum_(j>=3) z^j/(2j))
     =(1-z)^(-1/2) exp(-z/2-z^2/4).

The number of configurations per simple graph is 2^N. Divide their count
by (2N-1)!!; the resulting simplicity probability is

  [z^N] ((1-z)^(-1/2) exp(-z/2-z^2/4))
       / [z^N] (1-z)^(-1/2),

which tends to exp(-3/4), by the usual coefficient asymptotics for an
analytic factor times (1-z)^(-1/2). The two internal graphs are independent,
so joint simplicity has limiting probability exp(-3/2)>0. Conditional on
simplicity they are exactly the desired independent uniform simple graphs.
Since every simple-model alternate has k>=2, its expectation is at most
the configuration expectation for k>=2 divided by joint simplicity.
The preceding bounds prove O(N^{-2}).

EXACT REAL EIGENSPACE REDUCTION
Relabel V- by the perfect matching. If C,D are the two internal adjacency
matrices, A=[[C,I],[I,D]]. The equation (A-I)(x,y)=0 is equivalent to

  y=(I-C)x,    (C+D-DC)x=0.

Thus dim ker(A-I)=dim ker(C+D-DC), with planted x=1. The uniqueness
theorem above excludes additional balanced sign vectors, not additional
general real vectors in this kernel. It must not be cited as shifted
nonsingularity on 1^perp.

WHY BOUNDED CYCLE/TWIN MOTIFS DO NOT GIVE AN OBSTRUCTION
For any cubic graph and a real eigenvector f with eigenvalue lambda!=0,
let S be its exact nonzero support, |S|=k, and let H include all edges
incident to S and all their endpoints. Every v in S has an S-neighbor
(otherwise lambda f_v=0). Every outside neighbor of S has at least two
neighbors in S (otherwise its eigenvector equation cannot cancel).
If e edges lie inside S and ell vertices outside S touch S, then

  boundary edges=3k-2e,  ell<=(3k-2e)/2,
  |E(H)|=3k-e,  |V(H)|=k+ell,
  |E(H)|-|V(H)| >= k/2.

For fixed k there are finitely many possible bounded H, and a fixed
colored H has expected O(N^{|V(H)|-|E(H)|}) embeddings in this planted
fixed-degree model (or probability zero). Hence the probability of a
nonzero eigenvalue eigenvector supported on at most K vertices is
O_K(N^{-1}) for every fixed K; for lambda=1 the minimum support is at
least two and the direct excess bound is O_K(N^{-1}). This excludes
bounded compact-support motifs as a nonvanishing-probability explanation
of excess multiplicity. It says nothing about supports growing with N.
