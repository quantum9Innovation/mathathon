Adaptive defect search: one-sided critical Schur complements
===========================================================
2026-11-14, cutoff. New research note; previous verified papers unchanged.

STATUS. The unrestricted target remains OPEN. The construction below is a
proved, conditional small-critical-core shortcut, not another claim that the
original arbitrary-factor N^2 target has been achieved. Its useful feature is
that it certifies a rank-one OUTPUT SPACE from any negative witness; it does
not approximate the leading eigenvector. The missing obligation is to handle
a large critical Schur complement at the target cost on every input. The
Schur/inertia and Lorentzian identities themselves are classical.

Target reread: docs/research-direction.typ and the authoritative beginning of
docs/research-state.txt. Original hard dimensions are n=p=N=m^(9/8), r=1,
theta=1/m, I=m(n+p)=m^(17/8), core/output budget m^(9/4+o(1)). Existing

1. Any negative witness is sufficient once the index is at most one
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Lemma 1. Let K be real symmetric, n_-(K)<=1, and v^T K v=a<0. Then

  R=K-(Kv)(Kv)^T/a >= 0.                                      (1)

Proof. Extend v to an invertible basis [v,V]. The first scalar pivot of
the congruence is a<0. Its Schur complement V^T R V has no negative
eigenvalues, by inertia additivity. Also Rv=0, so R is PSD on the whole
space. Consequently K is PSD on the Euclidean orthogonal complement of
Kv. No gap, normalization, or lower bound on |a| is needed in exact
arithmetic. For n_-(K)<=r, the analogous block formula applies to a
negative-definite r-column pivot, but finding such a pivot is not free.

The geometric distinction from PCA is substantial: v can be far from an
eigenvector. What matters is that its image Kv specifies a positive
hyperplane. Finding v and certifying n_-(K)<=1 remain computational tasks.

Use the companion's constant-accuracy support reduction. On its good event,

  M=Z R, G=Z^T Z approximately I, Q=RR^T,
  B_m approximately_(e_m) G^-1,
  K_t=t B_m-Q, D_t=tI+Q_0.                                    (2)

Here B_m is ONE fixed even finite Neumann polynomial, e_m=theta/1e9,
and Q_0 is explicit. All K_t actions are true-factor actions, not an
accurately formed Gram. The represented target Hhat=B_m^-1/2 Q B_m^-1/2
is analytical only. Its second eigenvalue approximates the physical
lambda_2(G^1/2 Q G^1/2) to relative 1+O(e_m).

If K_t is PSD on g^perp, output the physical left space range(Zg).
Indeed every physical vector in range(Z) has the form x=Z G^-1 y,
and x orthogonal to Zg iff y^Tg=0. Its norm squared is y^T G^-1 y,
whereas x^T MM^T x=y^T Q y. Thus Q<=tB_m on g^perp gives

  ||(I-P_range(Zg)) M||^2 <= (1+e_m)t.                        (3)

The output is C=P_range(Zg)M, formed with ordinary factor products. If
K_t>=0, output zero. Whole-support/zero-tail branches remain exactly as
in the companion; this note considers k=rank(M)>1 and lambda_2>0.

2. Certified far-positive elimination with no exact eigenbasis
-------------------------------------------------------------
Fix a SMALL CONSTANT coarse distortion H, say H<=1/1024. In the notation
of the companion, with J_D J_D^T=D_t, form explicit

  S0=J_D^-1 (tI-Q_0) J_D^-T,
  Kbar=J_D^-1 K_t J_D^-T.

The same coarse comparison used by the absolute-pencil construction gives

  -I<=S0<=I, ||Kbar-S0||<=2H, ||Kbar||<=2.                    (4)

Choose any tau in [8H,9H] avoiding the eigenvalues of S0. This is possible
with probability one using a scalar Gaussian with an atom-free bounded
transform. Apply the exact Gaussian-congruence LDL construction to the
EXPLICIT matrix S0-tau I. Extract a positive subspace and orthonormalize
it. Call its basis U_F. Complete it to an orthogonal [U_C,U_F]=U.

Orientation check: if Y^T(S0-tau I)Y=L Delta L^T, the PRIMAL positive
subspace is Y L^-T E_+, not Y^-T L E_+. Thus on range(U_F),
S0>=tau I. No spectral projector of S0 is assumed. If d=width(U_C),

  U^T Kbar U = [[C,B^T],[B,A]],
  6H I<=A<=2I, ||B||<=2.                                    (5)

All operations constructing U, including exact rank and orthogonal
complement algebra, cost ~m^(omega+zeta). Define d= k-n_+(S0-tau I).
This is the computable critical dimension for this threshold. It may be k.
The off-diagonal B need not vanish: this is a congruence positive subspace,
not an uncharged exact invariant-subspace computation.

3. Finite ONE-SIDED inverse polynomials for the far block
--------------------------------------------------------
Set W=I-A/2. On (5), 0<=W<=1-3H. For l chosen so that
(1-3H)^(l+1)/(6H)<=delta, the finite polynomial

  T_-=(1/2) sum_{i=0}^l W^i

satisfies 0<T_-<=A^-1 and ||A^-1-T_-||<=delta. Put
T_+=T_-+delta I, so A^-1<=T_+<=A^-1+delta I. No solve or root of A
is used. Degree O(H^-1 log(1/(H delta))) is O(log(1/delta)) at fixed H.
A Chebyshev residual polynomial and a certified scalar correction can
reduce H^-1 to H^-1/2; that improvement is not needed for the claim here.
These constants are not proposed as a practical implementation.

Form ONLY the d-column products needed for

  S_-=C-B^T T_+ B, S_+=C-B^T T_- B.                          (6)

They are explicit d-by-d matrices. A and its inverse are never formed.
All polynomial actions can be batched on the d columns of B. In particular
this is not d separate input scans or a hidden fine full Gram construction.

For delta<=1/8, introduce analytical full surrogates

  K_-=[[C,B^T],[B,T_+^-1]],
  K_+=[[C,B^T],[B,T_-^-1]].                                  (7)

Inversion order gives K_-<=U^T Kbar U<=K_+. Moreover

  0<=A-T_+^-1<=4delta I,
  0<=T_-^-1-A<=8delta I.                                    (8)

For the first inequality, write T_+=A^-1+E, 0<=E<=delta I,
and use I-(I+A^1/2 E A^1/2)^-1<=A^1/2 E A^1/2. For the second,
T_-=A^-1-E, 0<=E<=delta I, and ||A^1/2 E A^1/2||<=2delta<=1/4;
(I-X)^-1-I<=2X. Since ||A||<=2, the stated constants follow. Thus

  Kbar-8delta I <= U K_- U^T <= Kbar
       <= U K_+ U^T <= Kbar+8delta I.                        (9)

Schur/inertia additivity gives n_-(K_-)=n_-(S_-) and likewise for +.
The inverse symbols in (7) are used ONLY for this analysis. They are
never applied in the actual construction. The input conditioning and
lambda_1/lambda_2 never occur in the degree or precision of (6).

4. Exact negative-witness lift from the SMALL Schur complement
-------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose n_-(S_-)<=1. If S_->=0, then K_->=0, hence Kbar>=0.
If w^T S_- w<0, set v=[w;-T_+ B w]. Direct multiplication gives

  K_- v=[S_- w;0].                                          (10)

Lemma 1 says K_- is PSD on the orthogonal complement of this vector;
so is U^T Kbar U, since K_-<=U^T Kbar U. Undoing the congruences,
the physical output space is precisely

  range(Z J_D U_C S_- w).                                   (11)

This needs no T_+^-1, inverse of a fine matrix polynomial, or precise
eigenvector. Equation (11) includes the far-block coupling through the
Schur complement. Any claim that U_C alone is a valid output for d>1
would be false. For d=1 its full critical span is of course sufficient.

Negative count and witness of an explicit possibly singular S_- are
computable in ~d^(omega+zeta) with exact rank revelation, a nonsingular
principal support, and Gaussian-congruence LDL. A maximal independent
column set of a symmetric rank-s matrix indexes a nonsingular s-by-s
principal submatrix; pull back the witness by inserting zero coordinates.
Do not invert a singular S_- or silently assume all pivots are nonzero.

5. A threshold oracle and the ambiguous case
-------------------------------------------
Set epsilon=8delta. Undo (9) to obtain full physical-coordinate
surrogates with

 K-epsilon D <= Ktilde_- <= K <= Ktilde_+ <= K+epsilon D.     (12)

Use D<=2(tB_m+Q), valid on the coarse event. Let
rho=(1+2epsilon)/(1-2epsilon), epsilon<1/10. Two scalar inequalities are

  K-epsilon D >= (1+2epsilon) K_(t/rho),
  K+epsilon D <= (1-2epsilon) K_(t rho).                     (13)

The following three-way oracle is rigorous.

  BELOW: n_-(S_+)>=2. Then n_-(K)>=2 and t<lambda_2(Hhat).

  ABOVE/WITNESS: n_-(S_-)<=1. Then n_-(K)<=1 and
  t>=lambda_2(Hhat); Section 4 returns the actual rank<=1 output at t.

  NEAR: n_-(S_+)<=1 but n_-(S_-)>=2. Monotonicity of the negative
  index in Loewner order, (12), and (13) give
       t/rho < lambda_2(Hhat) <= t rho.                     (14)

In the NEAR branch run the construction once at t''=t rho^2. Its LOWER
surrogate is >=(1+2epsilon)K_(t rho), whose negative index is <=1.
Hence n_-(S_-(t''))<=1 and Section 4 gives an actual output. Its squared
threshold obeys t''<rho^3 lambda_2(Hhat). Choose epsilon=theta/1000;
together with e_m=theta/1e9, this is well inside the desired singular
error factor 1+theta. Nothing depends on an unknown gap or tail scale.

For global search, use the companion's positive polynomial-factor initial
bracket from Q_0; bisect to relative theta/O(1), updating endpoints by
BELOW/ABOVE, and return an ABOVE witness at the final upper endpoint if
no NEAR branch occurs. The number of calls is O(log(m/theta)). Conditional
small-core bounds must hold at every queried t, including the inflated
NEAR exit. They do NOT follow from a dimension bound at just one t.
All-outcome finite caps come from fixed polynomial degrees and fixed
bisection counts. Failed coarse-event bounds invalidate correctness,
not finiteness. LDL denominators are guarded as in the companion.

6. Exact cost and the conditional original-target improvement
-------------------------------------------------------------
Let
  Acal(b)=T_MM(n,m,b)+T_MM(p,m,b).
Core maps on b columns are dominated by this up to m^(omega+zeta).
Sections 2--5 at one threshold cost

  ~O(m^(omega+zeta)+Acal(d)),                                (15)

for constant H; theta and confidence add only logarithms. The cost
includes polynomial true-factor mass B_m, applications of A to d RHS,
forming C,B,S_+/-, explicit small inertia/witness, and the physical
one-column output lift. Coarse sketches cost ~I+m^(omega+zeta), and
their event is uniform over every adaptively chosen threshold. Exact
Gaussian-congruence exceptional events have probability zero for each
fixed conditional core; finitely many calls remain a null union.

Thus if the computable critical dimensions on the full capped search
are at most dmax, the build cost is

  ~O(I+m^(omega+zeta)+Acal(dmax)),                            (16)

and explicit rank-one expansion costs O(np). This is a conditional
theorem for arbitrary factors satisfying the stated critical-profile
condition, not the unconditional theorem requested by the user.

For n=p=m^(9/8), dmax<=m^(17/20)=m^.85, the rectangular AV bound gives

 exponent Acal(dmax)
   <= [(9/8)*1+1*(17/20)+(17/20)*(9/8)]^2
                        /[4*(9/8)*1*(17/20)]
    = 2.246333... < 9/4.                                   (17)

This is the sorted a>=b>=c branch (ab+bc+ca)^2/(4abc) of the
already-audited AV rectangular theorem. Its input-optimal branch does
not apply at c=17/20. The operative general condition is simply
Acal(dmax)<=m^(9/4+o(1)).
The conditional result therefore reaches the ORIGINAL theta=1/m accuracy
and explicit output budget when the critical core is sufficiently small.
It bypasses isotropic accuracy refinement in that class. The required
critical bound can fail maximally, even with an index-one threshold.

7. What has NOT been proved; raw-factor truncated-inertia obligation
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The dimension d can equal m: take a whole physical spectrum in a narrow
coarse H-band around t. Such a matrix can be easy (flat bulk), so d=m is
not a hardness proof, but (16) would then charge exact Gram-scale work.
No universal accounting argument bounds the sum or maximum of d(t).

A suggested dynamic LDL shortcut is to eliminate positive pivots until
the first negative pivot, then invoke Lemma 1. It does NOT avoid the
positive pivot cost: K=I-(1+gamma)uu^T, with a flat u, has every principal
restriction of size s<m/(1+gamma) positive. Near theta=1/m and gamma
of order theta, essentially every scalar pivot can be positive before
the negative one appears. This is a decisive counterexample to early
negative-pivot termination alone. In this particular example centering
reveals rank one immediately, so the example does not rule out a
combined centering/pivot algorithm.

Likewise a random low-dimensional restriction can miss the Lorentz cone.
For a uniform random s-dimensional subspace V and K=I-(1+gamma)uu^T,
its restriction is negative iff ||P_V u||^2>1/(1+gamma), whereas its
expected squared projection is s/m. Tiny gamma requires near-full
dimension with high probability. This is a rejection of RANDOM
RESTRICTION as the complete new mechanism, not a lower bound on raw
factor access or on algorithms that identify the centered rank-one part.

The live missing step is an input-optimal truncated-inertia construction
that either (a) finds a useful negative witness without approximating all
critical directions, or (b) certifies the critical bulk is flat enough
and then handles its centered small variation, without doing theta^-2
Gram sketching or theta^-1/2 full-input passes. Existing hierarchy tuning
does not meet this obligation. No claim that this scoped obstruction
completes the stronger research goal is intended.

The fixed even Neumann degree is O(log(1/theta)), with contraction <=1/3.
In particular (1/2)I<B_m<(3/2)I. The slightly sharper direct mass bound
||B_m-I||<=H+(1+H)e_m, together with the Q comparison, gives

  ||Kbar-S0||<=1/2, ||Kbar||<=3/2<2,
  D<=2(tB_m+Q).                                             (18)

Use a coarse positive threshold gamma in [3/4,7/8] instead of [8H,9H].
The same explicit LDL/orthogonalization gives the blocks (5), now with

  (1/4)I<=A<=2I, ||B||<=2.                                  (19)

  r_l(x)=T_l((9-8x)/7)/T_l(9/7),

Because r_l(0)=1, p_l is a polynomial, not a matrix solve. On [1/4,2],

  |p_l(x)-1/x|<=8rho_c^l, rho_c=(sqrt(8)-1)/(sqrt(8)+1)<1/2.

Thus l=ceil(log_2(16/delta)) gives error <=delta/2. Define

  T_-=p_l(A)-(delta/2)I, T_+=p_l(A)+(delta/2)I.              (20)

For delta<=1/8 these are positive and bracket A^-1, each within delta,

  S=S_+=C-B^T T_- B                                         (21)

satisfies ||S||<=2+4/(1/4)=18, and one action costs O(l) Kbar actions

10. Large critical bulk: certify a scalar-plus-low-rank Schur surrogate
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Lemma 2 (compression transfer). Suppose an explicitly represented

  S_tilde=a I_d+V C_s V^T, V^T V=I_s,
  ||S_tilde-S||<=tau,

has been constructed for (21). Define implicit scalar-plus-rank-s cores

  Slo=S_tilde-(tau+4delta)I,
  Shi=S_tilde+tau I.                                        (22)

Then there exist analytical full surrogates Klo,Khi such that

  Kbar-epsilon I<=U Klo U^T<=Kbar
     <=U Khi U^T<=Kbar+epsilon I,
  epsilon=2tau+8delta,                                      (23)

and n_-(Klo)=n_-(Slo), n_-(Khi)=n_-(Shi). No dense critical matrix
or inverse fine operator is needed to APPLY the resulting certificate.

Proof. Let S_-=C-B^T T_+B=S-delta B^T B. Thus
0<=S-S_-<=4delta I, and (22) gives Slo<=S_- and Shi>=S.
Define the critical block of Klo as C+Slo-S_-, with the same off-diagonal
B and far block T_+^-1. Define that of Khi as C+Shi-S, with far block
T_-^-1. These are analytical definitions, not computational requirements.
Their Schurs are EXACTLY Slo and Shi. The critical change in Klo is
negative semidefinite with norm at most 2tau+4delta; its far change is
negative semidefinite with norm <=4delta. The analogous changes in Khi
are positive with norms <=2tau and <=8delta. This proves (23).

If n_-(Slo)<=1 and w^T Slo w<0, a hypothetical full negative witness
[w;-T_+Bw] maps under Klo to [Slo w;0]. Therefore (1) gives the actual
physical output space

  range(Z J_D U_C Slo w).                                   (24)

The hypothetical far witness is NEVER formed. If Slo is PSD, output zero.
This is the useful escape from needing to invert or diagonalize the full
critical operator. Only its scalar-plus-low-rank approximation is used.

Inertia is inexpensive and exact on the represented surrogate:

 n_-(aI+VC_sV^T+zI)
    =n_-(C_s+(a+z)I_s)+(d-s)1_{a+z<0}.                     (25)

The zero case is handled exactly. Small-core LDL gives negative vectors
in range(V). If a+z<0, missing negative directions come from ker(V^T),
and fresh projected Gaussian vectors span the requested finite number
almost surely; exact rank is checked. This does not require selecting
precise small-core eigenvectors. A single direction is needed in (24).

11. A finite adaptive surrogate algorithm and a BEST-center rank bound
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The next theorem describes the actual centered approximation construction.
It is not necessary to assume that a useful scalar center is supplied.

For the matrix S in (21) and nu>0, define the intrinsic centered rank

  c_nu(S)=min{rank L: S=a0 I+L+E, L=L^T, ||E||<=nu}.         (26)

Equivalently minimize, over real a0, the number of eigenvalues of S
outside [a0-nu,a0+nu]. The algorithm need not compute this minimum.
It uses a posteriori residual tests, and its cost is bounded by this
quantity on the good event. In particular c_nu=0 for a flat bulk and
c_nu<=ell for a flat bulk with ell arbitrary positive OR negative spikes.

A naive ONE-Rayleigh center is insufficient: one O(1) spike biases a
typical Rayleigh by O(1/d), which is already theta when d=m,theta=1/m.
The additive target below is theta/constant, so that bias can make a truly
flat bulk appear numerically full rank. The following median fix avoids
that flaw.

Lemma 3 (compressed-median centering). If S=a0I+L+E,
rank(L)<=ell and ||E||<=nu, then for EVERY orthonormal W with b columns,
b>=2ell+1, each middle eigenvalue of W^T S W is in [a0-nu,a0+nu].

Proof. W^T L W has rank <=ell, so at most ell eigenvalues of this
compression can lie above a0+nu and at most ell below a0-nu, by min--max.
For b>=2ell+1 a middle index avoids both sets. This holds for any W,
not just a favorable random compression. A median computed to additive
nu therefore gives |a-a0|<=2nu. In particular S-aI has at most ell
singular values larger than 3nu.

Pseudocode for Surrogate(S,d,tau,beta):

  (i) Try s=0 first, and then s=1,2,4,..., capped at d.
      If 2s+1>=d, form S explicitly on the d coordinate RHS, use it
      exactly as S_tilde, and exit. Its cost is within the final cap.

  (ii) Otherwise form an orthonormal W with b=2s+1 columns (coordinate
       columns suffice), compute W^T S W, and find its middle eigenvalue
       to absolute nu=tau/512 by bounded bisection/inertia. The initial
       bracket [-18,18] and a fixed O(log(1/tau)) cap suffice. Call it a.
       Inertia/LDL is ordinary small dense linear algebra; no ordered
       eigenvalue oracle or gap condition is assumed. Put E_a=S-aI.

  (iii) If s>0, draw independent Gaussian Omega of width s, apply a fixed
        power E_a^q to it, and orthonormalize to obtain V. Use exact rank
        revelation if dependencies occur. Form C_s=V^T E_a V and set
        S_tilde=aI+VC_sV^T. For s=0 use S_tilde=aI.

  (iv) Draw a FRESH Gaussian g and run the norm test below on the actual
       self-adjoint residual R=S-S_tilde. If its returned upper bound
       Gamma<=tau, accept. Otherwise continue to the next trial width.
       All trials, their powers and their failure allocations are capped.

The median step uses an approximate numerical eigenvalue ONLY for a SMALL
explicit core, with additive bounded-spectrum accuracy. Exact sign counts
and field bisection implement it finitely, with logarithmic accuracy cost.
It is not a hidden small-tail eigenvalue oracle for S itself.

Norm test. Pick a power of two u at least

  log_2(100 sqrt(d)/beta_test^(3/2)).

Compute y=R^u g, and return

  Gamma=(100 ||y||/beta_test)^(1/u),                         (27)

using repeated positive square roots. For a top-absolute-eigenvalue unit
vector v of the conditionally fixed R, Gaussian anticoncentration gives
|v^T g|>=beta_test/100 except probability <=beta_test. Hence
||R||<=Gamma on this event. Independently, Markov gives
||g||<=sqrt(d/beta_test) except probability beta_test. Thus, simultaneously,

  ||R||<=Gamma<=2||R||.                                    (28)

When R=0 the test returns zero exactly. Scaled power iteration can avoid
representational overflow, but (27) is the finite exact-arithmetic object.
Freshness matters: a g reused to BUILD V would not justify this bound.

Range-finding degree and proof. On the coarse event, |a|<=18 and
||E_a||<=36. Let P_G=(100d/beta_range)^3, and choose

  q >= log_2(16*36*P_G/tau).                                (29)

The Gaussian argument from the companion can be proved directly. Let
H0={|lambda(E_a)|>tau/64} and J={|lambda(E_a)|>tau/32}.
If s>=|H0|, the Gaussian head has full row rank, with pseudoinverse
norm at most C d^(3/2)/beta_range, while the Gaussian tail Frobenius
norm is at most sqrt(ds/beta_range), except total probability O(beta_range).
Use the inverse head to represent the strong eigenvectors in J EXACTLY
in their head coordinates and zero out the weak head coordinates. The
tail leakage after q powers is <=2^-q P_G. Therefore for P=VV^T,

 ||(I-P)E_a|| <=36*2^-q P_G+tau/32 <tau/8,
 ||E_a-P E_a P|| <=2||(I-P)E_a|| <tau/4.                    (30)

This is an operator approximation, not a per-vector or Frobenius claim.
Signs of eigenvalues do not matter because the argument uses absolute
powers. Norms of intermediate powers may be extremely small or large;
no finite-word stability is asserted. QR after each power leaves the
represented range unchanged and is the natural conventional realization.

If s>=c_nu(S), nu=tau/512, Lemma 3 ensures |H0|<=s, because 3nu<tau/64.
Thus (30) and the independent norm test accept with high probability.
Doubling then bounds the accepted width by O(c_nu(S)+1), unless the
full-core branch is reached; in that case d<=4c_nu(S)+3, so the same
bound holds. Allocate beta/(C(j+1)^2) over trials and independent range/
norm events; there are at most 2+ceil(log_2 d) trials. All logarithms in
(27)--(29) are dimension, theta and beta logarithms. The algorithm always
terminates, using full formation at its final trial even on failures.

12. A general adaptive threshold theorem, with conventional costs
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Set

  tau=theta/64, delta=theta/512,
  epsilon=2tau+8delta=3theta/64,
  rho=(1+2epsilon)/(1-2epsilon).                             (31)

At each threshold compute its coarse far/critical decomposition; call
Surrogate; and use (22),(25) for the BELOW/ABOVE/NEAR oracle of Section 5.
Use the initial bracket [rho0/(4k),4k rho0] from the explicit Q_0
coefficient ratio rho0=e_2(Q_0)/e_1(Q_0). It is valid at H=1/4,e_m<=1/64.
Bisect at most ceil(log_2(256k^2/theta))+1 times, or until b<=rho*ell.
In a NEAR branch, inflate to t*rho^2 and make the final surrogate/witness
call. At a final bracket exit, use t_out=b*rho^2. As in (13), its lower
surrogate has index <=1, so the actual output (24) exists. Any larger
coarse positive margin or smaller approximation residual may only help.

Correctness. The full surrogate gap is epsilon D by (23), so Section 5
applies verbatim. The final threshold is at most rho^3 lambda_2(Hhat).
For theta in (0,1], direct expansion gives rho^3<=1+theta (with epsilon
as in (31)). The physical mass factor is

 (1+e_m)/(1-e_m)<=1+theta/31<=1+theta.

Thus the squared residual is at most (1+theta)^2 lambda_2(H), giving
the requested singular-error factor 1+theta. The same statement applies
to arbitrary fixed inputs, with failure <=delta_total after allocation
to coarse sketches and all capped fresh residual tests/range calls. The
conditioned analysis at adaptive thresholds is legitimate: the norm-test
and range randomness is fresh conditional on the entire preceding state.

Cost. For each executed threshold t let c(t)=c_(theta/32768)(S_t).
This is a diagnostic complexity measure, NOT an assumed rank bound. Put

  c_* = max over all executed/final thresholds c(t).

On the same event, the build bound is

  ~O(I + m^(omega+zeta) + Acal(c_*+1) + Acal(1)),             (32)

with padding at width m and dimension/confidence/accuracy logarithms.
The full core threshold algebra and all orthogonalizations cost m^omega
per call and hence only a logarithmic overhead in (32). The s RHS
applications of the Schur use the SAME degree-l far inverse polynomial,
so a block action costs ~Acal(s), not s separate adaptive solves. A full
trial at width d is covered when c_* is comparable to d. The runtime
on any failed event is capped by the full-core (exact-Gram-scale) route;
only the instance-sensitive bound (32) is a high-probability bound.

Using conventional arithmetic instead gives

  ~O(I + m^3 + I(c_*+1)) build, plus O(np) rank-one expansion. (33)

Here all m-by-s maps cost <=m^2 s, all thin QR steps cost <=m s^2,
and s^3<=m^2 s; these are <=I s since n,p>=m and s<=m. The fixed
Chebyshev far degree is ceil(log_2(8192/theta)). No fast MM, spectral
function oracle, or asymptotically enormous exponent construction is
needed for (33). The actual norm/capture constants still deserve a
numerical-analysis treatment before calling this a stable algorithm.
Standard QR/Cholesky/LDL, blocked matrix products, and fixed polynomial
recurrences are the intended conventional operations.

The adaptive theorem automatically handles:
  * a small coarse critical block (c_*<=max d(t));
  * an arbitrarily large critical block with a nearly flat Schur bulk;
  * that bulk plus a few positive or negative Schur spikes.
It does not need to identify which situation occurs in advance. It is
correct for all other inputs too, but then (32) may have c_*=Theta(m).

Original hard case: if c_*<=m^.85 throughout the capped search, (32)
attains build m^(9/4+o(1)) and explicit output N^(2+o(1)) at theta=1/m.
This is genuinely broader than the small-d condition of Section 6, but
still an INSTANCE-SENSITIVE theorem. There is no proof that c_* is small
for unrestricted AB. The original unpromised target is still open.

13. A combined counterexample to the currently proposed easy exits
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The following family simultaneously defeats "low negative index implies
small critical dimension", "few scalar-centered Schur spikes", and
"a random small restriction finds the negative witness". It is NOT an
algorithm lower bound, and raw-factor access might yet exploit it.

Let D=V diag(d_i) V^T with 1<=d_i<=2 spread through the interval, and
choose an independent uniform unit vector u. For eta much smaller than
the coarse distortion, define

  K=eta D^1/2 [I-(1+gamma)uu^T] D^1/2, gamma>0.             (34)

It has exactly one negative eigenvalue, all positive bulk eigenvalues
are spread on scale eta, and for theta<<eta a best scalar-centered
approximation to error O(theta) can still have rank Theta(m). Taking
eta below the coarse critical margin makes the whole block critical.
For any s-dimensional subspace W independent of u conditional on D,
its restriction has a negative vector exactly when

  ||P_range(D^1/2 W) u||^2 > 1/(1+gamma).                    (35)

The mean projection is s/m. For small gamma, a small random subspace
misses the negative cone. Rank-one inertia alone therefore supplies no
dimension or centered-rank bound. If V were the coordinate basis, one
could exploit diagonal centering; take generic V to avoid mistaking that
special algebraic representation for a universal shortcut.

This can be realized by actual factors with no conditioning pathology:
take physical target H=I-K, which is positive definite for eta small,
choose an orthonormal tall A, and B with BB^T=H. Theta-scale negative
curvature is obtained by gamma of order theta/eta, up to the bounded
D congruence constants. Thus the unresolved case is compatible with
well-conditioned factors, an arbitrarily flat-looking coarse spectrum,
and only one truly needed output direction.

NEXT OBLIGATION. Exploit raw factors to find or exclude that Lorentz-cone
witness without constructing the broad positive Schur bulk. The new
conditional/adaptive theorem does not settle this remaining class.

14. Fixed rank-r extension and an explicit conventional algorithm
----------------------------------------------------------------
The one-negative-direction geometry extends without greedy deflation.
If K has q<=r negative eigenvalues and W^T K W is negative definite of
order q, inertia additivity gives

 K-KW(W^T K W)^-1 W^T K >=0.                                (36)

For the lower full surrogate of Lemma 2, take all q negative directions
W_c of Slo and analytically extend to [W_c;-T_+B W_c]. Its dual images
are [Slo W_c;0]. Thus the output space is

 range(Z J_D U_C Slo W_c), width q<=r.                       (37)

This is a simultaneous certificate, with NO r-fold greedy precision loss.
Use j=r+1 instead of 2 in the initial coefficient ratio and compare each
inertia count to r. The same scalar shift inequalities and final inflation
apply unchanged. If k<=r the exact-support branch handles a zero tail.
Every oracle failure or unexpected final count is guarded by returning
zero on that outcome; it does not trigger an unbounded search or an
uncontrolled division. This guard is irrelevant on the correctness event.

The fast build bound becomes

  ~O(I+m^(omega+zeta)+Acal(c_*+1)+Acal(r)),                   (38)

plus np+T_MM(n,r,p) for all explicit output entries. Classical arithmetic
gives ~O(I+m^3+I(c_*+r+1)) construction and O(npr) expansion. In (26),
the scalar-plus-rank parameter is still the best centered rank of the
whole critical Schur; it is NOT automatically bounded by the requested
output rank r. In particular (38) is not the proposed unrestricted Ir
theorem. It is a checkable instance-sensitive interpolation to exact cores.

Conventional ThresholdSchur(A,B,r,theta,delta_total):

  1. Form constant-distortion sketches of the two factors, support/whiten
     the left factor, restrict to product support, form Q_0. If k<=r,
     return the exact product factors. Store only core maps and raw A,B.

  2. Fix the even inverse-mass polynomial B_m once, with relative error
     theta/64. Obtain the initial positive bracket for lambda_(r+1)
     from Q_0, using a characteristic coefficient ratio and core inertia.

  3. At each capped bisection threshold t, factor D=tI+Q_0. Compute a
     positive subspace of S0-gamma I, gamma in [3/4,7/8], by pivoted
     symmetric LDL; orthogonalize it and its complement. Define the
     Schur operator (21) using the FIXED Chebyshev inverse (20).

  4. Use median-compression/rank-doubling Surrogate from Section 11.
     Accept only after its fresh residual upper test is <=theta/64.
     Count inertia of the scalar-plus-small-core Slo and Shi by (25).
     If n_-(Shi)>r set the lower threshold; if n_-(Slo)<=r set the
     upper threshold; otherwise use the inflated NEAR exit.

  5. At the final inflated upper threshold, get all negative directions
     W_c of Slo, form the physical vectors in (37), thin QR them, and
     return C=P_physical AB via factor actions. Explicitly form its
     n-by-p entries with classical or fast rank-r multiplication.

All heavy operations here are ordinary matrix products, QR/Cholesky,
symmetric pivoted LDL, Gaussian test vectors, and bounded-spectrum
fixed-degree Chebyshev recurrences. Ordinary LDL/charpoly costs are cubic
in the SMALL core; no fast matrix multiplication is required to state
or run the conventional theorem. A tuned implementation could reuse
coarse decompositions and reduce calls, but no such speedup is charged.

Comparison with the GENERIC worst-case Krylov bound only:
if c_*+r is bounded while theta decreases polynomially, (33)/(38) have
only logarithmic accuracy factors, whereas the generic ordinary-Krylov
construction contains I r/sqrt(theta). The error guarantee and output
rank are the same. Exact QR/core-SVD still costs O((n+p)m^2+m^3), while
this class uses input-linear factor processing plus m^3 and small-rank
actions. These are comparisons of stated upper bounds in the indicated
class, not an empirical hardware claim or a global SOTA certificate.
Sections 18--19 give the stronger clustered-Krylov comparison hazard:
they may MATCH this new profile cost. Thus this paragraph does not
establish a new asymptotic frontier over adaptive classical methods.

Finite precision limitation. Several operations need a separate robust
analysis: inertia near a zero pivot, whitening a nearly rank-deficient
factor, power-range QR, and one-sided inverse errors. The exact theorem
does NOT authorize an implementation to label a floating-point eigenvalue
as positive merely because it is numerically small. A stable version can
use certified additive residual bounds and include those errors in tau,
but such a theorem has not yet been supplied. The practical-theoretical
claim is that the building blocks and degree growth are standard and
moderate, not that all their finite-word certification is already proved.

15. Prior-art correction and scope of possible contribution
-----------------------------------------------------------
The parent identified van der Veen's 1996 Schur low-rank approximation
method (primary PDF https://sps.ewi.tudelft.nl/pubs/schur93.pdf), which
explicitly uses negative/positive spaces and hyperbolic QR to parameterize
spectral-error approximants. This reinforces that (1),(36), and the
negative-space geometry are CLASSICAL; they are not a novelty claim here.

The candidate contribution to audit is narrower: true-factor matrix-free
constant-conditioned far elimination, one-sided finite Schur surrogates,
and adaptive scalar-plus-low-rank compression certified by fresh norm

range finding and scalar-plus-low-rank algebra are themselves standard.
No inspected primary source has yet been shown to contain or exclude

16. Optional fully classical far basis and surrogate diagonalization
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The parent suggested a clean replacement for the far positive-space LDL.
It avoids sensitive exact pivot selection in the STANDARD-arithmetic
version while retaining the fast exact-field theorem as a separate option.

Lemma 4 (fixed-budget random-pair Jacobi). If S0 is symmetric with
||S0||<=1, there is a finite O(k^3 log(k/beta)) field/square-root routine
returning orthogonal Q_J and diagonal Lambda_J such that

  ||Q_J^T S0 Q_J-Lambda_J||<=1/16                            (39)

with probability >=1-beta. No eigenvalue gap or scale ratio is used.

Proof. Let e be the squared off-diagonal Frobenius norm, initially <=k.
A Jacobi rotation on a pair i<j zeros its off-diagonal entry and reduces
e EXACTLY by 2 a_ij^2. For a uniform pair, conditional expectation is

  E[e_next|current]=(1-2/[k(k-1)])e.

After M>=k(k-1) log(256k/beta)/2 rotations, E[e_M]<=beta/256.
Markov then gives e_M<=1/256 except probability beta, proving (39).
Each rotation and update of Q_J cost O(k). For k=1 use the exact scalar.
The 2-by-2 diagonalization uses only field operations and a positive
square root; ties/zero off-diagonals have explicit identity branches.
Uniform pairs can be generated with finite Gaussian draws if random bits
are not primitive: draw k independent Gaussians and choose the index of
the minimum, then draw k-1 fresh Gaussians on the remaining indices and
choose the other minimum. Symmetry gives a uniform ordered distinct pair;
draws/comparisons cost O(k), already within the rotation bound. Ties have
probability zero and any deterministic tie rule keeps the cap finite.

Select columns of Q_J whose diagonal Lambda_J entries are >=13/16.
By (39), on their span S0>=3/4 I. All other columns form U_C. Thus (19)
holds on the joint coarse/Jacobi event, without deciding any tiny true
eigenvalue or factoring an almost singular S0-gamma I. The resulting
critical profile c_*(t) refers to THIS actual random far basis; no claim
is made that it equals the exact spectral-threshold critical dimension.
Allocate beta over all capped threshold calls.

The same fixed-budget Jacobi argument works on each bounded small core
at absolute tolerance eta: replace beta/256 by beta*eta^2, and scale the
initial Frobenius bound by its known dimension and norm. Its O(s^3)
cost gains only logarithms in 1/eta and 1/beta. This can replace the
compressed-median eigenvalue bisection, since sorted approximate diagonal
entries differ from the true ordered eigenvalues by at most eta (Weyl).

It can also replace small-core LDL for S_tilde. Apply Jacobi to
C_s=V^T(S-aI)V to tolerance tau/32; let C_app=Q_c diag(c) Q_c^T be
the ACTUAL matrix represented by the approximate diagonalization. Use

  S_tilde=aI+(VQ_c)diag(c)(VQ_c)^T

in the fresh norm test. Projection error <tau/4 from (30), plus the
additional tau/32, leaves residual <9tau/32 and the norm upper test
<=9tau/16<tau. Therefore the numerical-rank acceptance proof still holds.
Inertia and negative basis of the represented Slo/Shi now use only the
signs of a+c_i+z and the scalar bulk sign. No actual-eigenvalue sign
approximation is asserted: these scalars define the NEW surrogate,
and the residual test absorbs its discrepancy from the true operator.

This classical variant uses standard QR, Cholesky, Jacobi and polynomial
matrix actions throughout its numerical subspace stages. The initial
condition-free bracket can still use exact characteristic coefficients
as in the main theorem; their classical construction has O(m^3) arithmetic
cost. The variant is NOT a stable finite-word theorem: QR/whitening near
rank loss and reliable residual upper arithmetic remain separate issues.
It merely removes an exact-eigenvector or tiny-LDL-pivot requirement from
the adaptive approximation mechanism, at the same conventional bound (33).

17. Strengthening the failure example; its first version is gap-easy
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The parent/independent reviewer correctly noted that (34) is NOT a hard
Krylov instance: its sole negative eigenvalue is isolated from positive
bulk on a CONSTANT fraction of the scale eta. Ordinary shifted Krylov
can find its negative vector in logarithmic degree. Furthermore t=1 in
that example is above the actual physical second eigenvalue by order eta,
not theta. Section 13 disproves only the three implications explicitly
listed there. It must not be used as evidence of a new runtime frontier.

A stronger test for the CURRENT easy-exit criteria is a generically
rotated matrix with spectrum

  -3theta, theta, and a dense positive grid from theta to eta,
  theta<<eta<<1.                                           (40)

Take H_phys=I-K. It is SPD, its top two eigenvalues are 1+3theta and
1-theta, and t=1 is now within O(theta) of the correct tail threshold.
With a constant-distortion coarse proxy, ||S0||<=1/2+O(eta)<3/4 for
small eta, so the exact coarse far-positive space of Section 9 can be
empty. The broad grid prevents a best scalar-plus-low-rank approximation
at tolerance O(theta) from having sublinear rank: for any bounded-
condition D congruence, separated lower/upper positive spectral quantiles
still force c_nu=Omega(m) (choose quantiles separated by more than that
fixed condition factor). The negative unit eigenvector u obeys

  K>=theta I-4theta uu^T.

Consequently every restriction W with ||P_W u||^2<=1/4 is PSD.
A small random subspace still fails to expose the negative witness.
For eta=m^-1/2 and theta=1/m, the plain width/gap Krylov upper bound
is sqrt(eta/theta)=m^1/4, above the target input-pass budget m^1/8.
This is NOT a proof that every polynomial or factor-aware method needs
that many actions. The displayed spectrum is a test family for future
algorithms, not a query lower bound or a solution of the research goal.

18. Strong novelty warning: clustered Krylov may match the new profile
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The parent's further audit points out a serious possible subsumption.
If the exact critical Schur has S_exact=aI+rank-s+O(theta), then

  Kbar=L diag(S_exact,A)L^T,
  A in [1/4,2], ||L|| and ||L^-1|| bounded by absolute constants.

For a>theta, the rank-zero positive bulk therefore has eigenvalues in
two CONSTANT-MULTIPLICATIVE bands [c a,C a] and [c,C], up to rank-s
outliers and O(theta) perturbation. If a<=O(theta), the small band is
already within the allowed additive gray band; if a<0 with multiplicity
larger than s+r, an r+1-negative-direction decision is easy in principle.

There is a polylogarithmic-degree polynomial that suppresses both
positive bands while remaining >=1 at every negative scalar. Normalize
a low-band Chebyshev filter p_low at zero. Degree q=O(log(1/epsilon))
suppresses [c a,C a], but its magnitude on the far band may grow as
(C'/a)^q. Multiply a normalized far-band Chebyshev filter p_far. It is
bounded by one between zero and the far band and exponentially small
on [c,C]; degree O(q log(1/a)+log(1/epsilon)) offsets all p_low growth.
For x<0 both normalized factors are >=1. All degrees are polylogarithmic
in m/theta, and a Gaussian block of width O(s+r) can force the positive
outlier head coordinates to zero while capturing strong negative modes.

The last step turning such a clustered-spectrum Krylov span into an
actual rank-r certified output still needs a precise standard theorem
or a local robust extraction proof; a width-(s+r) projector cannot simply
be returned as rank r. Nevertheless the construction already makes it
unsafe to claim that (38) improves the strongest adaptive/clustered
ordinary Krylov complexity on its own profile class. The new theorem is
currently an ALTERNATE CERTIFIER with a precise computable diagnostic,
not an established asymptotic frontier or a seminal breakthrough.

The unrestricted original problem remains open after this note. Future
work must exploit something beyond low index, scalar-centered rank,
coarse positive-block elimination, and generic clustered-polynomial
filtering, or prove a substantially stronger raw-factor truncated-inertia
construction. No further parameter tuning of the old isotropic hierarchy
is being offered as an answer to the stronger user goal.

19. Rank-r extraction lemma strengthens the clustered-Krylov concern
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The parent supplied the following local lemma; its short proof closes
the upper-threshold rank truncation issue left in Section 18.

Let T be PSD, ||T||<=2, and lambda_(r+1)(T)<=1. Put B=T^2. Let E
have orthonormal columns spanning the true B-eigenspace above 1+zeta,
so width(E)<=r. Suppose Q is orthonormal and
||(I-QQ^T)E||<=epsilon. Let U be the top-r Ritz space of Q^T B Q,
lifted into the ambient space. Then

  ||(I-P_U)T||^2 <=1+zeta+4(epsilon+4epsilon/zeta)^2.        (41)

Proof. Write B E=E Lambda, Lambda>(1+zeta)I. If A_Q=Q^T B Q
and X=Q^T E, then

  A_Q X-X Lambda=-Q^T B(I-QQ^T)E,

with norm <=4epsilon. The lower Ritz block after removing its top r
eigenvalues has norm/eigenvalues <=1 by min--max, since the ambient
(r+1)-st B eigenvalue is <=1. Project onto that lower block. The ordered
Sylvester separation by zeta gives solution norm <=4epsilon/zeta.
For completeness this follows by the semigroup integral solution:
after shifting the lower spectrum to <=0 and the upper to >=zeta,
the norm integrand is at most exp(-zeta t)||R||. Thus the true E space
has leakage from U at most epsilon+4epsilon/zeta. Finally

  B<=(1+zeta)I+4P_E,

and restrict this inequality to U^perp. This proves (41). In particular
epsilon<=c zeta^(3/2) makes the residual <=1+O(zeta). There is no
unjustified monotonicity of a rank-r truncation of an enlarged basis.

The two-band polynomial in Section 18 can achieve such capture with
polylogarithmic degree and Gaussian width O(s+r). Thus even the RANK-r
upper-threshold output of our profile theorem is likely achievable by
ordinary clustered Krylov in the same arithmetic order. A complete
global below/near threshold wrapper still needs to be written if this
is to be used as a formal subsumption theorem; it is already enough to
withdraw any frontier novelty claim for the current adaptive profile.

20. Direct dual-space extraction without tiny surrogate eigenvalues
------------------------------------------------------------------
In the classical Jacobi variant the actual Slo has a KNOWN orthonormal
negative eigenspace W_-. Since Slo maps this space invertibly onto itself,
span(Slo W_-)=span(W_-). Therefore use

  range(Z J_D U_C W_-)

directly instead of scaling its columns by possibly tiny negative
eigenvalues. This avoids dividing by or propagating a tiny negative
Rayleigh pivot in the physical lift. A direct square completion proves
the claim: for the lower full surrogate,

  [x;y]^T Klo [x;y]
    =x^T Slo x+(y+T_+ Bx)^T T_+^-1(y+T_+Bx).

If x is orthogonal to W_-, both terms are nonnegative. No inverse T_+
is computed in obtaining the output; it appears only in the identity.

If weak negative modes of Slo in [-tau,0) are omitted, the same argument
gives lower bound -tau||[x;y]||^2 on the corresponding complement.
This charges an additional tau D weighted error, which the constants
can absorb. This observation improves the numerical specification of
the alternate certifier but does not change its complexity frontier.

21. Further open escape: the dual cone and a resolvent dimension
----------------------------------------------------------------
For a NONSINGULAR symmetric index-one K, K is PSD on g^perp exactly when

  g^T K^-1 g<=0, g!=0.                                     (42)

This follows from inertia of the bordered form, or directly from a
Lorentzian diagonalization. For K=diag(-a,b_2,...,b_m), a,b_i>0, a
standard Gaussian g defines a valid hyperplane precisely when

  g_1^2 >= a sum_{i=2}^m g_i^2/b_i.                         (43)

The relevant difficulty can thus be a RESOLVENT effective dimension
a tr(K_+^-1), rather than Euclidean eigenvector overlap. The dual cone
can be wide while the primal negative-witness cone is narrow. However,
at a valid tail threshold some positive b_i can be O(theta), so the
dimension in (43) is not universally small. More importantly, selecting
or certifying a successful random g requires the sign in (42), an
indefinite inverse or equivalent certificate. No target-cost selector
has been proved. Repeating untested random outputs is NOT amplification,
and combining them would violate the requested rank-one width.

This remains a possible route for a new raw-factor truncated-inertia
method. It is recorded as a precise missing oracle, not a completed
algorithm or another claimed solution of the original target.
