Hierarchical regularized absolute pencils: finite invariant and scoped limit
Independent exact-arithmetic derivation, 2026-11-14

STATUS

There is a positive two-level construction, and a fixed-depth extension,
that preserves the alpha*h strong-block transfer. The right invariant is
RELAXED: F-kappa K F^(-1)K<=C h D for a fixed kappa>1, together with
+-K<F and a positive hD floor. Requiring the original kappa=1 weighted
defect at every level is unnecessarily restrictive.

The direct uniform polynomial refinement costs ~H/h, rather than the
desired ~sqrt(H/h). An elementary degree lower bound proves that the
square-root ratio cannot hold for this class of uniform polynomial
congruences, even on a commuting, realizable coarse-proxy family. This
is a scoped obstruction, not a lower bound for rational, adaptive, or
other factor-access algorithms.

Despite the worse ratio, the proved hierarchy expands the growing-rank
output-optimal accuracy regime. A concrete two-level example below uses
N=m^(59/50), theta=m^(-11/25), and every r<=m^(14/25), and still has
construction N^(6724/3363+o(1)) and explicit total N^(2+o(1)). The
original N=m^(9/8), theta=m^(-1) target remains out of reach of this
isotropic-sketch hierarchy, even under the more optimistic desired ratio.

No manuscript/state file was edited. All claims are in the same model as
docs/absolute-pencil-spectral.tex: exact field operations, positive scalar
square roots, comparisons, and Gaussian sampling. There is no finite-word
stability or global novelty claim.


1. FIXED TRUE PENCIL AND MATRIX-FREE FINE PROXIES

Use the coarse H-accurate product support from the verified manuscript:

  M=Z L B_orig, Z=A W, G=Z^T Z,
  Q=L B_orig B_orig^T L^T, Q0=L Qbar_0 L^T,
  I/(1+H)<=G<=I/(1-H), (1-H)Q<=Q0<=(1+H)Q,
  D(t)=tI+Q0.

Fix 0<theta<=h<=H/64 and H<=1/64. Choose the EVEN true-factor Neumann
polynomial B_m approximating G^(-1) relatively to
epsilon_m=theta/10^9. It is fixed at every threshold. Define

  K(t)=tB_m-Q.

The analytical target is Hhat=B_m^(-1/2)Q B_m^(-1/2), with eigenvalues
within factors 1+-O(epsilon_m) of the true squared singular values.
No true Gram or true matrix root is formed.

At fine accuracy h, store only the sketched factors A_h=S_h A and
B_h=B_orig T_h^T. Their simultaneous Loewner event yields operators

  G_h=W^T A_h^T A_h W, Q_h=L B_h B_h^T L^T,
  (1-h)G<=G_h<=(1+h)G,
  (1-h)Q<=Q_h<=(1+h)Q.

Coarse whitening gives ||I-G_h||<=2H<1. A fixed EVEN Neumann polynomial
B_hmass=sum_{i=0}^{l_h}(I-G_h)^i approximates G_h^(-1) relatively to
h/100, with l_h=O(log(1/h)). It is applied through the fine factors,
not through A or B_orig. Set

  K_h(t)=t B_hmass-Q_h.

Inverse Loewner comparison and the Neumann errors give, conservatively,

  -8hD<=E:=K_h-K<=8hD.                                  (1)

For detail, B_hmass differs from G^(-1) relatively by at most 2h, and
B_m differs relatively by epsilon_m<<h; since G^(-1)<=1+H, their
Euclidean difference is bounded by a small constant times hI.
The right-proxy error is bounded by hQ<=hQ0/(1-H)<=2hQ0.
These imply (1) with substantial slack. All comparisons hold uniformly
for EVERY t>0. There is no fine true-factor norm/bracket computation.

Write

  Acal(b)=T_MM(n,m,b)+T_MM(p,m,b),
  J_h(b)=T_MM(s_A(h),m,b)+T_MM(s_B(h),m,b),
  s_A(h)=min(n,~O(m/h^2)), s_B(h)=min(p,~O(m/h^2)).

The smaller core products are bounded by these costs, since n,p and
the sketch dimensions are at least m. K_h block actions cost ~J_h(b);
K block actions cost ~Acal(b). Fine embeddings cost ~I to apply, but
NO fine Gram is formed. The identity fallback is explicit in J_h.


2. THE RELAXED QUADRATIC INVARIANT IS SUFFICIENT

Let F be any SPD actual effective metric satisfying

  -F<K<F,
  F-kappa K F^(-1)K<=C hD,                               (2)

with fixed kappa>=1 and C>0. The second left side need not be PSD.
The ordinary defect Delta=F-KF^(-1)K is PSD, but may be much larger
than hD on directions far from the threshold. This is allowed.

2.1 Strong-action transfer

For any inverse factor R with F=R^(-T)R^(-1), set
C_F=R^T K R and T=I-C_F. Suppose U is orthonormal and

  ||(I-UU^T)T||<=1+c_alpha alpha,
  b=(1+c_alpha alpha)^2-1,
  kappa*b<=1.

For w perpendicular to U, symmetry gives
w^T(C_F^2-2C_F)w<=b||w||^2. Writing w=R^(-1)y,

  2y^T K y>=y^T K F^(-1)K y-b y^T F y
    >=(1-kappa b)y^T K F^(-1)K y-b C h y^T D y
    >=-b C h y^T D y.                                   (3)

Thus the alpha*h gain survives, with constants depending on kappa,C.
The physical dual subspace is range(Z R^(-T)U); the inverse factor in
this lift is a genuine computational obligation addressed in Section 6.

2.2 Quantile min--max also survives

For alpha sufficiently small relative to kappa, put
beta_alpha=alpha/(1-kappa alpha^2). Then

  c<=beta_alpha(1-kappa c^2) for every c in [-1,alpha].    (4)

Proof: beta_alpha*kappa*c^2+c-beta_alpha is convex, vanishes at c=alpha,
and is nonpositive at c=-1 when beta_alpha(kappa-1)<=1. For example
alpha<=min(1/10,1/(4kappa)) suffices. Apply (4) on the bottom-j C_F
eigenspace if its j-th increasing eigenvalue is <=alpha. Undoing R and
using (2) gives K<=beta_alpha*C*hD on a dimension-j subspace.
Since D<=2(tB_m+Q), generalized min--max bounds lambda_j(Hhat) below
by t times 1-O(C alpha h). The opposite side follows similarly if
needed. The global bisection wrapper and its uncertain exit therefore
still work at resolution alpha=Theta(theta/h). No spectral gap or
lambda_1/lambda_j ratio is introduced.

2.3 Finite relative perturbations preserve (2), without commutation

If (1-delta)F_*<=Fbar<=(1+delta)F_* and
F_*-kappa K F_*^(-1)K<=C hD, then

  Fbar-kappa(1+delta)^2 K Fbar^(-1)K
    <=(1+delta)[F_*-kappa K F_*^(-1)K]
    <=(1+delta)C hD.                                    (5)

This follows only from inversion order and congruence; K,F_*,Fbar need
not commute. If BOTH F_*-K>=c hD and F_*+K>=c hD, and F_*<=B D,
taking delta=O(h/B) preserves domination and the floor. No inference
F_*>|K| is made for noncommuting K and F_*.
Equation (5) is the required finite relative/quadratic invariant.


3. A COMPLETE TWO-LEVEL PRECONDITIONER LEMMA

Assume the explicit coarse F0=J0 J0^T from the verified absolute-pencil
construction has, at every t>0,

  8HD<=F0<=2D, +-K<F0,
  0<=F0-KF0^(-1)K<=36HD.                                (6)

Put eps=h/H<=1/64 and P=(1+eps)F0=J_P J_P^T. By (1), +-K_h<P.
The normalized fine operator C_h=J_P^(-1)K_h J_P^(-T) is symmetric
with spectrum in [-1,1]. This is the ONLY scalar-functional commutation
used below: functions of C_h commute with C_h. No D/error commutation
is assumed.

3.1 A Young inequality controls the noncommuting fine-proxy error

Since F0>=8HD and -8hD<=E<=8hD,

  E F0^(-1)E<=8 h^2/H D.

The quadratic Young inequality with parameter eps gives

  K F0^(-1)K
   <=(1+eps)K_h F0^(-1)K_h
      +(1+1/eps) E F0^(-1)E.

Combining this with (6) yields

  P-kappa_P K_h P^(-1)K_h<=50HD,
  kappa_P=(1+eps)^3<=2.                                (7)

The weighted constant is safely below 50: the error term before the
outer factor 1+eps is 8(eps+eps^2)H, added to 36H. This handles the
otherwise dangerous cross terms without replacing them by a spurious
O(h/sqrt(H))D norm error.

3.2 An ideal function, used only to prove the actual finite approximation

Set eta=8eps and f_eta(c)=sqrt(c^2+eta^2)+eta. Define analytically

  F_*=J_P f_eta(C_h)J_P^T.

For eta<=1/8, f_eta>=|c|+eta, min(f_eta)=2eta, and f_eta<=1+2eta<=5/4.
Consequently

  F_*>=128hD, F_*<=3D, F_*+-K>=56hD.                    (8)

The exact scalar identity f_eta-c^2/f_eta=2eta gives

  F_*-K_hF_*^(-1)K_h=2eta P.

By (7) and F_*<=5P/4, this implies

  F_*-2K_hF_*^(-1)K_h<=800hD.

Use K_h=K+E, quadratic Young with parameter one, and
E F_*^(-1)E<=hD/2 from (8). It follows that

  F_*-4KF_*^(-1)K<=802hD.                              (9)

The original kappa=1 weighted defect is generally NOT small here:
2eta P is of order (h/H)F0, and F0 can be order D on far-spectrum
directions. Equation (9), rather than a false O(hD) claim for that
defect, is the point of the relaxed invariant.

3.3 The actual finite polynomial metric

Compute a real polynomial p with

  |p(c)*sqrt(f_eta(c))-1|<=tau, tau=h/10^6,
  c in [-1,1].

Section 4 gives a finite construction of degree ~eta^(-1). Apply

  R=J_P^(-T)p(C_h), Fbar=R^(-T)R^(-1)

only as operators. Neither Fbar nor p(C_h)^(-1) is formed. Functional
calculus gives (1-4tau)F_*<=Fbar<=(1+4tau)F_*. Equations (5),(8),(9)
therefore prove ACTUAL finite inequalities

  100hD<=Fbar<=4D, Fbar+-K>=55hD,
  Fbar-5K Fbar^(-1)K<=1000hD.                           (10)

All constants are deliberately loose. This is a full two-level lemma,
uniform in every positive threshold and all multiplicities. It does not
assume an exact absolute value or matrix square root.


4. FINITE POLYNOMIAL CONSTRUCTION AND ITS TRUE DEGREE

The needed scalar function is g_eta(z)=(sqrt(z^2+eta^2)+eta)^(-1/2).
Take the positive branches on the real interval. On the Bernstein ellipse
with parameter rho=1+eta/4, the imaginary part has magnitude at most
eta/4. There Re(z^2+eta^2)>=15eta^2/16>0, so both principal branches
are analytic and |g_eta(z)|<=eta^(-1/2).

The Laurent representation under z=(w+w^(-1))/2 and Cauchy's estimate
bound the Chebyshev coefficients by 2 eta^(-1/2)rho^(-k). Interpolate
at N dyadic Gauss--Chebyshev nodes, with N a power of two. Chebyshev
aliasing and the geometric tail give uniform interpolation error at most

  24 eta^(-3/2)rho^(-N).

Choose N by repeated squaring of rho^(-N) until it is at most
tau*eta*sqrt(eta)/1000. Then N=O(eta^(-1)log(1/(tau*eta))). The error
is at most tau/2 and min_{[-1,1]}g_eta>=1/sqrt(1+2eta)>0.8, so the
desired relative tau guarantee holds and p is positive.

The interpolation polynomial is an ACTUAL finite arithmetic object:
N is dyadic; its Chebyshev nodes are real parts of dyadic roots of unity,
constructed by positive half-angle square roots and complex-pair field
operations. Each node value uses two positive scalar square roots and
division. A dyadic DCT, implemented by the usual divide-and-conquer DFT
recurrence, computes coefficients in O(N log N) real operations once
those roots are available. Equivalently, the elementary quadratic DCT
could be charged explicitly, but the divide-and-conquer algorithm removes
that unnecessary scalar cost. No cosine, integration, eigenvalue, exact
matrix-root, or coefficient oracle is invoked.

Chebyshev recurrence applies p(C_h) using N fine-operator actions on a
block. In this model the cost is therefore

  degree(p)=~O(H/h), NOT ~O(sqrt(H/h)).                   (11)

The function has complex branch points at +-i eta, an interior absolute-
value smoothing scale. Section 9 proves that the loss in (11) is
unavoidable for the stated class of uniform polynomial congruences.

6. THE DUAL LIFTING OBLIGATION AND ITS EXACT PREIMAGE SOLUTION

Naively copying the rank-one inverse-root hierarchy leaves a real gap:
R=J_P^(-T)p(C_h), but the physical strong-certificate lift is
Z R^(-T)U=Z J_P p(C_h)^(-1)U. An approximate inverse polynomial in
that output can destroy the exact dual complement and can introduce a
leading-to-tail sensitivity. One may NOT omit or silently approximate it.

There is an exact algebraic solution that uses only forward polynomials.
Put Pp=p(C_h), A0=J_P^(-1)K J_P^(-T), so T=I-Pp A0 Pp.
For the FINAL projection filter take a fresh standard Gaussian Xi and
use the elliptical start Omega=Pp Xi. Define

  S0=I-A0 Pp^2,
  Wout=filter(S0)Xi.

The intertwining T Pp=Pp S0 proves EXACTLY

  Yout=filter(T)Omega=Pp Wout.

Thus the desired physical dual space is exactly

  range(Z J_P Pp^(-1)Yout)=range(Z J_P Wout).              (12)

No inverse Pp is computed. Select any independent columns of Wout by
core CUP and orthonormalize their physical lift by fast Gram--Cholesky.
The orthogonal projector onto range(Yout) exists for the proof but need
not be explicitly constructed by the algorithm.

The nonuniform start changes only logarithms in the elementary filter
lemma. Uniform scalar approximation gives

  kappa(Pp)=O(eta^(-1/2))=O(sqrt(H/h)).

In a T eigenbasis, any head row covariance of Omega=Pp Xi has eigenvalues
between sigma_min(Pp)^2 and sigma_max(Pp)^2. The square-head least singular
value is therefore at least sigma_min(Pp) times that of a standard
Gaussian square matrix. The tail Frobenius norm is bounded using
sigma_max(Pp)||Xi||_F. A union bound, with no independence assumption
between these blocks, gives

  ||Omega_tail Omega_head^dagger||
     <=poly(k,kappa(Pp),1/beta)

with failure at most beta. This only changes the logarithm in the
Chebyshev degree. The quantile procedure still uses independent STANDARD
Gaussian starts on T; only this final filter uses the carried preimage.

S0 can be nonnormal, but is similar to T through Pp. The exact recurrence
and identity (12) are the proof, rather than an unjustified symmetric-
Lanczos claim for S0. Its actions cost the same two Pp polynomials and
one true A0 action as the symmetric T action. Its norm/recurrence growth
may depend on eta, but not on an input singular-value ratio. No numerical
stability claim is made.

8. FIXED-DEPTH EXTENSION: PRECISE INDUCTIVE INVARIANT

For any FIXED depth ell, suppose 0<h_ell<...<h_0<=1/64,
h_j/h_(j-1)<=1/1024, and theta<=h_ell. Fine inverse-mass/proxy
construction in Section 1 gives the same error +-8h_jD at every level.
The mass B_m is the ONE true-factor polynomial throughout all levels.

An inductive sufficient invariant for actual finite F_j is

  8h_jD<=F_j<=B_jD, +-K<F_j,
  F_j-kappa_j K F_j^(-1)K<=C_j h_jD,
  kappa_0=1, kappa_j=5 for j>=1,
  B_0=2, C_0=36,
  B_j=2 B_(j-1), C_j=100(C_(j-1)+1).                    (14)

Here constants depend only on fixed depth. To prove the induction use
P=(1+eps)F_(j-1), eps=h_j/h_(j-1), eta=8eps. Young's inequality gives

  P-kappa_P K_j P^(-1)K_j<=C_P h_(j-1)D,
  kappa_P=kappa_(j-1)(1+eps)^3<=6,
  C_P<=2(C_(j-1)+1).

With eps<=1/1024, 2eta*kappa_P*max(f_eta)<1, so the ideal function
again gives F_*-2K_jF_*^(-1)K_j<=16 C_P h_jD. The proxy-to-true Young
step adds at most 2h_jD and resets kappa to four. Relative polynomial
accuracy

  tau_j=h_j/[10^6(1+B_(j-1))]

and equation (5) reset kappa to five and fit the C_j constant in (14).
The ideal h_jD floor/margin are the same as Section 3, while its upper
bound is below 2B_(j-1)D. The chosen tau_j preserves the floor and margin
independently of whether any error commutes. Thus (14) is a genuine
finite invariant for every level, not a formal recurrence assuming
exact absolute values. Choose the outer alpha=theta/(A_ell h_ell), with
fixed A_ell sufficiently large for C_ell,kappa_ell; Sections 2 and 5
then give the same gap-free spectral target.

Let R_j be the ACTUAL inverse factor, so F_j=R_j^(-T)R_j^(-1).
Starting from explicit R_0=J0^(-T), set

  R_P=R_(j-1)/sqrt(1+eps),
  A_j=R_P^T K_j R_P,
  R_j=R_P p_j(A_j).

No R_j inverse is applied. For a block of width b, action costs obey

  c_j(b)=~O_ell((h_(j-1)/h_j)[c_(j-1)(b)+J_(h_j)(b)]).

Products of ratios telescope to h_0/h_ell, and products of logs remain
polylogarithmic because ell is FIXED. Under the same raw-width core
condition, the total construction bound is

  ~O_ell(I+G_(h_0)+m^(omega+zeta)
    +Acal(r+1)sqrt(h_ell/theta)
    +sum_{j=1}^ell J_(h_j)(r+1) h_(j-1)/sqrt(h_ell*theta)). (15)

The explicit-core action term is absorbed into j=1, since J_(h_1)
already dominates the smaller core multiplication. No intermediate
spectral-norm searches are needed: all preconditioners are valid at all
positive thresholds.

The multiple-level dual output also has an exact preimage solution.
Let S=R_0^(-1)R_ell, so R_ell=R_0 S, and A0=R_0^T K R_0. Each factor
p_j(A_j) has condition O(sqrt(h_(j-1)/h_j)), hence

  kappa(S)<=O_ell(sqrt(h_0/h_ell)).

No commutation is needed for this product bound. Use the final start
Omega=S^T Xi, T=I-S^T A0 S, and carry
Wout=filter(I-A0 S S^T)Xi. Then Yout=S^T Wout exactly, so the
physical dual span is range(Z R_0^(-T)Wout). Only the explicit COARSE
inverse is used in that lift. Applying S,S^T uses R_ell plus explicit
coarse matrices, at the already charged cost. Elliptical Gaussian
capture adds only the logarithm of kappa(S), a parameter ratio rather
than an input condition number.

For N=m^(1+d), rank blocks inside the relevant input-optimal ranges,
theta=m^(-b), h_j=m^(-a_j), the true-input bound fits output if
a_ell>b-2d. Intermediate constraints from (15) are

  2a_j-a_(j-1)<3d-b,

after taking a_ell close to b-2d. The maximal recurrence approaches
3d-b with contraction 1/2. Thus for d=.18 and suitable coarse a_0
near .07, any b<.45 is feasible at sufficiently large FIXED depth,
provided one keeps all finest sketch rows strictly below ambient.
For example growing ranks m^.1 remain in every required input-optimal
block range. This matches the limiting scalar-stochastic accuracy
envelope but retains factor-block batching for growing rank. No theorem
for dimension- or accuracy-dependent growing depth is asserted.


9. A SCOPED DEGREE LOWER BOUND FOR UNIFORM POLYNOMIAL CONGRUENCES

Let p be a real polynomial of degree n, and assume

  |x|p(x)^2<=1 for every x in [-1,1],
  p(0)^2>=1/(C eps), 0<eps<=1/(16C).                    (16)

Then n>=1/(20C eps). Here is a direct Bernstein proof. Let
M=max_{[-1,1]}|p|, attained at x_*. Domination gives |x_*|<=1/M^2.
Take y=4/M^2. Then |p(y)|<=M/2, |x_*-y|<=5/M^2, and both points
are in a fixed interior subinterval when M^2>=16. Bernstein's inequality
there gives |p'|<=2nM. Thus

  M/2<= (5/M^2) 2nM,
  n>=M^2/20>=1/(20C eps).

These assumptions are exactly necessary for a scalar uniform polynomial
inverse congruence F_new=F_prev p(C)^(-2): domination +-K<=F_new means
|c|p(c)^2<=1, while at K=0 the relaxed fine invariant forces
F_new<=C hD, hence p(0)^2=Omega(H/h) when F_prev=Theta(H D).

The obstruction is realizable inside the coarse Gram model, rather than
an arbitrary inconsistent pencil. Take G=I, Q0=tI, Q=t(I-Hx/2) on a
