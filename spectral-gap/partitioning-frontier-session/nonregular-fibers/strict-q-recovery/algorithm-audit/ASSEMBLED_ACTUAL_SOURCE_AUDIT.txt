1. Frame promise and quantifiers

The strict symmetric slot weights sum to one at every old type and are
each below one half, so each total degree is at least three. Positive
size balance and connected off-diagonal support imply
alpha_min >= rho=1/[K D^(K-1)]. A one-type frame is covered separately
by alpha_1=1. Internal degree zero is absent, and internal degree one is
an ordinary matching when feasible. No class-size parity restriction
other than actual factor admissibility is introduced.

There are finitely many integer true frames under fixed K,D. A connected
consistent frame fixes its proportions. Choosing one strict certificate
per qualifying frame makes the minima of positive q_* and c_* positive
over this finite family. The algorithm never receives or computes that
certificate, the true class count, proportions, or true frame.

2. Exact count and explicit factorial remainder

Root plus any d_i-1 neighbor labels uniquely determine the omitted
label of a legal binary vector star, giving exactly (2^r)^(d_i) stars.
The root-star multinomial chooses root labels already. Each cross
factor contributes category bijections divided by all pool bijections;
an internal factor contributes offdiagonal category bijections and
diagonal double-factorial pairings. Reversal/even-diagonal restrictions
and the loop convention are correct, so the formula counts every
(configuration,Y) exactly once.

The root, cross and internal upper remainder counts are respectively
1, a^2, and 2a^2 units of log(Dn+1)+1. For an internal factor the more
explicit bound is a(a-1)/2+2a+2 <=2a^2 for a>=2. Thus
F=K+K(K+3)a_*^2/2, a_*=2^K, safely covers all factors including boundary
count entries. The number of types is at most (n+1)^(K a_*^D), giving
B=K a_*^D+F. Padding one-hot alphabets of size one or other r<=K to
a_* preserves the zero masses and justifies the same remainder there.

3. Weighted entropy identity and binary moment

Only slots with the same old neighbor type are symmetrized. This keeps
legal support and pooled edge tables and raises star entropy. Weighted
lossless deletion gives coefficient 1-q_ij-q_ji on an unordered pair
entropy. Internal factors use half the directed mass and two root
entropy copies, giving exactly 1-2q_ii. The remaining root coefficient
alpha_i[1-sum_j d_ij q_ji] vanishes by symmetric normalization.

All mutual-information coefficients and all omission weights are
positive. Therefore Phi<=0 for every compatible binary type, for every
candidate R. No certificate or connectedness condition is imposed on R.
Summing the exact count yields the polynomial moment. The simple-factor
conditioning probability stays bounded below: absent and matching factors
cost one; the stated internal and cross Poisson means give a total at
most K^2D^2. The fixed-factor labelled-stub multiplicities are constant
within each simple factor model. The conditioned expectation is therefore
under precisely the intended independent uniform simple-factor law and
is unconditional on recovery, including rare large kernels.

4. Explicit global real one-hot entropy gap

For actual retained law P_it and the product law Q_it, the divergence
used is KL(P_it||Q_it)=J_it+sum_(s!=t) I(X_i;Y_is). It is finite because
the product of marginals is positive on every positive actual tuple.
Let E=sum w I and J0=sum alpha_i sum_t J_it. Then
  L=-Phi >=c_* E+q_* J0,
and
  sum alpha_i sum_t kappa_it
  =J0+sum alpha_i(d_i-1)sum_s I(X_i;Y_is)
  <=J0+2(D-1)E <=C_kappa L.
The factor two includes internal edges correctly.

For the squared-norm test f_it, each occupied candidate row has total
mass d_i by legality, even under the product law because the root law
is unchanged. Thus 0<=f_it<=2D^2 on both laws. Pinsker yields
|E_Q f-E_P f|<=sqrt(2)D^2 sqrt(kappa_it). Summing the exact variance
identities gives (d_i-2)S_i+d_i B_i, where B_i>=0 is the same for every
omission. Individual expectation differences need not be positive; the
proof uses their absolute values after summing, in the valid direction.

Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz uses sum alpha_i d_i<=D, giving
  sum alpha_i(d_i-2)S_i <=D^2 sqrt(2D C_kappa L).
Reversibility gives sum alpha_i S_i=sum alpha_j d_j(1-||rho_j||^2),
which is at least 3 delta(P). Squaring proves exactly
  L>=9 delta(P)^2/[2D^5 C_kappa].
No alpha_min or positive candidate-mass bound was silently inserted.
The boundary and unsymmetrized cases are preserved, and delta=0 makes
every old root law a point mass. Matching marginals then make all stars
deterministic and the integer legal equation is QT=TR.

5. Macroscopic candidate bound

The same factorial contribution applies to integer one-hot candidates,
with at most r^(d_i) legal stars. Their defect is exactly the minimum
number of row changes to a planted point map, divided by n. The global
gap and count give the displayed polynomial times exp(-gamma_Q eta^2 n)
bound. There are only finitely many bounded integer candidate quotients,
so a finite union and Markov exclude every macroscopic candidate at once.
This count is separate from the binary moment; it does not treat modular
one-hot equations as integer equations.

6. Mixed-degree support estimate and local closure

For a bad support S, 2a+b=sum_(v in S)d_v>=d_min*s, b>=2w and e=a+b.
Hence e-s-w>=((d_min-2)/2)s. This uses the sum of actual degrees, not a
false common-degree identity. Every active typed pool has at least rho*n
stubs. For s<=rho*n/(4D), every denominator of a prescribed internal pair
or cross pair is at least rho*n/2. The displayed witness count permits
invalid typed prescriptions only as an upper bound.

Reconstructed the expected count as
  exp(s+w) 2^(Dw)(4D/rho)^e (s/n)^(e-s-w)(s/w)^w,
and bounded the final factor by exp(s/euler). The explicit C absorbs all
degree bounds and the at most (Ds+1)^2 choices of e,w. Thus the stated
C^s(s/n)^ceil(cs) is valid. The eta choice 1/(4 e C^2) works uniformly
for c>=1/2: C e^c eta^c=C(e eta)^c<=1/2. Summing from s=2 gives
O(n^(-2c)). Simple graphs exclude bad singletons, and simplicity costs
only a constant. The support event is common to all fields and R.

For a near-point integer candidate, the chosen eta leaves a clean
vertex in every old block because the changed vertices and their
neighbors number at most (D+1)|S|<n_i. At that vertex the exact equation
gives each row of QT=TR. Thus V=Y-JT satisfies AV=VR over the reals and
its small row support must vanish. This closes small disagreements,
including maps that would create tiny candidate classes.

7. Unknown frame, maximum-r correctness and memory

The algorithm enumerates all 1<=r<=K and bounded nonnegative integer R,
not only strictly feasible candidates. The planted (k,Q) is included.
Column vectorization gives exactly I_r tensor A-R^T tensor I_n over F_2.
Every integer one-hot candidate lies in that binary kernel. The algorithm
enumerates its full basis combinations, then checks standard unit rows,
nonempty classes and the original integer equation.

On the structural event each accepted Y=JT with T onto, so r<=k. The
planted k-class candidate is present, so the maximum r is k. An onto
map between k-element sets is a permutation. Hence unknown k and Q are
recovered without extra input. No partial enumeration can be certified
as a completed maximum. Keeping a binary basis, workspace, current and
best vectors uses O_K(n^2) bits, without retaining the solution list.

8. Unconditional bit work and the capped clock

Dense binary elimination costs O_K(n^3), and a deliberately loose
O_(K,D)(n^2) bound per kernel vector includes forming its basis sum,
row/nonempty checks and exact bounded-degree integer checks. Class-size
counters have O(log n) bits but still fit that bound. The unconditional
moment therefore gives base E T<=C n^(B+2), including rare graphs; no
structural success event is used in this expectation.

The main source places a clock ONLY on the capped implementation. Its
threshold is n^(B+4) BASE operations, so that clock has O(log n) bits,
and the actual capped work is O(n^(B+4)log n)<=O(n^(B+5)). Markov on
the base T bounds interruption by C n^-2. On interruption the algorithm
returns FAILURE/ABSTENTION, never an unfinished maximum.

An earlier version of this reviewer's independent companion incorrectly
called an UNcapped clock O(log n) bits. That sentence was corrected:
uncapped base work can be exponential, but h_R<=Kn implies a deterministic
bound C n^3 2^(Kn), hence O(n) clock bits. Charging that overhead still
gives expected O(n^(B+3)). The assembled main source under review never
relies on the incorrect uncapped-clock bound. Its untruncated algorithm
needs no clock, and E base<=C n^(B+2) already implies its conservative
E total<=C n^(B+3) statement.

9. Examples and limits

The K5-minus-edge example has degree-three endpoints incident to three
weight-1/3 edges; each degree-four vertex has two weight-1/3 and two
weight-1/6 edges. All sums are one and all weights are strictly inside
(0,1/2). Its independent matching lifts are included for every N. The
source does not extend to boundary weights, all minimum-degree-three
bases, noisy edits, growing K/D, deterministic lifts, or spectral
simplicity. The separate K_(3,12) nonidentifiability obstruction does
not satisfy the promise and is not contradicted.
