FREE/WIRED RESISTANCE COMPARISON AND ALL-EDGE LOCAL-BUDGET BOUND
============================================================
Research proof, 2026-11-15. Only this session directory is edited.

MAIN RESULT (ELECTRICAL)

Let G be an infinite connected simple unit-conductance graph of maximum
degree at most d>=3, and let e=uv be any existing edge. Write r=Rf(G;u,v)
and w=Rw(G;u,v) for free and wired effective resistances. Then

              w >= r/[1+(d-2)r/2] >= (2/d)r.                (1)

Consequently

                    (1+r)/(1+w) <= 2d/(d+2).               (2)

Both inequalities are exact on the d-regular tree: r=1 and w=2/d.
No bridge assumption is needed. The proof is finite network elimination
followed by exhaustion; it does not assume free and wired resistance
coincide. Section 6 gives the implication for fractional single-edge
response. Electrical sharpness does not by itself give a matching
spectral-count construction.

1. ENERGY CONVENTIONS AND THE EXHAUSTION

For any graph K write

 E_K(f)=sum_{xy in E(K)} (f(x)-f(y))^2.

There is one summand per unoriented edge. Unit conductances are implicit;
the finite reductions below may create nonnegative conductances.

Delete e and call the result H. It has at most two components, and each
component contains u or v. Either root has degree at most d-1 in H;
other vertices have degree at most d. H need not be connected.

Let dist_H(x,{u,v}) be the minimum distance to either root in its
component. For integer R>=1 let

 V_R={x:dist_H(x,{u,v})<=R},  S_R={x:dist_H(x,{u,v})=R},
 H_R=H[V_R].

These are finite, and H_R exhausts H. Every component of H_R meets
{u,v}; a completely exhausted finite component may have no shell vertex.

Define four finite variational quantities:

 Cf_R = min {E_{H_R}(f): f(u)-f(v)=1};
 Cw_R = min {E_{H_R}(f): f(u)-f(v)=1, f|_{S_R}=0};
 p_R  = min {E_{H_R}(f): f(u)=1, f|_{S_R}=0};
 q_R  = min {E_{H_R}(f): f(v)=1, f|_{S_R}=0}.

The difference constraint is intentional: even in the wired problem we
optimize the common shift of the two terminal voltages relative to the
grounded shell. If u,v lie in different components, Cf_R=0.

Free conductance Cf(H;u,v) is the increasing limit of Cf_R. Equivalently
Rf(H;u,v) is the decreasing limit of finite free resistances, with
Rf=infinity and Cf=0 when H disconnects u and v.

The other limits are

 Cw_R downarrow Cw(H;u,v),
 p_R downarrow cap_H(u),  q_R downarrow cap_H(v),              (3)

where

 Cw(H;u,v)=inf {E_H(f): f in c00(H), f(u)-f(v)=1},
 cap_H(x)=inf {E_H(f): f in c00(H), f(x)=1}.

Indeed extend a shell-zero function by zero outside V_R. Every edge
from V_R to its complement has its V_R endpoint in S_R, so the full
energy is unchanged. The resulting admissible families increase and
their union contains every finitely supported function. This proves
(3) directly, including finite-component and zero-capacity cases.

2. A FINITE COMPARISON THAT RETAINS EVERY BOUNDARY VERTEX

Eliminate vertices of H_R other than u,v and the INDIVIDUAL vertices
of S_R. Do not collapse S_R before this elimination. The result is an
ordinary nonnegative-conductance graph on {u,v} union S_R, with exactly
the minimized original energy at any assigned retained voltages.

For completeness this requires only the following one-vertex identity.
If x is eliminated and its incident conductances are c_xi with
s=sum_i c_xi>0, minimizing over f(x) replaces its star by edges ij of
conductance c_xi*c_xj/s. Existing ij conductances are added. This follows
by completing the square in sum_i c_xi(f(x)-f(i))^2. Repeating eliminates
all interior vertices, preserving nonnegative conductances and energy.
Each component meets a retained root, so isolated interior components
present no obstruction; zero-degree vertices may simply be dropped.

In this reduced graph let

 c = direct conductance of uv,
 a = sum of conductances from u to vertices of S_R,
 b = sum of conductances from v to vertices of S_R.

There may also be edges between shell vertices. All a,b,c are >=0.

For the FREE problem, every energy includes the nonnegative direct
term c(f(u)-f(v))^2. Hence

                            Cf_R >= c.                      (4)

For the WIRED problem all shell voltages vanish. The reduced energy
as a function of terminal values U,V is exactly

                     c(U-V)^2 + a U^2 + b V^2.

Thus, by minimizing with U-V=1,

                         Cw_R=c+ab/(a+b).                    (5)

The shell-shell terms vanish. Similarly, set U=1 and minimize V, or
set V=1 and minimize U, to obtain

 p_R=a+cb/(c+b) >= a,
 q_R=b+ca/(c+a) >= b.                                       (6)

Every 0/0 expression in this note is defined as zero. These formulas
remain valid for a component having no shell, or for a=b=0.

The function h(x,y)=xy/(x+y), continuously extended at (0,0), is
nondecreasing in either nonnegative argument. Equations (4)--(6) give

                    Cw_R-Cf_R <= h(a,b) <= h(p_R,q_R).       (7)

Take R to infinity using (3). There is no need to prove convergence
of the individual reduction coefficients a,b,c. We have proved the
more general escape-conductance inequality

 Cw(H;u,v)-Cf(H;u,v)
     <= cap_H(u)cap_H(v)/(cap_H(u)+cap_H(v)).                 (8)

The same argument works for other locally finite networks whenever
the displayed capacities and exhaustion limits are finite.

3. THE DEGREE BOUND ON THE SINGLETON CAPACITIES

Fix x=u or v. Its degree in H is at most d-1. Put Q=d-1>1. Within
the H-component of x, let rho(y)=dist_H(x,y). The number of edges
crossing levels h,h+1 is at most Q^{h+1}: the root has at most Q
neighbors, and every other vertex at positive distance has at least
one neighbor toward the root and at most Q neighbors farther out.
All edges join equal or consecutive levels.

Use the finitely supported trial function

 f_N(y)=(Q^{-rho(y)}-Q^{-N})/(1-Q^{-N})  when rho(y)<N,
         0                                  otherwise,

and set it zero on any other component. It has f_N(x)=1, and

 E_H(f_N)
 <= sum_{h=0}^{N-1} Q^{h+1}
       [(Q^{-h}-Q^{-h-1})/(1-Q^{-N})]^2
  = (Q-1)/(1-Q^{-N}).

Edges within a distance level have zero energy. Taking N to infinity,

                  cap_H(u), cap_H(v) <= d-2.                (9)

Finite components automatically have capacity zero, since the
function constant one on that component is finitely supported.

Combining (8), (9), and monotonicity of h gives

                    Cw(H;u,v)-Cf(H;u,v) <= (d-2)/2.         (10)

4. RESTORING THE EDGE AND PROVING THE RESISTANCE INEQUALITY

In both free and wired variational problems the restored unit edge
adds exactly (f(u)-f(v))^2=1 to the energy. Thus

 Cf(G;u,v)=1+Cf(H;u,v),
 Cw(G;u,v)=1+Cw(H;u,v).

Set r=Rf(G;u,v)=1/Cf(G;u,v), w=Rw(G;u,v)=1/Cw(G;u,v),
and k=(d-2)/2. In particular 0<r<=1. Equation (10) gives

                         1/w <= 1/r+k,

which proves w>=r/(1+kr). Since r<=1 and 1+k=d/2, this also gives
w>=(2/d)r. This proves (1).

For the exact logarithmic-budget ratio,

 (1+r)/(1+w)
   <= (1+r)(1+kr)/(1+(k+1)r)
    = 1 + kr^2/(1+(k+1)r).

The right side is increasing on r in [0,1], since the derivative of
its nonconstant term is

             kr[2+(k+1)r]/[1+(k+1)r]^2 >= 0.

Its value at r=1 is 2d/(d+2), proving (2). The weaker inequality
w>=(2/d)r alone also implies (2), since (1+r)/(1+2r/d) increases
on [0,1] for d>=2.

5. SHARPNESS, DEGREE TWO, AND A REFINED VERSION

For the d-regular tree, removing e separates two (d-1)-ary trees.
Each root capacity is d-2, since the radial trials above attain the
capacity bound by the usual series/parallel recursion or matching
equal-split unit flow. There is no free u-v conductance after deletion,
while the wired conductance after deletion is (d-2)/2. Thus r=1,
w=1/(1+(d-2)/2)=2/d. Both (1) and (2) attain equality.

For d=2 an infinite connected simple graph is a ray or a two-sided
path. Alternatively the radial linear trial function at a root of
degree at most one in H has energy <=1/N, so both root capacities
in (8) vanish. Hence w=r=1 for every edge of G and the ratio is one.

More generally retain the actual capacities in (8). With
K=cap_H(u)cap_H(v)/(cap_H(u)+cap_H(v)),

             w>=r/(1+Kr),
 (1+r)/(1+w)<=1+Kr^2/(1+(K+1)r).                             (11)

Thus a local nonbridge with r<1 has a strictly smaller universal
ratio upper bound than 2d/(d+2). In particular, approaching the latter
ratio forces r to approach one. An edge need not be a bridge in every
finite approximant to have r=1 in its rooted limit.

In fact, for a fixed infinite rooted graph, equality in (2) holds only
on the d-regular tree. Here is an elementary rigidity check. The scalar
bound in Section 4 is strictly increasing when d>2, so equality forces
r=1. If e belonged to a finite cycle with alternative path length m,
the edge and that path alone would have resistance m/(m+1)<1; Rayleigh
would force r<1. Thus e is a bridge. The two root capacities p,q must
then satisfy h(p,q)=(d-2)/2, which forces p=q=d-2 by (9).

For either half, write M_h for its number of edges crossing levels
h,h+1 about the root. If M_h<Q^{h+1} at any fixed level h, the trial
energy in Section 3 is smaller in the limit than d-2 by at least

        [Q^{h+1}-M_h]*(Q^{-h}-Q^{-h-1})^2 > 0.

Consequently capacity d-2 forces M_h=Q^{h+1} at every level. Equality
in the elementary branching count implies each nonroot vertex has
exactly one parent and Q children, and no within-level edges. Each
half is therefore the full rooted Q-ary tree, as claimed. In particular,
a sequence attaining the maximal ratio, or the maximal spectral upper
coefficient in (17), can have only the d-regular tree as an edge-rooted
local subsequential limit (provided it attains that coefficient).

6. APPLICATION TO ALL EXISTING-EDGE DOUBLINGS

Let 0<alpha<1 and W_{d,alpha}^{all}(epsilon) be the supremum of the
strict relative correction count for doubling any existing unit edge
of any finite connected simple host of maximum degree at most d.
Use the response notation from local-global-budget-session:

 A_n=L(G_n), B_n=A_n+b_nb_n*,
 C_n=A_n^{-alpha/2}(B_n^alpha-A_n^alpha)A_n^{-alpha/2}
      on constants-perpendicular,
 tau_n=b_n* A_n^+ b_n=R_eff(G_n;u_n,v_n).

The already proved finite determinant identity is

          sum_j log(1+lambda_j(C_n))=alpha log(1+tau_n).      (12)

Pass to an edge-rooted locally convergent subsequence of G_n, with
limit (G,u,v), and to a further subsequence with tau_n->ell in [0,1].
If G is finite, bounded-degree rooted convergence makes G_n eventually
isomorphic to G, so epsilon_n times any spectral count tends to zero.
Assume G infinite and put r=Rf(G;u,v), w=Rw(G;u,v).

THE FINITE LEVERAGES ARE BOUNDED BY LOCAL FREE RESISTANCE:

For every fixed finite connected subgraph F of G containing e, the
rooted identification embeds F into G_n for all sufficiently large n.
Adding the other edges/vertices of G_n cannot increase resistance, so

                      tau_n <= R_eff(F;u,v)

eventually. Exhaust G by such finite F; by the definition of free
resistance their resistances decrease to r. Consequently

                              ell <= r.                     (13)

This simple Rayleigh argument uses free resistance. Replacing r by w
in (13) would be unjustified and false in bridge examples.

For completeness the opposite comparison w<=ell follows by transporting
any fixed compact-support energy test f from G to G_n. The variational
characterization gives tau_n>=|f(u)-f(v)|^2/E_G(f) eventually. Taking
the supremum over such f gives the full local leverage sandwich

                              w <= ell <= r.

THE COMPULSORY LOCAL DETERMINANT IS WIRED:

The analytic results proved in
  ../local-global-budget-session/local-upper-notes.txt,
  ../local-global-budget-session/fredholm-notes.txt
apply unchanged to nonbridges. They establish a positive trace-class
local relative operator C with

          sum_j log(1+lambda_j(C))=alpha log(1+w),            (14)
          lambda_j(C)<=liminf_n lambda_j(C_n)                (15)

for each fixed j. Specifically, polynomial locality transfers the two
finite-support Gram matrices for A_n^alpha and B_n^alpha-A_n^alpha;
Ritz min-max plus density of A^{alpha/2}c00 gives (15). The rank-one
resolvent formula, trace-norm regularization A+delta, and Fredholm
determinant continuity give (14) even if inf spec(A)=0. None of those
steps uses the bridge condition; only the former substitution tau_n=1
used it.

For epsilon_n downarrow0 and q_n=#{j:lambda_j(C_n)>epsilon_n},
subtract the first k modes from (12):

 (q_n-k)_+ log(1+epsilon_n)
   <= alpha log(1+tau_n)-sum_{j=1}^k log(1+lambda_j(C_n)).

The right side is always nonnegative, so this also holds if q_n<k.
Take limsup using (13), (15), and epsilon/log(1+epsilon)->1; then
let k increase and use (14). The result is the general local-budget
bound

 limsup_n epsilon_n q_n
       <= alpha log[(1+ell)/(1+w)]
       <= alpha log[(1+r)/(1+w)]
       <= alpha log[2d/(d+2)].                              (16)

For fixed epsilon>0 the counts are finite and uniformly bounded by
alpha log2/log(1+epsilon). Their supremum over hosts is an integer
maximum whenever the host class is nonempty. Taking maximizing hosts
along any sequence realizing the supremal limiting upper coefficient,
then applying the preceding compactness argument, proves

 limsup_{epsilon downarrow0} epsilon W_{d,alpha}^{all}(epsilon)
                         <= alpha log[2d/(d+2)].             (17)

Thus the bridge upper coefficient extends to ALL existing-edge
doublings at the same maximum degree. This note supplies the electrical
and finite-leverage parts; it uses the previously proved analytic
Fredholm/Ritz lemmas explicitly identified above. Any matching lower
construction in the bridge subclass would immediately give equality
for the all-edge class. No such lower construction is claimed here.

7. SOURCES AND VERIFICATION STATUS

The comparison (8) and its deductions are proved in full above. No
literature theorem asserting this particular comparison is assumed.
Two independent derivations in this session checked the finite formulas
(4)--(7), the shell exhaustion, and the degree estimate. An independent
read-through of the full note, including Section 6 and finite-component
edge cases, found no gap.

For standard finite network elimination, a stable primary source is
Florian Dorfler and Francesco Bullo, Kron Reduction of Graphs with
Applications to Electrical Networks, arXiv:1102.2950 (2011), published
IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems I 60(1), 150--163 (2013).
The authors' PDF states the Schur-complement Laplacian reduction and
its electrically equivalent terminal network in the introduction:
https://motion.me.ucsb.edu/pdf/2011d-db.pdf
https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.2950

For free/wired exhaustion conventions and network principles, use
Russell Lyons and Yuval Peres, Probability on Trees and Networks,
author-hosted text, Chapters 2--3 and 9--10:
https://rdlyons.pages.iu.edu/prbtree/book.pdf
The derivations in this note make its needed conventions explicit.

check_completions.py is a numerical diagnostic on finite cores filled
out to maximum degree by independent infinite regular-tree branches.
Free resistance is the finite-core resistance; wired resistance is
the inverse of L_core+((d-2)/(d-1))diag(d-deg_core). For seed 230611,
2000 generated cores for each d=3,4,5,6 (up to 30 core vertices) gave
no violation, with tree cores attaining equality to rounding precision.
The proof does not depend on these calculations.
