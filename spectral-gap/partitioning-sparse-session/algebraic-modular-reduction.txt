An elementary modular reduction for a general algebraic quotient mode
===================================================================
15 November 2026. Logical supplement for the primary session's general
frame investigation; not a complete general-frame theorem by itself.

Suppose integer adjacency matrices A_N possess a fixed algebraic integer
eigenvalue lambda. Let f be its monic irreducible minimal polynomial in
Z[t]. If dim_R ker(A_N-lambda I)>=m+1, every (N-m)-minor of A_N-tI is
an integer polynomial vanishing at lambda, and hence is divisible by f
in Z[t]. Monicity is enough for integer polynomial long division.

For ANY prime p where f has a root lambda_p in F_p, these minors all
vanish after t=lambda_p and mod-p reduction. Thus real failure forces
dim_Fp ker(A_N-lambda_p I)>=m+1. This avoids undefined attempts to reduce
an irrational real number modulo p and requires no choice of a real
embedding after reduction.

There are infinitely many unbounded such primes. For degree1 this is
immediate. For an irreducible polynomial of degree>1, f(0)=c is nonzero
and f has no integer root. Schur's elementary argument proves infinitely
many primes divide its nonzero integer values: if all such primes lay
in a finite set with product P, evaluate at x=cPk. The integer f(cPk)/c
is congruent to1 modulo P and has absolute value>1 for large enough k,
yet every prime factor would have to divide P, a contradiction.
Every prime divisor of a nonzero f(x) supplies a root x modp. Any fixed
finite set of bad primes (2, prescribed degree factors, nonzero quotient
minors/discriminants) can therefore be removed while retaining unbounded
root primes. This is weaker than complete splitting, which is unnecessary.

Consequently, if a finite-field moment argument at every fixed such prime
gives E|ker(A_N-lambda_p I)|<=C p^m+o_N(1) with C independent of p,
then limsup_N Pr(real extra nullity)<=C/p. Letting these fixed primes
tend to infinity proves no extra real multiplicity. Only the reduction
logic is established by this note; the relevant moment bound for the
actual matrix/frame model must be proved separately.

For a two-by-two integer quotient with positive cross entries the
irrational case has a shorter proof: its conjugate larger eigenvalue is
Perron and simple on any connected host, forcing the smaller eigenvalue
simple as well. See irrational-quotient-simplicity.tex. The elementary
Schur reduction is potentially useful when a higher-dimensional frame's
informative algebraic conjugacy class does not contain its Perron root.
