1. Normalization and the graph-specific scale bounds
-----------------------------------------------------
Common scaling by 1/d reduces the update to uu^T with u=e_p-e_q,
||u||^2=2 and leverage tau. Multiplying the returned factor by d^(alpha/2)
restores the physical matrix and the same relative Loewner bounds.
For the mean-zero dipole potential v=A^dagger u, the maximum principle
bounds its coordinate range by v_p-v_q=tau. Hence ||v||^2<=n tau^2.
Spectral Holder interpolation gives u^T A^-alpha u<=tau^alpha 2^(1-alpha).
Neither inequality contains a spectral endpoint or conductance ratio.

2. Relative tails and positive logarithmic quadrature
------------------------------------------------------
The low-tail trace is bounded by writing u=Av and taking
sup_lambda lambda^(2-alpha)/(lambda+t)^2=K_alpha t^-alpha.
The transformed positive integrand before dt is therefore bounded uniformly
by K_alpha||v||^2, yielding the stated O(T||v||^2) low tail. The high tail
uses R_t^2<=t^-2 I, yielding O(T^(alpha-1)u^T A^-alpha u).
The chosen cutoffs control both tails by their allocated constants, using
only n,1+tau,epsilon. Their logarithms are O_alpha(L).

For complex t=e^(y+i theta), |theta|<=a<pi/2, Re s(t)>=0, so the
rank-one denominator has modulus >=1. The complex resolvents are bounded
by real positive resolvents using |lambda+t e^(i theta)|>=
cos(theta/2)(lambda+t). Their rank-one matrix norms are dimension-free.
The strip boundary integral is bounded by a constant times
 c_alpha integral_0^infty t^alpha u^T R_t A^-alpha R_t u dt=alpha*tau.
The equality follows from the scalar beta integral at each positive lambda.
Operator-valued contour shifting and the triangle inequality then give the
stated exponentially small infinite-trapezoid error. The same low/high
positive envelopes bound discarded grid sums geometrically. Thus q=O(L^2),
qh=O(L), and all positive node weights are justified without a matrix norm
factor depending on n. The Frobenius bound follows from
 t^(alpha+1)lambda^(1-alpha)/(lambda+t)^2<=K_alpha,
summed against the spectral weights defining tau.

3. Clipping and exact feasible-flow column accuracy
-----------------------------------------------------
An energy-accurate initial potential has terminal error <=eta*tau, so
its endpoint order is correct on the success event. Common coordinate
clipping fixes those terminal values, decreases every edge energy, and
preserves u^T x. The identity ||x-v||_A^2=x^T A x-2u^T x+tau proves
that clipping cannot increase the energy error. Subtracting its mean
preserves these quantities and gives the stated Euclidean range bound.

On the augmented graph, exact feasibility implies
 D^T f+g=vhat and the error divergence e=D^T(f-f*)=-(g-g*).
Electrical optimality makes total excess energy exactly the squared
flow-distance energy. Thomson gives ||e||_(A^dagger)^2<=E, while the
ground part gives ||e||^2<=tE. Spectral Holder then yields
 ||A^-alpha/2 e||^2<=t^(1-alpha)E
                    <=zeta^2 t^-alpha ||vhat||^2.
The bias from vhat-v has the separate bound obtained from
sup_lambda lambda^(1-alpha)/(lambda+t)^2=O(t^(-1-alpha)).
These are exactly the two terms in the flow-error lemma. No generic
shifted-potential error is being silently treated as a relative vector error.

The flow moments follow from total energy <=2||vhat||^2/t and
||g||^2<=2||vhat||^2; ||vhat-g||^2<=6||vhat||^2. Outputting original-edge
divergence makes every column mean zero exactly. Tree flows can be recovered
by subtree sums after non-tree flows are stored; this costs O(m+n) per flow.

4. Raw factor errors and moment certificates
----------------------------------------------
A scalar shifted energy solve gives |u^T xhat-s(t)|<=kappa*s(t).
Clipping its scalar value at zero preserves this error bound; denominators
stay >=1 and their square-root weights change by relative O(kappa).
After weighting columns, the initial-voltage error has Frobenius norm
O(eta sqrt(tau J)), weight error has norm O(kappa sqrt(tau J)), and
feasible-flow error has norm at most
O(zeta sqrt(n tau^2 J t_plus)). These match the displayed sufficient budget.
Combining with ||SS^T-TT^T||<=(2||S||+||T-S||)||T-S|| gives the claimed
raw relative matrix error after the quadrature allocation. All tolerances
can be capped at a fixed constant; their inverse logarithms are O_alpha(L)
even when tau is arbitrarily small. No log(1/tau) term is forced.

For each column, its retained original-edge flow is feasible for that
column's divergence. Thomson therefore certifies its A^dagger energy by
its computed flow energy. Summing weighted energies gives a genuine
computable H0 bound for tr Z^T A^dagger Z. N0=||Z||_F^2 is explicit.
Geometric node sums h sum t_j^a<=K_a t_plus^a give the two polynomial
raw-moment bounds. The mean-zero property is essential and is satisfied.

5. Small Gram construction without fractional vector actions
--------------------------------------------------------------
For mean-zero Z the fractional inverse identity gives exactly
 H=Z^T A^-alpha Z=c_alpha integral s^-alpha Z^T(A+sI)^-1 Z ds.
The low/high trace tails are O(H0 S_minus^(1-alpha)) and
O(N0 S_plus^-alpha). The logarithmic envelope
min(H0 s^(1-alpha),N0 s^-alpha) has integrated mass
O(H0^alpha N0^(1-alpha)). Both endpoint logarithms and its accuracy
logarithm are O_alpha(L) after the raw-moment bounds. A fixed-strip contour
argument therefore yields N=O_alpha(L^2) Gram nodes without a spectral gap.

A relative-energy error theta in column j gives bilinear error
<=theta sqrt(Z_i^T R_s Z_i) sqrt(Z_j^T R_s Z_j). Its Frobenius norm is
at most theta tr Z^T R_s Z. Symmetrization does not increase the norm bound.
The integrated/grid envelope controls all solve errors using only O(L)
accuracy logarithms. Forming a node Gram costs O(nq^2) and q shifted solves.
There is no hidden application of A^-alpha or a dense whitening matrix.

6. Jacobi arithmetic, truncation, and exact error/rank constants
----------------------------------------------------------------
The max off-diagonal pivot has square at least the off-diagonal Frobenius
square divided by q(q-1). A Jacobi rotation removes twice this square,
so the contraction fraction 2/[q(q-1)] is correct. Scanning all entries
costs O(q^2) per rotation, and O(q^2 log(2+||H||_F/epsilon)) rotations
suffice. Updating the matrix and accumulated orthogonal factor fits in
that cost. Rotations use arithmetic, comparisons and positive square roots;
no eigenvalue-separation assumption or exact general eigensolver is needed.
The required stopping target is the off-diagonal Frobenius NORM <=epsilon/16,
consistent with the preceding explicit residual requirement in the source.
The source's phrase 'that norm' after mentioning its square should be read
this way; spelling out 'Frobenius norm' would remove a minor ambiguity.

Total approximate Gram error <=epsilon/8 implies retained diagonalized
Gram eigenvalues >epsilon/2 correspond to H eigenvalues >3epsilon/8.
Raw error epsilon/8 then forces that many C eigenvalues >epsilon/4.
The determinant budget gives the stated strict rank bound, including
integer thresholds and r=0. On the discarded subspace,
||(I-UU^T)H(I-UU^T)||<=5epsilon/8. Combining its PSD residual with the
raw +/-epsilon/8 error yields exactly
 -epsilon/8 A^alpha <=Delta-XX^T<=3epsilon/4 A^alpha.
The near-sharp variant similarly has total error
rho*epsilon/4 + [(1-rho/2)epsilon+rho*epsilon/4]=epsilon,
and retained true eigenvalue threshold (1-rho)epsilon. Its added accuracy
cost is logarithmic in 1/rho, as claimed.

7. Unconditional work, primal-dual certification, and confidence
------------------------------------------------------------------
The first reviewed copy had only success-event polynomial moment bounds;
that alone would not bound runtime on arbitrarily bad primitive outputs.
The added guards and preliminary procedure repair this issue.

For every potential w, the dual value D0=2u^T w-w^T A w is <=tau;
for every feasible original-graph flow f, E0=f^T W^-1 f is >=tau.
Thus every accepted interval 0<D0 and E0<=2D0 certifies
 tau<=E0<=2tau, independently of primitive success. Constant-accuracy
potential and feasible-flow calls have a positive joint probability of
acceptance. For instance their energy errors imply D0=tau-||w-v||_A^2,
while a constant relative flow guarantee gives E0 close enough to tau.
Independent repetition has constant expected trials and nearly-linear
expected preliminary work. The accepted upper bound can schedule every
subsequent cutoff, tolerance and a priori polynomial moment cap.

Raw moment certificates are checked before using them to create Gram
cutoffs or tighter accuracy requests; oversized certificates terminate in
failure. Jacobi has an a priori iteration cap and likewise terminates in
failure if needed. Those guards never fire on the simultaneous successful
solve event. They prevent rare outputs from introducing hidden conductance
or condition-number dependence into expected postprocessing work. Raw
primitive runtimes depend on size and prescribed relative tolerance, not
on the magnitude of their arbitrary real demands.

Feasible-flow success is amplified by least computed primal energy, because
all candidates have the same demand/optimum and every candidate is feasible.
Ordinary potential solves can use pairwise energy-distance median selection;
its extra pairwise energy evaluations cost only logarithmic overhead times
sparse arithmetic. Union bounding the O(qN+q+1) prescribed events gives
failure probability <=delta. Scalar and algebraic work, including raw flow
extraction, Gram formation, Jacobi scans, and final ZU multiplication, is
charged. The primitive counts are therefore consistent with expected
nearly-linear work up to the stated logarithmic factors, on all outcomes.

8. Scalar model, preconditioning, and precise limits
-----------------------------------------------------
The general fixed-alpha claim explicitly charges scalar real powers. For
alpha=1/2, dyadic-root nodes 2^(j/H), H a power of two of order L, can
be constructed using repeated positive square roots and arithmetic;
log(2), pi and fixed scalar constants can be approximated by convergent
series at the allocated budgets. Relative weight/node errors can be
included in the same raw/Gram scalar error allocations. At this fixed
alpha no general spectral-function or decomposition oracle is hidden.

The Loewner relative bound implies the stated quality of
M=L^alpha+XX^T, since M>=L^alpha. Its inverse is not free: the explicitly
charged conditional Woodbury costs retain a base fractional inverse action.
The theorem asserts a PSD factor with two-sided relative error; it does not
assert XX^T<=Delta, a sublinear dynamic update, a finite-word theorem,
or an arbitrary-SPD construction. The maximum principle and exact feasible
flows are material graph-specific inputs. Historical priority is open.
