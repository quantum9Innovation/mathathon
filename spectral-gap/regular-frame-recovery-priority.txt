FINDING

I found no inspected primary theorem that directly yields the proposed recovery
of the planted fine partition from uncolored adjacency for every fixed connected
simple d-regular base, d >= 3, without an old/new spectral separation assumption.
This is a bounded search conclusion, not a firstness certificate. Existing work
does establish nearby detection, separated spectral recovery, community uniqueness
under degree hypotheses, entropy-counting frameworks, and cover recognition on
special inputs. Those distinctions must survive into any announcement.

The defensible candidate scope, if the fresh proof audits pass, is:
For every fixed connected regular frame Q of degree d >= 3, with its number of
classes bounded by a fixed K, recover from the graph alone the planted partition
as the finest equitable partition with at most K nonempty classes, with high
probability in the stated independent-factor model. In particular Q=A(B) may be
the adjacency matrix of any fixed connected simple d-regular graph B; its factors
are independent uniform perfect matchings, i.e. the usual random N-lift model.
The algorithm is not supplied B, the quotient labels, or edge colors. The stronger
structural assertion is that every bounded-class equitable partition coarsens
the planted one. This would identify the partition up to relabeling classes,
and then its quotient by counting neighbors.

This scope is more specific than general recovery of a graph's 'base': a graph
can have multiple quotient maps, and a fixed base can itself cover a smaller
graph. The condition 'finest among partitions with at most K classes' is part of
the mathematical target, with K fixed and at least the planted number of classes.
There is no assertion here for growing K, arbitrary unstructured graphs, noisy
lifts, or degree two. A singleton partition is always equitable, so omitting
the bound K would change the target completely.

1. NOISELESS LIFT DETECTION IS EXPLICITLY PRIOR

Dmitriy Kunisky and Xifan Yu, Computational hardness of detecting graph lifts
and certifying lift-monotone properties of random regular graphs (2024).
Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17012
Actual PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.17012
Read: abstract, Introduction including Definition 1.1 and the noiseless/noisy
distinction, related-work discussion, and full-text searches for recovery and
uniqueness. Not a full 66-page proof audit.

Their Introduction explicitly treats the unmodified detection problem as easy:
the lift inherits the exact eigenvalues of the base, whereas a uniform random
regular graph typically has none of a fixed list of nontrivial exact eigenvalues.
They add rewiring noise to remove this algebraic distinction before proposing
hardness for Ramanujan bases. Their stated target is distinguishing ensembles;
the inspected passages do not give blind reconstruction of all fine fibers.
Accordingly a fiber-recovery theorem should not be sold as the first efficient
way to recognize noiseless lift structure, and it would not contradict their
noise-robust hardness conjecture. Their bipartite example of recovering the
ordinary two-coloring is also not recovery of a finer lift partition.

2. GENERAL FRAME RECOVERY ALREADY EXISTS UNDER SPECTRAL SEPARATION

Gerandy Brito, Ioana Dumitriu and Kameron Decker Harris, Spectral gap in random
bipartite biregular graphs and applications, CPC 31 (2022), 229-267.
Primary published text:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/combinatorics-probability-and-computing/article/spectral-gap-in-random-bipartite-biregular-graphs-and-applications/FF0544B9780460143733B7A5317D6CC1
Read in this session's preceding frame/RSBM review: Sections 6.2-6.3 and Theorem
6.2, including the frame assumptions and degree-scaling conclusion.

Their frame model and spectral community-recovery application are direct prior
art. Theorem 6.2 uses a nonsingular frame and scaling of its edge degrees so the
frame eigenspace separates from the remaining spectrum. This is not the proposed
all-fixed-degree assertion for simple regular bases, including singular quotient
matrices or quotient eigenvalues within the new-spectrum bulk. Credit the model
and the separated-recovery theorem. An assertion that no prior algorithm recovers
general frames would be false.

3. COMMUNITY UNIQUENESS HAS A MULTICLASS PRECEDENT, WITH OTHER HYPOTHESES

Sayar Karmakar and Moumanti Podder, Uniqueness of communities in regular
stochastic block models.
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/2002.05577v2
Read previously in this session: model, Introduction, Theorem 3.1 and its degree
conditions. Not the entire proof.

The paper proves multiclass community uniqueness under sufficiently large degree
and comparability assumptions. Its setup has equal class sizes, positive integer
degree entries, and restrictive lower bounds; it does not supply the simple-base
lift case with zero diagonal and cross degrees zero or one. It is nonetheless a
close structural predecessor: uniqueness of regular community labels is not a
new question. Distinguish extension of its parameter/model range from invention
of the uniqueness problem, and distinguish uniqueness from a blind polynomial
algorithm that searches over unknown quotient frames.

4. MULTITYPE ENTROPY COUNTS ARE CLOSE METHOD PRIOR ART

Amir Dembo and Theo McKenzie, The Gaussian Wave for Graphs of Finite Cone Type
(2026).
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04386v1
Read previously in this session: Definition 1.6, Theorem 1.8 and its following
qualification, Proposition 2.3, and the relevant colored-star / edge counts of
Section 3, including Lemmas 3.1-3.2. Not a full-paper audit.

Their model includes random lifts of regular bases. The multitype star/edge
entropy framework and configuration/bipartite matching enumeration are close
prior tools, with further credits there to earlier typical-process entropy work.
The inspected statements give exponential-scale counting and typical-process
constraints, not a polynomial bound for all finite-field intertwiners or a
blind fine-fiber recovery theorem. Theorem 1.8 samples eigenvectors from spectral
windows; its discussion permits exceptional planted eigenvectors. It cannot be
used as an assertion that every exact quotient eigenvalue has only its old
eigenspace, or that arbitrary one-hot intertwining labels obey the Gaussian-wave
conclusion. Any eventual proof should explain what the all-slot equality and
small-support argument add to this established entropy framework.

5. COARSEST EQUITABLE PARTITIONS AND COLOR REFINEMENT HAVE A DIFFERENT TARGET

Paolo Boldi, Violetta Lonati, Massimo Santini and Sebastiano Vigna, Graph
fibrations, graph isomorphism, and PageRank, RAIRO ITA 40 (2006), 227-253.
Primary full PDF: https://www.numdam.org/article/ITA_2006__40_2_227_0.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1051/ita:2006004
Read: abstract and introductory definitions; minimum-base discussion on printed
pages 231-232, including footnote 2; Section 7, printed pages 247-248, and its
partition-refinement / graph-isomorphism distinction. Not a full-paper read.

The minimum-base fibers are the coarsest equitable partition; their computation
is partition refinement. This classical task does not recover the finest
bounded-size partition of a regular graph. Starting from one color, all vertices
of a d-regular graph stay in the same color class, whereas the proposed fine
fibers may number k > 1. Computing a canonical labeling after individualization
also does not automatically solve a hidden cover-map recognition problem.

Mikhail Isaev, Tamás Makai, Brendan D. McKay, Paweł Prałat, Jane Tan and Maksim
Zhukovskii, Canonical Labelling of Random Regular Graphs, ICALP 2026.
Primary: https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol374-icalp2026/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.114/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.114.html
Full version: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17567
Read: abstract, Introduction, color-refinement definition and Theorem 1.
This paper explicitly notes that uncolored regular graphs cause color refinement
to stop at one class. Its theorem concerns refinement after a nontrivial initial
coloring, for the uniform random regular ensemble under its degree assumptions;
it is not a fine-fiber theorem for arbitrary fixed-base lift ensembles.

6. COVER RECOGNITION: WATCH THE MEANING OF 'REGULAR COVER'

Jiří Fiala, Pavel Klavík, Jan Kratochvíl and Roman Nedela, Algorithmic Aspects of
Regular Graph Covers.
Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/1609.03013
Read: abstract and Sections 1.1-1.3, definitions and principal algorithm scope.

Here a regular cover means a cover arising from a semiregular automorphism-group
action, also called a Galois/deck-group cover. It does not mean an arbitrary cover
whose source and target graphs are d-regular. Their principal algorithm exploits
planar source graphs and group structure. General random lifts need not be Galois
covers, and their source graphs need not be planar. Their discussion also records
worst-case NP-completeness of H-Cover for fixed regular targets of degree at least
three, citing Kratochvíl-Proskurowski-Telle, Covering regular graphs, JCTB 71
(1997), 1-16. I inspected the later primary paper's statement, not that original
hardness proof. Worst-case recognition hardness is compatible with a high-
probability algorithm for a promised random-lift distribution.

7. COVER AUTOMORPHISMS DO NOT GIVE UNIQUENESS OF A HIDDEN PROJECTION

Alon Amit and Nathan Linial, Random Graph Coverings I: General Theory and Graph
Connectivity, Combinatorica 22 (2002), 1-18.
Primary DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s004930200000
Primary publication record and abstract:
https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/random-graph-coverings-i-general-theory-and-graph-connectivity/
Read: the primary abstract / publication record. Attempts to access the original
full PDF via the author's site and CiteSeer failed; I do NOT report a fresh full
read or a theorem-level uniqueness audit of this paper.

Its abstract establishes the random independent-matching cover model and typical
connectivity. Searchable mirrored text discusses unlabeled coverings and
automorphisms, but that alone is insufficient for a priority conclusion. In
particular, an automorphism preserving a specified projection, an automorphism
of the uncolored total graph, and a second locally bijective projection to a base
are three distinct notions. Even absence of all graph automorphisms need not
exclude a second equitable partition. This paper must not be cited as proving
or disproving fine-fiber uniqueness without checking the exact original category
of isomorphisms. Likewise, recovering quotient vertex names up to Aut(B) is not
the same issue as existence of a different fiber set partition.

8. FROZEN COLORINGS AND OLD/NEW SPECTRUM: RELATED BUT INSUFFICIENT STATEMENTS

Guillem Perarnau and Giovanne Santos, Random lifts of very high girth and
their applications to frozen colourings, DMD 2024 abstract.
Primary: https://dmd2024.web.uah.es/files/abstracts/paper_18.pdf
Read: abstract and opening model/application discussion of the six-page note;
not a complete proof audit. The source concerns high-girth lifts and frozen /
partially frozen colorings. Local resistance to recoloring does not by itself
imply uniqueness of all global equitable partitions. In a lift of K_(d+1), the
planted covering coloring imposes stronger neighbor-color counts than a proper
(d+1)-coloring. A theorem about ordinary coloring or recoloring therefore cannot
be substituted without checking the exact local constraint and conclusion.

Classical global new-eigenvalue bounds for lifts constrain the new spectrum near
the universal-cover spectrum; they do not exclude a collision with an old
eigenvalue lying inside that spectrum. Recent local laws and delocalization also
do not assert such an exact collision exclusion merely by giving estimates on
windows larger than the microscopic spacing. Conversely, the proposed equitable
partition algorithm need not prove absence of all extra old-eigenvalue vectors:
an extra vector need not produce another one-hot quotient map. Keep real
eigenvalue simplicity, finite-field kernel moment bounds, and combinatorial
partition rigidity as separate statements.

RECOMMENDED CLAIM AND LIMITS

Subject to fresh mathematical verification, the most promising precise claim is
blind polynomial recovery of the finest bounded-class equitable partition for
every fixed connected regular frame of degree at least three, including every
fixed connected simple regular-base random-lift ensemble, with no quotient/new-
spectrum gap hypothesis. This covers models excluded by positive large-entry
community assumptions and separated spectral recovery. Illustrative bases may
include Petersen or cube graphs, but examples do not replace quantifier checks.

Do not claim first lift detection, first frame recovery, first community
uniqueness, first entropy method, general polynomial cover recognition, recovery
under rewiring noise, or recovery by ordinary one-dimensional color refinement.
Do not say the finest partition exists uniquely on every regular graph; that is
the proposed high-probability rigidity conclusion for the specified model.

The search covered random-cover uniqueness, random-lift reconstruction / base
recovery, alternative cover maps, equitable-partition rigidity, cover
automorphisms, canonical labeling, frozen coloring, and fixed-eigenvalue / old-new
collision terminology, plus primary sources above. It found no immediate direct
subsumption, but it is not an exhaustive priority certification. The candidate
source remains the proposal hash stated at the top; later proof changes require
a mathematical delta audit and may change this scope assessment.
