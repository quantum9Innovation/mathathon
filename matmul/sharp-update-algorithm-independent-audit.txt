1. INTRINSIC NORMALIZATION AND THE SINGULAR CORE
----------------------------------------------
Exact thin rank-revealing QR U=QR gives Q^TQ=I_k and E=QCQ^T,
where C=RJR^T in the square-root note or C=RKR^T in the bridge.
For c=||C||_F, orthonormality gives

  ||E||=||C|| <= c <= sqrt(k)||E||.

If c=0 the update is exactly zero. The computation costs
O(Nr^2+r^3), within Nmr+m^3 because r<=m<=N. Cancellation in the
supplied factors does not enter the error normalization or iteration
count. No eigenvalue, sign decomposition or inverse of C is needed.

For a positive shift in the PSD-endpoint case, or a nonreal shift z in
the general Hermitian case, define V=R Q, M=Q^T RQ and

  H=(I+MC)^(-1), K=C H.

The determinant lemma and invertibility of both endpoint resolvents
prove I+MC is invertible even when C is singular. Woodbury gives

  H=I-Q^T R'Q C,
  K=C(I+MC)^(-1)=(I+CM)^(-1)C,
  R-R'=V K V^T.

Thus K is symmetric (complex symmetric for a complex shift) and its
rank is exactly rank(C). The transpose, not adjoint, is correct for a
real symmetric matrix's complex resolvent. The endpoint resolvent bound
gives ||H||<=1+c/t at every relevant positive/imaginary shift. This
identity is the substantive reason that a nearly critical downdate or a
singular signed core requires no additional condition parameter.

2. PSD SQUARE-ROOT NORMALIZATION AND THE APPROACHING STRIP
-------------------------------------------------------
For t=c exp(z) the square-root note's normalized integrand is

  F(z)=c exp(3z/2)[(A+tI)^(-1)-(B+tI)^(-1)]/pi,

and the desired difference is sqrt(c) integral F. The factor c, rather
than c^(3/2), in this normalized F is correct.

For PSD endpoints and |arg t|<=d<pi,

  |lambda+t| >= cos(d/2)(lambda+|t|), lambda>=0.

Bounding resolvents separately and by their difference yields exactly
E1. On the real axis both resolvents lie between zero and I/t, so their
difference has norm at most 1/t, also for an indefinite update. The
second bound is c/t^2. Hence ||F(x)||<=1, as used in the scalar budget.

The computed p is a positive lower approximation to pi, with p>3.
Putting d=p-L^(-1/4) gives d>2 for L>=1 and pi-d>=L^(-1/4).
Since sin((pi-d)/2)>=(pi-d)/pi, the envelope constant satisfies

  M_d<=2*pi*sqrt(L)<8*sqrt(L).

Consequently the proof is uniform for the single algorithm's widening
strip. It does not illegitimately interchange two fixed-strip limits.

The interval errors imply L=log(1/rho)+O(1) and p=pi+o(1).

For retained intervals meeting [-2(L+ell),2(L+ell)],

  J_0 ell<=4L+6ell<=202L,
  h_j<=2pi^2/ell<1,
  C_0=2M_d exp(d^2+1/16)<=2^20 sqrt(L).

The Gaussian boundary estimate is the one actually proved in the
supporting manuscript. Since h_j=2p d/Q_j and p<=pi, its trapezoid
exponent 2pi d/h_j is at least Q_j; replacing pi by p therefore improves
the required error inequality instead of perturbing an ideal lattice.

The omitted-window bound 128sqrt(L) exp(-L-ell) and the summed infinite
trapezoid bound 4C_0 J_0 ell exp(-L-3ell) are each below e^(-L)/64
already for L>=1 with the stated buffers. For discarded lattice tails,
b-h_j>=2sqrt(L)+15, so (b-h_j)^2>=2L+128. Monotone Gaussian-tail

For node counting, Q_j<= (L-m_j)_+ +3ell, and

  integral (L-|x|/2)_+ dx =2L^2.

The upper Riemann-sum and density overhead are O(L ell+ell^2), and

  q<=L^2/(p d)+O(L^(7/4))
    =L^2/pi^2+O(L^(7/4)).

All constants here are independent of spectra and dimensions. A simple
coarse check for the displayed bound is b<=ell, Q_j<=100L and

The Gaussian lattice sum h sum exp(-(nh-u)^2)<=sqrt(pi)+2h gives

4. POSITIVE WINDOW WEIGHTS WITHOUT A FUNCTION OR INTEGRAL ORACLE
--------------------------------------------------------------
The clipped interval W is obtained by comparisons. On it |w|<=R_0,
where R_0=4sqrt(L+1), so z=w^2/2<=8(L+1). For D=ceil[64(L+1)],
Taylor's real negative-exponential remainder gives

  |P_D(w)-exp(-w^2/2)|
     <= z^(D+1)/(D+1)!
     <= (e/8)^(D+1) <= 2^(-D).

It follows that |P_D^2-exp(-w^2)|<=3*2^(-D). Integrating this bound
over W, adding the tails beyond R_0 and the tiny p-normalization error,
gives the stated uniform absolute window error W2 (and certainly its
allowed relaxed constant 8). Absolute error suffices for tiny weights.

The square of a real polynomial is nonnegative. Its integral over any
positive-length W is positive because P_D is not the zero polynomial.
Thus the actually computed coefficients are nonnegative even when the
window is tiny. A zero-length or empty interval contributes zero and can
be omitted. No approximation is made after this exact polynomial
evaluation, so severe coefficient cancellation in floating point does
not invalidate the exact-arithmetic assertion.

Convolution precomputes P_D^2 in O(D^2); its primitive is an ordinary
degree-O(D) polynomial with explicitly divided coefficients. Horner
evaluation at the two clipped endpoints costs O(D) per node. Therefore
all weights cost O(D^2+qD)=O(L^3) field operations. There is no erf,
exp or integration primitive in this construction.

Multiplying the absolute weight error by the coarse q bound, h_j<1
and ||F(x)||<=1 gives an error below e^(-L)/16. The actual coefficient
mass is at most 2^10(L+1), as used later.

5. POSITIVE EXPONENTIAL NODES, DERIVATIVE AND POLE PERTURBATION
------------------------------------------------------------
All log nodes satisfy |x|<=B_0=2L+3ell+b=O(L+1). The least power of
two M_0>=8(B_0+1) satisfies M_0<=16(B_0+1). Thus |x/M_0|<=1/8.
Taylor's remainder, for N_0=ceil[64(L+1)], gives positive P and relative
error at most 2^(-2N_0). In particular

  |log(P^M_0)-x| <=2M_0*2^(-2N_0)
                    <=exp[-32(L+1)].

This logarithm is analysis only. Computing P and then its power by
repeated squaring uses O(L) arithmetic per node, or O(L^3) overall.
The positive physical node c*P^M_0 has an accuracy independent of the
magnitude of c. It remains on the positive real axis exactly.

For D_t=R_A-R_B, the identity D_t'=-R_A D_t-D_t R_B is valid without
commuting A and B. Together with ||D_t||<=min(1/t,c/t^2), it gives
||F'(x)||<=7/2<4. Therefore the logarithmic pole perturbation, weighted
by the actual coefficient mass, contributes below e^(-L)/16 in the
normalized problem. Replacing 1/pi by 1/p has the separately stated
tiny error. The final physical weights a_hat*t_hat*sqrt(t_hat)/p are
nonnegative and use only arithmetic and positive scalar square roots.

6. COMMON RAW PSD INVERSES, CORE ERRORS AND FIXED ITERATION COUNT
---------------------------------------------------------------
The preceding baseline audit supplies the common even-Neumann inverse
polynomial, with 0<=Rhat-R_A<=eta I/t simultaneously for all positive
shifts on one OSE event. This is an operator, not an RHS-dependent
stopping rule; hence Mhat=Q^T Rhat Q is exactly symmetric.

Write mu=1+c/t. If eta(c/t)mu<=1/2, inverse perturbation gives

  ||Hhat||<=2mu, ||Khat||<=2c mu,
  ||Khat-K||<=2eta c^2 mu^2/t.

With eta<=1, ||V||<=1/t, ||Vhat||<=2/t and ||Vhat-V||<=eta/t.
Expanding first in one V factor, then the other, then K gives the
coefficients 4+2 and 2, proving P1 exactly as stated.

After multiplication by t^(3/2), its scaled scalar powers are
s^(-1/2), s^(-3/2), s^(-5/2) and s^(-7/2). Since
e^(-B_1)<=s<=e^(B_1) and sum a_hat<=2^10(L+1), C=2^14 in P2 is
sufficient. The selected least even ell_inv>=16(B_0+L+10), with
eta=4^(-ell_inv), ensures eta<=1, the core invertibility condition and
the claimed error bound with a large universal margin. Its length is
O(L+1), with no downdate-margin or spectral condition dependence.

Khat=C(I+Mhat C)^(-1) is exactly symmetric and has rank rank(C),
also when C is singular. If C>=0 it is PSD. If C<=0 and B>=0, write
C=-D^2 analytically: I-D M D has inverse norm at most mu, so its
small perturbation I-D Mhat D remains positive definite under the
same condition. Hence Khat<=0. This justifies both one-sign variants
without a doubled factorization.

7. WIDTH, COST, FAILURE AND THE COMPUTATIONAL MODEL
---------------------------------------------------
The specified congruence elimination is valid: search all remaining
diagonal entries first; only when all vanish use a nonzero off-diagonal
entry a_ij and the invertible basis shear e_i to e_i+e_j, producing
diagonal 2a_ij. Each nonzero pivot reduces rank by one. A zero residual
can be omitted. Scaling nonzero pivots by sqrt(abs(pivot)) yields
exactly rank(C) signed columns at each node, in O(r^3) core arithmetic.
Multiplication by Vhat costs O(Nr^2), and no eigenvalue calculation
is hidden.

The combined quadrature, scalar, pole and solve errors are bounded by
sqrt(c)e^(-L). Since sqrt(c)<=k^(1/4)||E||^(1/2) and e^(-L)<=rho,
the requested intrinsic error follows. L=log(1/eps)+O(log(2r)), giving
the claimed leading width, uniformly over dimensions and spectra.

The full work is the stated

  ~O(Nm+m^3)+O(Nr^2+r^3)
    +O(Nmr L^3+m^3 L^2+L^3).

The r<=m<=N inequalities absorb every m^2r, Nr^2 and r^3 term.
All scalar setup/evaluation has been counted. Ceilings, powers of two
and interval/lattice indices can be found by bounded comparison loops
or binary searches; even direct bounded searches fit within O(L^3).
No floor/log/exp real primitive is needed.

All nodes and iteration counts are fixed before the solves. On a failed
sketch, singular core inverses or failed PSD tests return zero after
bounded work. On the good event no such test fails. G0+tI is always
SPD, even on a bad event. Thus the operation cap applies to every
random outcome. The one OSE event controls all shifts/RHS at once;
there is no union bound over infinitely many poles and no extra failure
or dimension factor in the width. Explicit N-by-N expansion costs
O(N^2*r*q), as separately charged.

8. GENERAL SIGNED RAW RESOLVENT: INDEPENDENT DERIVATION
------------------------------------------------------
For S=XJX^T, arbitrary real symmetric J, put G=X^TX and G0=L^TL.
On the OSE event X=ZL with

  (1-eta0)I <= Z^TZ <= (1+eta0)I, eta0=1/4,
  G-G0=L^TDL, ||D||<=eta0.

For z=1/zeta with zeta=it, the bridge's Woodbury equation, coarse
inverse and iteration are correct even when J is singular. Multiplying
the coefficient error by L gives exactly

  L e_(ell+1)=z K0(I-zK0)^(-1)D L e_ell,
  K0=LJL^T.

K0 is real symmetric. For purely imaginary z,
||zK0(I-zK0)^(-1)||<=1. Also the exact physical correction is
[(I-zS)^(-1)-I]h and has norm at most ||h||. Using the two Z singular
value bounds therefore gives the explicit physical operator estimate

  ||Rhat_zeta-R_zeta||
     <=sqrt((1+eta0)/(1-eta0))*eta0^ell/t
     =sqrt(5/3)*eta0^ell/t.

Thus C0<2 works independently of J, the base spectrum, rank deficiency
and the physical RHS. The statement applies simultaneously to every
column of Q and indeed every h, so there is no columnwise norm loss.

Let B0_z=A0_z^(-1)J. It is complex symmetric. The implemented finite
inverse operator has terms z^j B0_z[(G-G0)B0_z]^j. Each is the
alternating palindrome B0_z, G-G0, ..., B0_z and is exactly symmetric
under transpose. The outer physical resolvent formula preserves that
symmetry. Real coefficients and the same fixed iteration count give
Rhat_(-it)=conj(Rhat_(it)) exactly. Neither positivity nor normality
of the approximate operator is required.

The core perturbation proof in section 6 now applies in complex norm,
with nu=sqrt(5/3)*eta0^ell<=1. This proves bridge (6)--(7), including
the same constants and singular-C rank bound.

9. WHOLE-LINE INTEGRALS, SCALAR QUALIFICATION AND ORDER-OPTIMAL COST
------------------------------------------------------------------
With D=R_S-R_T, Im D_(it)=t[(S^2+t^2)^(-1)-(T^2+t^2)^(-1)]. Hence
the even-map difference is +c_e integral t^alpha Im D_(it)dt.
The real part is S(S^2+t^2)^(-1)-T(T^2+t^2)^(-1), so the odd-map
difference is -c_o integral t^alpha Re D_(it)dt. Both signs in (8)
are correct. The complex branches are single-pole combinations.

For analytic quadrature, real/imaginary notation on the real y-axis is
extended by the holomorphic paired-resolvent expressions
(D_(it)-D_(-it))/(2i) and (D_(it)+D_(-it))/2. It is not a claim that
literal complex conjugation is holomorphic. These identities are
already supplied by the source's rational-function derivation.

At t=a exp(y), |Im(plus/minus i t)|>=a exp(Re y)cos(d) on
|Im y|<=d<pi/2. This proves the claimed strip envelope by separate
resolvents and their difference, with no base norm. The uniform
trapezoid construction is therefore valid. Weighting (7) gives the
displayed scaled powers, whose geometric sum is bounded by a fixed
multiple of s_min^(alpha-4). Taking nu<=c_alpha*rho^(4/alpha)
makes this O(rho*a^alpha) and ensures the inverse perturbation
condition. Only O_alpha(log(1/rho)) raw iterations are needed.

Intrinsic a<=sqrt(r)||E||, and rho=eps/[C_alpha(r+1)], convert this
to eps||E||^alpha. At each real node, both real and imaginary parts
share the same [Re Vhat,Im Vhat] feature block and have rank <=2r.
Combining even/odd coefficients before factor extraction therefore
does not increase the node rank beyond 2r for a real homogeneous map.

All raw, small-core, factor extraction and setup operations have the
claimed conventional bound; the arbitrary dense symmetric J only adds
m^2r thin operations or m^3 setup. A fixed dyadic alpha can compute
t^(alpha+1) by scalar square roots and powers. Dyadic tail cutoffs
can be selected by comparisons with successive powers of 2^(-alpha),
without an unsupported reciprocal-exponent root primitive.

For arbitrary real non-dyadic alpha, the source explicitly requires
additional charged scalar-power evaluations. The audit preserves that
qualification. It does not infer a bit-independent arithmetic-only
implementation of a^alpha for arbitrary alpha.

10. RECTANGULAR DILATION, PARITY AND EXPLICIT OUTPUT
----------------------------------------------------
The dilation uses ambient dimension N+m, latent width 2m and update
rank at most 2r. Since N>=m, all setup/thin actions remain inside the
same ~O_alpha(Nmr+m^3) bound. The update norm is exactly ||Delta||,
so intrinsic normalization gives the claimed error after block
extraction, including at singular endpoints.

The blockwise OSE preserves the reverse Gram sandwich and enforces
the exact parity algebra. With Qsign the latent signature,

  Qsign B0_z Qsign=-B0_(-z),
  Qsign(G-G0)Qsign=G-G0.

The degree-j alternating term acquires (-1)^(j+1), giving one overall
minus sign after replacing z by -z. The external resolvent scaling
then proves P Rhat_(it)P=-conj(Rhat_(it)) as stated.

The physical core identity can be written

  Dhat=Rhat E_d(I+Rhat E_d)^(-1)Rhat,

which requires no inverse of Rhat. Invertibility of the middle matrix
follows from the same small-core determinant lemma. Since P E_d P=-E_d,
direct substitution gives P Dhat P=-conj(Dhat). Thus diagonal blocks
are purely imaginary and off-diagonal blocks are real, exactly rather
than approximately. Their complex ranks are at most rank(E_d)<=2r.
Removing the scalar i from a purely imaginary block gives a real matrix
with the same rank; a real off-diagonal block also has unchanged rank.
This is why extraction costs 2r per node rather than 4r.

The representation is independent of the arbitrary QR basis chosen
for the update: the physical formula uses E_d itself. A generic
realification may initially use 4r columns; exact thin QR and small
rank factorization compress each extracted block to at most 2r in
O(Nr^2+r^3) work. The polar block is rectangular and can use ordinary
rank factorization, not a nonexistent rectangular signed Cholesky.

Right absolute-power output costs O(m^2*r*q), and polar output
O(Nm*r*q), both absorbed by ~O_alpha(Nmr+m^3). Left absolute-power
N-by-N expansion has the stated extra O(N^2*r*q) cost. No alpha=0
discontinuous polar-factor conclusion is valid or claimed.

11. MAKING THE SHARP HALF-STRIP/GENERAL-ALPHA UPGRADE LITERAL
-----------------------------------------------------------
The bridge deliberately states its sharp upgrade conditional on the
shared scalar realization. The preceding audit supplies that realization;
the following modular parameter adaptation makes the conclusion precise.

For alpha=1/2 whole-line maps, use the same computed p<=pi but set
d=p/2-L^(-1/4), not p-L^(-1/4). The poles remain purely imaginary
because only the proof strip changes. Its distance to pi/2 is at least
L^(-1/4), and cos(d)>=2L^(-1/4)/pi. The normalized logarithmic
integrands therefore have a polynomial envelope C sqrt(L). With this
half-strip, the same Gaussian/windows, clipped polynomial squares,
positive exp-node approximation and common raw solves apply. Enlarging
the coarse q bound by a universal constant, if needed, does not affect
the leading coefficient or scalar O(L^3) work. The actual integrand's
real-axis norm and derivative have uniform constant bounds, by the same
resolvent-difference argument, so all weight/node perturbations remain
within the budget. A common half-strip node is factored at width 2r
for real maps and at width r for a single complex branch.

For fixed general alpha use g(x)=alpha*(-x)_++beta*x_+ and retain
windows meeting [-(L+ell)/alpha,(L+ell)/beta]. Use the same
Q_j=max(ell,L-min_(I_j)g+3ell), h_j=2p d/Q_j. Bounds depend on fixed
alpha through the interval lengths and envelope constants. Fixed
alpha-dependent buffers can replace 32 and 16, or a finite-small-L
fallback to the already proved uniform-grid algorithm can be used.
The Gaussian proof then gives

  q <= L^2/(4p d alpha beta)+O_alpha(L^(7/4)).

The retained nodes still have |x|=O_alpha(L+1). Degree-O_alpha(L)
Taylor squares and scaled exponential polynomials therefore cost
O_alpha(L^3) scalar arithmetic, with exponentially small absolute
window and logarithmic node errors. The normalized integrand derivative
is O_alpha(1), from ||D_t'||<=2||D_t||/t. Small-core errors need only
nu=exp[-C_alpha L], hence O_alpha(L) fixed raw iterations. All scalar
power qualifications from section 9 remain in force.

At the limiting strip boundaries, q times the proved node ranks gives

  PSD power:          r/(4*pi^2*alpha*beta),
  one complex branch: r/(2*pi^2*alpha*beta),
  real whole-line:    r/(  pi^2*alpha*beta),
  rectangular maps:  r/(  pi^2*alpha*beta)

as the leading coefficients of log^2(1/eps). Rectangular extraction
uses its actual 2r node rank, not the generic dilation's realification
bound. For alpha=1/2 these become r/pi^2, 2r/pi^2, 4r/pi^2 and
4r/pi^2, respectively. Thus the conditional sharp implementation gate
is closed in the specified exact-arithmetic/scalar-power models.
