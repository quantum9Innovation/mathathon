3. Weighted entropy lower: normalization and degree transfer

Finite weighted error forces a double zero at x=1 after cancellation.
Thus division by (x-1)^2 lowers numerator degree by two. The Mobius
map gives a diagonal type[m/m] approximant to H, with continuous bounded
endpoint values. Subtracting 1/2 preserves this degree bound.
The factor 2 in A_m >= 2 E_(m,m)(G) follows from f_2=(x-1)^2/2.
The exact inversion identity H(x)+H(1/x)=1 makes G odd.

After removing actual poles on the interval, a real denominator has a
constant sign. Choosing it positive validates the denominator-weighted
odd rationalization. Its numerator and denominator retain degree <=m;
its denominator remains positive, and the error bound follows pointwise
from positive weights. No uniqueness or minimax existence theorem is
silently needed. The resulting odd rational is u R(u^2), with degree
parameter at most floor(m/2), including the even-m numerator restriction.

4. Endpoint Markov lower and fixed prefactor

The representation for G through F and the integral for J=-F are exact.
The removable values at zero and the endpoint value at one are correct.
Splitting at v=1/2 and substituting w=4y-3 gives precisely the displayed
density (t-1)/(2(t+3)^(3/2)) on [1,5]. It is comparable to t-1 by positive
fixed constants, so the primary theorem applies with alpha=1.

The complementary integral is analytic in a fixed complex neighborhood.
Subtracting a degree-ell polynomial from a type[k/k] rational consumes
at most ell diagonal degrees. With ell=ceil(K sqrt(k+1)) and K log(rho)
strictly greater than 2pi, the polynomial error is negligible compared
with exp(-2pi sqrt(k)); the degree shift changes the latter by a fixed
factor. Hence the subtraction really proves a fixed-prefactor lower.
It does not prove only an exponent limit or conceal a polynomial factor.

Restricting y to [1/2,1] costs the claimed factor 1/sqrt(2). The parity
half-degree then produces exp(-pi sqrt(2m)) with the correct direction

The positive m-kernel class lies within type[m+1/m], even if a zero node
contributes a quadratic term. The earlier positive upper therefore gives
the stated matching sandwich for A_m and P_m.
