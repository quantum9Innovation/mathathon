Independent reconstruction of the new argument
-----------------------------------------------
Write s(x)=sum_i x_i, delta=d1-d2, and choose a fixed odd prime p>d2.
Let e_x be the pointwise sup discrepancy and eps=sum_x e_x.

1. Single-block kernel/affine estimates.
For x=a*1, (C-delta I)x=d2*a*1, so only a=0 is in the kernel modulo p.
For nonconstant x, the reference probability at delta*x equals
p^(-N+1) exactly when d2*s(x)=0, hence s(x)=0. There are at most
p^(N-1) such x. The reference expected kernel is therefore at most2,
and the true one at most2+eps.

For every fixed rhs b, each nonempty solution set of
(C-delta I)x=b is a translate of its kernel, pointwise for each realized
C. Thus the expectation of its size is <=2+eps uniformly in b. Replacing
C*x by U_x changes that entire affine sum by at most eps, giving the
source's bound2+2eps. In fact the reference affine sum is directly <=2:
there is at most one constant x satisfying d2*a*1=b, and its nonconstant
solutions satisfy d2*s(x)=s(b), numbering at most p^(N-1). The source's
weaker estimate is valid.

2. The two substitutions really have summable error.
Put P_x(r)=Pr(C*x=r), Q_x(r)=Pr(U_x=r). Independence of C1 and C2,
conditional on a fixed B, gives the exact expected-kernel sum
 sum_x,y P_x(delta*x-B*y) P_y(delta*y-B^T*x).
Replacing its first factor has absolute total error at most
 sum_x e_x [sum_y P_y(delta*y-B^T*x)] <=eps(2+eps).
After this replacement, replacing the second factor costs at most
 sum_y e_y [sum_x Q_x(delta*x-B*y)] <=eps(2+2eps).
All brackets are genuine fixed-right-hand-side affine sums. This avoids
the otherwise fatal factor p^N from a naive pointwise error estimate.
The claimed4eps+3eps^2 total error is correct and uniform in B.

3. Reference sum, all four types of pairs.
If x and y are nonconstant, both support constraints reduce to
d2[s(x)+s(y)]=0. They coincide, so there are at most p^(2N-1) pairs,
each of weight p^(-2N+2). Their contribution is <=p.

If x=a*1 and y=b*1, both equations are equivalent to a+b=0 because
the row/column sums of B are d2. Exactly p pairs contribute weight1.

If x=a*1 and y is nonconstant, the deterministic first equation gives
B*y=-d2*a*1. For each a its solution set has <=p^(N-rank_p B) points.
Each eligible nonconstant y contributes at most p^(-N+1) from the
second factor. Summing over the p values of a gives at most
p^(2-rank_p B). The opposite orientation gives the same bound because
rank(B)=rank(B^T). Therefore the final moment bound
 E|ker_Fp(A-delta I)| <=2p+2p^(2-rank_p B)+4eps+3eps^2
is valid. Cases where p divides N cause no problem: constant vectors
may then lie in the sum-zero hyperplane, but they are excluded from
the nonconstant count, and every upper bound remains true.

4. Uniform rank bound for the cross block.
A simple d2-regular biadjacency matrix has d2 nonzero entries per row
and per column. A chosen row's support meets at most
1+d2(d2-1) rows, including itself. Greedily choosing disjoint row
supports yields at least N/[1+d2(d2-1)] linearly independent rows over
every field. This uses entries1 and disjoint support, not a questionable
real-to-modular rank comparison. For fixed d2 this rank tends linearly
to infinity, uniformly over all admissible B.

5. Real kernel conclusion and the order of limits.
The integer vector (1,-1) is always in the kernel. Real nullity>=2
forces all(2N-1)-minors to vanish as integers, so modular nullity>=2
for every prime. On that event the modular kernel has at least p^2
elements. Markov and the moment bound give limsup_N P(failure)<=2/p.
The proof fixes p first, lets even N tend to infinity, and only then
lets admissible primes tend to infinity. It does not use a growing-prime
mixing theorem. Subtracting the guaranteed p kernel elements could improve
the displayed numerical bound to1/(p-1), but is unnecessary. The supplied
argument proves the real nullity is exactly one with probability1-o(1)
in the internal-matching model, uniformly over deterministic simple B.

Transfer details reconstructed without assuming matching unions uniform:
If P_n and Q_n are contiguous and 0<=f_n<=1, then E_P f_n->0 implies
E_Q f_n->0: for fixed t>0, Markov gives P(f_n>t)->0, contiguity gives
Q(f_n>t)->0, and E_Q f_n<=t+Q(f_n>t). Applying this bounded-function
criterion sequentially proves preservation under finite independent
products. Applying it to f_n(C1,C2), the conditional failure probability
over an independent B with any common distribution, adjoins that B.
Finally both loopless configurations are simple with probability bounded
below for fixed degree; conditioning on both being simple preserves
probability1-o(1) and gives independent uniform simple internal graphs.
Since the conditioning only concerns internal factors, B retains its
independent original law. Every transfer step in the source is valid.

Conclusion and exact scope
--------------------------
The audited derived proof establishes, for every fixed d1>=3 and d2>0,
along even block sizes N, that the uniform-simple RSBM adjacency on2N
vertices has dim_R ker(A-(d1-d2)I)=1 with probability tending to one.
It also permits any independently chosen sequence of simple d2-regular
cross matrices rather than only the uniform random cross model.
When d1 is odd, all admissible block sizes are already even. When d1
is even, this argument does not cover odd block sizes. It does not cover
d1=2. There is no claimed error rate, robustness guarantee, or fixed-small-
prime success probability tending to one. Exact arithmetic recovers the
balanced planted sign vector on the nullity-one event by the earlier
deterministic certificate proposition.
