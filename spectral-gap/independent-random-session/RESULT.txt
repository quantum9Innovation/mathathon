Research outcome: a sharp worst-case threshold for growing cubic bases
November 15, 2026

The concrete target was a random-lift theorem uniform over growing base
graphs. The session proves a sufficient sublinear range and disproves a
linear range, even for connected simple cubic bases. The proposed result
is a parameter refinement of an existing method. Its historical novelty
has not been established and it is not presented as a new spectral technique.

Main graph statement

For a deterministic connected loopless base G with D vertices and maximum
degree Delta, let G^(n) be its random n-lift using independent uniform edge
permutations. Let new denote the space whose coordinate sum is zero in each
base fibre. The proof gives

  P( ||A_(G^(n))|new|| >= rho(T_G)+epsilon )
     <= C (D/n) (Delta/epsilon)^9.

Here C is absolute and independent of the number of base edges. The
threshold is considered below Delta, with the boundary handled by a limit.
For bounded degree, D=o(n) therefore suffices for a uniform upper bound
rho(T_G)+o_P(1). In the d-regular case rho(T_G)=2sqrt(d-1).

For the converse, take a cycle of m diamonds K4 minus one edge. Connect the
two deficient-degree ports between neighboring blocks. This is a connected
simple cubic base on D=4m vertices. Its n-lift contains a diamond above one
block with exact probability

  p_n = sum_(k=1)^n (-1)^(k+1) / [k! (n)_k]
      = 1/n + O(n^-2).

The internal events are independent between blocks. Each diamond creates a
new adjacency eigenvalue arbitrarily close to

  L = alpha+2/alpha = 2.83597591908132... > 2sqrt(2),
  where alpha^3-alpha-2=0 and alpha>1.

Consequently, if m/n -> c>0, the probability of a new eigenvalue at least
L-epsilon has limit inferior at least 1-exp(-c), for each epsilon>0. If
m/n -> infinity, that probability tends to one. The sufficient range
D=o(n) is therefore sharp as a worst-case condition on simple cubic base
size alone. It is not claimed necessary for every individual base family.

Why this is a graph consequence

The upper proof uses matrix-valued random permutations and a rational trace
argument. A universal denominator replaces the source's generator-count
logarithm with a logarithm of the trace-polynomial degree. Fourier summation
absorbs that loss through nine derivatives. This removes the generator
logarithm from the finite tail bound, allowing all D=o(n), rather than only
D(log D)^a=o(n) from the directly displayed source bounds.

The lower proof transfers a finite Perron witness from the regular-tree
completion of the diamond into the lift using Friedman's covering
pushforward. Collisions of branches are harmless. Projection off the old
fibre space loses at most O(S/n), where S is the fixed witness support.
This last step ensures that the outlier belongs to the new spectrum.

The scalar exact mixed-product extrema in the current manuscript are not
inputs to this proof. No fractional-update or signing direction was used.

Checks and remaining obligations

The upper proof was independently reconstructed twice, and the lower proof
was independently accepted at its recorded hash. No unresolved internal
proof step was found. The five-page TeX document compiled successfully with
pdflatex. Exact source examples were rerun successfully. Numerical checks
covered 81 radial quotients and seven finite lift witnesses, including
deliberate branch collisions. These are corroboration, not premises of
the general written arguments.

The proof depends on CGVTvH's published exact first-order support theorem.
It does not reprove that entire theorem. No current mathematical gap has
been identified in the new derivation. Publication-level priority and
external expert review remain open. The exponent nine is not claimed optimal.
No complete critical spectral law or simultaneous adaptive-base event is
proved. No end-to-end formal verification has been performed.

The full source reads also identify incorrect intermediate assertions.
They do not refute the main spectral theorems. In particular the false
fundamental-order bound in the 2014 Friedman-Kohler text is already corrected
in the authors' 2019 sixth article, so it is not a new contribution here.
