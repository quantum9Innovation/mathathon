         Unknown-frame recovery in sparse block-regular graphs
          Moments, certified algorithms, and sharp ambiguity
                                         Research manuscript

                                          November 15, 2026


                                                Abstract
         We recover an unknown bounded-size degree frame and its planted classes in sparse block-
     regular graphs, from the graph and fixed order and degree bounds alone. Independent uniform
     simple internal and cross factors may have unequal class sizes, varying total degrees, and absent
     internal edges. The hypothesis is a symmetric omission certificate with weights in [0, 1/2)
     and unit physical row sums; zero weights are permitted. The plant is, with high probability,
     the unique finest bounded-class equitable partition. A bounded raw finite-field channel moment
     gives complete partition listing in unconditional expected nearly quadratic bit work and expected
     nearly linear space. Its proof uses heterogeneous root/output spaces and selected cancellation-
     edge certificates to handle inactive quotient channels. A certified complete-kernel algorithm
     charges every input before averaging. We also prove disjoint old and new spectra for all fixed
     connected simple regular base lifts, including bipartite bases and irrational old modes. Exact
     two-class ambiguity and hidden-prior Bayes error constants cover every a ≥ 0, b ≥ 1, a + b ≥ 3.
     Complete bases give sharp lift ambiguity, while cycle lifts have optimal success tending to zero.
     Separate prescribed-cross spectral theorems yield rational recovery under their stated internal
     degree hypotheses. Direct cover-recovery algorithms and classical counting, mixing and linear-
     algebra inputs are credited; historical priority is not certified.

1    Main results and relation to earlier work
An equitable partition records the exact number of neighbors a vertex has in each class. Its indicator
J satisfies AJ = JQ, where Q is the degree frame. We recover the finest partition with a fixed
bound on its class count. Neither that frame, its order, nor its class sizes are supplied. The bound
is essential, since the singleton partition is always equitable.
    The model has independent uniform simple regular internal factors and biregular cross factors
on a fixed connected reversible frame. Sizes may be unequal and total degrees may vary. Our
sufficient condition is the feasibility of the symmetric linear certificate

The weights belong to physical neighbor slots; an internal weight has no extra half factor. They
need not be supplied to the algorithm. Every common total degree d ≥ 3 is covered by qij = 1/d.
The condition also covers every connected frame with at least two types and internal degrees at least
two, and further mixed-degree frames with no internal edges. Some frames require zero weights;
those are included.
    Theorem 2.1 proves that every bounded-class equitable partition coarsens the plant, with failure
O(n−(dmin −2) ). The raw finite-field moment in Theorem 3.2 and the certified kernel algorithm give
complete partition listing in unconditional expected O(n  e 2 ) bit work, expected O(n)
                                                                                     e    workspace, and
expected O(n) output bits. The algorithm receives only fixed class-count and degree bounds. It
retains a partition with the largest class count and recovers the frame from integer neighbor counts.
A deterministic polynomial truncation preserves the structural rate.
    Independent lifts of every fixed connected simple regular base of degree at least three are a
special case, for every positive lift size. All old eigenvalues also retain their exact base multiplicities
with high probability, including bipartite bases and irrational old modes (Theorem 8.1). Com-
plete bases attain the structural ambiguity power, with explicit leading constant. The fixed-motif
occurrence coefficients have classical precursors in Friedman–Kohler [22]; the argument here also
classifies the alternatives contributing to recovery error. In contrast, unconditioned cycle lifts have
optimal hidden-fiber recovery success tending to zero even with their base supplied.
    The two-class results cover every fixed a ≥ 0, b ≥ 1, a+b ≥ 3, including assortative, equal-degree
and disassortative choices. We compute exact leading ambiguity and hidden-prior Bayes constants.
Separate theorems allow prescribed cross factors and identify fixed quotient eigenspaces, giving
exact rational row clustering. Those robust prescribed-cross quantifiers have their own internal-
degree hypotheses; they are not inferred from the random-cross theorem.
    Here O e suppresses logarithmic bit factors. All degrees, frame orders, fields, target dimensions
and class bounds are fixed. An always-correct Las Vegas listing algorithm terminates almost surely.

Recovering the particular hidden plant is a separate statistical event. The complete-kernel certifi-

Direct cover-recovery precedents. Fellows and Koblitz explicitly discussed recovery of the
four hidden fibers in random covers of K4 in their Kid Krypto construction [18]. Bernstein, Cottaar
and Lange’s 2023 evaluation of IPCC already applies echelon reduction to the public equations
(A + I)v = 1 and groups identical affine linear forms to expose those fibers [19]. Their reported
experiments include both exact four-fiber recovery and localized ambiguities. This is a direct
ancestor of the algebraic fiber-recovery method, and its use of Gaussian elimination and coordinate
grouping is not claimed as originating here. Our statements concern the explicitly proved fixed-
parameter ranges, raw moments, statistical constants and certified expected-cost composition.

Other recovery and spectral precedents. Brito, Dumitriu, Ganguly, Hoffman and Tran
proved rigidity, recovery under an adjacency outlier condition, and bootstrapping in the two-class
regular model [1]. Barucca proposed full recovery in equitable graphs beyond that regime, with
numerical evidence [2]. Karmakar and Podder proved multicommunity uniqueness for sufficiently
large positive degree entries under comparability hypotheses [9]; their scope is not restricted to as-
sortative frames. Brito, Dumitriu and Harris give clustering for supplied nonsingular frames under
degree scaling and eigenvalue separation [10]. Zhu and Zhu’s Lemma 9.1 already states planted-
eigenvalue simplicity in the random-cross two-class model [3], so no first-simplicity claim is made.
A norm bound alone does not exclude an exact collision inside the limiting bulk. Exact inherited
eigenvalues already make noiseless lift detection easy; identifying the finest hidden fibers differs
from that detection task and its noisy versions [11].

Counting, moments, and computation precedents. The graph-cover multinomial enumera-
tor and its entropy interpretation are classical [12]. Gaussian and lattice prefactors for positive in-
terior phases have close precedents in Mori–Tanaka [13, 14]. Huang’s thesis and Huang–Vontobel’s
loop-calculus argument also give uniform bounds on normalized raw cover expectations under a
loop-weight dominance condition [21, 20]. These results are credited rather than treating cover
prefactors or bounded raw expectations as new methods. Multitype star/edge entropy also appears
in the Gaussian-wave framework of Dembo–McKenzie [15].
     Mészáros proves stronger fixed-group mixing in global permutation/matching regular ensem-
bles [4]; vector alphabets there encode arbitrary matrix targets. Nguyen–Wood develop cokernel
moments and modular-to-real transfer [5], and Huang combines configuration counts, local limit
estimates and large deviations for sparse invertibility [16]. Our proof classifies the actual boundary
phases and controls their leakage with conditional switching and rank comparison. The sparse
algebra uses Wiedemann sequences and established preconditioners [7, 8], with full per-input cer-
tification and an explicit averaging argument.
     These comparisons delimit the results proved here. They do not certify historical priority. No
noise theorem, growing-parameter uniformity, simplicity of the new spectrum, or numerical-stability
claim is implicit.

A.6    Assembly of the strong-mixing sum
Constant inputs have zero error. Choose the nearconstant radius η so small that h(η)+η log(p−1) <
log p, and take the central window ∥ν − u∥2 ≤ B log(n + 1)/n, with B sufficiently large for (104).
These two regions are eventually disjoint. Near constants the actual maximum atoms sum to o(1)
by (100); the reference sum is o(1) because there are exponentially fewer than pn such inputs.
In the intermediate region the actual sum is O(n−2 ), and the reference sum is at most p times
the probability that iid uniform labels miss the central empirical window, which tends to zero by

Hoeffding and a fixed-label union bound. At the center the per-input error is o(p−n+1 ) uniformly,
and there are at most pn inputs. Their total error is o(1) with p fixed. This proves Theorem A.1.
