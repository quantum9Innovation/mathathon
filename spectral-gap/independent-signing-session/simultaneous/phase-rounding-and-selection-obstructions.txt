1. A useful positive reduction and its exact scope

Primary input: Greaves--Mohar--O, "Interlacing families and the Hermitian
spectral norm of digraphs", arXiv:1602.06274, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2.
For every finite simple graph G there is a Hermitian matrix H with entries
H_uv=i*s_uv on edges, s_vu=-s_uv in {+1,-1}, and

                  ||H|| <= rho(mu_G),                        (1)

where mu_G is the matching polynomial. Its spectrum is symmetric, which is
why the one-sided interlacing conclusion controls both sides in this model.
https://arxiv.org/html/1602.06274

Suppose phases theta_v and a constant c>0 satisfy

           |sin(theta_v-theta_u)|=c  for every uv in E(G).    (2)

Let D_vv=exp(i theta_v), K=D* H D, and take its entrywise real part. Then
Re K is a real symmetric matrix whose edge entries have modulus c. Hence
Re K=c A_sigma for a genuine real signing sigma. Moreover

  Re K=(K+conj(K))/2,  ||conj(K)||=||K||=||H||,

so triangle inequality and (1) give

                  ||A_sigma|| <= rho(mu_G)/c.                (3)

This proof is valid; it does not invoke the mixed-root recovery measure.
It is the mechanism being developed by the parent worker in this session.

If G admits a graph homomorphism f:G -> C_L for odd L=2m+1, choose

                  theta_v=pi*m*f(v)/L.

Use representatives f(v) in {0,...,L-1}. Across an edge the difference of
representatives is +/-1 or +/-(L-1). Since pi*m*L/L=pi*m, all four cases
have sine of the same absolute value

                   c=sin(pi*m/L)=cos(pi/(2L)).

Therefore

      ||A_sigma|| <= rho(mu_G) sec(pi/(2L))
                  <= 2 sqrt(d-1) sec(pi/(2L))                 (4)

for graphs of maximum degree at most d>=2. Thus a sequence with odd-cycle
homomorphisms of lengths L -> infinity has coefficient 2+O(L^-2).
The matching-root comparison in the last inequality is the standard MSS
universal-cover bound. No general implication from girth to these
homomorphisms is used.

2. Exact characterization of the constant-modulus phase hypothesis

The following elementary theorem precisely identifies the extra input (2).
It is stronger than merely observing that girth does not supply it.

THEOREM. Let G be a finite connected graph with an edge. Define

 c_*(G)=sup {c in [0,1]: phases theta_v satisfy (2)}.

If G is bipartite then c_*(G)=1. If G is nonbipartite and admits a
homomorphism to an odd cycle, let L(G) be the greatest odd integer L>=3
such that G -> C_L. Then

                    c_*(G)=cos(pi/(2L(G))).                  (5)

If G has no homomorphism to an odd cycle then c_*(G)=0. In particular,
chi(G)>=4 implies c_*(G)=0.

PROOF. The bipartite case follows by assigning theta=0 on one part and
theta=pi/2 on the other. Now suppose c>0 and put alpha=arcsin c in
(0,pi/2]. Work in the group R/(pi Z). Condition (2) says that each edge
has phase difference +alpha or -alpha. After choosing one root, every
vertex phase belongs to the root's coset of the subgroup generated by
alpha.

If alpha/pi is irrational, this subgroup is isomorphic to Z: two integer
multiples of alpha agree modulo pi only if the integers agree. Thus each
vertex has a well-defined integer k_v and edges satisfy k_v-k_u=+/-1.
The parity of k_v is a bipartition. This is impossible for nonbipartite G.

If alpha/pi=a/b in lowest terms, the subgroup is cyclic of order b, and
the same argument assigns k_v modulo b with differences +/-1. Hence
G maps to the b-cycle. Here b>=2; if b is even, k_v modulo 2 is again a
bipartition. For nonbipartite G, b must therefore be odd and at least 3.

The set of such odd cycle lengths is finite. Indeed, let ell be the length
of any fixed odd cycle in G. A homomorphism of that cycle to C_b gives an
ell-step closed walk with steps +/-1 modulo b. Its integer displacement
is odd and a multiple of odd b, hence is nonzero, and has absolute value
at most ell. Consequently b<=ell. In particular b<=L(G).

For odd b and any integer a,

 |sin(pi*a/b)| <= cos(pi/(2b)) <= cos(pi/(2L(G))).

This bounds every admissible c. Conversely, a homomorphism G -> C_L(G)
and the phase assignment in Section 1 attain the last quantity. If no odd
cycle homomorphism exists, the preceding alternatives exclude every c>0.
Finally every odd cycle is 3-colorable, so chi(G)>=4 excludes every such
homomorphism. This completes the proof.

Interpretation: allowing arbitrary global diagonal phases in this
constant-modulus projection argument does not enlarge its applicability
beyond bipartite graphs and graphs mapping to odd cycles. It does not give
a missing universal high-girth mechanism. Equation (5) optimizes the phase
constant in this reduction; it does not show that a better orientation H,
a nonconstant edge normalization, or another mechanism cannot do better.

3. A necessary spectral test for phases

There is also a simple necessary condition which applies before computing
L(G). If G is d-regular and (2) holds, put z_v=exp(2i theta_v). On each edge

                  Re(conj(z_u)z_v)=1-2c^2.

Thus

 z* A_G z / (z*z)=d(1-2c^2),
 lambda_min(A_G) <= d(1-2c^2),
 c^2 <= (1-lambda_min(A_G)/d)/2.                              (6)

For example, if lambda_min(A_G)>=-2sqrt(d-1)-epsilon, then

 c^2 <= (1+2sqrt(d-1)/d+epsilon/d)/2.

For fixed d>2 and epsilon -> 0 this stays strictly below one. Thus even
high-girth nonbipartite nearly Ramanujan bases cannot generally obtain a
vanishing loss from constant-modulus phase projection. This is a conditional
spectral obstruction; the algebra does not require an existence theorem for
such a sequence. It is not a lower bound on their optimal real signing norm.

4. Simultaneous endpoint selection already fails on arbitrarily long cycles

For odd ell>=3, consider the cycle C_ell. Switching reduces every signing
to its cycle product tau in {+1,-1}. Its characteristic polynomial is

             p_tau(x)=2(T_ell(x/2)-tau),                    (7)

where T_ell(cos t)=cos(ell t). One way to verify (7) is to diagonalize the
twisted cyclic shift: the eigenvalues are 2cos((2pi k+eta)/ell), with
eta=0 when tau=+1 and eta=pi when tau=-1. Both sides of (7) are monic of
degree ell with exactly those roots including multiplicity.

Every convex combination of p_+ and p_- equals

             2(T_ell(x/2)-s),  with -1<=s<=1.

This is real-rooted: between each consecutive pair of the ell+1 extrema
2cos(j*pi/ell), j=0,...,ell, it has one root for -1<s<1; the endpoint
cases follow by continuity. In fact the extrema at j=1,...,ell-1 give a
common interlacing. The full edge-signing family is an actual interlacing
family too: whenever at least one cycle edge is still random, the averaged
polynomial is their equal mean; only the last choice branches to p_+,p_-.

The equal mean is

             p_0(x)=2T_ell(x/2),

with extreme roots +/-r_ell, where

             r_ell=2cos(pi/(2ell))<2.

But every signing of this odd cycle has norm exactly 2. The tau=+1
signing has eigenvalue +2; the tau=-1 signing has eigenvalue -2 because
ell is odd. Consequently there is NO leaf simultaneously satisfying

         lambda_max<=r_ell  AND  lambda_min>=-r_ell.          (8)

This disproves the tempting simultaneous-selection implication even when
all convex combinations are real-rooted, the average has symmetric roots,
the family is a genuine signing family, and girth can be arbitrarily large.
The endpoint gap is exactly

        2-r_ell=2(1-cos(pi/(2ell)))
               =pi^2/(4ell^2)+O(ell^-4).

Scope: degree 2, and an obstruction to exact selection relative to the
matching roots. It does not disprove a 2+o_g(1) signing theorem, and the
gap tends to zero. The elementary cycle calculation is not claimed as a
new historical result. It gives an explicit test that any proposed
simultaneous-root-selection principle must pass.

The established MSS theorem selects a leaf's largest root; it does not
assert (8). Compare MSS arXiv:1304.4132 and GMO Lemma 3.1, which states
the one-sided selection property explicitly.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.4132
https://arxiv.org/html/1602.06274

5. A high-girth parity-repair obstruction with exact fraction 2/3

For any integer r>=2, let Theta_r consist of three internally disjoint
paths P1,P2,P3 of length r between the same two endpoints. It is simple
and subcubic, with girth 2r. It has exactly three cycles, all shortest and
even: C12=P1+P2, C13=P1+P3, C23=P2+P3, where + denotes XOR of edges.
They satisfy

                         C12+C13+C23=0.

If a_i is the product of edge signs on P_i, the cycle signs are a1*a2,
a1*a3,a2*a3; their product is +1. Hence they cannot all be negative.
Exactly two can be negative (take a1=-1,a2=a3=1), so the largest fraction
of shortest cycles which can simultaneously be made negative is 2/3.

This can be realized inside a connected simple cubic graph of girth 2r
with no other shortest cycles. Start with a finite connected simple cubic
graph R of girth >2r, whose existence is classical. For every missing unit
of degree of Theta_r, take a disjoint copy of R, subdivide one of its edges
with a new vertex w, and join w by one edge to the deficient core vertex.
The subdividing vertex had degree two and gains its third edge; the core
vertex gains exactly one. Every joining edge is a bridge. Thus the resulting
graph is cubic, connected, simple, contains the theta core induced, and has
girth exactly 2r. The only cycles of length 2r are the core's three cycles.

The same bridge-cap construction proves a useful elementary completion
lemma: any finite connected simple graph of maximum degree three and girth
at least g embeds induced in a connected simple cubic graph of girth at
least g. Attach one disjoint subdivided high-girth cubic cap per missing
incident edge. Varying the cap order gives infinitely many completions.

Scope: this defeats the blanket instruction to make all shortest even
cycles negative, even at arbitrarily high girth and in cubic graphs.
It is NOT a spectral counterexample and gives no lower bound on the best
norm. The parity identity is a direct specialization of the general odd
dependence obstruction in Suvagiya, arXiv:2607.17343v2, Section 5, so no
novelty claim is made for that principle.
https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17343v2

6. Current obstruction literature and the exact unresolved issue

Xu arXiv:2609.15591v4 establishes a cubic obstruction to the exact general
two-sided Ramanujan bound. Its seed is a triangle with trees, followed by
binary Schur-response amplification. It does not assert a general
high-girth asymptotic impossibility.
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15591v4
