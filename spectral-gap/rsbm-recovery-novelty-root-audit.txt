Conclusion
----------
The inspected primary literature does not provide a complete verified
all-parameter polynomial exact-recovery argument for the uniform-simple,
two-equal-block RSBM, for every fixed a>b>0 and every admissible N.
It does contain both the conjecture and a printed stronger simplicity
assertion whose cited proof has a scope gap. Therefore this audit does
NOT certify historical firstness, and it would be inaccurate to say that
the recovery conclusion or the algebraic idea had never been proposed
or stated before.

The defensible present contribution is an independently supplied complete
argument: a polynomial binary-kernel moment, quantitative Boolean entropy
rigidity, and exact enumeration plus verification. The aggregate-fiber
bound E[2^corank]<=O_{a,b}(N^24), valid for every fixed degree pair and all
admissible N, is more precise than the inspected existing entropy
statements and avoids relying on simplicity at a prescribed eigenvalue.
I found no directly applicable primary theorem that supplies this exact
moment or the complete recovery argument. This remains a bounded search
conclusion, not a proof of novelty.

For a scope that clearly exceeds the printed two-community lemma, the
general-frame direction is stronger: arbitrary fixed frame, internal
degree at least two, and potentially unknown quotient and number of
classes. Existing multiclass uniqueness and frame-clustering theorems
have significant degree or separation restrictions. HOWEVER the snapshot
of partitioning-multiclass-coarse-root.txt read here explicitly labels
itself an unaudited proposal. A novelty audit is not a correctness audit
of that expanded theorem. Its headline must wait for its separate full
proof and actual-source review.

The two-community model is important: independent uniform simple
a-regular internal graphs on N vertices each, and an independent uniform
simple b-regular bipartite cross graph. An ordinary independent-edge SBM,
a degree-corrected SBM, a globally degree-regular model with only total
interblock counts prescribed, and a general microcanonical blockmodel
are different laws. Their detection or logarithmic-degree recovery
theorems do not settle this fixed per-vertex block-degree problem.

2. The recovery conclusion was already conjectured and partly stated
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Barucca, Spectral partitioning in equitable graphs, PRE95,062310(2017),
Section IV.A, proposes recovering the two-equal-block labels through
extendedness/inverse participation ratio of adjacency eigenvectors.
The all-a>b>0 recovery statement is a conjecture backed by analysis and
numerics; the examples include the cubic (2,1) case. This is clear
priority for the all-parameter target and for its exact planted-eigenvector
interpretation, but not a proved uniform kernel-dimension estimate.
  https://arxiv.org/html/1610.02668v5
  https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.95.062310
Actual abstract and relevant recovery/conjecture section were read.

Brito--Dumitriu--Ganguly--Hoffman--Tran, Recovery and Rigidity in a
Regular Stochastic Block Model, arXiv:1507.00930v4/SODA2016, defines the
same simple-component law. The article assumes min(a,b)>=3 globally.
Its efficient theorem requires (a-b)^2>4(a+b-1) and even a; its uniqueness
result has a sufficiently-large-degree condition, and its majority
bootstrap has its own gap hypothesis. Those restrictions exclude (2,1)
and the full in-bulk regime. Their introduction distinguishes exhaustive
recovery from efficient recovery.
  https://arxiv.org/html/1507.00930v4
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.00930v4
Definition 1, the surrounding degree assumption, main theorem scopes,
and the cited random-lift proof mechanism were inspected.

Zhu--Zhu, arXiv:2312.03300v3, September25,2026, is especially important
for responsible wording. Lemma9.1 literally states planted-eigenvalue
simplicity without the separation/parity qualifications. That statement
would imply polynomial exact recovery by exact elimination. Yet its
proof cites the restricted Brito Section4 argument, which only separates
the planted eigenvalue from the new spectrum in the outlier regime.
Moreover, the paper's Introduction1.3 explicitly calls full recovery
open, while Theorem3.10/Corollary3.11 retain separation and even a.
Thus do not claim the conclusion was never printed, and do not silently
cite that lemma as a proved all-parameter result either.
  https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03300v3
Read the actual model/application, Lemma9.1 and its proof, and the
open-problem and parity discussions, not just a secondary summary.

3. Earlier noiseless algebraic algorithms: distinguishing is prior
------------------------------------------------------------------
Bandeira--Banks--Kunisky--Moore--Wein, Spectral Planting and the Hardness
of Refuting Cuts, Colorability, and Communities in Random Graphs,
COLT2021, Remark3 explicitly states all-parameter polynomial-time
DISTINGUISHING of the noiseless equitable model from uniform regular
graphs by its algebraic eigenvectors. Section1.1.4 also discusses how
Gaussian elimination defeats analogous noiseless XOR plantings and why
noise is needed in their hardness conjecture. These are substantial
antecedents for the algebraic viewpoint. The inspected statement does
not give complete label recovery, binary-corank control, or enumeration
of the full affine parity fiber.
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v134/bandeira21a/bandeira21a.pdf
Actual Remark3 and the surrounding noiseless/noisy discussion read.

Gaussian elimination, enumeration of an affine space with O(log N)
dimension, Gray-code traversal, and exact verification are elementary
algorithmic ingredients. The parity relaxation

  (A+(a+b)I) z = (b mod2) 1  over F2

is also an elementary consequence of the exact local opposite-neighbor
counts. A claim of a new generic elimination or list-enumeration
primitive would overstate the contribution. The substantial model-specific
step is proving its solution list has polynomial expected size while
the genuine integer-sign solutions are unique with high probability.

4. CLOSEST METHOD OVERLAP: multitype star/edge entropy is existing work
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Dembo--McKenzie, The Gaussian Wave for Graphs of Finite Cone Type,
arXiv:2603.04386v1, uses a prescribed-degree-matrix model containing RSBM.
Section3 already counts matching and bipartite configurations by colored
edge tables and derives a multitype star-minus-edge entropy functional,
extending Backhausz--Szegedy. This machinery must be credited. Theorem1.8
controls an eigenvector sampled from a shrinking spectral window; the
following discussion explicitly permits exceptional planted eigenvectors.
Its eigenvector-process hypothesis includes prescribed Green-function
covariances, which cannot be assumed for an arbitrary Boolean labeling.
Its printed exponential-scale counting error is not the polynomial
prefactor needed for E[2^corank]<=O(N^24). No finite-field moment or exact
community-recovery algorithm is supplied by these statements.
  https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04386v1
Fresh reading covered Definitions1.1/1.6, Theorems1.8/1.11, the explicit
planted-mode caveat, Proposition2.3, Lemmas3.1/3.2 and their configuration
count, and the entropy-maximizer interface. It was not a fresh full proof
audit of their Gaussian-wave theorem.

Logical comparison: their framework supplies the natural entropy
functional and qualitative typicality inequality. The present finite
alphabet proof must still establish the star-constraint upper bound,
uniform finite-N factorial control, and strict Boolean gap. In the root
proof the latter is a conditional-variance/Pinsker estimate valid already
at a=2, rather than an assumption that a planted Boolean vector has a
Gaussian-wave covariance. Therefore citing Dembo--McKenzie alone would
not complete the current recovery theorem. Conversely, presenting the
colored-star framework itself as new would also be inaccurate.

The aggregate-fiber refinement groups all local arrays by their three
aggregate edge tables before applying entropy. This removes the
degree-dependent count of p^(a+b) local types and leaves only polynomially
many fixed-alphabet edge tables. Its exponent24 is a useful precise
output; no optimality of24 or novelty of finite-fiber entropy as an
abstract technique is asserted.

5. Multiclass literature: broader than two blocks, but restricted
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Karmakar--Podder, Uniqueness of communities in regular stochastic block
models, arXiv:2002.05577v2, already studies k>=3 equal-sized classes and
heterogeneous symmetric degree frames. Theorem3.1 proves asymptotic
uniqueness under the stated comparability conditions and sufficiently
large matrix entries; the surrounding model assumes intra/cross degrees
exceed three. It proves identifiability, not the unknown-frame polynomial
intertwiner-enumeration theorem proposed here. Its statements cannot be
described as merely a two-block result, nor as all fixed internal degrees
at least two.
  https://arxiv.org/html/2002.05577v2
Read the actual Introduction, model, and Theorem3.1 with conditions
(3.1),(3.2), rather than relying on the abstract's "weak assumptions."

Brito--Dumitriu--Harris, Spectral gap in random bipartite biregular graphs
and applications, CPC31(2022), Section6 defines regular frame graphs with
general class proportions. Theorem6.2 obtains frame recovery after a
degree scaling Q -> kappa Q and assumes a nonsingular quotient spectrum
so that informative eigenvectors can be separated from the new spectrum.
This is established prior multiclass frame recovery; it does not give
arbitrary fixed unscaled frames, the degree-two boundary, or recovery
with unknown frame and number of classes.
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/combinatorics-probability-and-computing/article/spectral-gap-in-random-bipartite-biregular-graphs-and-applications/FF0544B9780460143733B7A5317D6CC1
Actual Sections6.2-6.3, Proposition6.1, Theorem6.2 and the RSBM comparison
were read. I did not audit the rest of that paper's spectral proof.

Consequently the existing even-N/internal-degree>=3 multiclass note at
0b41... has a meaningful checked scope beyond Zhu's printed binary
lemma: all fixed quotient eigenvalues (including algebraic ones), exact
old-space recovery, arbitrary deterministic cross graphs independent of
the internals, and no quotient spectral separation. Its use of Meszaros
mixing is a derived application of classical machinery. The newer coarse
proposal may further remove parity, reduce internal degree to two,
allow rational class ratios and unknown Q,k; these are substantive scope
differences only once the proposed full theorem is independently proved.
Random cross factors are essential in that proposal and should not be
confused with the earlier deterministic-cross guarantee.
