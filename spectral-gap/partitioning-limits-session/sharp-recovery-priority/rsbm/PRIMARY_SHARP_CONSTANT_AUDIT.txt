For fixed integers a>b>0, with BLOCK size N, the target claims

 t=a-b+1, nu=(a+b-2)t,
 C=((bt)!)^2(a!)^(2t) / ((t!)^2(b!)^(2t)a^(t(a-b))),

 P(K>=2)~C N^(-nu), P(K>=3)=O(N^(-nu-1)),
 and exact-recovery Bayes risk ~ (C/2) N^(-nu).
In particular (a,b)=(2,1) gives 4/N^2 ambiguity and 2/N^2 Bayes risk.
N here is half the graph order; this normalization is essential.

FINDING
-------
No inspected primary source states this C, the cubic 4/N^2 or 2/N^2,
the leading two-partition law, or a matching sharp ambiguity asymptotic.
The nearest RSBM predecessor is the overlap-union argument of Brito
et al. The nearest exact local enumeration formulas are established
fixed-subgraph probability estimates, especially Gao--Ohapkin and the
2025 generic/bipartite extension of Larkin--McKay--Tian. Thus a defensible
contribution boundary is the classification and exhaustive summation of
minimum flip witnesses, control of all other alternatives, and resulting
sharp ambiguity / decision-risk law. The local pairing/counting technique
itself should not be advertised as new.

1. CLOSEST RSBM OVERLAP COUNT: BRITO ET AL.
------------------------------------------
Gerandy Brito, Ioana Dumitriu, Shirshendu Ganguly, Christopher Hoffman,
Linh V. Tran, Recovery and Rigidity in a Regular Stochastic Block Model,
SODA 2016, pp.1589--1601; inspected arXiv v4, 4 May 2020.
https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611974331.ch108
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.00930v4
https://arxiv.org/src/1507.00930v4

Theorem 1 proves a.a.s. uniqueness for a>b>d' with d' sufficiently large.
Section 3.2.2 fixes overlap alpha N, with alpha=1/N,...,1/2. Its (3.13) is

 P(R_alpha)<=binom(N,alpha N)^2
   [P(Y>=1)^((a/8)alpha N)+P(Y>=1)^(N/2)], Y~Bin(b,2alpha).

Ranges are I1=(0,1/b^2), I2=[1/b^2,C0/b], I3=[C0/b,1/2]. Here R_alpha
means existence of an alternative mixed N-set inducing an a-regular
graph. Minimum flips have alpha=t/N in I1 eventually. Equation (3.15)
prints only <=1/N; its penultimate expression equals N^-2. After (3.20),
the text nevertheless asserts P(union R_alpha)<=tau^N. This does not
follow from (3.15). Moreover, internal expansion was assumed only a.a.s.
Consequently neither that exponential assertion nor an unconditional
polynomial failure rate should be credited as established by this proof.
There is no matching lower bound, leading coefficient, or Bayes law here.

Primary-source anchors in downloaded BDGHT_regular_SBM.tex:
 theorem lines177--183; overlap lines434--442; union bound614--617;

INDEPENDENT DIAGNOSIS OF THE EXPONENTIAL ASSERTION
------------------------------------------------
This is not a confusion between microscopic and macroscopic overlaps:
the final union explicitly includes I1. Independently of the new sharp
coefficient, the following fixed labeled witness precludes exponential
decay for that full union.

Take t fixed support vertices on each old side; place K_t on each.
Take bt further boundary vertices on each old side. Give every support
vertex b internal-boundary and b cross-boundary edges, and every boundary
vertex exactly one internal-support and one cross-support neighbor.
Every support vertex has degree (t-1)+2b=a+b, so its entire neighborhood
is specified. Flipping the two support sets preserves each vertex's
intra/inter degrees. A simple graph containing this fixed colored motif
therefore belongs to R_(t/N). It is feasible for all sufficiently large
admissible N. Standard bounded-degree fixed-pattern probabilities give
positive constant times N^(-e), where e=t(a-b)+4bt is fixed. This already
rules out an exp(-cN) upper bound, without needing a union over copies
or any claim concerning the sharp C. The a.a.s. uniqueness theorem is
unaffected.

2. KARMAKAR--PODDER
-------------------
Sayar Karmakar, Moumanti Podder, Uniqueness of communities in regular
stochastic block models, arXiv:2002.05577v2, 18 Jan 2021.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05577v2
https://arxiv.org/src/2002.05577v2

The model has k>=3 communities and a fixed degree matrix. Theorem 3.1
gives a.a.s. uniqueness for all sufficiently large entries under its
ratio hypotheses. Proposition 3.2 and Section 3 count mixed clusters
with overlap profile (alpha_1,...,alpha_k), use conditional degree-match
probabilities, and sum upper bounds. They do not isolate a finite
minimum-flip pattern or obtain a matching nonuniqueness lower bound,
sharp coefficient, or decision-risk law. This is related uniqueness
prior art, not a source of the claimed two-class constant.

Downloaded source: Second_draft_RSBM_multiple_communities.tex,
theorem lines212--226; overlap proposition232--240; model109.

https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.95.062310
https://lims.ac.uk/documents/paper-spectral-partitioning-in-equitable-graphs.pdf
https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02668 (latest v5, 30 Jun 2017)

Read the full published seven-page paper and v5 scientific TeX text.
Section IVA explicitly uses total degree c=3 and ratio r=2, i.e. a=2,b=1,
in Figs.2 and4. Equation (20) gives community-vector IPR=1/n; the nearby

no finite-switch count, nonuniqueness asymptotic, or Bayes constant in

5. CLOSEST EXACT LOCAL ENUMERATION FORMULAS
-------------------------------------------
Pu Gao, Yuval Ohapkin, Subgraph probability of random graphs with
specified degrees and applications to chromatic number and connectivity,
Random Structures & Algorithms 62 (2023), 911--934.
https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.21123
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.02216 (inspected v4, 26 May 2025)

Theorem4 supplies upper/lower products. For bounded degrees and fixed H,
its errors are O(1/N), giving

 P(H subset G)=(1+O(1/N)) product_v(d_v)_(deg_H(v))
                   / product_(j=1)^e(H)(M-2j+2).

Here M=sum d_v. This immediately supplies the internal prescribed-pattern
factor; it does not identify which RSBM patterns exhaust ambiguity.

John Larkin, Brendan D. McKay, Fang Tian, Subgraphs in random graphs with
specified degrees and forbidden edges, arXiv:2510.24276v1, 28 Oct 2025.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24276

Theorem4.3/Corollary4.4 and Theorem5.4/Corollary5.5 give generic and
bipartite products, including forbidden edges. In the fixed bounded-degree
regime they imply the standard leading products
 P(H subset G_internal)~product_v(a)_(h_v)/(aN)^e,
 P(H subset G_cross)~product_v(b)_(h_v)/(bN)^e.
These are established machinery for the target's local motif coefficient.
No RSBM recovery-error law appears in the inspected theorem statements.

For older enumeration background used explicitly by Brito, see
McKay--Wormald, Combinatorica11(1991),369--382, Corollary5.3, and
McKay--Wang, Linear Algebra Appl.373(2003),273--287, Theorem2:
https://doi.org/10.1016/S0024-3795(03)00506-8
The latter authors are Brendan D. McKay and Xiaoji Wang.

6. CUBIC TERMINOLOGY CHECK
--------------------------
An equitable (2,1) partition of a cubic graph is exactly a perfect
matching cut: every vertex has one neighbor across. Such cuts are
automatically balanced by counting matching edges from each side.
Van Bang Le and Jan Arne Telle, The Perfect Matching Cut Problem Revisited,
Theoretical Computer Science931(2022),117--130,
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2022.07.035
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.06399
defines this strict notion. Its text has no occurrence of 'random'; its
results concern complexity and algorithms. Searches for planted/random
cubic perfect matching cuts did not reveal a sharp probability law.
The weaker 'disconnected perfect matching' notion is not equivalent.
