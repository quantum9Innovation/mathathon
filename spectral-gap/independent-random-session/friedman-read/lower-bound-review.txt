1. Scope checked
----------------

The graph G_m consists of m >= 3 disjoint diamonds K_4 minus one edge,
with the two degree-two ports linked in a cycle. This is connected, simple
and cubic, with 4m vertices. The random lift is sampled using independent
uniform permutations on its undirected edges. The base is deterministic.
The old subspace consists of functions constant on every base fiber.
The theorem's new eigenvalue is adjacency restricted to its orthogonal
complement, even when the realized lift is disconnected.

The accepted finite claim is that, for R >= 0 and n >= 2 S_R,

  P{lambda_max(A_new) >= L_R - 6 S_R/n} >= 1-(1-p_n)^m,

with the definitions in equations (4), (6) and (7) of the note.

2. Algebra of the infinite diamond completion
---------------------------------------------

Let alpha > 1 solve alpha^3-alpha-2=0. The cubic is strictly increasing
above one. At sqrt(2) its value is sqrt(2)-2 < 0, and at two it is four.
Hence sqrt(2) < alpha < 2 and q=2/alpha^2 < 1.

Put t=1/alpha, L=alpha+2t and b=(L-1)/2. The port identity

  (L-t)(L-1)=4

is equivalent, after multiplication by alpha^2, to

  alpha^4-alpha^3-alpha^2-alpha+2=0.

This polynomial factors exactly as

  (alpha-1)(alpha^3-alpha-2).

Thus the core equations are L=1+2b and Lb=2+tb. At a branch vertex,
the parent contributes t^(-1) times its value and its two children
contribute 2t times its value. This gives L=t^(-1)+2t there.

The positive function with values one on the two central vertices,
b on the ports and b t^k on branch level k is therefore an adjacency
eigenfunction. Its squared norm is finite because q<1.

The weighted edge inequality gives a full operator norm bound. More
explicitly, first bound the absolute Rayleigh form of x by the Rayleigh
form of |x|, then apply the positive eigenfunction inequality on each
edge. This proves |<x,Ax>| <= L ||x||^2 for finitely supported x, and
then for all square-summable x by density. Together with the displayed
eigenfunction it proves that the completed graph has adjacency norm L.
The note's shorter statement of this argument is valid.

The numerical value is

  L = 2.83597591908131955125419187226404193771848520080643...

It is strictly larger than 2 sqrt(2). The numerical evaluation is
corroboration. The strict inequality follows symbolically because
alpha>sqrt(2) and alpha+2/alpha is strictly increasing above sqrt(2).

3. Truncation and the Perron replacement
--------------------------------------

At depth k>=1 there are 2^k branch vertices, counting both branches.
Thus the truncated support has

  S_R=4+sum_{k=1}^R 2^k = 2^(R+1)+2

vertices. Its squared trial norm is

  M_R=2+2b^2+2b^2 t^2 (1-q^R)/(1-q).

For R>=1, every boundary vertex loses its two children. The total
defect in <f,(L-A)f> is

  2^R (b t^R)^2 (2t) = 2b^2 t q^R.

For R=0, the two ports each lose one outward edge, giving the same
formula 2b^2t. Hence the displayed L_R is the exact trial Rayleigh
quotient for every R>=0 and converges to L.

The finite truncated graph is connected. Its nonnegative Perron vector
h has eigenvalue mu_R >= L_R and, after extension by zero, satisfies
A h >= mu_R h pointwise. The note correctly replaces the original
truncated radial trial vector by h. The trial vector alone does not have
the required pointwise inequality at its last level. This is a necessary
step, not an optional simplification, and it is present in the proof.

4. Covering pushforward, including collisions
--------------------------------------------

The inclusion of a diamond into any cubic host extends to a covering
from its infinite cubic completion to the host component. At each branch
vertex choose the two remaining incident host edges after fixing its
parent edge. Distinct branch vertices may map to the same host vertex.
The map is still locally bijective and extends indefinitely.

Because h is finitely supported, its pushforward g is a finite sum.
Local bijectivity gives exact commutation

  A_host pi_* h = pi_* A_completion h.

Consequently A_host g >= mu_R g. The vector g is nonzero and nonnegative,
and its support has at most S_R vertices. This deterministic implication
needs no probability estimate for a tree neighborhood. It remains valid
under branch collisions.

This is Friedman's covering pushforward, independently checked during
the complete new source read. Theorem 4.2 was verified in the supplied
arXiv source. This review did not compare the publisher's full PDF.

5. Projection to the new subspace
--------------------------------

On a base fiber let s_v be the number of nonzero values of g. Then

  |sum_i g(v,i)|^2 <= s_v sum_i |g(v,i)|^2,

and s_v<=S_R. Summing the squared norms of the fiber averages gives

  ||g_old||^2 <= (S_R/n) ||g||^2.

The old and new subspaces reduce adjacency. Since the host is cubic,
its adjacency norm is at most three. With
delta=||g_old||^2/||g||^2 <= S_R/n <= 1/2, subtraction of the old
quadratic form therefore gives

  Rayleigh(g_new) >= (L_R-3delta)/(1-delta)
                   = L_R-(3-L_R)delta/(1-delta)
                   >= L_R-6S_R/n.

Here 0<L_R<3, so the last coarse bound is valid. The bound delta<1
also proves g_new is nonzero. The variational principle applies directly
to the largest new eigenvalue. Its lower bound also lower bounds the
new operator norm used in the subsequent statement.

6. Exact probability of an embedded diamond
-------------------------------------------

Gauge the three spanning-tree edge permutations to identity inside one
diamond block. Conditional on those three permutations, the two chord
permutations remain independent and uniform. A lift of the diamond with
one vertex in each of its four distinct base fibers is precisely a
common fixed point of these two permutations. The vertex assignment is
injective automatically because those fibers are distinct.

For X equal to the number of such copies,

  E(X)_k=(n)_k / (n)_k^2=1/(n)_k.

Exact finite inclusion-exclusion gives

  p_n=sum_{k=1}^n (-1)^(k+1)/(k! (n)_k).

The factorial denominators are essential and are correct in the note.
Bonferroni gives

  1/n-1/(2n(n-1)) <= p_n <= 1/n,

so p_n=1/n+O(n^(-2)). Internal edge sets of distinct blocks are disjoint
and independently sampled. Their copy events are independent even though
gauge changes may relabel connector permutations, because each event is
a function only of its own internal edges. Thus the probability of at
least one copy is exactly 1-(1-p_n)^m. The connector permutations need
not be exposed.

The earlier exact source-step script independently checks this formula,
including exhaustive enumeration of all 576 permutation pairs at n=4.

7. Asymptotic quantifiers and sharp scope
---------------------------------------

Fix epsilon>0 first. Choose a finite R such that L_R>L-epsilon/2.
Only then send n to infinity. The support size S_R stays fixed during
that limit, so eventually both n>=2S_R and 6S_R/n<epsilon/2 hold.
The core event therefore implies a new eigenvalue at least L-epsilon.

If m_n/n tends to c in [0,infinity), then
(1-p_n)^(m_n) tends to exp(-c). If m_n/n tends to infinity, the same
failure probability tends to zero, without a restriction on how fast
m_n grows, using p_n>=1/n-O(n^(-2)). This proves the stated liminf and
the probability-one conclusion in the diverging ratio regime.

The argument establishes a lower bound for the spectral failure event.
It does not identify its exact probability or prove a full critical
spectral law. The note states a liminf rather than incorrectly equating
the spectral event with the core event.

Taking 0<epsilon<L-2sqrt(2) gives a fixed positive gap above the cubic
tree edge. With D_n=4m_n and c>0 this rules out a uniform almost-Ramanujan
claim admitting every connected simple cubic base of size proportional
to n. It is a worst-case statement about size alone, not a necessity
result for each possible base family. The note correctly distinguishes
these claims.
