Independent novelty and comparison completion audit
2026-11-14. Scope: sketch-preconditioned-spectral.tex, especially its arbitrary
two-factor, fixed-rank O(N^2) full-output consequence. No main/state edits.

Conclusion
----------
The arithmetic gain against the named Gram, ordinary-Krylov, and exact-product
routes survives this additional audit. I found no concrete published reduction
that already establishes the same general-two-factor guarantee and total cost,
and no concrete reduction invalidating the displayed output-optimal instance.
That is a scoped search result, not a novelty or complete-SOTA certificate.

The mechanism is substantially classical: full-subspace Gram sketches,
whitening, preconditioned Lanczos on a shifted pencil, random-start additive
eigenvalue approximation, Rayleigh recovery, and Schur/Nystrom deflation.
The defensible candidate contribution is their particular two-factor assembly,
global gap-free error accounting with h much larger than theta, and the
resulting optimized total arithmetic bound. Do not call preconditioned
Lanczos, sketch-refined eigenvectors, or Nystrom deflation new mechanisms.

Carried-forward audits

Closest antecedents and the precise missing implications
-------------------------------------------------------

1. Classical preconditioned shifted-pencil Lanczos

A. Knyazev, Preconditioned Lanczos Methods, Templates (2000).
https://netlib.org/utk/people/JackDongarra/etemplates/node415.html
The section explicitly applies Lanczos to T(B-mu A), with an SPD
preconditioner and its induced inner product, then updates mu using the
original pencil's Rayleigh quotient. It cites Scott (1981) and Knyazev
(1986/1987) for the earlier method and convergence analysis.
Thus shifting a pencil, preconditioning its zero eigenvalue, and recovering
an original Rayleigh vector are old. The source does not construct the
manuscript's two factor-Gram proxies or state its input/sketch/accuracy
tradeoff. A new application/runtime corollary must be distinguished from
a new eigenvalue iteration.

2. EPSI: a particularly close randomized eigenvector antecedent

R. Xu, Y. Lu (2025), What is a Sketch-and-Precondition Derivation for Low-Rank
Approximation? Inverse Power Error or Inverse Power Estimation?, v2.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07993
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.07993
Theorem 2.2 assumes an additive shifted proxy
X-3 eta I <= Xhat <= X-eta I and an already sufficiently accurate iterate;
its displayed local linear coefficient is proportional to eta/(lambda1-lambda2).
Theorem 2.4 explicitly assumes eta<(lambdak-lambda{k+1})/(3.5 sqrt(2)).
These statements are prior art for sketch accuracy affecting convergence rather
than the final accuracy floor. Their published estimates do not certify a
global guarantee when eta dominates an arbitrarily small eigengap. The
manuscript's h>>theta threshold wrapper cannot be replaced by merely citing
EPSI's local estimate. This is a limitation of that implication, not proof that
EPSI itself fails or cannot be analyzed more strongly.

3. RLOBPCG: randomized whitening plus preconditioned eigensolving

E. Epperly, T. Park, Y. Nakatsukasa (2026), Fast, High-Accuracy, Randomized
Nullspace Computations for Tall Matrices.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16797
Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3 concern the smallest singular vector of a
supplied tall matrix X. The theory ties embedding distortion to the relative
gap between its two smallest squared singular values, and the runtime retains
inverse-gap factors. It supplies no inexpensive tall Gram factor of the
possibly indefinite complement tG-GQ'G. This is strong mechanism prior art,
but not the general-product total-cost theorem under review.

4. A new 2026 sketched-power spectral comparator

S. Chenakkod, M. Derezinski, Accelerating Power Method with Fast Sketching
for Stronger Low-Rank Approximation (May 2026).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09755
https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09755v1
Theorems 2.1/2.3 return width O(k), with squared spectral error at most
(1+epsilon)*sigma{k+1}(X)^2 + (epsilon/l)*sum_{j>l}sigma_j(X)^2,
using ~l/epsilon^2 sketch width and ~1/epsilon powering depth. They provide
a valid stronger comparator than a generic unpowered range finder, but do not
give the manuscript's exact rank-r near-optimal spectral guarantee directly.

Concrete parameter audit of this possible reduction (my calculation): take
k=1 and a rank-m product with sigma2=...=sigmam=1. For 1<=l<=m, the displayed
bound becomes 1+epsilon*m/l. To certify error <=1+theta, it requires
epsilon*m/l <= 2theta+theta^2. Consequently
  l/epsilon^2 >= m^2/((2theta+theta^2)^2*l)
               >= m/(2theta+theta^2)^2.
At theta=m^(-25/59), this exceeds both inner rank m and the outer dimension
N=m^(59/50). A direct invoke of that theorem therefore does not obtain the
required reduced sketch. This calculation is about the published sufficient
guarantee, not a lower bound on the algorithm. Also, width O(k) is not the
same output-rank requirement as width exactly k.

5. Generalized PCA and generalized eigenvector oracle reductions

Sobczyk, Mladenovic, Luisier (NeurIPS 2024), PCA in matrix-multiplication time.
https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/21f6bdb10b7e019543daf35012e79210-Paper-Conference.pdf
Its Theorem 4.1 starts with a supplied covariance H=X^T X; covariance
construction is not an input-linear preprocessing theorem. Applying the
dense-pencil result to AB therefore still requires paying for true Grams
or supplying a new equivalent representation.

Allen-Zhu, Li (ICML 2017), Doubly Accelerated Methods for Faster CCA and
Generalized Eigendecomposition.
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/allen-zhu17b/allen-zhu17b.pdf
Such oracle reductions are relevant alternate wrappers once appropriate
linear-system oracles exist. They do not on their own construct the cheap
fine-scale complement oracle from separate A,B. Treating that missing oracle
as unit cost would be an invalid comparison. The manuscript's direct bounded
Lanczos argument makes this cost explicit.

6. Positive-shift PSD solvers and multilevel preconditioning

Derezinski, Musco, Yang (SODA 2025), Faster Linear Systems and Matrix Norm
Approximation via Multi-level Sketched Preconditioning.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05865
Theorem 1.2 supplies ~O(d^2+d_lambda^omega) positive-regularized solves for
an explicit PSD matrix X+lambda I, lambda>0. The more general theorem needs
an outlier/average-condition-number parameter. Importantly, Theorem 3.1 and
Algorithm 3 ALSO support supplied rectangular features X without forming
X^T X. It would be incorrect to dismiss the work as only explicit-Gram
algorithms. The missing implication here is access to a cheap positive Gram
factor of tG-GQ'G (or its inflated fine approximation), a signed/noncommuting
Gram expression. Forming its true dense matrix or such a factor is not free.
Its spectral-sum applications do not return the required product low-rank
factors. The nested-preconditioning mechanism is close prior art, but no
checked theorem supplies the signed two-factor threshold cost directly.

7. PSD/inertia testing with faster finite-Schatten query rates

Needell, Swartworth, Woodruff, Testing Positive Semidefiniteness Using Linear
Measurements (FOCS 2022; arXiv v4 2023).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03782
Theorem 17 gives a one-sided tester for lambda_min(E)<=-epsilon*||E||_{S_p}
using ~epsilon^(-p/(2p+1)) matvecs; a rejection can provide a negative witness.
Its normalization matters. For a<=m and ||E||2<=1, the only general bound is
||E||_{S_p}<=m^(1/p). To detect a spectral threshold -tau uniformly, the
certified query count becomes ~m^(1/(2p+1))*tau^(-p/(2p+1)).
Write tau=m^(-b), where here b=25/59-7/100<2. Its exponent exceeds b/2
by (1-b/2)/(2p+1)>0 for every finite p. Thus this direct substitution does
not improve the manuscript's tau^(-1/2) uniform guarantee. Nor does exact
inertia factorization avoid paying to materialize the true pencil.

Current exponent and arithmetic-model checks
-------------------------------------------
Amanov, van den Berg, Zuiddam (October 2026), Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 4.17:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30054
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30054
They state the rectangular bound over EVERY field, hence directly over R:
omega_R(1,k,1)<=1+k+1/(4k) for k>=1/2, and omega_R<=9/4.
Theorem 1.2 also provides the general three-exponent shape bound; it is not
restricted to square multiplication. Bounds mean algorithms with arbitrarily
small fixed positive exponent overhead, not finite-size practical speed.
The November all-fields exposition retains 9/4:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09716
No verified improvement invalidating these comparison exponents was found.

For N=m^(59/50), theta=m^(-25/59), h=m^(-7/100), fixed r, fixed delta:
  input exponent:       109/50;
  sketch row exponent:  57/50 < 59/50;
  sketched-Gram cost:   3362/1425 < 59/25 by 1/1425;
  threshold actions:    27811/11800 < 59/25 by 37/11800;
  square-core cost:     9/4 < 59/25;
  coarse estimate:      407/200 < 59/25;
  output:              59/25, exactly N^2.
With a sufficiently small fixed exponent overhead, all construction terms
remain o(N^2), including their logarithmic factors. For fixed r all r and
sqrt(r) multipliers are constants, and conventional expansion costs O(N^2r).
The named Gram/Krylov exponent is 7056/3481 in N; exact product is
25281/11800. These calculations match the manuscript.

This is exact-real arithmetic with comparisons, roots, exact support/rank
decisions, and exact factor actions. Bounded-norm Ritz problems need only
additive numerical accuracy and logarithmic tolerance overhead. There is no
finite-precision stability or bit-complexity guarantee for whitening, tiny
tails, or exact recovery. In particular, do not rephrase the result as an
unconditional fixed-precision O(N^2) implementation. The manuscript correctly
distinguishes these models. The zero-tail theorem is compatible with its
exact model because each right-side Nystrom step drops the true residual
rank exactly, even though its Rayleigh vector is approximate.

Recommended closest-antecedents conclusion
-----------------------------------------
The paper should describe a rigorously analyzed assembly of classical
sketching and preconditioned-eigenvalue tools, with a candidate new
separate-factor total-runtime corollary. The comparison against the checked
Gram/Krylov/product bounds is established within the stated model. The
additional primary-source checks above did not produce a subsuming theorem
or a concrete counter-reduction, but they do not establish a first-ever or
complete state-of-the-art claim. EPSI and classical shifted-pencil Lanczos
are the closest antecedents for the iteration; full-subspace Gram sketching
is the antecedent for setup; classical Nystrom/Schur identities are the
antecedents for deflation. Independent specialist review should assess the
novelty of their combined two-factor bound rather than of those components.

Additional independent review: two-level matrix-free fine proxies
---------------------------------------------------------------
Read the saved docs/multilevel-proxy-audit.txt after the preceding completion
audit. The proved two-level construction is mathematically sound under its
stated exact-arithmetic model, with one harmless displayed-endpoint correction
sent to its author. No shared mathematical source was changed here.

Endpoint correction: section 3's upper t endpoint needs numerator (1+h)^2.
Indeed section 4 gives lambda_h<=(1+h)^2 lambda and s_h<=lambda_h, so
  t <= (1+h)^2 lambda / [(1-h/8)(1-h)^2].
The proof only uses intersection with the original bracket t<=1.5 lambda,
so dropping that fine upper endpoint is also sufficient. This correction
does not change the algorithm, cost, or theorem.

The fine-product bracket needs no true-factor refinement and is valid.
P_H,Q_H are 2H-relative proxies for P_h,Q_h when h<=H/4 and H<=1/32.
Their support and the whitening W,L are unchanged. Apply the original
Rayleigh primitive to A_h,B_h with coarse distortion 2H and requested
parameter h; its true actions now cost J_h. The returned s_h is between
(1-h/8)lambda_h and lambda_h. Applying the two fine OSE inequalities gives
the stated bracket for the true product, and its lower endpoint is at least
(1-33h/8)lambda. The J_h sqrt(H/h) cost is absorbed by the final
J_h sqrt(H/theta), since theta<=h. No fine dense Gram is formed.

The fine complement MUST retain its fine mass matrix:
  K_h=t G_h-G_h Q'_h G_h.
In coarse whitening coordinates, ||G_h-G||<=h||G|| and
||Q'_h-Q'||<=h||Q'||. A direct expansion gives
  ||G_h Q'_h G_h-G Q'G||
    <= ||G||^2 ||Q'|| [(1+h)^3-1].
Together with the t(G_h-G) term, ||G||<=16/15, and ||Q'||<=2t,
this is below 10ht. Consequently Delta_h=F_h-K lies in [22,42]ht I.
The fine bracket implies K>=-5ht I, so F_h>=17ht I and the note's
conservative 16ht I is safe. Since Delta_H-Delta_h=F_H-F_h,
  0<=F_H-F_h<=42Ht I.
This yields D=J_H^(-1)F_h J_H^(-T) in [h/(3H),1], using h/H<=1/4.

The polynomial implementation is explicit and linear. Set a=h/(3H),
rho=(1-sqrt(a))/(1+sqrt(a)), c=(1+sqrt(a))^2/4, and
T=(1+a-2D)/(1-a). The Legendre generating function and |P_j(t)|<=1
on [-1,1] give
  D^(-1/2)=c^(-1/2) sum_{j>=0}rho^j P_j(T),
with truncation error at most rho^(k+1)/sqrt(a). Thus degree
O(a^(-1/2)log(1/(eta sqrt(a)))) suffices even for relative error eta.
The standard three-term recurrence needs k D actions and O(k) scalar
coefficient operations, using only arithmetic and sqrt(a). Repeated scalar
comparison chooses k, so no logarithm/trigonometric evaluation oracle is
required. Primary classical identities:
https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.12.E11
https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.14.E1  (Jacobi parameters alpha=beta=0).

For eta=h/1000 the polynomial is positive on the whole interval, so
R=J_H^(-T)p(D) is invertible and FIXED at a given threshold. The effective
preconditioner is exactly
  Fbar=R^(-T)R^(-1)=J_H p(D)^(-2) J_H^T.
The relative polynomial guarantee implies
(1+eta)^(-2)F_h<=Fbar<=(1-eta)^(-2)F_h. With ||F_h||<=6t, the
note's error ||Fbar-F_h||<=24eta t is safe. Its conservative
  21ht I<=Fbar-K<=43ht I, Fbar>=15ht I,
  spectrum(R^T K R) subset [-2,1]
then follows. Applying random-start Lanczos with norm bound 2 changes only
constants. The witness-64 and uncertainty-46 bounds are safe, and their sum
fits the existing tau=e_*/(512h), e_*=theta/8 choice. Actual Rayleigh values
and congruence preserve the inertia signs exactly.

Each outer action uses one O(I) true action plus
~sqrt(H/h) fine-proxy actions, each O(J_h). Hence the displayed
  I+G_H+m^omega+m^2/sqrt(H)
    +I sqrt(h/theta)+J_h sqrt(H/theta)+np
bound is correct. Replacing the polynomial by adaptively stopped inner CG
would require a separate inexact/nonlinear-oracle analysis; the saved note
does not make that substitution. Orthogonalization remains charged by the
outer I actions, since inner polynomial evaluation stores only O(m) vectors.

The new example checks exactly:

Fine rows have exponent 117/100<59/50, and J_h has exponent 217/100.

The conditional further-level recurrence in section 7 has correct displayed
arithmetic: a_j<=u/2+a_{j-1}/4 and a_L>=2(d-u) yield
u>=(6d-3*4^(-L)a_0)/(8-2*4^(-L)). Its limiting 3d/4 value is only a

Closest multilevel antecedent remains Derezinski-Musco-Yang. Its explicit
feature-input theorem already keeps preconditioner Grams implicit and solves

Addendum: final fixed-depth recursion audit (2026-11-14)
-------------------------------------------------------
This supersedes the earlier paragraph calling Section 7 conditional. The
revised, saved Section 7 now proves the fixed-L extension, including the
nested bracket/perturbation accounting. I independently checked that version
and found no further gap. An input-dependent growing-depth theorem remains
outside the result.

Take fixed L, h_0<=1/1024, h_j<=h_{j-1}/8, and theta<=h_L. Define
F_j=K_j+128h_j tI, with K_0=tI-Q_0. On the validity window
|t-lambda|<=16h_j lambda, the original metric satisfies
||G||<=1024/1023, t>=(63/64)lambda, and ||Q'||<=2t. The direct proxy
expansion gives ||K_j-K||<=10h_j t, including the coarse-whitening case.
Therefore
  K>=-17h_j tI, ||F_j||<=4t,
  118h_j tI<=F_j-K<=138h_j tI, F_j>=100h_j tI.
Validity at level j implies validity at all preceding levels. This uniform
window statement is necessary when a fine product's norm search evaluates
thresholds outside an earlier true-product bracket.

If Fbar_{j-1}=R_{j-1}^{-T}R_{j-1}^{-1} satisfies
  117h_{j-1}tI<=Fbar_{j-1}-K<=139h_{j-1}tI,
  Fbar_{j-1}>=99h_{j-1}tI,
then 0<=Fbar_{j-1}-F_j<=139h_{j-1}tI and
  spectrum(R_{j-1}^T F_j R_{j-1})
    is contained in [h_j/(2h_{j-1}),1].
The same explicit Legendre inverse-root polynomial, with relative error
h_j/1000, gives R_j=R_{j-1}p_j(D_j). Its effective Fbar_j differs from
F_j by at most 16h_j t/1000, so the displayed [117,139] and 99 bounds
reset at level j rather than accumulating over levels. The base case is
the exact factorization of F_0. The resulting true preconditioned threshold
operator has spectrum in [-1,1].

For the norm of product A_j B_j, expand the preceding true-lambda bracket
to [(1-h_j)^2 ell_{j-1},(1+h_j)^2 b_{j-1}]. Every threshold is within
8h_{j-1}lambda of lambda. On this search, the difference Fbar_{j-1}-K_j
lies in [115,141]h_{j-1}tI. Use the TRUE fine mass G_j, not G; its
eigenvalues are between .99 and 1.01. Actual-Ritz-value Lanczos therefore
has the stated witness and uncertainty bounds with conservative constants
256 and 256. The choice tau=e/(2048h_{j-1}), e=h_j/8, leaves enough
margin both for an uncertain witness and for the final lower-bracket call.
Translating its actual fine-product Rayleigh value s_j by
  ell_j=s_j/(1+h_j)^2,
  b_j=s_j/[(1-h_j/8)(1-h_j)^2]
gives a true-lambda bracket within (1+-5h_j)lambda. This closes the
next validity window. The old witness-64 constant for inflation 32 is not
being reused with inflation 128.

The implementation recurrence and telescoping are correct for fixed L:
  c_0=O(m^2),
  c_j=~O((J_{h_j}+c_{j-1})sqrt(h_{j-1}/h_j)).
Together with final sqrt(h_L/theta) threshold iterations this gives
  ~O_L(I+G_{h_0}+m^omega+m^2/sqrt(h_0)
       +I sqrt(h_L/theta)
       +sum_{j=1}^L J_{h_j}sqrt(h_{j-1}/theta)+np).
Each proxy-only norm refinement is absorbed in that displayed sum. Fresh
Lanczos starts after conditioning on previous outcomes and a union bound
over bounded call counts handle adaptive failures. Simultaneous OSE events
handle the adaptive congruences; no fresh embedding is required for each
threshold. Products of degree logarithms are still polylogarithmic for
fixed L, which is the scope of the statement.
