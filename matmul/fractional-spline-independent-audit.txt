2. DENSE ATOMS AND THE SINGLE-RANK-ONE PACKING
-------------------------------------------
After removing an atom c, the remaining complex measure nu has
|nu|({c})=0. For y!=c, the normalized kernel increment tends to zero,
since differentiability gives O(|h|) and alpha<1. Its absolute value
is at most one by scalar alpha-Holder continuity. Dominated convergence
against finite |nu| therefore proves o(|h|^alpha), in both real
directions, even with dense atoms or an arbitrary diffuse component.
No unjustified differentiation under the measure is used.

The integrated global operator-Holder bound is valid: translate the
known dimension-free estimate for |x|^alpha and integrate its norm
against |mu|. Functional calculus and the finite measure commute in
these finite matrices. This supplies the operator estimate needed for
packing, independently of differentiability of the remainder.

For the packing construction, S_ij=-T_ij/(c_i-c_j) is anti-Hermitian,
and e^(-tS)(C+tT)e^(tS)=C+t diag(T_jj)+O(t^2). The sign is correct.
The global operator-Holder bound sends the norm remainder to
O(t^(2alpha)); undoing the unitary costs O(t), because f is bounded
on a fixed compact neighborhood. Both are o(t^alpha) for 0<alpha<1.
Scalar tangent remainders are used only on the finitely many fixed
eigenvalues in each witness, so no unjustified dimension-uniform
remainder is required. The two diagonal blocks are p_j A_j and p_j B_j.

The endpoints remain in I for small t since finitely many selected

For atoms a_j, local constants are |a_j|^s H_s(|x|^alpha). Maximizing

  (sum_j |a_j|^[s/(1-alpha*s)])^(1-alpha*s).

Thus (3.4) has the correct exponent q=s/(1-alpha*s) and the single-cusp

The theorem restricts this assertion to interior atoms, as its packing
