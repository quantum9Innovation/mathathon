2. EXACT REDUCTION, PINCHING, AND LOW/HIGH COMPARISONS
---------------------------------------------------
Write H=H(alpha,s) and beta=1-alpha. For 0<s<=1, the finite-dimensional
rank-one reduction (2.1) is exact. To verify it, split E=B-A spectrally
as E_+-E_- and telescope the positive rank-one paths from A and B to
A+E_+=B+E_-. A step t vv* contributes at most the normalized rank-one
supremum times t^(alpha*s). Their sum is Tr|E|^(alpha*s). All endpoints
are positive, and the Schatten power triangle has coefficient one.

For Delta=(A+vv*)^alpha-A^alpha, ||v||=1, operator monotonicity and
vv*<=I give 0<=Delta<=I. Positive pinching increases the concave power
trace, so (2.2) has the correct direction. It is not an assertion about
an indefinite matrix or a pinched Schatten lower bound.

The Woodbury denominator 1+m(t) is positive. On the low spectral block,
m(t)>=m_0(t), so P Delta P<=Delta_0, where Delta_0 is a genuine positive
rank-one correction with both endpoints in [0,2I]. On the high block,
deleting this denominator gives Q Delta Q<=L, the positive derivative
Loewner matrix. No sign or order error occurs in these comparisons.

3. THE UNIFORM LOW-BLOCK BOUND
-----------------------------
The identity c_alpha integral_0^infinity t^alpha/(1+t)^2 dt=alpha is
correct. Subtracting alpha v_0v_0* leaves the analytic log-integrand
in (3.3). Each real node has rank at most two.

At |t|<=1 its norm is O_d(|t|^alpha), using the separate resolvent
bounds. At |t|>=1 the exact resolvent identity R-I/t=-AR/t gives

  R_0 E_0 R_1 = E_0/t^2 + O_d(|t|^-3),

because both endpoints have norm at most two. Thus no invalid
large-|t| Neumann expansion at a pole is needed. Subtracting the same
leading term from E_0/(1+t)^2 yields O_d(|t|^(alpha-2)). For
alpha>=1/2 these are bounded by the fixed slopes 1/2 and 1. The
sector constants are uniform in alpha and dimension for fixed d<pi.

The companion Gaussian lemma therefore gives a rank C(L+1)^2
approximant with norm error e^-L, with uniform C. Since ||Delta_0||<=1,
the complete layer-cake integral gives Tr Delta_0^s<=C/s^2 for
0<s<=1. The finite range of log thresholds is also covered by this
rank bound. Singular A_0 and v_0=0 cause no exception.

4. UNIFORMITY IN THE SHARP GAUSSIAN UPPER
---------------------------------------
One cannot merely use the fixed-alpha O_{a,b,d} term in the abstract
Gaussian lemma and call it uniform. Section 4 correctly returns to
its proof. The relevant log-integrand is the resolvent difference
BEFORE Woodbury:

  F(z)=c_alpha t^(alpha+1)(R_A(t)-R_B(t)).

Its analytic strip has width pi, its real-node rank is one, and on
each fixed closed substrip it has the envelope

  ||F(x+i eta)|| <= C_d beta min(e^(alpha*x),e^(-beta*x)).

The factor beta follows from c_alpha=sin(pi beta)/pi<=beta. Neither
resolvent sector estimate introduces 1/beta.

Here are the estimates from the companion proof with dependence kept:
put w=L^(3/4), a=alpha, b=beta, and kappa=max(a,b)<=1. The number of
retained Gaussian windows is O(L/(beta*w)+1). Each boundary integral
is O_d(beta*w*e^-m_j). Their quadrature errors therefore sum to
O_d(L e^(-L-3w)); the lattice truncation errors sum to
O_d(L e^(-2L)). The omitted-window tail has prefactor

  O_d(beta*(1/alpha+1/beta))=O_d(1).

Thus all errors fall below e^-L at a threshold L_d independent of beta.
In particular exponentially or superexponentially small beta does not
force the threshold to grow.

The triangular node-density integral is L^2/(2 alpha beta). Dividing
by 2*pi*d gives L^2/(4*pi*d*alpha*beta). The window/Riemann overhead
is O_d(beta^-1 L*w), the node-interval enlargement is
O_d(beta^-1 L^(5/2)/w), and rounding is smaller. This proves (4.1),
with the stated beta^-1 L^(7/4) remainder. At a fixed uniform threshold
the count is O_d(beta^-1), so the finite threshold range is controlled.

For eigenvalues in [0,1], layer cake is

  Tr Delta^s=s integral_0^infinity e^(-sL) N_Delta(L) dL.

Multiplication by beta*s^2 sends the main term to
1/(2*pi*d*alpha) and the remainder to O_d(s^(1/4)). This checks (4.2).
The limit d upward to pi is taken only after the small-parameter
limsup; no uniformity at the strip boundary is assumed.

5. LOEWNER KERNEL AND ALL FOURIER CONSTANTS
-----------------------------------------
Factoring the divided difference in logarithmic coordinates gives
exactly (5.1). With Fourier transform integral k(x)e^(-i xi x)dx,

  hat(k_beta)(xi)=2*pi*sin(pi beta)/
                    [cosh(2*pi*xi)-cos(pi beta)].

An independent derivation fixes the normalization: set a=beta/2 and
expand on x>0 as

  k_beta(x)=sum_{n>=0}(e^(-(n+a)x)-e^(-(n+1-a)x)).

Twice the real Laplace transform is twice the sum of
(n+a)/((n+a)^2+xi^2)-(n+1-a)/((n+1-a)^2+xi^2). Pairing the integers
turns this into the symmetric cotangent partial fraction sum. The
identity for Re cot(pi(a+i xi)) gives the displayed transform. The
paired differences converge absolutely. At beta=1/2 this reduces to
the transform of 1/[2 cosh(x/4)], and at xi=0 it gives
2*pi*cot(pi beta/2), providing two independent constant checks.

In a cell of length ell, periodization therefore gives Fourier
coefficients d_k=hat(k_beta)(2*pi*k/ell)/ell, exactly as in (7.1).
The zero mode is at most 4/(beta*ell). For nonzero k,

  d_k <= (pi^2 beta/ell)csch^2(2*pi^2*k/ell).

For 0<s<1/2 the decreasing summand has an integrable singularity at
zero. Both signs of k and the change of variable then give

  sum_{k!=0}d_k^s <= beta^s ell^(1-s) A_s,
  A_s=(pi^2)^s I_s/pi^2,
  I_s=integral_0^infinity sinh(u)^(-2s)du
     =2^(2s-1) B(s,1-2s),
  s I_s -> 1/2.

All factors two and pi in the eventual 1/(2*pi^2) coefficient agree.
The Fourier vectors need not be orthogonal in the spectral measure:
each rank-one summand has trace-s moment d_k^s m_j^s, which is all the
power triangle requires.

6. CORNERS AND THE ENTIRE PERIODIC IMAGE ERROR
---------------------------------------------
The decomposition k_beta(x)=e^(-beta*x/2)+r_beta(x) on x>=0 is exact.
The expression in (5.3) and
1-e^(-beta*z)=beta*z integral_0^1 e^(-t beta z)dt prove (5.4) on a
fixed half-strip. Removability at zero is harmless, and the only other
zeros of 1-e^-z are outside the narrow strip. At large positive real
part the denominator is uniformly separated from zero and the
remaining exponential decays at least as e^(-3 Re(z)/4).

For the corner lemma, truncate nonnegative nodes at R=C_0(L+1).
Outside this rectangle the kernel is O(beta e^-L). A Bernstein ellipse
of parameter 1+c/R about [0,2R] lies in the fixed half-strip. Its
Chebyshev error is O(beta R e^(-cN/R)), so N=O((L+1)^2) suffices.
The kernel p_N(a+b) has rank at most N+1 after collecting powers of a;
the rectangle indicators preserve this rank. A pointwise error delta
has weighted Frobenius norm at most delta*||p||_2||q||_2. Thus the
Euclidean factors in (6.2) are precisely

  beta^s (||p||_2||q||_2)^s,

not squared incorrectly. Counting and layer cake give the 1/s^2.

For every ell>=1 the full sum sum_{n>=0}r_beta(z+n ell) satisfies the
same analytic bound with absolute constants. This charges every short
image, not only the nearest one. The two short-image matrices are
Hankel corners with a+b=ell+z or ell-z. Reflection changes node
locations but leaves both Euclidean weighted-vector norms sqrt(m_j).

The long exponential image sums are exactly two separable kernels,
of total rank at most two. Their coefficients are bounded by
2/(beta*ell); their multipliers have norm bounded by e^(1/2) when
beta*ell<=1. Their moments are consequently O((C/(beta*ell))^s m_j^s).
These image operators can be indefinite. They are charged with the
power triangle, not discarded by an invalid Loewner comparison.

This proves (7.5) with absolute constants. Its proof in fact works for
every 0<s<1/2, although the frozen statement restricts to s<=1/4.
The only additional restriction in that proof is integrability of I_s.
Equality beta*ell=1 is allowed throughout, a fact used in section 12.

10. BROWNIAN MODEL, DOMINATION, AND ATOMIC MEASURES
-------------------------------------------------
For fixed u_i>0 and A_beta=diag(e^(u_i/beta)), m(t)<=a_min^-1, so

  (1+a_min^-1)^-1 L_beta<=Delta_beta<=L_beta.

The positive derivative map gives ||L_beta||<=alpha a_min^-beta<=1.
Its entries tend to v_i conjugate(v_j)e^(-max(u_i,u_j)), including
the diagonal. Thus the finite-matrix nonlinear limit is the weighted
Brownian Gram matrix with t_i=e^-u_i. Only fixed finite pieces are
needed before beta goes to zero.

The continuum uniform-PROBABILITY Brownian eigenvalues on [0,tau]
are tau/[pi^2(j-1/2)^2]; the power of tau is correct. Differentiate the
integral equation twice to obtain the Dirichlet/Neumann problem. The
stated finite-matrix formula also has the correct normalization.
Finite nodes can be kept positive and scaled below one before
realization. Consequently divergence for s<=1/2 follows without
putting a zero or infinite eigenvalue into the finite base.

For s>1/2, the two-node model has lambda_max>=1-d,
lambda_min>=d/8 for d<=1/2. Since lambda_max<=1, its moment is at
least 1-d+(d/8)^s>1 for sufficiently small d. Scaling below one
preserves a strict gap, proving the strict liminf in B3.

For the universal upper, Fourier comparison is valid: putting x=pi beta,

  cosh(2*pi beta xi)-1 >=2*pi^2 beta^2 xi^2
                           >=2*pi beta sin(pi beta)xi^2,
  1-cos x >=(x/2)sin x.

It follows that the Fourier transform of k_beta(u/beta) is bounded
by 1/(xi^2+1/4). The difference is positive definite, which proves
the Loewner domination by the Brownian Gram matrix. Pointwise kernel
comparison alone would not prove it, but is not what is used here.

The uniform j^-2 eigenvalue bound also handles arbitrary atoms.
Use T f(x)=integral_[x,1] f(t)dmu(t), with total mu mass at most one.
Quantiles of dx+dmu at levels 2k/n, after deduplicating and including
endpoints, leave each OPEN cell with length and interior mu mass at
most 2/n. Boundary atoms are captured exactly by the step operator
S f(x)=integral_[b,1] f dmu for x in (a,b). The residual is a direct
sum of local Volterra maps of norm at most 2/n, and rank S<=n+1.
Thus sigma_(n+2)(T)<=2/n and lambda_(n+2)(T*T)<=4/n^2. This argument
does not silently divide a large atom. The first eigenvalues are
covered by Tr K<=1. It proves the bounded B3 upper with the low block.

11. FIXED-s LOWER, CRITICAL LOG, AND ORIGINAL LOG-LOSS UPPER
----------------------------------------------------------
On uniform probability measure over fixed [a,b] inside (0,1), the
Brownian-minus-Loewner kernel is PSD by the preceding Fourier proof.
Its unweighted difference is

  d_beta(h)=e^(-|h|/2)(e^|h|-1)/(e^(|h|/beta)-1)>=0.

Substitute h=beta z and use e^h-1<=h e^h. The two-sided integral is
at most pi^2 beta^2, because
sum_{n>=0}(n+1/2)^-2=pi^2/2. The u-density is at most b/(b-a) and the
exterior weight at most b. Schur's bound is therefore the stated
pi^2 b^2 beta^2/(b-a). Dropping the positive constant rank-one part
of the Brownian kernel gives lambda_j(K)>=(b-a)/[pi^2(j-1/2)^2].
Weyl's inequality then preserves c'/j^2 for j<=c/beta. The nonlinear
denominator costs a bounded factor since a_min=b^(-1/beta). For each
fixed beta, this finite set of eigenvalues transfers to finite matrices
by arbitrarily fine bounded-interval step approximation. Summation
proves the pure-power lower for s<1/2 and logarithmic lower at s=1/2.

The uniform crude sinc count in (13.4) is also justified. The boundary
L1 bound is O(c_alpha(1/alpha+1/beta))=O(1); geometric tails have the
same uniform cancellation c_alpha/beta<=1. Mesh size c/(L+C_0) and
log endpoints of size (L+C_0)/alpha and (L+C_0)/beta give
O(beta^-1(L+1)^2) nodes with a uniform threshold. Therefore
lambda_j(Delta)<=C exp(-c sqrt(beta j)). The high compression inherits
this estimate and also C/j^2. Splitting at
C_0 beta^-1 log^2(e/beta) gives exactly the original B1 upper with
its logarithmic loss, and the logarithmic B2 upper. One may choose
C_0 absolute with c sqrt(C_0)>2; fixed-s constants absorb the remaining
integral factors. The low-block moment is bounded independently of beta.

Thus all three original fixed-s assertions pass. No exact fixed-s
leading coefficient or convergence of H to a Brownian supremum was
proved or implicitly used.

  T_s=O(1/s),  s T_s->1,
