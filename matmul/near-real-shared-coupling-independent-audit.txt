1. SAFE DISK, AFFINE COUPLINGS AND PARAMETER BUDGET
-------------------------------------------------
For B=tK^-1 and the certified safe compression spectrum outside [1/2,2],
the coarse inverse ((1+u)B_QQ-I)^-1 has norm <=3 on |u|<=1/3. The
three scalar cases in the source give this directly, including negative
coarse eigenvalues. Hence the true-metric defect contracts by <=3/8,
and ||F(u)^-1||<=24/5 throughout this analytic disk. On the vertical
line, the stronger coarse bound two and contraction <=1/4 hold.

The couplings are Gamma(u)=T_PQ-(1+u)B_PQ and
Lambda(u)=T_QP-(1+u)B_QP. Their norms are at most 5/24<1/4 on the
outer disk. For a complex basis the correct relation is
Lambda(u)=Gamma(conj(u))*, not the pointwise adjoint Gamma(u)*.
The source consistently preserves this distinction. It therefore
maintains analyticity for coefficient computation and Cauchy estimates.

The integers L,J are actually found by multiplication and comparisons.
Their conditions (3/5)^L,(3/4)^J<=delta/100 are more than enough for
both pointwise and formal-series errors, with
delta=epsilon/(100mu^2). No logarithm oracle or spectral-ratio iteration
count is hidden.

2. SHARED SERIES ACCURACY AND DISTINCT RHS APPROXIMANTS
------------------------------------------------------
The formal Richardson process starts from zero and truncates modulo u^J.
Every inverse series has an invertible constant term. Consequently its
computed coefficients are exactly the first J coefficients of the actual
L-step analytic operator action R_L(u)Lambda(u), not a surrogate Krylov
sequence with an unproved relation to that function.

The outer inverse norm and contraction give uniform coupling norm
and iteration-error bounds on |u|=1/3. Cauchy coefficient estimates
evaluated at |u|<=1/4 have geometric ratio <=3/4. Summing the truncated
iteration errors and the exact-function Taylor tail gives the displayed
(96/5)zeta[(3/8)^L+(3/4)^J] bound, which is <=zeta*delta.
Pointwise safe solves separately give RHS error <=delta||b||.

The proof correctly does NOT assume those two approximations arise from
one common approximate inverse operator. It uses only their separate
norm bounds, and uses the same U_hat in the Schur core and reconstruction.
This distinction is essential when the coupling coefficients are shared
but right-hand sides are solved at their actual poles.

4. THE q+kJ WIDTH IS A REAL BATCHING GAIN
----------------------------------------
The explicit coarse preconditioner is
A0(u)=(I+uT0)^-1 A00, with A00=(B_QQ-I)^-1 and T0=I+A00.
Its single Frobenius normal form, including its transformation, uses
Neiger--Pernet--Villard Corollary 5.2. The primary theorem was checked
directly at https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07345v2#S5.SS1; it includes the
transformation in O(m^omega(loglog m)^2) field operations.

I independently reconstructed the companion-series arithmetic. For a
size-d companion block, its last coordinate is one shifted sum of the
d input-coordinate polynomials divided by a scalar polynomial with
constant coefficient one. Constructing that shifted sum costs O(dJ):
if d>=J only the final J coordinates contribute, and J^2<=dJ; if d<J,
there are d shifts of length J. Fast scalar inversion/convolutions and
the remaining d row shift/add recurrences cost soft-O(dkJ) for k RHS.
Summing block sizes gives soft-O(mkJ). This does not perform J separate
dense powers or dense matrix-vector actions.

The two dense Frobenius basis changes batch all kJ coefficient columns.
Pointwise preconditioners use the same preparation, batching the supplied
q RHS columns. Their combined true D_QQ applications are one raw action
on at most q+kJ columns per iteration. Separate near and far coarse
preconditioner maps require only a constant number of batched transforms.
The initial true T-action on P, the physical RHS projection and final
physical lift fit this same width bound. Later sequential adaptive RHS
queries are correctly excluded from retroactive batching.

The pointwise guarantee implies Frobenius-relative block accuracy by
summing column errors. The separate epsilon/sqrt(q) adjustment for a
spectral action guarantee on a prescribed orthonormal input basis is
correct and prevents an unwarranted spectral-block claim.

5. SHARED POSTPROCESSING AND FAR POLES
-------------------------------------
The Schur polynomial coefficients W_a=Gamma0*C_a+Gamma1*C_(a-1)
are computed once. The source retains a=J, including the terminal term
Gamma1*C_(J-1), so the affine multiplication has no degree off-by-one
loss. This costs O(Jmk^2); q evaluations cost O(qJk^2), followed by
the stated q small inversions. The right-side Gamma actions cost O(mkq).

The Q reconstruction sums C_a times all scaled P-solution columns,
costing O(Jmkq). It does not materialize q separate m-by-k coupling
matrices or repeat the O(mk^2) Schur product for every pole. Avoiding
that old O(qmk^2) term is necessary when q and k both scale as sqrt(m).

For far poles |y|>=t/4, mu<=sqrt(17). The full coarse preconditioner
norm is <=mu and contraction <=sqrt(17)/8<3/5. Its true inverse norm
is bounded by sqrt(17)/(1-sqrt(17)/8)<9, so the source's supported
9(3/5)^L and physical 11(3/5)^L constants hold. One additional full-core
Frobenius preparation suffices. Far columns share raw block products
with the near/series columns. If every pole is far, the split and
coupling series can be omitted.

The claimed postprocessing terms are therefore exactly
Jmk^2+qJk^2+Jmkq+qk^omega, without an uncharged qmk^2 operation.
Small empty blocks can be skipped under the ordinary empty-matrix
convention; the analysis covers k=0 or an empty safe block as appropriate.
