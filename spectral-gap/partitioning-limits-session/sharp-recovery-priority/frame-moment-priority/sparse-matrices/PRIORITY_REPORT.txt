PRIMARY PRIORITY AUDIT: RAW FINITE-FIELD MOMENTS FOR STRICT-UPPER-CERTIFICATE FRAMES
November 15, 2026. Agent counterexamples. Search and actual-source comparison,
not an independent proof audit of the proposed theorem and not a firstness certificate.

Assessment
==========
No inspected primary result directly supplies the proposed bounded RAW expectation
for the full fixed mixed-degree symmetric frame model, now including forced-zero
omission weights in the latest channel packet. The strongest reasons are
specific: the sparse-matrix full-rank theorem is rectangular and proves a TRUNCATED
moment estimate; the symmetric configuration-model theorem controls normalized rank
and imposes a degree condition absent from the target; the general typed-factor-graph
theorem controls normalized logarithms of expectations. Exact finite-length
protograph codeword averages, including fixed labels, are already classical and
must receive substantial method credit.

One qualification matters: the RANDOM 2023 full-rank overview explicitly announces
a RAW first-moment bound under an annealed variational maximizer condition. Thus
it would be inaccurate to say that this literature never considers or bounds raw
kernel expectations. That statement is not a theorem for symmetric typed frames,
and the paper does not verify its variational condition in the present class.

The defensible possible contribution is the complete O(1) annealed bound in this
particular dependent, typed, symmetric model under the stated strict-upper omission
certificate, including heterogeneous affine support phases and correlated boundary
defects. Neither type counting, finite-field kernel moments in general, lattice
local limits, nor Bethe entropy should be presented as originating in this packet.

2. Coja-Oghlan et al.: normalized rank, then a truncated full-rank argument
=======================================================================
(a) Coja-Oghlan, Ergur, Gao, Hetterich, Rolvien, The rank of sparse random
matrices, Random Structures & Algorithms 62 (2023), 68-130; first online 2022.
Primary DOI https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.21085
Author repository PDF:
https://eldorado.tu-dortmund.de/bitstreams/cc86edc9-5053-4a01-90f1-717e4407482c/download
Local: rank-sparse-2022.pdf/.txt.

Model in Section 1.2, printed p70: independently sampled row/check degrees k_i
and column/variable degrees d_j, conditioned on equality of their sums; a uniform
SIMPLE bipartite Tanner graph given these degrees; nonzero entries supplied through
a measurable exchangeable-array-type rule, encompassing constant 1 and iid labels.
Degree moments of order 2+eta are finite. This is a bipartite support model, not a
symmetric matrix model with paired inverse blocks and prescribed neighbor types.

With d=E d_j, k=E k_i and their generating functions D,K, Theorem 1.1 states

  rk(A)/n -> 1-max_(z in [0,1]) Phi(z) in probability,
  Phi(z)=D(1-K'(z)/k)-(d/k)[1-K(z)-(1-z)K'(z)].

The convergence is uniform over their coefficient rule. Theorem 1.1 determines the
normalized logarithm of kernel size over a finite field. It supplies neither a
bounded raw expectation nor an arbitrary onsite matrix-target theorem. Section 2.3
explicitly discusses the limitations of first-moment kernel-size calculations.

(b) Coja-Oghlan, Gao, Hahn-Klimroth, Lee, Muller, Rolvien, The full rank condition
for sparse random matrices, Combinatorics, Probability and Computing 33 (2024),
643-707. DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/S096354832400021X
Primary publisher-hosted repository PDF:
https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/347509133/the-full-rank-condition-for-sparse-random-matrices.pdf
Preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.14090
Local: full-rank-2024.pdf/.txt.

Theorem 1.1, printed p645: k_i>=3, finite 2+eta moments, iid nonzero coefficients
in F_q (constant 1 is permitted), gcd(supp d_j) coprime to q, and

  Phi(z)<Phi(0) for every 0<z<=1

imply full ROW rank with probability tending to one. The paragraph immediately
after the theorem observes that Phi(0)=1-d/k, Phi(1)=D(0)>=0, so this strict
condition excludes d=k. Its subject is therefore rectangular matrices with d<k,
asymptotically more columns than rows. The square critical regular case is
explicitly left outside this theorem and discussed separately via Huang.

Proposition 2.1, printed p653, treats specified degree arrays with empirical
degree/moment convergence conditions (P1)-(P7), gcd preservation, the same strict
Phi condition, and a typical-kernel event O with probability tending to one. It
again concludes full row rank with high probability. Specified degree arrays do
not prescribe which TYPES of variable a check meets; such type margins are extra
constraints absent from the uniform Tanner model.

The exact moment distinction is documented in the primary proof, not inferred
from the title. For a uniform random right-hand side y and Z=#{x:Ax=y},

  E_A Z=q^(n-m),
  E_A Z^2=(E_A Z)(E_A |ker A|).                     Eqs (2.1)-(2.2), p649.

Here E_A conditions on the degree/coefficient-type information defined in the
paper. The event O in Eq (2.7) requires almost all coordinate pairs of a random
kernel vector to have nearly uniform joint law. The proved comparison is

  E_A[Z^2 1{A in O}] ~ (E_A Z)^2,                 Eq (2.9), p652,

not the same estimate without 1_O. Section 7.1, p676, expressly identifies its
task as estimating E_A[|ker A| 1_O]. Fact 7.1 reduces that quantity to nearly
equitable kernel vectors; Lemmas 7.2-7.3 estimate the remaining near-uniform
types through a second-order expansion and arithmetic lattice local limits.

The discussion on pp649-651 explains that exponentially rare matrices with large
kernels can dominate E|ker A|, despite a favorable typical rank. This is a direct
warning against reading the full-rank theorem as a raw-moment bound. Section 2.6,
p657, also explicitly describes the symmetric setting as an additional challenge.

3. Actual symmetric configuration results are normalized-rank results
==================================================================
(a) van der Hofstad, Muller, Zhu, The rank of sparse symmetric matrices over
arbitrary fields, RSA 66 (2025), e21258, first online 2024.
DOI https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.21258
Primary PDF:
https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/347509250/Random_Struct_Algorithms_-_2024_-_van_der_Hofstad_-_The_rank_of_sparse_symmetric_matrices_over_arbitrary_fields.pdf
Local: symmetric-rank-vhmz.pdf/.txt.

Eq (1.1) is a weighted Erdos-Renyi adjacency matrix: independent edge indicators,
fixed arbitrary symmetric nonzero weights, zero diagonal. Theorem 1.2, printed
p4, gives convergence in probability of rk_F(A)/n to min R_d, uniformly in the
fixed weight matrix, where

  phi_d(z)=exp(d(z-1)),
  R_d(z)=2-phi_d(1-phi_d(z))-[1+d(1-z)]phi_d(z).

It is not an exponential-nullity moment or a fixed-frame pairing theorem.

(b) Fresher and closer primary: the SAME authors, The asymptotic rank of adjacency
matrices of weighted configuration models over arbitrary fields,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02813, v1 August 4, 2025.
Actual downloaded PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02813v1
Local: config-rank-2508.02813v1.pdf/.txt.

Section 2.1 uses a GLOBAL UNCOLORED uniform pairing of all stubs with the specified
degree sequence. Assumption 2.1 asks for convergence in mean of the empirical
degree proportions to a law (p_k), with p_0<1, and convergence of the mean degree.
Assumption 2.3 requires log-concavity of the second derivative of
psi(z)=sum_k p_k z^k. Eq (2.4) first ERASES loops and merges parallel edges;
the nonzero edge weights are an arbitrary deterministic symmetric template.

Theorem 2.4 states

  rk_F(A_n)/n -> min_(z in [0,1]) R_psi(z) in probability,
  R_psi(z)=2-psi(1-psi'(z)/psi'(1))-psi(z)-(1-z)psi'(z).

Theorem 3.1 makes the convergence uniform in the nonzero template and also
permits the subcritical alternative sum_k k(k-2)p_k<=0. Section 3.5.1 transfers
other loop/multiple-edge conventions via o(n) rank changes and transfers to
simplicity conditioning with an additional finite second-moment condition. None
of these statements controls the exponential weight of the kernel on rare events.

The positive certificate does NOT imply their log-concavity condition. A concrete
counterexample to that implication is

  D=[[2,1],[1,6]], a_1=a_2=1,
  theta_11=theta_12=theta_21=1/3, theta_22=1/9.

Both weighted row sums are 1 and every active theta lies in (0,1/2). The total
degrees are 3 and 7. The empirical degree law has
psi(z)=(z^3+z^7)/2 and psi''(z)=3z+21z^5. At z^4=1/7,

  (log psi''(z))''=1/z^2>0.

Thus this admissible target frame violates Assumption 2.3 and is supercritical.
This is an independent elementary calculation, not a claim from the source.

Even where log-concavity holds, the prescribed per-vertex neighbor-type margins
are absent from the global pairing model. Conditioning an uncolored pairing
onto them can cost exponentially small probability: for a balanced all-cross
d-regular bipartition with m stubs on each side, the probability is
m!/(2m-1)!!=2^m(m!)^2/(2m)!, exponentially small in m. A whp theorem in the
uncolored model does not automatically survive that conditioning.

4. Fixed-base multitype exact kernel enumerators: real prior overlap
==================================================================
Dolecek, Divsalar, Sun, Amiri, Non-Binary Protograph-Based LDPC Codes:
Enumerators, Analysis, and Designs, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory
60(7) (2014), 3913-3941. DOI https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2014.2316215
Actual author PDF:
https://www.seas.ucla.edu/csl/files/publications/NBPB_ToDistribute_ForFeedbackProject.pdf
Local: nbpb-dolecek-2014.pdf/.txt (author manuscript pagination).
The underlying Sun 2013 thesis was also retrieved from the author's university:
https://www.seas.ucla.edu/csl/files/theses/thesis_ysun.pdf
Local: sun-2013-thesis.pdf/.txt. The journal paper is the main evidence here.

Definitions 3-4, manuscript pp2-3, define constrained nonbinary protograph codes:
each base edge has a FIXED nonzero finite-field label, each base node is copied
N times, and every base edge receives a permutation of its N copies. This model
has multiple fixed variable/check types and fixed edge labels. Thus a comparison
which dismisses LDPC prior as merely iid coefficient matrices would be wrong.

Theorem 3, Eq (23), manuscript p9, gives each replicated check's frequency-type
count as a sum of multinomial coefficients over local legal-word type tables.
Theorem 4, Eq (24), manuscript p10, gives the EXACT ensemble average with prescribed
root symbol-frequency tables d_i. In our notation its shape is

  E[# codewords with these type frequencies]
   = product_j A_(check j)^N(d_j)
       / product_i Mult(N; d_i,0,...,d_i,q-1)^(t_i-1),

where t_i is the degree of variable type i, and d_j collects the prescribed
frequency vectors on the slots incident to check j. The proof uses independent
frequency-preserving uniform interleavers. Theorem 2, Eq (14), gives the related
formula when nonzero edge labels are averaged too. Section III-C Eqs (16)-(21)
and Section IV-C Eqs (30)-(32) analyze normalized logarithmic growth rates.

These are genuine exact annealed counting formulas, not merely numerical code
designs. Summing their type counts gives a raw expected kernel cardinality for
their ensemble. What the inspected statements do NOT give is a global uniform
O(1) theorem under the present theta certificate. A zero limiting exponential
growth rate alone leaves open polynomial or exp(o(N)) divergence. Low-weight
minimum-distance conclusions do not control all codewords on rare matrices.

The literal Tanner-cover law also has independent permutations on its base
variable/check edges; identifying row and column vertex sets does not enforce
the inverse-permutation relations required by symmetric adjacency. Internal
pairings are likewise not supplied by the quoted Tanner construction. A normal
factor-graph recoding may be useful, but one would still need a theorem yielding
the full bounded moment after that recoding. Exact combinatorial formulas alone
do not complete the affine-coset phase and boundary estimates in the target.

  (1/n) log E[Z_G 1{G in U}]

6. Avoiding false distinctions: arbitrary R, extension fields, and cokernels
=======================================================================
The following elementary reductions are part of this comparison, not external
novelty claims.

For A over F_p, rank does not change after extending the field. Consequently,
an ordinary scalar kernel cardinality over F_(p^r) is EXACTLY p^(r nul_Fp A).
Thus a prior q-ary codeword expectation formula valid for arbitrary prime powers
already formally encodes every fixed unshifted p-power nullity moment. The main
missing conclusion is uniform boundedness in the required distribution, not
merely the introduction of a vector alphabet.

More generally, suppose some prior theorem for the SAME A_N law bounded every
fixed scalar-shifted kernel moment over every fixed finite extension K/F_p.
That WOULD imply bounded arbitrary-R intertwiner counts. To see this, take K
splitting R, let s=[K:F_p], and put R into triangular form. Successively solving
the columns of A Y=Y R gives at most |ker_K(A-lambda_j I)| choices for column j
after its predecessors are fixed. The linear system defining intertwiners has
the same nullity D over F_p and K. Hence

  Z_R(A)=p^D
   <= product_(j=1)^r |ker_K(A-lambda_j I)|^(1/s).

Holder's inequality transfers fixed scalar moment bounds to E Z_R(A). Nontrivial
Jordan blocks do not invalidate this upper bound. Therefore arbitrary R alone
should not be advertised as a conceptual separation from sufficiently strong
scalar-shifted moment universality over extensions.

Likewise, bounds on ALL fixed finite-group SURJECTION moments in the EXACT model
can yield raw Hom/kernel bounds: for an elementary abelian target G,
#Hom(M,G)=sum_(H<=G) #Sur(M,H). The finite sum includes all possible image
subgroups. It is the random-model hypotheses, not just the word 'cokernel', that
must be checked. The global-regular results in the earlier priority report were
already treated with this care and are not re-audited here.

Finally, whp bounded nullity, normalized-rank convergence, and convergence of a
corank distribution do not on their own imply bounded exponential moments. Even
deterministic nullity floor(sqrt(N)) has normalized nullity tending to zero while
p^(r nullity) diverges. Uniform integrability or explicit rare-event estimates
are indispensable for a RAW conclusion.
