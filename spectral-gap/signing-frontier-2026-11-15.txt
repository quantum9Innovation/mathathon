2. Exact tree resolvent calculation and a barrier

Put q=d-1. Let P_l be the nonbacktracking distance polynomials of the
d-regular tree: P_0=1, P_1=x, P_2=x^2-d, and
P_l=x P_(l-1)-q P_(l-2) for l>=3. Direct multiplication gives

  sum_(l>=0) P_l(lambda)t^l=(1-t^2)/(1-lambda*t+q*t^2).

The mixed pair (A,-A), on an actual tree, has matching weights 1/2:
the average determinant is the characteristic polynomial of A/sqrt(2).
In its adjugate, the unique path of length l has expected edge product
2^(-l); all remaining matching edges are vertex-disjoint from the path.
Thus its local tree resolvent is the weighted-tree resolvent multiplied
entrywise by (1/sqrt(2))^distance. With cavity h defined by

  z=1/h+q*h/2,   h(z)~1/z,

the ordinary diagonal tree resolvent is g=h/(1-h^2/2). Evaluating the
distance-polynomial series gives the following formal scalar expression:

  f_lambda(z)=h(1-h^2/4)/
      [(1-h^2/2)(1-lambda*h/2+q*h^2/4)].                 (T1)

Its first four coefficients agree with Lin--Zhou's exact high-girth
moment formulas. The matching branch ends at R=sqrt(2q), where
h=sqrt(2/q). The transport denominator first vanishes there at

  |lambda|=(3/sqrt(2))*sqrt(q).

Consequently, detecting an EXTERIOR POLE in this tree limit cannot yield
coefficient 2. This identifies the structural origin of the screenshot's
3/sqrt(2), instead of proposing that more moments alone reach 2.

Scope: this paragraph proves the displayed generating-function/tree
calculation and its pole threshold. A full truncated-moment impossibility
theorem additionally needs positivity and exact high-girth coefficient
transfer; the independent spectral_audit worker is investigating that.
This note does not claim those missing implications.

3. Weighted tuples do not repair exterior-pole detection

For a tuple (a_1 A,...,a_k A), Fourier-transversal averaging on a tree
replaces matching edge weight by tau=sum a_i^2/k^2. For the jth matrix
measure the path transport is b_j=a_j/k. The identical tree calculation is

 f_j,lambda=h(1-b_j^2*h^2)/
   [(1-tau*h^2)(1-lambda*b_j*h+q*b_j^2*h^2)],
 z=1/h+q*tau*h.                                         (T2)

At the matching edge R=2sqrt(q*tau), set
r_j=|b_j|/sqrt(tau)=|a_j|/sqrt(sum a_i^2). The exterior-pole threshold is
(r_j+1/r_j)*sqrt(q). A simultaneous upper/lower argument using positive
and negative tuple entries needs at least two selected nonzero entries.
Their squared r's sum to at most one; at least one is <=1/sqrt(2).
Since r+1/r decreases for 0<r<=1, its worst selected threshold is at least
3/sqrt(2). Equality is attained by the balanced pair. Adding repeated
copies, zero matrices, or unequal magnitudes cannot improve this specific
tree pole-detection mechanism. This is not a universal impossibility for
all tuple-based methods, nor an established signing theorem.

4. Routes beyond this barrier and exact gaps

Nonbacktracking Bass polynomials have the correct Ramanujan boundary,
but ordinary real-line interlacing controls a one-sided real spectral edge.
A circle-based interlacing selection or constrained interlacing for
P_l(A_sigma) would be a different mechanism. Neither is supplied here.

One-sided MSS plus high-girth odd moments is insufficient: taking the
negative unsigned adjacency of a non-bipartite high-girth Ramanujan graph
already gives a lower eigenvalue -d while its upper edge is near 2sqrt(q).
Vanishing short odd traces cannot remove this delocalized outlier without
a size-dependent loss. This is an argument against that proposed proof,
not against existence of a better signing of the same graph.
