1. Bottom spectrum zero does not invalidate the determinant identity
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Independently reconstructed the analytic argument before reading the full
Fredholm note. Let A>=0 be bounded, self-adjoint and injective, and assume
b in Dom A^-1/2 with tau=||A^-1/2 b||^2<infinity. Write p=alpha/2,
0<alpha<1. Then w(t)=A^-p(A+t)^-1 b is well-defined for every t>0:
on the bounded positive spectral interval, lambda^-alpha is bounded by
a fixed multiple of lambda^-1, integrable against b's spectral measure.
Tonelli and the scalar beta integral give exactly

 c_alpha integral_0^infinity t^alpha ||w(t)||^2 dt=alpha*tau.

Hence the rank-one relative integral with denominator 1+m(t),
m(t)=<b,(A+t)^-1b>, exists as a POSITIVE TRACE-CLASS Bochner integral C,
with Tr C<=alpha*tau. Multiplying it on both sides by bounded A^p gives
B^alpha-A^alpha=A^p C A^p by the resolvent identity. This uniquely defines
the bounded relative form because Ran A^p is dense. No inverse operator
on the full Hilbert space is being assumed bounded.

The regularization proof in fredholm-notes.txt is particularly clean.
For A_delta=A+delta I, its vectors w_delta(t) converge to w(t) and have
norm <=||w(t)||. The rank-one difference inequality
 ||xx*-yy*||_1<=(||x||+||y||)||x-y||
and the common integrable bound 2 c_alpha t^alpha||w(t)||^2 imply
||C_delta-C||_1->0 by dominated convergence. This handles the low spectral
endpoint rigorously in trace norm, not merely strongly or in operator norm.

At fixed delta>0, set X_s=A_delta+sbb*, and
R_s=A_delta^-p X_s^alpha A_delta^-p. This is I plus a C^1 trace-class
path; differentiation under its rank-one integral is justified by the
uniform lower bound delta. Fredholm logarithmic differentiation and trace
cyclicity are legitimate because inverses are bounded and derivatives are
trace class. Since
 A_delta^-p R_s^-1 A_delta^-p=X_s^-alpha,
the derivative is

 Tr(R_s^-1 R_s')
 =c_alpha integral t^alpha <b,X_s^-alpha(X_s+t)^-2b> dt
 =alpha <b,X_s^-1 b>
 =alpha*tau_delta/(1+s*tau_delta).

Integrating s from zero to one gives alpha log(1+tau_delta). Monotone
convergence gives tau_delta->tau, and trace-norm determinant continuity
gives

             Tr log(I+C)=log det_F(I+C)=alpha log(1+tau).

The logarithms are real because I+C_delta and I+C are positive and >=I.
One may even bypass a separate determinant-continuity citation: for positive
trace-class T,S, the C^1 path I+(1-t)T+tS and the bound on its inverse
imply |Tr log(I+T)-Tr log(I+S)|<=||T-S||_1, first for finite-rank
compressions and then by trace-norm approximation.


2. Finite-support generalized Ritz convergence
------------------------------------------------
For bounded-degree edge-rooted graphs, endpoint Laplacians have uniformly
bounded spectra, before and after the fixed unit update. A polynomial
matrix entry between transported finite-support vectors depends only on a
fixed larger rooted ball (including enough radius to read degrees).
Uniform polynomial approximation of the CONTINUOUS function x^alpha on
that compact interval therefore proves convergence of the two compressed
positive-power matrices. No inverse power or spectral gap is used.

If the limit graph is infinite and connected, its Laplacian is injective
on l2: zero energy forces a constant, and a nonzero constant is not l2.
For independent test vectors f_i, the Gram Q_ij=<f_i,A^alpha f_j> is
therefore strictly positive definite. The corresponding Q_n are eventually
SPD, and Q_n^-1/2 D_n Q_n^-1/2 converge as ordinary fixed-dimensional
matrices. Finite-graph constants do not disturb this: projecting every
transported f_i off constants changes neither Gram, since both endpoint
powers and their difference annihilate constants. SPD of Q_n also proves
that these projected vectors remain independent.

The span A^p c00 is dense in l2: boundedness of A^p preserves approximation
from c00, and injectivity makes Ran A^p dense. Approximate the first k
orthonormal eigenvectors of compact C by such images. Their Gram is close
to I and their C-form matrix is close to the top-eigenvalue diagonal,
including multiplicities. Thus one finite-support generalized Ritz pair
captures these k local eigenvalues to arbitrary accuracy. Transport it to
G_n and use generalized min--max, obtaining for every fixed j

             liminf_n lambda_j(C_n)>=lambda_j(C).

This proves precisely the local spectral capture needed for the whole
budget, without asserting strong convergence of the finite relative
operators or continuity of their inverse fractional powers.

3. Exact subtraction from the finite determinant budget
--------------------------------------------------------
A finite unit bridge doubled has leverage exactly one, so every finite
relative response has total log budget S=alpha log2. For q_n strict
correction eigenvalues above epsilon_n and any fixed k,

 (q_n-k)_+ log(1+epsilon_n)
       <=S-sum_(j=1)^k log(1+lambda_j(C_n)).

This inequality is valid also when q_n<k because the right side remains
nonnegative. Rearranging adds at most k log(1+epsilon_n), which vanishes
as epsilon_n->0. Since epsilon/log(1+epsilon)->1 and the finite total
budget uniformly bounds q_n log(1+epsilon_n), the fixed-j liminf yields

 limsup epsilon_n q_n
 <=S-sum_(j=1)^k log(1+lambda_j(C)).

Sending k to infinity and using the trace-class Fredholm identity proves

 limsup epsilon_n N_(epsilon_n)(C_n)
          <=alpha log[2/(1+tau_wired(G,e))].

It is the entire local logarithmic determinant that is compulsory.
No equality or flattening of the escaping global modes is assumed.

4. Wired resistance of a limiting bridge
------------------------------------------
The bridge property survives local convergence, since any alternative
finite path in the limit would eventually occur in the finite hosts.
For one half of its deletion, the root has degree <=d-1, and the number
of edges crossing distance levels h,h+1 is <=(d-1)^(h+1). Edges within a
level contribute zero to radial energy. The displayed truncated geometric
radial trial function has energy <=(d-2)/(1-(d-1)^-R), as verified by
summing the geometric series. Consequently half capacity <=d-2 and its
wired resistance r is >=1/(d-2), including recurrent halves with r=infinity.
A finite half has zero capacity because its constant function is finitely
supported on that finite half, hence likewise r=infinity.

For prescribed root values a,c, the infimum of the two independent half
energies is a^2/r_+ +c^2/r_-. Adding the unit bridge term (a-c)^2 and
minimizing at a-c=1 gives total infimum 1+1/(r_++r_-). The approximating
finite-support functions on the two halves can be combined, so this is an
exact variational identity, also for infinite r. Therefore

 tau_wired=(r_++r_-)/(1+r_++r_-),

with value one if either half resistance is infinite. Equality with
||A^-1/2 b||^2 is the spectral-theorem variational formula. Since A is
bounded, c00 is an energy core, so the finitely-supported test convention
agrees with the inverse quadratic form. Thus tau_wired>=2/d, with equality
for the two regular (d-1)-branching tree halves. This does not assert
attainment of the spectral count coefficient by finite graphs.

5. Uniform bounded-degree bridge conclusion
----------------------------------------------
The finite determinant budget bounds every count by a finite integer.
Its supremum over the nonempty host class is therefore attained as an
integer value; choosing maximizing hosts at any epsilon sequence is valid.
Edge-rooted bounded-degree graphs have a locally convergent subsequence.
If its connected limit is finite, a rooted ball larger than its diameter
exposes every incident edge, forcing the connected finite hosts eventually
to be isomorphic to that graph. Their count times epsilon tends to zero.
If the limit is infinite, Steps 1--4 apply. This proves

 limsup_(epsilon->0) epsilon W^bridge_(d,alpha)(epsilon)
            <=alpha log[2d/(d+2)].

For d=3 the coefficient is alpha log(6/5), improving the mandatory-one-
outlier bridge bound alpha log(4/3). This audit proves the upper bound and
its required infinite-dimensional identity. It does not prove its sharpness,
apply the half-tree resistance formula to nonbridges, or certify priority.
