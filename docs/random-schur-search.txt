Random far spaces: a resolvent-profile theorem and its limitation
==============================================================
2026-11-14, research_runs. Dedicated research note; no manuscript edited.

Status
------
There is a rigorous positive result: a Haar random far space is uniformly
positive with a core width controlled by a REGULARIZED POSITIVE RESOLVENT
TRACE, rather than by the smallest positive eigenvalue. It is a direct
application of Gaussian escape geometry, proved below with an explicit
width estimate. This gives a conditional profile-dependent Schur algorithm.

The same analysis does not yield the original unrestricted target. For an
arcsine positive bulk, a deterministic interlacing inequality forces the
core-width times interval-Chebyshev inverse degree to be Omega(m), for
EVERY far space. Flat-plus-low-rank instances evade that inverse cost via
their separate centered low-rank structure. These statements are not
lower bounds against arbitrary raw-factor algorithms or more powerful
multilevel/spectrum-aware inverse methods.

1. Random-far theorem with a regularized trace profile
----------------------------------------------------
Let K be real symmetric m-by-m, with r negative eigenvalues -nu_j,
0<nu_j<=nu, and nonnegative eigenvalues lambda_i. Let ||K||<=L.
Zero eigenvalues are included among the nonnegative lambda_i. Define

  T(b)=sum_i 1/(lambda_i+b),
  d_eff(b)=b*T(b), b>0.

Let F be a Haar random subspace of codimension s, and let V_F be any
orthonormal basis of F. For a>0 put

  E_a={x: ||x||=1, x^T K x<=a}.

For every b>0, its Gaussian width obeys

  w(E_a)<=sqrt(r)+sqrt((nu+a+b)*T(b)).                    (1)

Proof in spectral coordinates. On E_a,

  sum_i lambda_i x_i^2 <= nu*||x_-||^2+a <= nu+a.

Therefore sum_i (lambda_i+b)x_i^2<=nu+a+b. For a Gaussian vector g,
weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives

  g^T x <= ||g_-||+
       sqrt((nu+a+b)*sum_i g_i^2/(lambda_i+b)).

Take supremum and expectation, then Jensen. This proves (1); no
convexity or knowledge of the negative eigenvectors is needed.

A slightly sharper one-parameter resolvent form is available. For
0<u<1/(nu+a), the SPD matrix H_u=I+u(K-aI) satisfies
x^T H_u x<=1 on E_a. Thus E_a is contained in an ellipsoid and

  w(E_a)^2 <= tr(H_u^{-1})
    =sum_j 1/(1-u(nu_j+a))
       +sum_i 1/(1+u(lambda_i-a)).                       (2)

Indeed the supremum of g^T x over that ellipsoid is
sqrt(g^T H_u^{-1}g), and Jensen proves the trace bound. This also
allows optimizing u using the actual negative magnitudes. In particular
u=1/[2(nu+a)] gives the convenient bound

  w(E_a)^2 <= 2r+2*d_eff(nu+a).                           (3)

The elementary positive-eigenvalue comparison used here is
2(nu+a)+lambda_i-a >= nu+a+lambda_i.

Gaussian escape theorem. If G_s is an s-by-m standard Gaussian matrix,
then, with probability at least 1-exp(-t^2/2),

  inf_(x in E_a) ||G_s x|| >= sqrt(s-1)-w(E_a)-t.         (4)

Since ker(G_s) is a Haar far space, a strictly positive RHS implies

  V_F^T K V_F > a I.                                    (5)

Consequently either of (1) or (2) supplies an explicit sufficient width.
For example it suffices that

  sqrt(s-1) > sqrt(2r+2*d_eff(nu+a))
                     +sqrt(2 log(1/delta)).              (6)

Up to universal factors this is

  s=O(r+d_eff(nu+a)+log(1/delta)).                        (7)

If the required width exceeds m, no nontrivial far space is asserted.
The zero-dimensional far space/full-core fallback is always available.

2. What the theorem does and does not make algorithmic
-----------------------------------------------------
The spectral profile is an ANALYSIS parameter, not an assumed free oracle.
An actual random critical space is easy: orthogonalize an m-by-s Gaussian
matrix to U_C and set Pi_F=I-U_C U_C^T. Householder reflectors or the
projection suffice; a dense m-by-(m-s) far basis is not required.

On the good event (5), write in critical/far coordinates

  K=[[C,B^T],[B,A]],    aI<A<=LI.

One-sided fixed Chebyshev inverse polynomials T_-<=A^{-1}<=T_+
to absolute error zeta have degree

  q=O(sqrt(L/a)*log(poly(L,1/a,1/zeta))).                 (8)

Choose zeta<=1/(2L). Then the analytical matrices with far blocks
T_+^{-1} and T_-^{-1}, respectively, bracket K in Loewner order.
Their operator errors are at most 2L^2*zeta. Their Schur complements
are C-B^T T_+ B and C-B^T T_- B, and are explicit s-by-s cores.
This is the same one-sided Schur certificate mechanism as in the
independent adaptive-defect note; no inverse fine matrix is formed.

If one true K action on s vectors costs O(I*s), and I>=m^2, the
ordinary-arithmetic cost is

  ~C_setup + ~I*s*sqrt(L/a) + O(s^3) + explicit output.    (9)

The ambient projection and ordinary QR terms are
O(m*s^2*sqrt(L/a)), dominated by I*s*sqrt(L/a) when s<=m.
Any dense core setup, e.g. m^3 for conventional coarse whitening, is
charged in C_setup. It must not be called input-linear in a regime
where m^3 itself exceeds the output budget.

A parameter trial can first validate A>=a/2 using a randomized minimum-
eigenvalue approximation to additive a/4 and an actual returned quotient.
Its cost ~I*sqrt(L/a) is dominated by (9). This is a high-probability
validation, not an exact PSD oracle. A finite search can test geometrically
spaced widths and a-values under geometrically increasing cost budgets,
with fresh failure allocations. A bound for an adaptive implementation
must explicitly include this scheduling and confidence overhead. The
profile theorem alone is not such an implementation theorem.

3. Flat positive bulk: tiny negative magnitude allows a tiny random core
---------------------------------------------------------------------
For K=I-(1+gamma)u u^T, ||u||=1, the eigenvalues are -gamma and
m-1 copies of 1. Hence d_eff(b)=(m-1)b/(1+b). Taking a~gamma gives

  s=O(1+m*gamma+log(1/delta)),    A>=Theta(gamma) I.

This avoids the bad conclusion obtained by trying to FIND a negative
witness in a random small restriction. Here the SMALL random space is
critical, and its LARGE orthogonal complement is the positive far space.

There is an exact calculation. If z=||U_C^T u||^2, then

  lambda_min(A)=(1+gamma)z-gamma,
  z~Beta(s/2,(m-s)/2).

For s~m*gamma+log(1/delta), the positive margin is of order s/m-gamma.
It is not a constant margin for s=o(m). A constant-conditioned far
block requires a linear-size random core in this flat example, unless
one exploits additional structure in its inverse.

At gamma=1/m and constant confidence, the small random core has constant
size, but interval-Chebyshev inversion costs ~sqrt(m). Consequently
the raw input work is still ~I*sqrt(m), like ordinary rank-one Krylov.

The centered-flat hybrid DOES remove this cost: A is exactly scalar
identity minus rank one. Its nontrivial direction can be found from a
single generic action of A-I, and its inverse is then a scalar-plus-
rank-one Sherman--Morrison formula. A compression median also finds the
unknown scalar exactly using three columns in the noiseless rank-one
case. This is standard centered low-rank structure; the random split
does not itself discover a new uniformly cheap inverse.

For the parent's congruence example

  K=eta D^{1/2}[I-(1+gamma)u u^T]D^{1/2},

if D is explicitly available, D^{-1/2} metric coordinates reduce it
exactly to the previous example up to eta. In unweighted coordinates,
negative magnitude is at most eta*gamma*lambda_max(D), while the
positive eigenvalues are at least eta*lambda_min(D), by rank-one
interlacing. The crude flat estimate becomes s=O(1+m*gamma*kappa(D)
+log(1/delta)). Thus arbitrary coordinate anisotropy cannot be ignored.
If D or a good substitute is unavailable cheaply, the weighted random
split is another missing access-cost obligation.

4. Arcsine positive bulk: a precise obstruction to this one-level route
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Consider an arbitrarily rotated matrix with eigenvalues

  -nu,  lambda_i=sin^2(pi*i/(2m)), i=1,...,m-1.

For m^{-2}<=b<=1,

  T(b)=Theta(m/sqrt(b)),   d_eff(b)=Theta(m*sqrt(b)).       (10)

This follows directly by comparing sin(pi*i/(2m)) with i/m and summing
1/(b+(i/m)^2); no limiting density assumption is needed. It is the
usual arcsine density lambda^{-1/2}(1-lambda)^{-1/2} profile.

With a~nu, (6) gives s=O(1+m*sqrt(nu)+log(1/delta)). At nu=1/m,
the core width is ~sqrt(m) and inverse degree is ~sqrt(m), so (9)
costs ~I*m, the classical Gram scale. Optimizing a in the regularized
trace certificate gives s~m*sqrt(nu+a), hence

  s/sqrt(a) ~ m*sqrt(1+nu/a) >= m.                        (11)

There is also a deterministic dimension/conditioning explanation. For
ANY far space of codimension s, min--max gives

  lambda_min(V_F^T K V_F) <= lambda_s
                         <= (pi^2/4)*(s/m)^2.            (12)

Indeed F intersects the span of the bottom s+1 eigenvectors, on which
every Rayleigh quotient is at most lambda_s. If s<=m/2, the largest
compressed eigenvalue is bounded below by a positive constant, again
by interlacing. Thus its interval condition number is Omega((m/s)^2).
The width times the interval-Chebyshev degree is therefore Omega(m).
For s>m/2 the width alone is already Omega(m).

This rules out beating the Gram scale by THIS random-split plus
unpreconditioned, interval-certified far-inverse accounting on this
profile. It is not a lower bound for actual spectrum-adapted CG on
every compression, a structured inverse, multilevel preconditioning,
or a new raw-factor partial-Krylov algorithm. A matrix with a known
eigenbasis can of course be much easier than its interval bound.

5. Relation to the flat-Schur claimed frontier and clustered Krylov
----------------------------------------------------------------
The new centered-Schur shortcut has a serious ordinary Krylov comparator.
If K=L diag(S_exact,A)L^T, kappa(L)=O(1), A in a fixed positive
interval, and S_exact=aI+rank_k+O(theta), then for a>theta the PSD
base L diag(aI,A)L^T has two constant-factor positive spectral bands:
one at scale a and one at constant scale. A rank-k perturbation moves
only O(k) eigenvalues outside their union, by interlacing.

There is an explicit polynomial separating these two bands from x<=0.
For bands [a,Ca] and [c,L], choose

  p1(x)=(1-x/(Ca))^u,
  p2(x)=(1-x/L)^v,  v=O(u*log(1/a)).

On the small band p1 contracts by exp(-Omega(u)); on the far band
the contraction of p2 cancels p1's at-most-(O(1/a))^u growth.
At x<=0 both factors are at least one. Hence degree
O(log(1/epsilon)*log(1/a)) suffices. If a=O(theta), a shift O(theta)
creates a small positive band at scale theta within the allowed error.

An oversampled Gaussian block of width O(r+k) can annihilate the
positive-outlier coordinates analytically BEFORE filtering, using the
pseudoinverse of its rows in the O(r+k)-dimensional exceptional space.
No degree-k polynomial root annihilation is necessary. The standard
randomized range bound then captures the negative head to arbitrarily
small polynomial-in-theta accuracy using only logarithmic filter depth.

217--288, 2011, arXiv:0909.4061; Theorem 9.1 is the deterministic
filtered range bound, and its Gaussian analysis controls the inverse.

Rank-r extraction itself does not block this comparison. For PSD H,
||H||<=L, lambda_(r+1)(H)<=1, let E span eigenvectors above 1+delta.
If ||(I-P_Q)E||<=epsilon and U are the top-r Ritz vectors of Q^T H Q,
then, for each such exact eigenvector e with eigenvalue lambda,

  (Q^T H Q-lambda I)Q^T e=-Q^T H(I-P_Q)e.

Every discarded Ritz value is <=1 by min--max. Summing the per-vector
bounds gives

  ||(I-P_U)E||_F <= sqrt(r)*(epsilon+L*epsilon/delta).

Since H<=(1+delta)I+L P_E,

  ||(I-P_U)H^{1/2}||^2
    <=1+delta+L*r*(epsilon+L*epsilon/delta)^2.             (13)

For strong ACTION residual of a bounded PSD T, use H=T^2 in this
formula (L<=4), not H=T; then (13) bounds ||(I-P_U)T||^2.
Choosing epsilon polynomially smaller than delta^(3/2)/sqrt(r)
costs only logarithms in filter degree. No precise individual leading
eigenvector or singular-value-ratio dependence is required.

This establishes a serious conditional UPPER/NEAR-threshold comparison
at ~I*(r+k) plus m^3 preprocessing and ordinary core/output work.
A complete adaptive global wrapper still needs all below-threshold
count cases, parameter discovery and confidence caps. Therefore the
flat-Schur algorithm should currently be described as a new certificate
construction/instance-sensitive route, not as a proved improvement over
all established polynomial Krylov compositions on its conditional class.

6. The trace-profile cost also has a standard filtering comparator
---------------------------------------------------------------
For a>0, let k(a) be the number of nonnegative eigenvalues of K below a.
On the remaining positive interval [a,L], the usual normalized Chebyshev
polynomial

  p_q(x)=T_q((2x-L-a)/(L-a)) / T_q((-L-a)/(L-a))

has |p_q(x)|<=epsilon for degree
q=O(sqrt(L/a)*log(1/epsilon)); take q even. On x<=0 it has p_q(x)>=1.
Treat the r negative directions and k(a) small-positive directions as
the exceptional space. A Gaussian block of width O(r+k(a)) can set
every undesired small-positive coordinate to zero analytically before
filtering, as in Section 5. HMT's deterministic filtered-range bound
then captures the negative head with arbitrarily small polynomial-in-
theta error. Rank-r Ritz extraction handles the upper/near case.

This standard composition has input arithmetic

  ~I*(r+k(a))*sqrt(L/a).                                  (14)

Since k(a)<=2*d_eff(a)<=2*d_eff(nu+a), (14) is no larger than the
regularized-trace Schur accounting (9), up to constants/logarithms.
It does not require the exact negative magnitude nu as a supplied value.
Very weak negative directions may be ignored after an allowed O(theta)
threshold shift; no logarithm of an arbitrarily tiny negative eigenvalue
is necessary for a relative-accuracy LRA guarantee.

Thus the random-far theorem is a useful explicit geometric statement and
may inform certificates or more structured inverse algorithms, but its
current input-pass bound alone is not a new frontier even in the generic
K-action model. A claimed improvement would need an additional cheap
Schur inverse, a stronger raw-factor access property, or another mechanism
that beats this deflated/oversampled polynomial comparator. The global
adaptive below-threshold wrapper remains a separate obligation in both
compositions; this note does not assert its complete automatic subsumption.
