Positive features after coarse cancellation: a scoped obstruction
2026-11-14

Status
------
The concrete candidate tested here is AFFINE SQUARE COMPLETION: use a cheap
explicit coarse positive threshold preconditioner and a true signed correction
to manufacture positive Gram features without computing a fine Gram root.
It fails uniformly near a critical eigenvalue. The failure is sharp for the
entire affine-feature family, including arbitrary numbers of stacked features
and coefficients chosen with knowledge of the shift and coarse distortion.

This does not rule out nonlinear positive features, row-dependent construction,
matrix factorizations, stochastic solvers with signed/nonconvex components,
or a general improvement to spectral product approximation. It is not an AMM
lower bound. No new total-runtime upper bound is claimed in Sections 1-5.

1. Exact square-completion identity
----------------------------------
For a true positive threshold matrix F, let F0>0 be a coarse matrix and write
E=F0-F, with E symmetric. The obvious first-order feature correction is

  X(E)=F0^(1/2)-(1/2)F0^(-1/2)E.

It can be applied using coarse small-core operations and a true E action.
The product is exactly

  X(E)^T X(E)=F+(1/4)E F0^(-1)E.                          (1)

The remainder is positive semidefinite, which is attractive for a Gram
representation. However its norm is bounded by ||E||^2/(4lambda_min(F0)),
not by ||E||^2 alone. For the threshold regime, ||E||=Theta(Ht) and the
coarse smallest eigenvalue is Theta(Ht), so the bound is Theta(Ht), not
Theta(H^2 t). The critical direction destroys quadratic error improvement.

The scalar example is exact:

  F=g>0, F0=g+H, E=H,
  X(E)^2=g+H^2/[4(g+H)].                                 (2)

If g/H tends to zero, its additive error approaches H/4 and its relative
error diverges like H/(4g). This is already compatible with fixed common
coarse Gram information: take true P=1, true Q=1, coarse P0=1,Q0=1-H,
and threshold t=1+g. The true complement is g and the coarse complement
is g+H. These are valid H-relative Gram proxies. Adding the usual O(Ht)
coarse inflation changes only constants in the same counterexample.

2. Square completion is optimal among first-order affine features
----------------------------------------------------------------
Consider an arbitrary stacked feature matrix X(E)=U+VE satisfying

  U^T U=F0, U^T V=V^T U=-I/2.

These conditions make its constant term and linear signed correction exact.
Projection/Cauchy--Schwarz gives

  V^T V >= V^T U(U^T U)^(-1)U^T V=F0^(-1)/4.

Therefore

  X(E)^T X(E)>=F+(1/4)E F0^(-1)E.                        (3)

Equality is attained by Section 1's choice. Adding affine feature rows or
rotating their feature space cannot improve this quadratic bias. Even for a
more general linear map L(E), with X(E)=U+L(E) and correct symmetric first
variation, any unit v satisfies

  ||L(E)v||^2 >= [v^T E v]^2/[4 v^T F0 v].                (4)

This follows by Cauchy--Schwarz and
2<Uv,L(E)v>=-v^T E v. The scalar obstruction thus survives arbitrary
linear matrix correction maps, not just right multiplication VE.

3. Sharp relative-approximation obstruction without derivative matching
----------------------------------------------------------------------
One might allow biased coarse features and settle for a constant-relative
Gram approximation. Even that fails for arbitrary small critical gaps.

Let f(x)=g+x on [0,H], g,H>0. Let phi(x) be ANY affine vector-valued
feature map, of arbitrary finite feature dimension, and q(x)=||phi(x)||^2.
Define kappa=(g+H)/g and r=sqrt(kappa). The exact smallest possible
uniform relative error is

  eta_star=(r-1)^2/(r^2+6r+1).                            (5)

In other words, if (1-eta)f(x)<=q(x)<=(1+eta)f(x) throughout the interval,
then eta>=eta_star, and equality is attainable by one scalar affine feature.

Proof: for x=tau H, 0<=tau<=1, affine interpolation and the triangle
inequality imply

  sqrt(q(x)) <= (1-tau)sqrt(q(0))+tau sqrt(q(H)).

The assumed relative bounds consequently force

  (1-eta)/(1+eta)
    <= [(1-tau)sqrt(g)+tau sqrt(g+H)]^2/(g+tau H).

The minimum of the right side occurs at tau=1/(r+1) and equals

  c=4r/(r+1)^2.

Thus eta>=(1-c)/(1+c), which is (5). Conversely, take q to be
2/(1+c) times the squared affine chord joining sqrt(g) and sqrt(g+H).
The ratio of that chord squared to f ranges exactly from c to 1, so this
choice attains the lower bound at the endpoints and at the minimizer.

As H/g grows, eta_star=1-O(sqrt(g/H)) tends to one. Thus no fixed
relative quality eta<1 is possible for a family of positive features affine
in an unresolved coarse signed correction. Replacing x by H-E gives
exactly a scalar coarse threshold F0-E with the same coarse data across
the whole interval. This obstruction permits coefficients to depend on H
and g; it is not an artifact of a badly chosen Taylor coefficient.

4. Randomizing the correction adds covariance bias
-------------------------------------------------
Suppose a random matrix Z is an unbiased estimator of E and use the same
square-completion formula. Its mean Gram is

  E[X(Z)^T X(Z)]
    =F+(1/4)E[Z^T F0^(-1)Z]
    =F+(1/4)E^T F0^(-1)E
       +(1/4)E[(Z-E)^T F0^(-1)(Z-E)].                    (6)

Independent samples or a cross estimator can remove a covariance term
algebraically, but a cross product need not be a positive sample Gram.
Removing this positive bias by subtraction loses the promised positive
feature representation.

Small deterministic residual does not imply small stochastic variance.
For example, let a covariance be P=I_m and sample an orthonormal-basis
row uniformly. The unbiased one-row Gram estimate is

  Y=m e_i e_i^T, i uniform in {1,...,m}.

Even when the coarse proxy is EXACTLY P0=P=I, the centered estimator
Z=Y-I has mean zero but

  E[Z^2]=(m-1)I.                                          (7)

For a minibatch of b independent rows, this second moment becomes
(m-1)I/b. Therefore the variance in (6) is not O(||P-P0||^2), nor
does it automatically decrease with the coarse embedding error. A genuine
row-residual/control-variate construction needs an independent variance
proof; merely subtracting the coarse mean is insufficient.

Equation (7) is ALSO why a signed stochastic route remains possible and
interesting: taking b=Theta(m) makes the matrix second moment constant at
O(m^2) raw row work, and signed SVRG does not require positive features.
That route is a distinct question and is not excluded by this audit.

5. Cost, original target, and primary-reference scope
----------------------------------------------------
An affine corrected feature action in (1) costs a true signed operator
action, O(I), plus coarse O(m^2) work. Generating a full-width batch must
charge the corresponding rectangular factor products. More importantly,
even a free construction cannot fix (2)-(5); the representation fails at
the level of relative Gram quality.

For N=m^(9/8), theta=1/m, the original construction target is
~I+m^(9/4+zeta), with I=m^(17/8). To reduce the main threshold's true
action factor sqrt(h/theta) to at most m^(1/8), one would need h of
order m^(-3/4) or smaller. An explicit coarse H of constant accuracy is
cheap, but its gap ratio H/h then diverges. The affine positive-feature
quality in (5) approaches one rather than a useful fixed distortion.
This quantifies why this proposed shortcut does not close the original gap.

6. Independent audit of a distinct SIGNED stochastic-pencil route
----------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Constant-distortion coordinates and probabilities

Use the main paper's constant-accuracy coarse whitening W,L, so

  G=W^T A^T A W, Q'=L B B^T L^T,
  I/(1+H)<=G<=I/(1-H), H<=1/16.

For a safe shift t>=lambda=||AB||^2, ||Q'||<2t. Write

  u_i=W^T a_i for original A rows a_i^T,
  v_j=L b_j for original B columns b_j,
  G=sum_i u_i u_i^T, Q'=sum_j v_j v_j^T.

Estimate all feature squared lengths simultaneously with O(log((n+p)/delta))
Gaussian JL coordinates, using A(W Omega) and B^T(L^T Omega). This costs
~O(I+m^2), not a transform of every row by W or L. Conditional on the JL
event, sampling tables can be constructed so

  p_i>=||u_i||^2/(4trG), q_j>=||v_j||^2/(4trQ').

The normalizing traces need not be computed: normalize the approximate row
lengths. Zero features get probability zero. The good simultaneous length
bounds imply positive probability for every nonzero feature and the displayed
domination. Condition on these preprocessing outcomes for the analysis.

6.2 Minibatch actions and the signed unbiased estimator

Let R=u_i u_i^T/p_i for i sampled from p. Then

  E R=G, E R^2=sum_i (||u_i||^2/p_i)u_i u_i^T
                    <=4tr(G)G.

For a batch of exactly m independent rows, Ghat=(1/m)sum R,

  E Ghat^2=(1-1/m)G^2+(1/m)E R^2<=20I.

The identical calculation for Q' gives E Qhat^2<=20t^2I because
trQ'<=2mt and ||Q'||<=2t. The crude constants cover rank-deficient
support dimension a<=m as well.

To apply Ghat to x, compute W x once, take m raw-row dot products and
weighted raw-row sums, then apply W^T once. This costs O(m^2). No
individual sampled feature u_i is formed by an O(m^2) transform.
Likewise Qhat costs O(m^2), with L^T before the raw-column batch and L
afterward. Indices and sampling tables cost only O(m) per batch.

Draw G0,G1,G2,Qhat independently and define

  Khat=tG0-(G1 Qhat G2+G2 Qhat G1)/2.

Use the SAME sampled G1,G2,Qhat in the reversed branch. This makes Khat
symmetric. Independence gives exact unbiasedness for

  K=tG-G Q' G.

Conditioning successively on the independent operators gives

  E||G1 Qhat G2 x||^2<=20*20*20*t^2||x||^2.

The symmetrized product has at most the same second-moment bound by the
inequality ||(a+b)/2||^2<=(||a||^2+||b||^2)/2. Combining tG0 gives

  E||Khat x||^2<=16040t^2||x||^2.                         (8)

Each Khat action uses a constant number of the preceding raw-batch actions,
so costs O(m^2), independent of n,p. A true K action still costs O(I).
All sampling remains unbiased conditional on the JL tables; preprocessing
randomness does not invalidate the independent minibatch conditioning.

6.3 Nonconvex-component SVRG and acceleration

Normalize K and Khat by t. Suppose K/t>=mu I for a certified mu>0.
Set V=16040. For f(x)=x^T(K/t)x/2-b^T x, quadratic energy gives

  E||Khat(x-x*)/t||^2
      <=V||x-x*||^2
      <=(2V/mu)[f(x)-f(x*)].                              (9)

This is precisely Garber2016 Theorem 9's nonconvex-component variance
inequality with S=V/mu. Its SVRG contraction takes O(V/mu^2) samples.
A fresh sampled Hessian must act on the SINGLE difference x-x_ref:

  grad_est=Khat(x-x_ref)/t+(K/t)x_ref-b.

Applying independently sampled Hessians to x and x_ref would lose the
control-variate cancellation. The full anchor gradient costs O(I), so a
constant expected contraction costs ~O(I+m^2/mu^2).

For a literal finite-sum interpretation, index components by all finite
minibatch tuples sigma and put

  psi_sigma(x)=Prob(sigma)[x^T(Khat_sigma/t)x/2-b^T x].

The probability cancels in the sampled gradient formula. The algorithm
never enumerates these tuples or multiplies their probabilities. The full
expectation gradient is evaluated directly through A,B in O(I).

Add proximal regularization rho||x-y||^2/2. For rho<=1 the component
second moment remains an absolute constant and strong convexity becomes
mu+rho, so the oracle contraction costs ~O(I+m^2/(mu+rho)^2).
Frostig2015 accelerated approximate proximal point (APPA), Theorem 1.1,
requires a strongly convex whole objective and a linearly converging primal
oracle; it does not require the sampled quadratics to be convex. Its stated
regularization condition is rho>=2mu; constant multiples do not affect the
optimization below. In that regime the total work is

  ~sqrt(rho/mu)[I+m^2/rho^2].

Choose rho of order m/sqrt(I), which is at most one because I>=2m^2.
If mu is within a constant factor of or exceeds that scale, the unaccelerated
method already costs O(I).
Thus the solver cost is

  ~O(I+I^(3/4)m^(1/2)/sqrt(mu)).                          (10)

This statement includes only logarithmic accuracy and failure dependence.
Repeated fresh SVRG contraction epochs give exponentially decreasing
expected relative function gap. Markov's inequality after O(log(1/(epsilon
delta))) epochs supplies a high-probability relative energy guarantee.
The same argument applies to randomized APPA's expected contraction or to
high-confidence inner oracles. No 1/delta work factor is necessary.

Additional primary APPA reference checked in this coordination:
R. Frostig et al., Un-regularizing: approximate proximal point and faster
stochastic algorithms for empirical risk minimization (2015).
https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.07512
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.07512

6.4 Scope and adaptive-rank caution

The positive-feature obstructions in Sections 1-5 do NOT exclude this signed
solver. In fact (8)-(10) use the independent minibatch second moments that
Section 4 shows must be accounted for, rather than assuming a small mean
residual implies small variance. These are standard SVRG/APPA tools with
a product-specific sampler and access-cost reduction. This potentially
stronger known-template corollary must be included in any novelty assessment
of the earlier multilevel spectral theorem.

For rank-r deflation, G probabilities can remain fixed, but reusing the
initial Q' feature-length probabilities requires an additional argument.
The latent residual projector R_i is generally oblique; Q'_i<=Q' controls
the sum of transformed feature outer products, not each feature length.
A safe implementation recomputes B^T(R_i^T L^T Omega) at each stage,
with fresh JL randomness conditional on the past, at ~O(I+m^2) cost.
Then the preceding sampler analysis applies stagewise. This adds only
~O(Ir) before the separately required spectral/deflation accounting.

7. PRODUCT-RANK-SENSITIVE COORDINATES: INDEPENDENT AUDIT
-------------------------------------------------------
The proposed reduction to k=rank(AB) coordinates is valid. It requires
carefully distinguishing product representation from a representation of
the entire A factor, and it admits a particularly clean compressed deflation
implementation. This addendum also checks the runtime cap on bad embedding
outcomes and an exact-compression comparison that must accompany the claim.

7.1 Coordinate construction and exact identity on the good event

Let the original coarse A-support dimension be a, W be m x a and
L=W^dagger, so LW=I_a. Define

  Q'=L B B^T L^T, Q0=L Q_H L^T.

On the common OSE event, Q0 and Q' have the same kernel and range.
Moreover range(Q')=range(LB), and AW is injective on R^a because W's
columns lie in range(A^T). Thus

  k=rank(Q0)=rank(LB)=rank(AB).

Construct an orthonormal U spanning range(Q0), and put

  Wstar=WU (m x k), Lstar=U^T L (k x m),
  Zstar=A Wstar, Bbar=Lstar B.

Then Lstar Wstar=I_k and

  M=AW LB=AW U U^T LB=Zstar Bbar.                         (11)

For every right projection Pi, LB Pi remains in range(U). Consequently
M Pi=Zstar Bbar Pi exactly, including all adaptive orthogonal deflations.
There is no need for U to change after a deflation.

Lstar need not equal Wstar^dagger. Also A=AWstar Lstar is generally
FALSE. Neither identity is required by the stochastic solver or the
spectral wrapper: they use only (11), the positive mass

  Gstar=Zstar^T Zstar=U^T G U,
  I/(1+H)<=Gstar<=I/(1-H),

and Qstar=Bbar Bbar^T. The spectral relation with the physical product
uses Gstar^(1/2)Qstar Gstar^(1/2), so it remains exact.
The coarse mass is exactly I since Wstar^T P_H Wstar=I_k, and its
coarse B-side proxy is Q0star=U^T Q0 U=Lstar Q_H Lstar^T.

For an exact arithmetic construction of U, select independent columns C
of Q0, form C^T C=R^T R by Cholesky, and set U=CR^(-1). Fast rank
selection, multiplication, Cholesky and the formation of Wstar,Lstar cost
~O(m^omega) in the same model as the main paper. No exact eigenvalue or
SVD oracle is required for the support basis.

7.2 Deflation entirely in k-dimensional latent coordinates

Maintain V_i=Bbar^T C_i with orthonormal columns, D_i=Bbar V_i=Qstar C_i,
and S_i=I_k-D_i C_i^T. Then

  C_i^T Qstar C_i=I,
  Bbar_i=Bbar(I-V_i V_i^T)=S_i Bbar,
  Qstar_i=S_i Qstar S_i^T=Qstar-D_i D_i^T,
  M_i=Zstar Bbar_i.

For a physical cPCA witness z_i, put s_i=z_i^T M_i M_i^T z_i and

  c=S_i^T Zstar^T z_i/sqrt(s_i),
  v=Bbar^T c, d=Qstar c.

The new v is unit and orthogonal to V_i; hence

  S_{i+1}=S_i-dc^T=(I-dc^T)S_i.

The coarse proxy updates as

  Q0star_{i+1}=(I-dc^T)Q0star_i(I-cd^T),

at O(k^2) cost. Initial Loewner bounds imply the corresponding current
relative bounds by congruence for every adaptive S_i. This argument does
not invoke Lstar=Wstar^dagger or A=AWstar Lstar. If one instead retains
the original m-dimensional R_i updates, their low-rank actions cost O(mi)
with i<=k; those are O(mk) as well, while any m^2 core update is <=I.
The compressed implementation is simpler and avoids that extra work.

7.3 k-row sampling, costs and confidence

The features are now u_j=Wstar^T a_j and v_j=Lstar b_j, both k-vectors.
The current B-side features are S_i Lstar b_j. Their Gram traces are
bounded by k times their operator norms, so the Section 6 moment proof
works with batch size exactly k, not m. With the same constants,

  E Ghat^2<=20I_k, E Qhat_i^2<=20t^2 I_k,
  E Khat_i^2<=16040t^2 I_k.

A sampled action performs only two skinny Wstar/Lstar transforms, a
batch of k raw rows or columns of length m, and O(k^2) S_i actions.
Its cost is O(mk). JL length tables are obtained with A(Wstar Omega)
and B^T(Lstar^T S_i^T Omega); they cost ~O(I+mk). Use fresh B-side
JL randomness conditional on the stage history. Gstar probabilities may
stay fixed. The exact full gradient continues to cost O(I).

The shifted-system SVRG/APPA bound becomes

  ~O(I+I^(3/4)(mk)^(1/4)/sqrt(mu)).                      (12)

The moment and cost proofs are dimension-sensitive; this is not a change
of notation that still samples m rows or transforms each sampled row.

7.4 Combination with the stronger spectral certificate

Sections 1-2 and 5 of docs/block-threshold-audit.txt were read as part of
this audit. Their moving-tail cPCA-to-Nystrom argument and generalized
AppxPCA implementation transfer to Gstar without alteration. They need
the product representation M_i=Zstar Bbar_i and the constant-conditioned
mass, not the missing whole-A identity. The physical lift is still
z=Zstar x/sqrt(x^T Gstar x), and it has zero component outside range(M).

In particular, cPCA leakage epsilon below (1-gamma)lambda_1 implies

  ||M(I-V_ell V_ell^T)||^2
    <=sigma_{ell+1}(M)^2/[(1-gamma)(1-epsilon)^ell].

Choosing gamma=theta/4 and epsilon=theta/(4ell) controls the denominator;
the inverse solver needs gap mu=Theta(theta), while epsilon enters only
logarithmically. Thus there is NO sqrt(ell) iteration loss once this
stronger cPCA wrapper is used. The older Rayleigh-only Nystrom chain would
retain that loss and cannot justify the improved statement by itself.

With ell=min(r,k), the resulting fixed-width guarantee has bound

  ~O(I(1+ell)+m^omega
       +ell I^(3/4)(mk)^(1/4)/sqrt(theta)
       +np+T_MM(n,ell,p)),                               (13)

with the usual rank-r relative spectral error, no eigengap or factor-energy
promise, and the same failure allocation as the constituent audited results.
If r>=k, at most k positive residual stages recover M exactly. Include
the np writes even when ell=0. This is a composition of separately audited
sampler, shifted-solver, cPCA and deflation arguments; global novelty remains
a separate question.

7.5 All-outcomes cap by the TRUE product rank

The implementation computes khat=rank(Q0), which need not equal k on bad
embedding outcomes. Nevertheless khat<=rank(AB) ALWAYS, independent of
the OSE event. Indeed P_H=A^T S^T S A has range contained in range(P),
so W's columns, and hence L's rows, lie in range(P). Therefore

  rank(Q0)=rank(L B T^T)
        <=rank(LB)<=rank(PB)=rank(AB).                    (14)

The final equality uses that A is injective on range(P). Use khat for all
computed batch dimensions and cap the stage count at min(r,khat). On the
good event khat=k and (11)-(13) give the claimed approximation. On bad
events approximation can fail, but the batch/stage dimensions still do not
exceed the true k, so the k-sensitive work bound is not merely a successful-
outcome bound. As in the parent algorithm, all solver loops and normalization
failure exits must have the stipulated finite caps; this argument alone does
not justify an unbounded retry-until-success implementation.

7.6 Exact skinny-factor comparison

The same coordinate reduction gives a strong competing route: materialize
the EXACT factors Zstar=A Wstar and Bbar=Lstar B at cost

  T_MM(n,m,k)+T_MM(k,m,p).

Then compute reduced k-dimensional Grams, a small exact spectral factorization,
and retained-direction lifts. The relevant reduced Gram cost is
T_MM(k,n,k)+T_MM(k,p,k), plus k^omega and output/lifting. Consequently
one must not compare (13) only to full-m Gram formation or scalar Krylov.

For example, k<=sqrt(m) lets current dual-exponent bounds materialize both
skinny factors at input-time exponents. Their Grams even cost <=I with
classical multiplication because (n+p)k^2<=(n+p)m. Exact compression can
already meet the original high-accuracy construction target on this class;
no stochastic novelty claim is justified merely from that specialization.

Use the full primary rectangular bound, not just square blocking:
Amanov--van den Berg--Zuiddam, arXiv:2610.30054, Theorem 1.2,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30054
For sorted exponents a>=b>=c, the bound is a+b if c(a+b)<=ab;
otherwise it is (a+b+c)/2+(ab/c+ac/b+bc/a)/4.

An illustrative regime where (13) improves these named materialization
bounds is

  N=m^(59/50), k=m^(24/25), theta=m^(-469/1000).

Its stochastic construction exponent is 4719/2000=2.3595<2.36, giving
O(N^2) total explicit output with suitable fixed exponent slack. The exact
skinny-factor materialization bound is 16736281/7080000~=2.363881, and
its reduced Gram exponent is only 6889/2950~=2.335254, so materialization
dominates that named route. Full-rank stochastic construction has exponent
2.3695 and full Gram has exponent about 2.391864. These are comparisons
between audited upper bounds, not lower bounds on exact compression or a
certified global state-of-the-art claim.
