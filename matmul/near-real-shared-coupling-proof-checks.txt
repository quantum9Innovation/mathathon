Concrete algorithmic improvement
--------------------------------
In supported raw coordinates S=ZKZ*, ||Z*Z-I||<=1/8, supply a certified
coarse split for B=tK^-1 with spec(B_QQ) outside [1/2,2] and
||B_PQ||<=1/16. Let k be the selected dimension. For q supplied poles
t+i*y_j and their supplied RHS columns, put mu=max|w_j|/|y_j| and
J,L=O(log(2mu/epsilon)). The raw batch width is
  R=q+kJ,
rather than the old q(k+1). For R<=m, total work is

  C_split + ~O(m^omega) + O(Nm*R^(omega-2)*L)
    + O(mk^2 J+qk^2 J+mkq J+qk^omega).

Every normalized action has error <=epsilon||h_j||. Per-column bounds
give a Frobenius-relative block bound; spectral action on an orthonormal
q-column basis needs epsilon/sqrt(q), adding only logarithms.

The certified-buffered-coarse-split.txt theorem, separately reviewed at
SHA256 6e114f90aba4d736ee17cb97b7c8de2c01232fbf8b0c6bb2b1963e1b6b579ef9,
now supplies that split at expected m^(omega+o(1)) work, with k bounded
by the genuine coarse count in [t/3,3t]. This is a separate composition;
the shared-series proof does not hide an exact eigensystem oracle.

Proof checks that matter
-----------------------
On the OUTER analytic disk |u|<=1/3, u=(w-t)/t, the safe coarse inverse
has norm <=3, contraction <=3/8, and true safe inverse norm <=24/5.
The two affine offblocks Gamma(u),Lambda(u) have norm <=1/4. At complex
u, Lambda(u) is NOT Gamma(u)*. Their analytic coefficient families are
kept separate throughout.

Formal-series Richardson computes exactly the first J coefficients of
R_L(u)Lambda(u). Cauchy's bound on the outer disk controls its evaluation
on |u|<=1/4. Coupling error <=zeta*delta and independently obtained RHS
error <=delta||b|| suffice. The proof correctly reuses the SAME approximate
coupling in the small Schur core and the final Q reconstruction; it does
not require both approximations to arise from one common approximate
inverse. The supported error constant is 279/32<9; physical error is
<11mu^2*delta. The chosen delta=epsilon/(100mu^2) leaves slack.

One coarse Frobenius preparation handles both polynomial coefficients
and pointwise shifted columns. Companion series recurrences and scalar
convolutions cost ~O(mkJ), while dense basis maps batch all kJ coefficient
columns. This avoids an uncharged sequence of J dense matrix-vector
actions. Actual raw iterations batch the q RHS and kJ coefficient
columns already available at each step; no later adaptive query is
retroactively included.

Schur coefficient products are precomputed ONCE. Their degree-J terminal
coefficient is retained, and q small cores are evaluated by Horner.
Final coupling reconstruction uses shared coefficient matrices and q
small vectors. Consequently there is NO hidden O(qmk^2) preparation.
Far poles use the ordinary fixed-cone resolver with one additional
shared coarse preparation and contraction <3/5.

At q,k<=~sqrt(m), N=m^(9/8) and the research direction's feasible
omega=9/4 convention, postprocessing is at most ~m^2 and raw/core
work fits m^(9/4+o(1)). This proves a larger-band RESOLVER regime;
it does not prove a new multiplication exponent or final LRA theorem.
