NONSYMMETRIC SQUARE-MATRIX THEOREM: FULL PROOF
----------------------------------------------
Let K be ANY n-by-n matrix over the fixed odd base field Fp, not
necessarily symmetric. Put k=rank K and h=n-k. Define the 2n-by-2n
symmetric dilation

  S = [ 0    K  ].
      [ K^T  0  ]

For x,y in Fp^n,

  S(x,y) = (K y, K^T x).

The two output coordinate blocks are independent. Thus

  im S = im K direct-sum im K^T,
  rank S = rank K + rank K^T = 2k,
  ker S = ker K^T direct-sum ker K,
  dim ker S = 2h.                                       (A)

This uses ordinary field rank and has no diagonalizability or analytic
gap assumption. S has 2 nnz(K) nonzero entries.

Apply the existing full symmetric-kernel algorithm to S. Its expected
bit cost is

  soft-O((2n)(2 nnz(K)+2n)(2h+1))
      =soft-O(n(nnz(K)+n)(h+1)).                          (B)

For bounded row/column sparsity, this is soft-O(n^2(h+1)). Even if K
has a large nonsymmetric Jordan structure, the argument applies to
symmetric S; it does not silently apply the symmetric preconditioner
theorem directly to K.

Let V be the returned complete base-field basis of ker S. Project
each column to its LAST n coordinates, obtaining at most 2h vectors.
Every projected vector lies in ker K. Conversely, for every y in
ker K, (0,y) belongs to ker S and is a linear combination of V; its
last-coordinate projection expresses y as a combination of the
projected columns. Those columns therefore span all of ker K.

Select h independent projected vectors by incremental elimination,
or take their RREF as row vectors and transpose the selected canonical
rows back into columns. This costs O(n h^2), at most O(n^2(h+1)),
and O(nh) base-field storage. Normalize on h pivot rows J to obtain
R with

  K R=0,       R[J,:]=I_h.                               (C)

All of these operations are over the BASE field, after the already-
certified descent for S. Formula (B) plus this selection gives a full
right-kernel basis on EVERY fixed K in the asserted expected cost.
The full rank-zero and rank-n cases are included.

A COMPACT DETERMINISTIC RIGHT-KERNEL CERTIFICATE
------------------------------------------------
The certificate need retain only:

  * the extension field and D,u,v data for S;
  * the shifted finite-sequence length ell for S;
  * the canonical right-kernel basis R and its pivot rows J.

It need NOT retain the complete S kernel basis or a left-kernel basis.
A verifier constructs S from K, validates the extension and nonzero
scalings, recomputes the 2(2n) shifted scalar terms for S, runs exact
BM to recompute ell, and requires

  ell=2(n-h),       K R=0,       R[J,:]=I_h.               (D)

The unconditional shifted lower bound gives rank S>=ell. By (A),
rank K>=ell/2=n-h. The h independent columns in (C) give rank K<=n-h.
These inequalities agree and certify the ENTIRE right kernel, exactly.
An omitted basis column plus a dimension-adjusted false rank cannot
pass the recomputed BM test. The full accepted S certificate always
has even ell=2k, so requiring even ell and the equality in (D) loses
no successful full-kernel output.

Verification takes soft-O(n(nnz(K)+n)) bits: constructing/applying S
has the same sparse order, recomputing its sequence is quadratic,
checking KR costs O(nnz(K) h)<=O(n nnz(K)), and checking the pivot
identity costs O(h^2)<=O(n^2). Certificate size is
O_p(nh+n log n) bits. A version of this independent verifier is
implemented and tested in fresh_run_check.py as verify_right().

WHY ONLY p^h MAY BE ENUMERATED
--------------------------------
After obtaining R, enumerate ONLY its p^h base-field coefficient vectors
with the odometer update. This lists ker K in O_p(n p^h) bit operations
and permits any desired exact integer/label checks on the right vectors.
There is no reason to enumerate the p^(2h) vectors of ker S.

If an input ensemble has the one-sided moment

  E p^h <= C,

then h+1<=p^h controls the expectation in (B), and
E[O(n p^h)] controls right-kernel enumeration. Hence the full
nonsymmetric right-kernel/listing pipeline has unconditional expected
soft-O(n^2) bitwork in the sparse square case using ONLY that moment.
No bound on E p^(2h) is needed.

Such a squared moment cannot be inferred from the stated input.
For example, let h=n with probability p^(-n) and h=0 otherwise.
Then E p^h<2, while E p^(2h) is of order p^n. Enumerating the entire
symmetric-dilation kernel would invalidate the near-quadratic averaging
argument for this valid moment profile. Projection before enumeration
is therefore an essential part of the reduction.

CLAIM BOUNDARIES
-----------------
The nonsymmetric extension above is a proved algorithmic reduction,
not a new random-matrix moment theorem. It is for square K, so left
and right coranks agree. For a rectangular K, the symmetric dilation
is still valid, but its total corank can be much larger than the right
corank; the same right-moment-only expected-cost statement would need
an additional argument. No such rectangular statement is claimed.
