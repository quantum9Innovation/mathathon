1. Fixed instance and exact output condition
------------------------------------------
The diagonal matrix is fixed before the Gaussian seed. Its unique head
is 1+4/m, its second eigenvalue is one, and all other eigenvalues are
strictly between 1-a and one. For all sufficiently large m its condition
number is bounded by an absolute constant.

For any nonsymmetric rank-one output with column line span(u), projection
of the error proves ||(I-P_u)S||<=1+1/m. The quadratic form of S^2-t^2 I
on u-perp has just one positive direction. Eliminating that direction
and maximizing the remaining weighted linear functional gives exactly
condition (4). The complex case uses the Hermitian projection and the
same weighted Cauchy--Schwarz calculation. Omitting the eigenvalue-one
coordinate from its nonnegative right side is legitimate. The constants
d_h>=6/m and t+lambda_i<=3 give the asserted factor 4/(am) in (6).

2. Weighted Chebyshev and finite-grid identities
----------------------------------------------
The basis (7) is genuinely orthonormal for dnu/(cosh(eta)-x).
The Poisson moments are z^j/sinh(eta). They make T_j-zT_(j-1)
orthogonal to every lower degree, with squared norm z. The j=1
endpoint and the separate constant normalization also check directly.

At cosh(zeta), eta<=zeta<=3eta, the second exponential summand in
the displayed Chebyshev difference is nonpositive. The first gives
|T_j-zT_(j-1)|<=2eta exp(j*zeta). Summing squares, with z>=exp(-1),
1-exp(-2zeta)>=zeta/2 and sinh(eta)<=2eta, gives a coefficient below
24eta exp(2D*zeta), so the stated 32 bound has slack.

The Gauss-grid reciprocal sum is T_n'(A)/(n*T_n(A)), which is exactly
tanh(n*eta)/sinh(eta). Polynomial division of a degree-at-most-2D
product by A-x leaves a quotient of degree at most 2D-1, within the
Gauss exactness range when D<=n. The discrete Gram is therefore exactly
I-delta_n*phi(A)phi(A)^T. The discrepancy norm is bounded by
64 exp(-2(n-D)eta), giving (12) for the chosen degree and large m.

The pointwise leverage bound (13) is also valid. For a nonconstant
basis element, use |cos(ju)-cos((j-1)u)|<=2 sin(u/2), followed by
2(a+2s)^2/(a^2+4z*s^2)<=4/z. Its bound is below 12 per element.
The constant ratio is coth(eta/2)<=3/eta for 0<eta<=1.

3. Gaussian block and the simultaneous polynomial quantifier
-----------------------------------------------------------
For Z=||g||^2, E[Z*1_(Z>16b)]<=(b+2)/16. Rotational symmetry thus
gives the truncated covariance coefficient mu_b>=13/16. The PSD
Kronecker summands V_i have norm at most R_0=16bL_0, expectation sum
between (13n/32)I and nI, and total centered variance at most R_0*n I.
The centered norms are at most R_0, rather than requiring twice that:
both V_i and E V_i lie in the order interval [0,R_0 I].

The primary independent matrix Bernstein statement was checked directly
in the full Mackey--Jordan--Chen--Farrell--Tropp2014 PDF, Corollary5.2,
printed p922:
  https://lmackey.github.io/papers/matstein-aop14.pdf
Its hypotheses are independent centered Hermitian summands with a uniform
norm bound, precisely what is used here. At t=n/8 its denominator is
3R_0*n+2R_0*(n/8)=52bL_0*n. The exponent is therefore n/(3328bL_0),
as asserted in (14). The bad lower-Gram event implies a deviation of at
least 5n/32, so using the smaller deviation n/8 is safe.

The matrix lower bound implies (15) simultaneously for every coefficient
vector, including complex coefficient vectors and coefficients chosen
after observing the entire seed. It is not a fixed-polynomial estimate
incorrectly applied to adaptively fitted polynomials. Removing the PSD
truncation only enlarges the lower Gram.

4. Probability exponents and the block size range
------------------------------------------------
The head evaluation inequality is deterministic in all polynomial
coefficients. Combining the bulk Gram lower with the exact necessary
condition forces ||g_h||^2>=1/(2aK_D). For the stated kappa_c,
2D*zeta<=(2/25)log m. Since eta<=2/sqrt(am), this gives exactly
m^(21/50)/(128sqrt(a)) as the chi-square threshold.

The head row has b<=m^(1/8) independent Gaussian entries. Its mgf at
1/4 is 2^(b/2), so the threshold event has probability at most
exp(-gamma_c*m^(21/50)) for large m. The bulk term has dimension
b(D+1)=O_c(m^(5/8)log m) and exponent at least a positive multiple
of m^(3/8)/log m. Both are bounded by C_c exp(-gamma'_c*m^(1/3))
after changing constants and the threshold. This proves the claimed
small success probability for the ENTIRE random Krylov subspace.
The radial normalization observation for one start is correct because
the Krylov span is invariant under nonzero scaling of that column.

5. The cone-action consequence has the advertised narrow model
-------------------------------------------------------------
The identity F_z(S)=[I-a_z(S-I)]^-1 is exact, with a_z=z/(1-z).
The cone gives |a_z|<=1/c and the fixed spectrum gives ||S-I||<=c/4.
The geometric polynomial's operator remainder is therefore at most
(4/3)*4^(-L-1), uniformly over every admissible pole. These are coherent
linear operator approximations for all right-hand sides, but they need
not be the resolvents of one common exact backward matrix.

Returning those polynomials on the supplied start block keeps every
feature in the same degree-D Krylov span, even with adaptive poles,
repetition, nonlinear coefficient fitting or complex conjugate features.
For fixed polynomial precision m^-K their degree is O_K(log m), below
the excluded degree. More generally the error allowance at degree D
already permits this bad response family, proving the stated necessary
precision logarithm ONLY for the uniformly robust fixed-start span model.

New independent right-hand sides, composition on previous responses,
exact extra information, common-backward-resolvent promises, and raw
factor information are not covered. The source explicitly preserves
each of these limitations. No arithmetic lower bound is inferred.
