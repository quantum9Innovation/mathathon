PRIMARY BLACK-BOX EXACT-LINEAR-ALGEBRA AUDIT
15 November 2026

Conclusion
==========

A certified ~O(n^2)-bit recovery algorithm is available, conditional on the real planted eigenvalue being simple. The existing qualitative RSBM simplicity theorem then supplies overall success 1-o(1); no rate for that graph event is inferred. The black-box ingredients are classical. Certification does not require n right-hand sides or cubic elimination.

The clean certificate is: generate the actual scalar sequence u^T T^i v for i=0,...,2m-1, run Berlekamp--Massey, and accept nonsingularity only when the resulting MINIMUM polynomial has degree m and nonzero constant term. Here T is an m by m matrix obtained from the desired minor by explicitly invertible preconditioners. This is deterministic certification, not a Freivalds-style probabilistic verification.

WHY THE DEGREE-m TEST IS A CERTIFICATE
====================================

For any actual m by m T over a field, the infinite sequence s_i=u^T T^i v has minimum generating polynomial f_s dividing the minimum polynomial of T, hence dividing chi_T. Its degree is at most m. The first 2m terms determine f_s exactly by the standard linearly generated sequence/Berlekamp--Massey theorem used in both sources.

If the recovered polynomial f has degree m, divisibility and degree imply f=chi_T. If additionally f(0) is nonzero, T is nonsingular. This implication is deterministic for every u,v and every preconditioner, including unlucky choices. A verifier may recompute the 2m scalar terms and BM, using the same O(m) matrix calls and O(m^2) extra field work. No dense Krylov matrix inversion is needed.

One may equivalently reason through the Hankel matrix H=(s_{i+j})_{0<=i,j<m}. Full scalar minimum degree means the reachability and observability matrices both have rank m, so H is nonsingular. This observation is not an instruction to compute det(H) by cubic elimination.

IMPORTANT: an arbitrary degree-m polynomial satisfying f(T)v=0, even with f(0) nonzero, is NOT a certificate of invertibility. For example T=diag(0,1), v=e_2, f=(x-1)^2 has those properties while T is singular. The polynomial must be the actual scalar MINIMUM polynomial and have full degree, or an equivalent cyclicity certificate must be supplied.

EXPLICIT CERTIFIED TRIAL OVER F_p
===============================

Let G be an m by m matrix given by a sparse matvec black box. Let p be an odd prime and S=F_p^*, so |S|=p-1. Draw independently:

  * unit upper and lower triangular Toeplitz U,L, with their 2m-2 free entries uniform in S;
  * X=diag(x_1,...,x_m), with x_i uniform in S;
  * u,v uniform in F_p^m.

Set T=U G L X. Every multiplier is deterministically invertible. Compute the first 2m terms s_i=u^T T^i v. Run BM. If deg(f)<m or f(0)=0, return FAIL for this trial. If deg(f)=m and f(0) is nonzero, accept G as nonsingular. This conclusion has zero probability of being incorrect.

Success analysis, assuming G is nonsingular:

  (a) KS Theorem 2 gives a leading-minor-profile failure probability at most m(m+1)/(p-1).
  (b) Conditional on that profile, Wiedemann's Section V lemma says that disc(chi_{UGLX}) is a nonzero polynomial in the diagonal variables. Its total degree is at most m(m-1). Schwartz--Zippel over S bounds squarefreeness failure by m(m-1)/(p-1).
  (c) A squarefree characteristic polynomial makes T nonderogatory. Random v and then random u preserve its degree-m minimum polynomial with total failure at most 2m/p, by KS Lemma 1 and its column analogue.

Consequently the success probability of this certified trial is at least

  1 - 2m^2/(p-1) - 2m/p >= 1 - 4m^2/(p-1).

This is a conservative derived bound, not a quoted theorem. No claim is made that diagonal scaling alone cyclicizes every nonsingular matrix: the preceding generic-leading-minor preconditioner is necessary for this particular proof.

Each T matvec calls G once, adds O(m) diagonal work, and performs two Toeplitz matvecs. The source implements these by polynomial multiplication in O(m log m loglog m) field operations. Thus the sequence and BM together use O(m) calls to G and O(m^2 log m loglog m) additional field operations.

SOLVING ONE RIGHT-HAND SIDE AFTER CERTIFICATION
=============================================

For G z=b, first solve T y=U b and return z=L X y. Since the accepted f=chi_T and f(0) is nonzero, Cayley--Hamilton gives

  y = -f(0)^-1 sum_{i=1}^m f_i T^{i-1} (U b),
  f(x)=f(0)+sum_{i=1}^m f_i x^i.

Evaluate this by Horner in at most m-1 further T applications. The total number of calls to the original sparse G is below 3m, up to harmless endpoint conventions. An explicit residual check is cheap and may be included. There is no n-right-hand-side solve.

APPLICATION TO THE SIMPLE PLANTED RSBM EIGENVALUE
===============================================

Let A have n=2N vertices, fixed total degree D=d_1+d_2, planted lambda=d_1-d_2, and M=A-lambda I. Form the principal (n-1) by (n-1) minor G obtained by deleting row and column 1. If the real kernel of M is exactly span(sigma), with every sigma_i in {+1,-1}, then G is nonsingular over Q. Indeed M is symmetric of rank n-1, and adj(M)=c sigma sigma^T with c nonzero; its (1,1) cofactor is nonzero.

Use a prime p for which det(G) is nonzero modulo p, certify that fact by the trial above, and solve

  G x_rest = -M_{rest,1},   x_1=1.

On this real-simplicity event, the solution is exactly sigma/sigma_1 modulo p. Decode residues 1 and p-1 as signs. Check over the INTEGERS that the resulting sign vector has balanced blocks and satisfies A sigma=lambda sigma. Reject if either test fails.

Acceptance is unconditional certification: a nonzero mod-p minor implies a nonzero integer minor, hence rank_Q(M)>=n-1; the verified integer kernel vector implies rank_Q(M)<=n-1. Therefore the real kernel is one-dimensional, the verified sign vector spans it, and the admissible partition is unique up to global exchange. This also prevents an accidental finite-field eigenvector from being mistaken for an integer sign vector.

POLYNOMIAL-SIZE PRIME AND FULL BIT CHARGING
=========================================

For fixed degree, each row of G has Euclidean norm at most

  R=sqrt(D+lambda^2),

so Delta=det(G), if nonzero, satisfies |Delta|<=R^m. Among primes in [P,2P], the number dividing Delta is at most m log(R)/log(P). Rosser--Schoenfeld's cited bound gives at least 3P/(5 log P) primes in that interval. Thus a uniformly chosen prime from the interval is bad with probability at most

  5m log(R)/(3P).

Take, for example,

  P=ceil(100 n^2 (1+ceil(log_2(max(2,R))))).

This is Theta_{D,lambda}(n^2), ensures p has O(log n) bits, makes the bad-prime probability O(1/n), and makes the preconditioning/projection failure probability below a fixed small constant. Finite exceptional small n can be handled by direct exact elimination.

A fully explicit prime sampler is to sieve through 2P once and list primes in [P,2P], then choose uniformly from that list. Sieve cost is ~O(P)=~O(n^2) bit operations and O(P) bits plus the prime list; this quadratic-space choice is acceptable if only the requested time bound is imposed. It removes any reliance on a primality-testing oracle or uncharged prime search. Standard binary arithmetic on O(log n)-bit residues costs polylog(n) bits per field operation, including inversion, so each trial and its one-right-hand-side solve cost ~O(n^2) bit operations in fixed degree.

Repeat the entire certified trial with fresh prime and preconditioner a bounded O(log(1/epsilon)) number of times. Conditional on det(G) nonzero, each trial has success probability greater than 1/2 for all sufficiently large n, so the chance of returning FAIL is at most epsilon. The time is ~O(n^2 log(1/epsilon)); for epsilon=n^{-c} this is ~O(n^2). Every accepted output is correct.

DISTINGUISH GRAPH FAILURE FROM ALGORITHMIC FAILURE
================================================

If det(G)=0 over Q, no prime/preconditioner can pass the nonsingularity certificate. Therefore an uncapped retry-until-success loop is NOT an unconditional expected-time Las Vegas algorithm on the entire random-graph distribution: the exceptional graph event has positive probability. Use the bounded-attempt correct-or-FAIL algorithm, or state expected time only conditional on real simplicity.

The baseline kernel theorem's fixed-prime mixing and qualitative contiguity establish real simplicity with probability 1-o(1). The large prime p used in this algorithm is justified separately by the deterministic Hadamard/bad-divisor argument above. It is not substituted into the fixed-prime mixing theorem. Overall recovery probability is 1-o(1)-epsilon, and no polynomial rate for the first o(1) term is claimed.

Novelty boundary: this is an explicit specialization and certification/bit-complexity assembly of classical Wiedemann and preconditioning results. It is not a new sparse exact linear algebra algorithm. Any research novelty must come from the independently justified RSBM simplicity theorem or a genuinely new extension of its parameter range.
