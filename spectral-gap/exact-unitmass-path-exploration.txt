Exact finite unit-mass path realization: a verified obstruction
==============================================================
Research worker: spectral_audit; November 15, 2026.
Status: complete counterexample proof, pending independent review of this file.
The previously frozen closure manuscript is unchanged.

Question
--------
Can every simple positive determinant-admissible relative spectrum be realized
EXACTLY by a single edge increase on a finite unit-mass weighted path, allowing
additional correction eigenvalues that are EXACTLY zero?

Answer: NO. Closure is essential even for a target having just two distinct
positive correction eigenvalues. The obstruction below holds at alpha=1/2,
leverage tau=1. It allows arbitrary finite path length and arbitrary strictly
positive path conductances. It is not an obstruction to the already proved
approximation theorem, which permits additional small positive eigenvalues.

Explicit excluded target
------------------------
The target positive relative eigenvalues are

 q1=5/4,       q2=4 sqrt(2)/5,

with all remaining relative eigenvalues equal to one. They are distinct and
strictly greater than one, and q1*q2=sqrt(2)=(1+tau)^alpha.
Equivalently the correction spectrum has exactly two positive entries
1/4 and 4 sqrt(2)/5-1, with all remaining entries zero.
This target cannot arise from one edge increase on ANY finite ordinary
unit-mass weighted path at these alpha,tau.

1. Rank of a fractional correction equals active cyclic dimension
----------------------------------------------------------------
On the common positive support, set A=L|_(1-perp), u=sqrt(Delta w)b,
where b=e_i-e_(i+1), and 0<alpha<1. The Stieltjes rank-one identity is

 B^alpha-A^alpha
  =c_alpha int_0^infty t^alpha (A+t)^-1 u u^T (A+t)^-1
                         /(1+u^T(A+t)^-1u) dt.

For a real x, its quadratic form is zero iff
u^T(A+t)^-1 x=0 for almost every t>0. This scalar function is analytic,
so it vanishes for all t>0. Expanding at infinity gives
u^T A^k x=0 for every k>=0. Hence the kernel of the difference is the
orthogonal complement of K=span{u,Au,A^2u,...}. Conversely each vector
orthogonal to K clearly lies in the kernel. Since K is invariant under
A and all its powers, conjugation by A^(-alpha/2) preserves this kernel.
Thus the relative correction has rank exactly dim K.

In the full Laplacian coordinates, the same K is generated by
b,Lb,L^2b,... and lies in 1-perp. Therefore an EXACT target with exactly
two positive correction eigenvalues forces dim K=2. Small nonzero
extra eigenvalues do not suffice for exact realization.

2. All paths whose edited edge has active dimension two
-------------------------------------------------------
If the farthest endpoint of the path is at distance r from the set of
edited-edge endpoints, then b,Lb,...,L^r b are linearly independent.
Indeed L^k b has zero coordinates beyond graph distance k from those
endpoints; at a vertex at distance k on either arm its coefficient is
the nonzero product (with a sign) of the unique sequence of k edge
weights. For the farthest arm this yields one new nonzero coordinate
at every step. Hence dim K>=r+1.

Consequently dim K=2 permits only paths on at most four vertices.
A two-vertex path has active dimension one and is excluded. On three
vertices the edited edge is an endpoint edge and the active dimension
is exactly two. On four vertices the edited edge must be the middle edge.
Write its edge weights as (a,m,c), a,m,c>0, and b=(0,1,-1,0)^T. Then

 Lb=(-a,a+2m,-c-2m,c)^T.

If the active dimension is two, L^2b=s Lb+p b for real s,p.
The first and fourth coordinates give, respectively,

 s=2(a+m),       s=2(c+m),

so a=c. Conversely a=c makes the antisymmetric subspace invariant;
its dimension is two and contains b. Hence this condition is also
sufficient. This completes the classification for EVERY finite path.

3. The two canonical matrix families
------------------------------------
Overall common scaling does not change relative spectra. In a suitable
orthonormal basis of the active space the two families reduce to

 A=[[1+kappa*x,-sqrt(kappa)*x],[-sqrt(kappa)*x,x]],
 B=A+tau*e1 e1^T,       x>0,

where kappa=1/3 for a three-vertex path, and kappa=1 for a four-vertex
path with equal outer weights. In both cases det A=x and
(A^-1)11=1, so the leverage is exactly tau.

Three vertices: set the edited edge weight to a and the other weight
to c. In the basis whose first vector is its normalized edge vector,
A before scaling is
 [[2a+c/2,-sqrt(3)c/2],[-sqrt(3)c/2,3c/2]].
Divide by 2a. Taking x=3c/(4a) gives kappa=1/3. The physical increment
Delta a=tau*a becomes tau*e1e1^T after this scaling.

Four vertices: with equal outer weights a and middle weight m, an
antisymmetric orthonormal basis gives [[a,-a],[-a,a+2m]], with edit
2*tau*m in the second coordinate. Reorder the basis and divide by 2m.
Then x=a/(2m) and kappa=1, giving exactly the displayed canonical form.

4. Exact two-by-two square-root trace formula
---------------------------------------------
This calculation was derived by root and independently reconstructed here.
Set K=sqrt(1+tau), h=1+kappa, and s=sqrt(x). For alpha=1/2,

 det Q=K,
 tr Q=(K+1)*N/sqrt(P),
 N=h*s^2+(K+1)*s+K,
 P=(h*s^2+2s+1)(h*s^2+2K*s+K^2)
  =N^2+kappa*(K-1)^2*s^2.

Derivation: for any SPD two-by-two matrix X,
 sqrt(X)=(X+sqrt(det X) I)/sqrt(tr X+2 sqrt(det X)).
Using det A=s^2, det B=K^2 s^2, tr B=tr A+tau and
(A^-1)11=1 in tr(A^-1/2 B^1/2) gives the displayed formula.
The identity for P follows by multiplying its two quadratics and
collecting coefficients. No commutation of A and B is assumed.

Since N/s=h*s+(K+1)+K/s, the minimum of N/s is
K+1+2 sqrt(h*K), attained exactly at s=sqrt(K/h). Therefore

 min_(x>0) tr Q
 =(K+1)/sqrt(1+kappa*(K-1)^2/
                    [K+1+2 sqrt((1+kappa)*K)]^2).       (*)

For kappa>0, the ratio
kappa/[K+1+2 sqrt((1+kappa)*K)]^2
is strictly increasing with kappa. One can differentiate its square
root: after differentiating sqrt(kappa)/(K+1+2 sqrt(K)*sqrt(1+kappa)),
the numerator has the positive factor
K+1+2 sqrt(K)/sqrt(1+kappa). Thus the smaller of the two admissible
path-family minima is at kappa=1.
At tau=1 it equals 2.4080334082788246... . The corresponding smaller
relative eigenvalue is 1.0157544894662653... . These decimals are
illustrative; the exclusion below uses exact inequalities only.

5. An elementary rational gap excludes the target
-------------------------------------------------
Set tau=1, so K=sqrt(2), and note 0<kappa<=1 for both classified path
families. From (*) and K>1,

 K+1+2 sqrt((1+kappa)*K) > K+3,

and consequently every active-dimension-two unit-mass weighted path
satisfies the uniform lower bound

 tr Q > (K+1)/sqrt(1+(K-1)^2/(K+3)^2)
      = (5+4K)/sqrt(14+4K) > 12/5.

The final inequality is exact: after squaring positive quantities it
reduces to 424 sqrt(2)>591, which follows on squaring since
2*424^2=359552>349281=591^2.

But the proposed target has

 q1+q2=5/4+4 sqrt(2)/5 < 12/5,

because sqrt(2)<23/16 (square both positive sides).
This contradicts the universal path trace lower bound. Hence no such
path can exactly realize that simple positive target, regardless of
its length or edge weights.

6. The excluded target IS attained by an ordinary SPD rank-one pair
-------------------------------------------------------------------
This also follows from the independently developed exact matrix-interior
theorem, but it has a direct two-by-two proof here. Allow kappa to range
continuously over all positive values in the canonical SPD matrix family.
At x=K/(1+kappa), the trace in (*) decreases continuously from K+1
as kappa->0 to 2 sqrt(K) as kappa->infinity. The target trace
T=5/4+4K/5 lies strictly between these limits: T<K+1 is immediate,
and T>2 sqrt(K) follows from the unequal positive target pair having
product K (strict arithmetic-geometric mean).
Thus a finite kappa>0 gives trace T and determinant K, exactly the
two prescribed eigenvalues. More explicitly set beta=sqrt(K), c=K+1,

 w=[c^2-2 beta T]/[c(T-2 beta)],
 kappa=w^2-1,       x=K/(1+kappa).

All denominators are positive and w>1. Substitution in (*) verifies the
trace exactly. A is SPD since det A=x>0 and A11>0, its update is positive
rank one, and its leverage is one. Thus the obstruction separates exact
unit-mass PATH realization from exact general SPD realization, rather
than merely testing an inadmissible abstract target.

7. Meaning and limits
----------------------
The earlier unit-mass path closure theorem remains correct. Its path
blowup introduces additional strictly positive relative correction
eigenvalues that vanish only in a limit. Exact zeros impose a cyclic-rank
constraint which prevents arbitrary enlargement of the finite path.
Rounding vertex masses is therefore not merely an inconvenient proof
step that a continuous-degree argument automatically removes.

This disproves the requested exact path extension at its full stated
scope. For the displayed target the determinant budget is already fully
used, so additional positive eigenvalues cannot rescue exact realization
of those leading entries at the same tau either: they would increase the
product beyond sqrt(2). Extra positive modes are possible when leading
entries consume strictly less than the budget, or when tau is increased.
The obstruction does not preclude exact realization on other graph classes
or with arbitrary positive vertex masses. It supplies no claim about
historical novelty of this obstruction.
