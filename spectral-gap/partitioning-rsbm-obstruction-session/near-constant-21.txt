RSBM(2,1): finite-field kernel vectors near the constant phases
Prepared 2026-11-15. Independent derivation; no novelty claim.

RESULT AND LIMIT ORDER
Fix any prime p. For a sufficiently small positive epsilon_p, the total
contribution to E|ker_Fp M| of types within epsilon_p (total variation)
of one of the p constant phases is

                         p+O_p(N^{-1}).                 (NC1)

This holds for the degree-two configuration model and after conditioning
both internal graphs simple. One may take epsilon_p=c/(p-1)^2 for an
explicit, very small universal c>0. The error in the proof is bounded
by O(p(p-1)^2/N) once epsilon_p has been chosen. The limit is N->infinity
for each fixed p, and only then p->infinity. No simultaneous growth
regime for p and N is claimed.

Thus the constant phases contribute exactly p in the sequential limit.
This closes the near-constant part of the finite-field route. It does
not control the uniform phase and does not itself establish the full
O(p) kernel moment.

MATRIX AND SUPPORT INTERPRETATION
Use the notation of finite-field-21-entropy.txt:

  M=[[C_+-I,-I],[-I,C_--I]],
  M(x,z)=0 iff C_+x=x+z and C_-z=x+z.

Every constant vector (c 1,c 1) belongs to ker M. Subtract it, and map
(x,z) to the adjacency vector f=(x,-z). Then Mf=0 in the block-variable
sense is exactly Af=f on the original cubic graph. (More precisely,
M(x,z)=0 iff (A-I)(x,-z)=0.) This correspondence preserves the total
number K of nonzero coordinates. It is valid in every characteristic,
including p=2.

For counting it is convenient to undo the matching relabeling and use
an independent random cross perfect matching. Exchangeability of the
second internal configuration model makes the kernel count and total
support count identically distributed under this change. Every vertex
has two labeled internal stubs and one labeled matching stub.

NECESSARY SUPPORT CONDITIONS
Let S be the exact support of any nonzero f with Af=f over F_p, and
let K=|S|. Loops in the configuration model count twice on the diagonal.

1. Every vertex of S has at least one incident stub leading into S.
   Otherwise its adjacency sum is zero, contradicting f_v!=0.

2. Every vertex outside S incident to S has at least two such incident
   stubs. With exactly one, its adjacency equation has one nonzero
   summand and cannot vanish. This condition counts multiplicity, so it
   remains valid for parallel edges and in characteristic two.

Let e be the number of edges with both endpoints in S, with a loop
counted as one edge. There are b=3K-2e boundary edges, and let ell be
the number of their outside endpoints. Then

  e>=ceil(K/2),    0<=ell<=floor(b/2)<=K,
  h=e+b=3K-e,     h-K-ell>=K/2.                         (NC2)

Here h counts all graph edges incident to S. A support of size one is
impossible: its matching neighbor is outside S and has exactly one
incident edge from S. Thus K>=2.

PROBABILITY OF SPECIFIED STUB PAIRS
Write n=2N. Suppose K<=n/8=N/4 and fix any h specified disjoint stub
pairs, every pair incident to S. A pattern inconsistent with the
internal/cross stub colors has probability zero. For a compatible
pattern, let q_+,q_- be its internal edge counts, and m its cross edge
count. Since there are only 2K internal stubs on S and K cross stubs,

  q_++q_-<=2K,    m<=K,    q_++q_-+m=h.

An internal configuration has probability

  1/[(2N-1)(2N-3)...(2N-2q+1)]

of containing q specified pairs. A random cross matching has probability
1/(N)_m of containing m specified cross pairs. Every denominator here
is at least N/2 when K<=N/4: for internal edges use q<=2K, and for the
matching use m<=K. Independence of the three pairings therefore gives

  P(all specified stub pairs occur) <= (2/N)^h=(4/n)^h. (NC3)

SHARP ENOUGH ENUMERATION OF SMALL SUPPORTS
Let F_K count vertex sets S of size K satisfying the two necessary
support conditions. For fixed K,e,ell as in (NC2), choose S, choose its
outside neighbor set, pair 2e of S's 3K stubs internally, and injectively
match the remaining b stubs to stubs at the ell outside vertices. We
may drop per-vertex degree constraints to upper-bound the count. Thus

 E F_K <= sum_(e,ell)
    binom(n,K) binom(n-K,ell)
    binom(3K,2e) (2e-1)!! binom(3ell,b) b! (4/n)^h.   (NC4)

As usual (-1)!!=1 and binom(0,b)=0 for b>0. Empty powers are one.
This enumeration includes loops and parallel edges, and may overcount
invalid colored patterns; both features are safe for an upper bound.
Every actual support has its entire incident stub pattern among the
patterns enumerated in (NC4).

For completeness, apply the bounds

  binom(n,K)<=(en/K)^K,
  binom(n-K,ell)<=(en/ell)^ell,
  binom(3K,2e)<=2^(3K),  (2e-1)!!<=(3K)^e,
  binom(3ell,b)<=2^(3ell),  b!<=(2K)^b.

After collecting powers of n and K, the summand in (NC4) is at most

  e^(K+ell) 2^(3K+3ell) 3^e 2^b 4^h (K/ell)^ell
       * (K/n)^(h-K-ell).

For ell=0 the ratio factor is defined as one. Since ell<=K,
(K/ell)^ell<=exp(K/e). Since e<=3K/2, b<=2K and h<=5K/2, the entire
prefactor is at most 2^(20K). For K>=2 the number of (e,ell) pairs is
at most (K+2)^2<=4^K. Equation (NC2) and K/n<1 consequently imply

  E F_K <= C^K (K/n)^(K/2),    2<=K<=n/8,             (NC5)

where C=2^24 is a convenient conservative universal constant. No
asymptotic estimate depending on K is hidden here.

SUM OVER FIELD VALUES AND MESOSCOPIC SUPPORT SIZES
For each S of size K there are at most (p-1)^K vectors with exact
support S. Let V_K count vectors f with Af=f and exact support K. Set

  B_p=C^2(p-1)^2.

Then (NC5) gives

  E V_K <= (B_p K/n)^(K/2),    2<=K<=n/8.             (NC6)

Choose

  epsilon_p=min(1/8, 1/(16 e B_p)).

For g_K=(B_p K/n)^(K/2),

  g_(K+1)/g_K <= sqrt(e B_p (K+1)/n) <= 1/2

for every 2<=K<=epsilon_p n, once n is sufficiently large for the fixed
p. Hence

  sum_(2<=K<=epsilon_p n) E V_K <= 2g_2=4B_p/n.        (NC7)

The estimate covers every small and mesoscopic support size up through
a positive fraction epsilon_p n. The extremely small explicit radius
is immaterial to the fixed-p compactness argument in the entropy note.

CONSTANT TYPE NEIGHBORHOODS
If a local profile type T is within epsilon_p of the constant profile
(c,c,c,c), then at most epsilon_p N coordinates have a nonconstant
profile. In particular, the total number of coordinates of x and z
different from c is at most 2epsilon_p N=epsilon_p n. Subtracting c
therefore sends every nonconstant vector contributing to this type
neighborhood into the vectors counted by (NC7). Thus all p constant
neighborhoods contribute at most

  p + p*4B_p/n = p+O(p(p-1)^2/N)                       (NC8)

in the configuration model, for sufficiently large N for each fixed
p. There are exactly p constant vectors, supplying the lower bound p.
It is unnecessary to prove the converse containment of neighborhoods.

CONDITIONING SIMPLICITY
Let S_N be the event that both internal configurations are simple.
The independent cross matching is always simple. As established in
combinatorics-21.txt, P(S_N)->exp(-3/2)>0. Constants contribute exactly p
after conditioning, while the nonconstant sparse contribution is at
most its unconditioned expectation divided by P(S_N). Consequently

  p <= E[number from constant neighborhoods | S_N]
     <= p + (4p B_p/n)/P(S_N) = p+O_p(N^{-1}).         (NC9)

Rare loop or parallel-edge components are already included in the raw
bound (NC7), so they do not invalidate this particular near-constant
estimate at fixed p. No assertion is made that arbitrary raw moments
are uniformly controlled when p grows with N. Conditioning simplicity
can improve the sparse rate, but that improvement is unnecessary here.

STATUS
Near-constant phases: controlled by (NC1), including all sublinear
supports and sufficiently small positive-density supports.
Uniform phase: untouched; its Gaussian/lattice prefactor remains the
obstacle to a complete first-moment proof.
