Lean verification of hierarchical absolute spectral approximation

Status and scope

  Full formalization is IN PROGRESS under the active goal. Completion
  requires the actual randomized algorithm-existence, success-probability,
  and arithmetic-runtime assertions of paper Theorems 1 and 2 and their
  corollaries, without additional algorithmic certificate assumptions.
  The conditional transfer theorems below are intermediate lemmas, not
  substitutes for those main results. The goal is not achieved yet.

  The mathematical review of the complete paper, including the remaining
  algorithmic dependencies, is docs/hierarchical-mathematical-review.txt.
  The original manuscript and PDF were not changed.

Start with the theorem statement

  HierarchicalAbsoluteSpectral/Spectral.lean:
    theorem hierarchical_spectral_error

  Its conclusion, exactly as in the checked source, is:

    (U * (Uᵀ * (A * B))).rank ≤ r ∧
    ‖A * B - U * (Uᵀ * (A * B))‖ ≤
      (1+θ) * tailSingularValue (A * B) r

  The output is C=U(U^T AB), so the returned factors are U and U^T AB.
  U has w orthonormal columns with an explicit hw : w<=r hypothesis;
  w may be zero or smaller than r. Thus rank loss and a zero optimal
  tail are not removed by the theorem statement.

  A : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin m) real; B : Matrix (Fin m) (Fin p) real.
  The target rank r appears explicitly, separately from output width w.
  The scope Matrix.Norms.L2Operator selects the spectral/operator norm,
  rather than the entrywise maximum norm or the Frobenius norm.
  The scope MatrixOrder selects the Loewner order: A<=B means B-A PSD.
  tailSingularValue M r is Mathlib's LinearMap.singularValues r on the
  Euclidean linear map represented by M. Mathlib is ZERO-indexed, so
  this is exactly paper sigma_(r+1)(M), extended by zero past dimension.

What the principal theorem assumes

  These are genuine hypotheses of the Lean theorem, not proved construction
  facts hidden behind an axiom. Inspect them in Spectral.lean or Audit.lean.

  hU, hw       Orthonormal physical output columns and width<=target rank.
  hF, hK       F is positive definite and K is real symmetric.
  hinvariant   F-kappa K F^-1 K <= C h D, paper equation (9).
  hidentity    Z K Z^T = t Z Bm Z^T - (AB)(AB)^T.
  hmass        Z Bm Z^T <= (1+epsilon) I.
  hD           Z D Z^T <= 2t I + 2(AB)(AB)^T.
  hstrong      On v perpendicular to U, for y=Z^T v,
                 y^T K F^-1 K y - 2 y^T K y <= b y^T F y.
               This is the quadratic strong-action certificate in Lemma 6.
  htail        t <= [(1+x)/(1-x)] [1/(1-epsilon)] sigma_(r+1)(AB)^2.
               This combines near-threshold and mass comparisons.
  Scalar       b,C,h,t,theta,x,epsilon nonnegative; theta<=1;
               kappa*b<=1; b*C*h,x,epsilon<=theta/1000.

  The paper provides much smaller scalar budgets. The proof derives the
  physical Rayleigh bound from these matrix hypotheses, proves the combined
  scalar budget, bounds the norm of the orthogonal projection residual, and
  proves its rank bound. Neither the desired spectral error nor an existence
  assertion about a fast successful randomized algorithm is assumed as an
  opaque proposition. The remaining certificate-construction obligations
  are nevertheless substantial and remain outside the formal theorem.

Paper to Lean map

  Each locator below is FileStem.declaration. All declarations have the
  Lean namespace HierarchicalAbsoluteSpectral, without the file stem.

  Lemma 3, relative perturbation
    MatrixInequalities.relative_perturbation
    MatrixInequalities.inverse_order
    Actual real matrices and ordinary matrix inverses. The inverse-order
    proof uses positive block matrices and Schur complements. The formal
    relative-perturbation lemma needs only the paper's upper relative bound;
    its lower bound and delta<1 are not needed for this particular step.

  Lemma 5, noncommuting cross terms and ideal relaxation
    Refinement.weighted_young_vector
    Refinement.weighted_young
    Refinement.ideal_refinement_relaxation
    Refinement.finite_relaxation_reset
    These prove the Young inequality, the coefficient-four ideal bound,
    and the finite reset to five with Cnext=100(C+1). The ideal theorem
    explicitly assumes the root identity, the weighted fine-error bound,
    the inflated coarse invariant, and Fstar<=(5/4)P. It does not construct
    the ideal matrix or finite approximating polynomial.

  Lemma 6, transfer
    Scalars.near_scalar_inequality
    Scalars.relaxed_strong_transfer_scalar
    MatrixInequalities.relaxed_strong_action_transfer
    MatrixInequalities.physical_transfer_rayleigh
    Spectral.projection_error_of_complement_bound
    Spectral.hierarchical_spectral_error
    The physical Rayleigh theorem uses u=b*C*h, which is slightly sharper
    than replacing b by 7*alpha as in the paper. The spectral theorem then
    uses the physical projection and the actual product singular value.

  Section 5, combined error budget
    Scalars.final_error_budget
    Scalars.threshold_error_budget
    The hypotheses u,x,epsilon<=theta/1000 are weaker than the paper's
    u<=7theta/10^6, x<=4theta/10^6, epsilon=theta/10^9.

  Section 6, exact carried lift, equation (16)
    Carry.carried_intertwining
    Carry.polynomial_intertwining
    Carry.carried_filter_identity
    Carry.exact_carried_preimage
    Carry.exact_physical_lift
    These hold for every real polynomial and arbitrary noncommuting matrix
    factors. The carried-preimage theorem explicitly requires S invertible.
    It even holds without requiring R0 invertible because Mathlib's inverse
    is total and the matrix identity remains valid in that stronger form.
    In the manuscript both factors are invertible on the good event.

  Section 8, recurrence and equation (22)
    Schedule.cost_recurrence_unfold
    Schedule.true_cost_cancellation
    Schedule.fine_cost_cancellation
    Schedule.loglog_sq_isLittleO_log
    The cancellation theorems use s=sqrt(rho), m=N*s^2, theta=s^5,
    and hj^2=H*h0*s. This is precisely the paper's square-root schedule.
    The last declaration proves (log log m)^2=o(log m), the logarithmic
    statement behind the subpolynomial overhead. It does not derive a
    total algorithmic runtime from code for all matrix operations.

  Corollary 7, optimized parameters
    Scalars.optimized_parameters
    Scalars.optimized_parameter_in_family
    Exact square-root identities, the intersection of both exponent gaps,
    reciprocal alpha*, and membership of d* in the theorem's open interval.

  Practical cutoff
    Schedule.first_level_cutoff
    The first acceptance test forces rho<=1024^-4, hence N/m>=2^40.
    The decimal dimension examples are generated by the Python review script.

Constructed dependencies added while pursuing the full goal

  InverseMetric.lean constructs the metric of an inverse factor and proves
  its positivity, inverse, congruence identities and actual scalar inflation.

  WeightedError.lean derives E F^-1 E <= (a^2/c)D directly from
  -aD<=E<=aD and cD<=F using a positive block matrix and Schur complement.
  fine_error_previous_metric and fine_error_ideal_metric now prove the two
  weighted errors previously assumed by the local ideal-algebra lemma.

  Inflation.lean derives equation (10), including the explicit value
    Cp=(1+epsilon)C+8*kappa*epsilon*(1+epsilon)^2,
  its bound Cp<=2(C+1), and the normalized proxy domination.

  IdealMetric.lean constructs f_eta(A) using the proved matrix spectral
  calculus and verifies positivity, both margins, its upper/lower bounds,
  and f_eta(A)-A f_eta(A)^-1 A=2eta I. This calculus is used analytically;
  it is not an additional computational primitive in the algorithm.

  AnalyticRefinement.constructed_ideal_refinement constructs the ideal
  metric from the preceding inverse factor and proves its 128h lower
  bound, upper bound, both 56h margins and coefficient-four relaxed defect.
  Its root identity and weighted error are derived internally.

  PolynomialMetric.refinedInverseFactor_relative derives invertibility and
  relative metric closeness for the actual finite factor Rp*p(Aj) from
  the polynomial's scalar approximation bound on the spectrum.

  FiniteRefinement.finite_refinement_invariant proves all inequalities of
  (9), the actual finite inverse-factor invertibility, the 55h margins,
  Bnext=2B and Cnext=100(C+1), from the preceding invariant and the
  uniform scalar polynomial approximation. The latter still needs its
  full degree-bounded construction theorem; it is not hidden as an axiom.

  StrongAction.strong_action_dual_certificate derives the quadratic strong
  certificate from the actual operator-norm condition. Its physical-span
  version proves the required orthogonality after column orthonormalization.

  PhysicalPencil.lean derives the physical mass bound, D bound and exact
  pencil identity from the support factorization and its coarse Gram
  estimates. SupportFilter.support_filter_spectral_error integrates these
  and StrongAction into the spectral conclusion. Core/physical widths may
  differ from the requested rank, including rank loss.

  DyadicNodes.lean constructs dyadic Gauss--Chebyshev nodes by nested scalar
  square roots and complex-pair field arithmetic. It proves their analytic
  node formula, interval membership, distinctness, the actual Lagrange
  interpolant's node values and its degree bound. This does not yet prove
  the required uniform interpolation error or the FFT implementation cost.

Remaining work for a full formalization of Theorems 1 and 2

  1. A formal finite exact-real arithmetic algorithm and its operation-cost
     semantics, including the fixed matrix-multiplication kernel.
  2. Product-support/sketch construction, all failure-event bounds, and the
     companion's exact characteristic-coefficient/inertia oracle.
  3. Lemma 4's uniform analytic Chebyshev interpolation error, finite dyadic
     DCT implementation and degree/cost bounds. The finite metric algebra
     from that scalar error bound is now proved. Also construct the actual
     fine/true Neumann inverse masses and instantiate the fine-proxy bounds.
  4. The min--max/eigenvalue near-threshold step, Gaussian head/tail bounds,
     and the strong operator-norm bound for the actual filtered Gaussian
     output. The conversion of that norm to hstrong is now proved.
  5. Adaptive conditional probability accounting, uniform depth bounds,
     every primitive's cost, all-outcome caps, finite-size fallback and
     output generation. The cost cancellations alone do not prove these.
  6. The absolute-error adaptive corollary and the exact-Gram comparator
     implementation are mathematically reviewed but not formally proved.

  Consequently this development is useful as a checked algebraic foundation
  and an explicit reduction theorem. It is not an end-to-end formal
  verification of the randomized near-quadratic algorithm.
