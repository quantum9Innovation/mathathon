1. Exact one-step Hankel embedding (checked algebra)
-------------------------------------------------
For arbitrary raw A(n,m), B(m,p), use the discrete-time system

  x_{j+1}=B u_j,   y_j=A x_j,   state transition F=0.

The only nonzero Markov parameter is AB. Its infinite Hankel operator is
diag(AB,0,0,...) between past and future sequence spaces. Its nonzero
Hankel singular values are exactly sigma_j(AB). Its controllability and
observability Gramians are BB^T and A^T A. These are algebraic identities;
the Gramians are not supplied to an algorithm at zero cost.

Any stable state-space reduced system of state dimension <=r whose Hankel
error is <=gamma gives a matrix approximation C=A_r B_r of rank <=r,
by taking the first Markov block. A block compression of an operator has
norm at most that operator's norm, so ||AB-C||<=gamma. In particular an
optimal Hankel-norm approximation would solve the matrix task, even if its
later Markov parameters are nonzero. This is a genuine sufficient route;
it does not require the reduced dynamics to remain zero.

A continuous-time embedding is equally exact: state transition F=-I,
input sqrt(2) B, output sqrt(2) A. The Hankel operator is

  (Hf)(t)=2 e^{-t} AB integral_0^infinity e^{-s} f(s) ds.

Since ||e^{-t}||_L2=1/sqrt(2), its singular values are again those of AB.
Its two Gramians are BB^T and A^T A. Taking the exponential-mode temporal
compression of any reduced Hankel operator gives factors of width r,
with no larger error, and can be computed from reduced-state resolvents.
The discrete embedding above avoids that extra resolvent entirely.

Primary scope checked from publisher/repository abstracts:
  K. Glover (1984), All optimal Hankel-norm approximations of linear
  multivariable systems and their L-infinity error bounds,
  https://doi.org/10.1080/00207178408933239 .
The optimal error is the relevant Hankel singular value, but known formulas
start with balanced realizations / Lyapunov information. The embedding is
not by itself a raw-factor algorithm. It must bypass this balancing cost.

2. Low-rank Riccati solution exactly encodes the wanted positive space
------------------------------------------------------------------
Let H be a real symmetric threshold matrix with at most r POSITIVE
eigenvalues, e.g. H=Q-t G^{-1}. Consider the zero-cost CARE

  H X+X H-X^2=0.                                           (R1)

For nonsingular H, the unique PSD solution making H-X strictly stable is

  X_*=H+|H|=2H_+,   rank(X_*)<=r,   H-X_*=-|H|.           (R2)

Proof: diagonalization verifies existence. For uniqueness of a stabilizing
solution X, subtract the two Riccati equations to obtain the homogeneous
Sylvester equation

  (H-X)(X-X_*)+(X-X_*)(H-X_*)=0.

Both coefficient matrices are stable, so the only solution is zero. This
proof does not assume that the unknown X commutes with H.

Thus a useful overlooked possibility is a low-rank STABILIZING CARE
algorithm from raw H-actions. Its target solution intrinsically has rank r
even when the positive/negative bulk is spectrally irregular. This differs
from approximating a full inverse or square root, whose bulk is full rank.

Crucial initialization issue: standard low-rank CARE algorithms with
stable drift and low-rank forcing do not apply automatically. Here the
forcing is zero and H has r unstable modes. Starting at X=0 returns the
nonstabilizing zero solution. A stabilizing rank-r initial X would already
provide almost the wanted dual-subspace certificate.

For X_0=cI with c>||H||, Newton--Kleinman is well-defined and obeys

 (X_j-H)X_{j+1}+X_{j+1}(X_j-H)=X_j^2.                    (R3)

Every iterate commutes with H, and on an H-eigenvalue h the scalar update
is x_{j+1}=x_j^2/[2(x_j-h)]. It converges to 2 max(h,0); the scalar startup
is logarithmic in 1/|h| and subsequent convergence is quadratic. However
the iterates are full rank before truncation, and each Lyapunov solve is
not a free raw-factor operation. Claiming a logarithmic-pass algorithm
from this scalar convergence alone would omit the substantive cost.

For h<=0, x_j(h)<=c/2^j, by x_j-h>=x_j. Hence after
j>=log_2(c/tau), the exact iterate has a rank-r approximation in operator
norm <=tau, WITHOUT any positive bulk spectral regularity. This is a
potential compression invariant worth exploiting. It does not establish
that all earlier full-rank Newton iterates can be computed cheaply.

3. Why nonsymmetric low-rank stabilization is not the final certificate
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Generic one-input state feedback can stabilize many unstable modes, unlike
a symmetric negative update. Thus a cheap nonsymmetric feedback does not
by itself imply that H has <=r positive eigenvalues, or that H is NSD on
the Euclidean orthogonal complement of a rank-r space. The norm certificate
needs symmetric geometry, or a quantitatively controlled metric transfer.

For example a shifted controllability Gramian W may satisfy

  (H+alpha I)W+W(H+alpha I)=g g^T.

If W is SPD, H-g g^T W^{-1} is stable by a Lyapunov identity. Nevertheless
this rank-one nonsymmetric feedback is possible for a generic H with MANY
positive eigenvalues. W can be extremely ill-conditioned, and computing
W^{-1}g from raw factors is uncharged. It cannot replace the inertia proof.

4. A low-rank-forced CARE and a robust residual certificate
--------------------------------------------------------
There is more positive algebra than the zero-forcing formulation shows.
For any E>=0, define

  X_E=H+sqrt(H^2+E).                                      (R4)

It satisfies H X_E+X_E H-X_E^2+E=0 exactly. If H^2+E is positive
definite, H-X_E=-sqrt(H^2+E) is strictly stable. Moreover

  0 <= X_E-2H_+ <= sqrt(||E||) I.                         (R5)

The square-root operator is monotone, and
sqrt(H^2+E)<=sqrt(H^2+||E||I)<=|H|+sqrt(||E||)I. Thus random
low-rank E=alpha^2 Omega Omega^T with alpha||Omega||<=eps preserves
an intrinsic rank-r tail eps, without spectral regularity of H's bulk.

This square-root update / Riccati relation is NOT claimed new. A directly
relevant primary source is Shmueli, Drineas and Avron (2023/2024),
Low-rank updates of matrix square roots,
https://doi.org/10.1002/nla.2528 . Their algorithmic discussion explicitly
charges inverse access, including matrices E_base^2+low-rank. It does not
make an inverse of our threshold H cheap.

The following residual-to-output lemma is a useful checked addition. Its
priority has NOT been audited. It removes an explicit stabilizing test
for sufficiently accurate PSD approximate solutions with random forcing.

Lemma R (observable low-rank forcing selects the stabilizing branch).
Let H=H^T, ||H||<=1, and let U span its ENTIRE strictly-positive
eigenspace, of dimension q<=r. Let Omega have s columns and suppose

  ||Omega||<=L_Omega,   sigma_min(U^T Omega)>=beta>0.

The q=0 case requires no output space and can be omitted. Let 0<eps<=1,
alpha=eps/L_Omega, E=alpha^2 Omega Omega^T. Let X>=0 be ANY matrix,
possibly depending arbitrarily on Omega, for which

  ||R(X)||<=eta,
  R(X)=H X+X H-X^2+E,
  eta <= eps^3 beta^2/(100 L_Omega^2).                    (R6)

Then

  ||X-2H_+|| <= 4 eps.                                   (R7)

No eigenvalue gap within the positive or negative spectrum, no lower
bound on the positive H-eigenvalues, and no separate stability check on
H-X is assumed. The fixed head rank q<=r is part of this lemma's scope;
it is not an unrestricted below/above-threshold inertia algorithm.

Proof. Put S=X-H, so R(X)=H^2+E-S^2. A top eigenvector of X shows
||X||<=1+sqrt(1+||E||+eta)<3, hence ||S||<4.
Suppose S has a spectral subspace V below -eps, with orthonormal basis V
and S V=-V T, eps I<T<4I. Write X_V=V^T X V>=0. Compressing the
residual gives

  V^T R V=V^T X^2 V+X_V T+T X_V+V^T E V.

Thus T X_V+X_V T<=eta I. The inverse of the positive Lyapunov map
Y->TY+YT preserves PSD order (its integral formula proves this), so

  0<=X_V<=eta/(2eps) I,
  ||X V||^2<=3eta/(2eps),
  ||V^T E V||<=7eta/eps.                                 (R8)

The identity H V=V T+X V and Sylvester separation of the nonpositive
H-block from T>=eps imply

  ||(I-UU^T)V||<=sqrt(3eta/(2eps^3))=:d.                  (R9)

For completeness, the separated Sylvester inverse has norm <=1/eps
by the semigroup integral; no intrablock gap is used. By (R6),
d<=beta/(4 L_Omega). Since beta<=L_Omega, this also gives d<=1/4.
For any unit v in range(V),

  ||Omega^T v||
    >= beta sqrt(1-d^2)-L_Omega d >= beta/2.

But (R8) gives
||Omega^T V||<=sqrt(7eta/(alpha^2 eps))<beta/2, a contradiction.
Therefore S>=-eps I. Its positive part S_+ obeys

  ||S-S_+||<=eps,
  ||S_+^2-(H^2+E)||<=eps^2+eta.

For PSD A,B, ||sqrt(A)-sqrt(B)||<=sqrt(||A-B||): sandwich A,B by
their norm difference, use square-root monotonicity and the scalar
bound sqrt(x+d)-sqrt(x)<=sqrt(d). Consequently

  ||S-sqrt(H^2+E)||<=eps+sqrt(eps^2+eta)<=3eps.

Combine with (R5) to obtain (R7).

Gaussian parameters. For fixed H, U^T Omega is a q-by-s standard
Gaussian matrix. With s a suitable universal constant times
r+log(1/delta), the usual net/chi-square estimate gives beta>=sqrt(s)/2
with high probability. The elementary Markov bound
L_Omega=sqrt(2ms/delta) suffices for the other event. Hence the requested
residual eta is only inverse-polynomial in m, eps, 1/delta. Driving such
a residual needs logarithmic PRECISION factors; this does not yet bound
the number or raw cost of optimization / rational-iteration steps.

Computable residual: if X=Y Y^T has k columns and E=alpha^2 Omega
Omega^T, then R(X) has range within span(Y,H Y,Omega), dimension at
most 2k+s. One raw H action on Y and small QR/core algebra therefore
compute its norm (or a rigorous norm upper surrogate) with cost
O(I*k+m*(k+s)^2+(k+s)^3), including all factor actions. This is a real
certificate access advantage: checking the Riccati residual does not
require testing stability of H-X or constructing H's dense Gram.

Extraction: (R7) implies that retaining X-eigenvectors with eigenvalues
>8eps gives at most q<=r columns. On their orthogonal complement,
v^T H v<=v^T H_+ v<=(v^T X v+4eps||v||^2)/2<=6eps||v||^2.
A direct physical threshold transfer still needs to be stated with the
normalized pencil's metric error budget.

6. H-sandwiched forcing: a condition-free graph-angle theorem
----------------------------------------------------------
This is a stronger new candidate framework than plain random forcing.
Its algorithmic novelty has not been audited. All identities and bounds
below are local derivations, not claims imported from a control paper.

Let H=H^T, ||H||<=1, with q<=r positive eigenvalues. Work analytically
on supp(H); its kernel can be added with zero feedback throughout. Put
D=|H|>0 and J=sign(H), so H=DJ=JD. Let Omega obey

  ||Omega||<=L,   sigma_min(U_+^T Omega)>=beta>0.

Choose alpha>0 satisfying

  alpha^2 <= beta^2/(4L^4),

and use the WEIGHTED low-rank forcing

  E=alpha^2 H Omega Omega^T H.                            (W1)

Forming its factor alpha H Omega needs only one raw H-block action.
Define

  S=sqrt(H^2+E),  Delta=S-D,
  X=H+S,  Y=S-H,  a=alpha^2 L^2,  c=1+a.

Then

  0<=Delta<=(a/2)D,             a<=1/4, c<=5/4,            (W2)
  Y >= (alpha^2 beta^2/5) D.                              (W3)

Thus the stabilizing and antistabilizing graph spaces are quantitatively
transverse with an inverse-polynomial bound independent of every H
eigenvalue ratio and every intra-head or intra-tail gap.

Proof of the useful derivative sandwich. Since E<=aD^2,
D<=S<=sqrt(1+a)D, proving (W2). Let Z be the PSD solution of

  D Z+Z D=E.

The square-root resolvent representation and W=(D^2+tI)^-1/2 E
(D^2+tI)^-1/2, 0<=W<=aI, give

  Z/(1+a) <= Delta <= Z.                                 (W4)

Indeed the integrand for Delta is the congruence of W(I+W)^-1,
whereas the derivative integrand for Z is the congruence of W; their
Loewner ratio lies between 1/(1+a) and 1 at every positive t.

In a D-eigenbasis, the normalized derivative is

 (D^-1/2 Z D^-1/2)_ij
   =alpha^2 [sqrt(d_i d_j)/(d_i+d_j)] (J Omega Omega^T J)_ij.

The Cauchy kernel in brackets is PSD, since it is the Gram kernel of
the functions sqrt(d_i) exp(-d_i t) in L2(0,infinity); its diagonal is
1/2. Schur-product positivity and Omega_+ Omega_+^T>=beta^2 I yield

  (D^-1/2 Delta D^-1/2)_++ >= A I,
  A=alpha^2 beta^2/(2c),
  ||D^-1/2 Delta D^-1/2||<=a/2=:b.

Now Y_rel=D^-1/2 Y D^-1/2=2P_-+Delta_rel. For vectors x,y in
the positive/negative J-spaces, respectively,

  [x;y]^T Y_rel [x;y] >= A||x||^2-2b||x||||y||+2||y||^2.

Our alpha choice gives b^2<=A/2. Young's inequality therefore bounds
the RHS below by (A/2)||x||^2+||y||^2 >=(A/2)(||x||^2+||y||^2).
Since c<=5/4, A/2>=alpha^2 beta^2/5, proving (W3).

In particular, with

  C=(2S)^1/2,   T=C^-1 X C^-1,

we have

  0<=T<= (1-gamma)I,    gamma=alpha^2 beta^2/12.           (W5)

This follows from I-T=C^-1 Y C^-1 and 2S<=2sqrt(c)D.

Accuracy choice. Taking additionally alpha^2<=2eps/L^2 ensures
||X-2H_+||=||Delta||<=eps. Both alpha and gamma then have only inverse-
polynomial size in eps,m,1/delta on the elementary Gaussian event in
Section 4. The construction never needs to apply D, J, S or C; these
are proof coordinates. Its actual forcing uses only H Omega.

7. Explicit polylogarithmic rational CARE convergence
---------------------------------------------------
Consider the Hamiltonian

  Hamiltonian=[[H,I],[E,-H]].

Its two invariant graph bases are [I;-X] and [I;Y], with right action
-S and S, respectively. For a conjugation-closed list of left-half-plane
shifts sigma_j, let r be the associated product of Cayley factors. At
complete conjugate pairs r is real on the real axis and r(-z)=1/r(z).
Apply these Cayley transforms to the starting graph [I;0], obtaining
[M;N], and put X_j=-N M^-1. This is the standard Cayley/RADI iterate.
Under the stated detectability (which follows from the full positive-head
rank of Omega), these iterates are PSD and bounded above by X; this is
the usual RADI order property, not a new assertion of monotonicity after
numerical truncation.

There is a useful exact error formula. Decompose [I;0] into the two
graph bases, with coefficients (2S)^-1Y and (2S)^-1X. Direct block
algebra gives

 X-X_j=C r(S) T^(1/2)
   [I-T+T^(1/2) r(S)^2 T^(1/2)]^-1
   T^(1/2) r(S) C.                                       (W6)

This form remains valid when T is singular. For invertible T the middle
three factors may equivalently be written
[T^-1-I+r(S)^2]^-1. From (W5),

  ||X-X_j|| <= (2/gamma) max_(lambda in spec S)
                                  lambda |r(lambda)|^2. (W7)

This is an ABSOLUTE-error bound. It does not introduce lambda_min(S).

A simple non-galactic shift schedule suffices. Choose dyadic a-values
from tau to >=||S||, and for each a use the pair sigma=-a+ia,-a-ia.
The pair's real Cayley factor is

  r_a(lambda)=((lambda-a)^2+a^2)/((lambda+a)^2+a^2).

It lies in [0,1] for lambda>=0; for a<=lambda<=2a it is <=1/5.
Thus one full dyadic cycle has magnitude <=1/5 on [tau,||S||], and
at most 1 everywhere nonnegative. Repeat K cycles to obtain |r|<=rho
on that interval. Choose tau=gamma*delta_target/8 and
rho^2=gamma*delta_target/(8 max(1,||S||)). Equation (W7) gives error
<=delta_target/4 (and hence <=delta_target with room for constants).
The number of individual shifts is

  O(log^2(1/(gamma delta_target))).                       (W8)

Every actual closed-loop shifted system is H-X_j+sigma I. Its
Hermitian real coefficient implies inverse norm <=1/|Im sigma|<=1/tau.
Thus this schedule also supplies a polynomial lower bound on shift
imaginary parts, not merely an existence theorem for Zolotarev shifts.
That bound is useful for perturbation-controlled compressed simulation.

This DOES NOT assert that the shifted systems are computationally cheap.
Woodbury reduces their low-rank feedback to H+sigma I resolvents. For
our factored-product threshold, those still contain the unresolved
near-threshold cancellation. No full raw-factor runtime has been proved.

8. Absolute-error rank of every exact RADI iterate
------------------------------------------------
The root-exponential rational approximation connection was independently
identified by the parent during this derivation. It gives a rigorous
way to bound numerical rank without positive-bulk regularity.

For any 0<zeta<1 and 0<=x<=L0, use

 sqrt(x)=(2/pi) integral_-infinity^infinity
                         x exp(y)/(exp(2y)+x) dy.

Truncate at |y|=O(log(L0/zeta)) and use a trapezoidal step
h=Theta(1/log(L0/zeta)). The integrand is a translated sech function;
its Fourier transform decays as 1/cosh(pi*frequency/2), so Poisson
summation bounds infinite-trapezoid error exponentially in 1/h,
uniformly for x in [0,L0]. The two tails are bounded geometrically.
This gives a positive-weight proper rational approximant of degree
ell=O(log^2(L0/zeta)), all of whose poles are negative, and uniform
error <=zeta. A fully explicit coefficient/rounding statement is still
to be written if this is promoted to a manuscript theorem.

For T0=H^2 and rank(E)=s, each shifted resolvent difference has rank <=s:

 (T0+aI)^-1-(T0+E+aI)^-1
  =(T0+aI)^-1 E (T0+E+aI)^-1.

Therefore r_ell(T0+E)-r_ell(T0) has rank <=s*ell and approximates
Delta=sqrt(H^2+E)-|H| within 2zeta. It is PSD for the positive-weight
Stieltjes quadrature. Explicit low-rank factors follow from Woodbury
and the s-column solves (H^2+aI)^-1 factor(E). This is constructive
relative to those resolvents, not a free raw algorithm.

It follows that X=2H_++Delta has a 2zeta approximation of rank
r+O(s log^2(1/zeta)), without any minimum-eigenvalue assumption.
Every exact RADI iterate 0<=X_j<=X inherits the SAME numerical-rank
bound by eigenvalue monotonicity. This genuinely controls intermediate
rank growth for the exact iterates, rather than inferring it only from
the final rank-r-plus-eps statement.

Truncating an iterate destroys its exact PSD-residual/order invariant;
that issue is not being hidden. A valid implementation can instead
simulate the fixed-shift exact sequence with norm-controlled local
errors and PSD spectral truncation. The inverse bounds following (W8)
make a perturbation proof plausible with only polylogarithmic rank
growth even after very small local tolerances. The full quantitative
one-step perturbation and total-operation proof remains to be supplied.

10. A bounded filter for relative PSD projection of a signed raw product
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The parent identified an actual cheap-resolvent class: an UNSHIFTED
symmetric signed product S=X J X^T, X tall/raw and J a supplied small
symmetric core. Imaginary shifts can be preconditioned by a coarse
positive Gram embedding; the real threshold S-tI still lacks that
property. The raw-resolvent theorem is being proved by the parent/raw
worker, not silently assumed proved here. This section supplies a sharp
filter/range/extraction composition if that theorem holds with all costs.

The candidate target is stronger than additive error eps||S||:

  rank(C)<=r, C>=0, ||C-S_+||<=eps||S_+||,
  build ~O(input_size*r+m^3),

when S has at most r positive eigenvalues. There is no dependence on
||S_-||/||S_+||, factor conditioning or a positive spectral gap. The
width is the positive index, known from the supported small core.
This is NOT a solution of the original real-threshold AB problem.
Nor is a frontier novelty claim established: rank-r positive-update
secular methods are a serious comparator, especially at r=1.

Normalize T=S/v so lambda_max(T_+)<=1/2 and v<=C||S_+|| for an
absolute C. Section 11 gives a core-only normalization with square
roots and no magnitude-ratio search. Use the bounded one-sided filter

  f(x)=x_+/(1+x^2),   F=f(T).

Its positive range equals that of S_+, its rank is <=r, its eigenvalues
lie in [0,2/5], and it vanishes on all nonpositive eigenvalues.

Uniform ALL-REAL rational approximation. The identity

 |x|/(1+x^2)=(2/pi) integral_0^infinity
                        x^2/[(x^2+t^2)(1+x^2)] dt        (P1)

has low-tail error <=2t_min/pi and high-tail error <=2/(pi t_max),
uniformly for every real x. After t=exp(y), the integrand is

 [|x|/(2(1+x^2))] sech(y-log|x|),

with the x=0 branch identically zero. Its amplitude is <=1/4.
Poisson summation and the Fourier transform of sech give exponentially
small infinite-trapezoid error, uniformly on the ENTIRE real line.
Truncating t to [Theta(eta),Theta(1/eta)] with step Theta(1/log(1/eta))
therefore gives a proper rational r_abs of degree O(log^2(1/eta)) with
only imaginary poles and

  sup_(x real) |r_abs(x)-|x|/(1+x^2)|<=eta.

Then

  f_hat(x)=[x/(1+x^2)+r_abs(x)]/2                         (P2)

approximates f uniformly within eta/2. Also f_hat(0)=0 EXACTLY.
The poles are +/-i and +/-i t_j. No real threshold solve occurs.
Crucially the filter is uniformly bounded even if S has arbitrarily
large negative eigenvalues; approximating x_+ directly on a finite
interval would introduce an unnecessary negative/positive norm ratio.

Finite algebraic nodes. To avoid treating exp/log as free primitives,
choose L=ceil(log_2(128/eta)) by repeated halving and the least power
of two Q with 16Q>=L; compute b=2^(1/Q) with log_2 Q positive square
roots. Use half-shifted nodes t_j=b^(j+1/2), -QL<=j<=QL. Their omitted
tails are <=O(2^-L). The analytic step is h=log(2)/Q, and Poisson
alias error decays as exp(-pi^2 Q/log(2))<2^(-20Q). Since 16Q>=L,
this is smaller than the required eta budget. The trapezoid weight
is 2log(2)/(pi Q).
Approximate this single fixed-constant ratio to relative O(eta) using
geometric rational series for log(2) and Machin arctangent series for
pi, in O(log(1/eta)) field operations. Thus coefficient preparation
also needs only field operations, square roots and comparisons, with
polylogarithmic accuracy cost. A manuscript statement should fix all
constants and arithmetic-error allocations explicitly.

Each rational term uses only imaginary resolvents: x/(1+x^2) is the
average of (x-i)^-1 and (x+i)^-1, while (x^2+t^2)^-1 is their
antisymmetric difference divided by 2it. Products can use one extra
fixed resolvent or a partial fraction expansion. Half-shifted nodes
avoid coincident t=1. Coefficients require only polynomial accuracy
losses in eta; no exponentially large coefficient has been introduced.
When applying the rational function by Woodbury, analytically cancel
its zero-at-zero identity term and return the result in range(X), so
the algorithm never adds ambient-kernel noise.

Low-rank reconstruction and constants. Let q=rank(S_+)<=r, and draw
Gaussian Omega with s=O(q+log(1/delta)) columns. In the true supported
eigenbasis assume ||Omega||<=L_Omega and
sigma_min(Omega_head)>=beta_Omega. Only the m-dimensional support is
relevant because f_hat(0)=0. Write chi=L_Omega/beta_Omega, choose
the function-action error eta, and form Y=f_hat(T)Omega, Q=orth(Y).
The usual direct identity (not a gap-dependent angle theorem) gives

  ||(I-QQ^T)F|| <= eta*chi.                              (P3)

Indeed F=F Omega Omega_head^dagger U_head^T, and (I-QQ^T)Y=0.
Apply f_hat a SECOND time to Q and form B=Q^T f_hat(T)Q. Its distance
to B0=Q^T F Q is at most eta. B0 is PSD, rank<=q and norm<=2/5.
Clip B eigenvalues into [0,2/5] and retain at most q positive values;
the resulting Bhat differs from B by at most eta, by Weyl. Therefore

  Fhat=Q Bhat Q^T >=0, rank(Fhat)<=q,
  ||Fhat-F||<=2eta(chi+1).                               (P4)

Recover the positive part using only the small PSD core:

  psi(z)=[1-sqrt(1-4z^2)]/(2z), psi(0)=0.

For z in [0,2/5], psi(z)=z+z^3+2z^5+5z^7+... has positive Catalan
coefficients, and psi'(2/5)=25/12. Telescoping powers proves the
OPERATOR norm Lipschitz bound

  ||psi(A)-psi(B)||<=(25/12)||A-B||

for PSD A,B with norm<=2/5. Since psi(f(x))=x for 0<=x<=1/2,

  ||v psi(Fhat)-S_+|| <=(25/6) v eta(chi+1).              (P5)

If v<=64||S_+||, eta=eps/[300(chi+1)] suffices. Return the factors
of v psi(Bhat), lifted by Q. The final rank is <=q<=r. There is NO
unfiltered S-Ritz action after rangefinding, which would allow the
large negative bulk to contaminate the output.

Gaussian chi is polynomial in m and 1/delta with s=O(q+log(1/delta));
even a crude Markov/Frobenius norm bound suffices, so the rational
degree and all solver tolerances remain polylogarithmic in m,1/eps,
1/delta. If the requested block width reaches the supported dimension,
the exact core/Gram branch costs at most the same input*block budget.

11. Positive norm normalization using square roots only
------------------------------------------------------
An earlier signed-coefficient positive-root bound used general dth
roots; these are not free in a field/square-root model. The following
repair avoids both a general-root oracle and a log-magnitude loop.
This is close to classical Graeffe/root-radii approximation, and no
novelty is claimed for that ingredient.

Let K be the explicit supported symmetric core of order k<=m, with
largest positive eigenvalue a>0. Its positive index is first checked
by exact core inertia. Choose an even power of two q>=log_2 k,
q=O(log k), and form K^q by O(loglog k) core matrix squarings. It is
PSD with eigenvalues mu_j^q, where mu_j=|lambda_j(K)| are decreasing.
Obtain its nonzero elementary coefficients e_j from a cubic core
characteristic-polynomial routine. Then

  mu_j^q/j <= e_j/e_(j-1) <= (k-j+1)mu_j^q.              (P6)

For the lower bound extend every (j-1)-subset by a missing one of the
top j indices and count each j-subset at most j times. For the upper
bound remove the largest-index element from each j-subset; its value
is <=mu_j^q and every (j-1)-subset has at most k-j+1 extensions.

Taking qth roots needs ONLY log_2 q successive positive square roots:

  a_j=(e_j/e_(j-1))^(1/q) in [mu_j/2,2mu_j].             (P7)

Newton log-concavity implies that the a_j decrease. The predicate
n_+(K-a_j I/4)>0 is therefore monotone in j. Binary-search its first
true index j*. If mu_l=a, then a_l<=2a, so the predicate is true by
l and j*<=l. Consequently

  a/2 <= a_(j*) <4a.

Thus [a_(j*)/4,2a_(j*)] brackets a within factor 8 after O(log k)
core inertia calls. All costs are ~O(k^3) classically, only square
roots are used, and there is no dependence on the relative magnitude
of the negative spectrum. Zero roots are simply omitted after the
nonzero coefficient support ends.

If S=Z K Z^T with g_lo I<=Z^T Z<=g_hi I, the positive max satisfies
g_lo*a<=||S_+||<=g_hi*a, by the generalized Rayleigh quotient. Taking
v=4g_hi*a_(j*) gives 2||S_+||<=v<=16(g_hi/g_lo)||S_+||.
For coarse ratio g_hi/g_lo<=4 this is the constant 64 used in (P5).

13. A direct thin-Gram graph contraction for a signed product
----------------------------------------------------------
This is a second actual-solver candidate for the unshifted signed-product
class, with no shifted resolvent. The parent independently checked the
algebra and small-core reconstruction below. A general dense-core split
implementation/priority gate is stated at the end, not hidden.

Assume the supplied/processed core is J=diag(D_+,-D_-), with q<=r
strictly positive diagonal entries b_j in D_+, and nonnegative entries
a_i in D_- (zero core modes may be included). Let

  S=Z J Z^T, G=Z^T Z=I+E,
  L_q=1+ceil(log_2(max(1,q))), ||E||<=1/(100 L_q).

Such embedding accuracy needs only polylogarithmically more coarse
sketch rows than constant distortion. No full tall transform is needed:
the eigencoordinates of the small core, if available, can be carried as
core maps and applied to thin blocks only.

Let the positive invariant graph of GJ be V=[I;T]. Introduce the
WEIGHTED graph W=D_- T D_+^-1. Define

  F(T,W)=E_-+ - E_-- W - T E_++ + T E_+- W.               (G1)

The invariance equation is equivalent to

  D_- T+T D_+=F(T,W) D_+,
  T=A(F), W=B(F),                                        (G2)

where A,B are the entrywise multipliers

  A_ij=b_j/(a_i+b_j),   B_ij=a_i/(a_i+b_j).

Every denominator is positive, including when a_i=0. Also A+B=1,
so at a fixed point T+W=F.

Elementary Schur-multiplier bound. Each of A,B has operator-norm
multiplier norm <=L_q, independently of all a_i,b_j. Order the columns
by b_j. Each row is monotone in [0,1]. Write it as the integral of its
0/1 level sets; these masks have a prefix in each row (reverse columns
for the opposite monotonicity). Decompose every row prefix into dyadic
intervals. At any fixed dyadic level, the column intervals are disjoint
and every row participates in at most one rectangle. Thus the associated
row sets are also disjoint, and the block extraction map has norm <=1.
There are <=L_q levels. Integration proves the claim.

On the product ball ||T||,||W||<=1/10, ||F||<=1.21||E||. The map
(T,W)->(A(F),B(F)) therefore maps the ball into itself. In the max norm,

 ||F(T,W)-F(T',W')||
   <=2.2||E|| max(||T-T'||,||W-W'||),

so its contraction factor is <=2.2 L_q||E||<1/4. Starting at zero,
O(log(1/eps)) iterations obtain operator error eps. The fixed point is
the positive graph: continuously scale E from zero, preserving this
contractive branch; G remains SPD and the inertia of G^(1/2)JG^(1/2)
does not cross zero on the nonzero support. The q-dimensional branch
therefore carries precisely all positive eigenvalues.

Per iteration, E_--W and E_+-W require one G action on the q-column
block [0;W]. E_-+ and E_++ require one initial q-column G action. All
other products are small/thin. Thus the genuine raw cost is
O(input_size*q+mq^2) per iteration, with O(mq) graph storage. There is
no use of an indefinite inverse, and no multiplication by all m raw
columns in a changing basis.

Reconstruction without a negative-bulk precision loss. Define

  L_phys=[I;-W], Y=Z L_phys,
  H_head=G_++-G_+-W, K=D_+ H_head.

The identities J[I;T]=L_phys D_+ and (GJ)[I;T]=[I;T]H_headD_+
give S Y=Y K. Hence, writing G_Y=Y^T Y,

  S_+=Y [K G_Y^-1] Y^T.                                (G3)

The bracket is symmetric PSD. For an approximate Wtilde use
Ytilde=Z[I;-Wtilde], Ktilde=D_+[G_++-G_+-Wtilde] and

  Btilde=sym(Ktilde (Ytilde^T Ytilde)^-1).

Clip Btilde to PSD and output Ytilde (Btilde)_+ Ytilde^T. All Gram
matrices of this q-column output are computable with thin raw actions.
Because ||W||<=1/10 and G near I, all the output Grams and their
inverses have absolute constant bounds. Formula (G3), ordinary product
perturbation bounds and Frobenius/small-core clipping therefore give
error O(poly(q)*||D_+||*||Wtilde-W||). A direct bounded-PSD clipping
argument may sharpen the poly(q) to a constant; either version needs
only logarithmically smaller iteration tolerance. Positive congruence
bounds give ||S_+||=Theta(||D_+||), so this is relative positive-part
error independent of ||D_-||. In particular, NEVER form Ytilde^T S
Ytilde: approximate subspace leakage could multiply the huge negative
bulk. The action Ktilde is the controlled head-only formula above.

Dense-core implementation gate. Whitening an arbitrary raw X generally
turns its supplied J into a dense indefinite core. Exact orthogonal
diagonalization is not a field/square-root primitive; naive absolute
Jacobi accuracy relative to ||J_+|| may hide log(||J_-||/||J_+||).
Thus the diagonal-core derivation is complete as a conditional solver,
while a magnitude-free sign-block reduction still needs justification.
The direct bounded-filter method in Section 10 avoids this split gate.

Potential split repair: tridiagonalize the small core in O(m^3), use
Graeffe's positive normalization, and selectively bisect ONLY the
positive interval to absolute tolerance eps*||J_+||/poly(m). Clustered
block inverse iteration with exact tridiagonal solves should compute
the positive invariant block at O(m^3 polylog(m/eps)), with the large
negative spectrum increasing rather than decreasing separation. This
needs a fully finite proof or a matching primary theorem. Negative
blocks need not then be diagonalized: tridiagonalization plus r shifted
SPD tridiagonal solves applies the Sylvester maps within the core budget.

Priority warning. Graph/Riccati invariant-subspace methods are classical.
Primary references located include Brandts, The Riccati algorithm for
eigenvalues and invariant subspaces of matrices with inexpensive action,
LAA358 (2003), https://doi.org/10.1016/S0024-3795(02)00392-0 ; and his
Computation of Invariant Subspaces of Large and Sparse Matrices,
https://cubo.ufro.cl/index.php/cubo/article/view/1709 . These describe
Picard/Newton/Riccati subspace refinement and Sylvester solves. The
candidate contribution to compare is narrower: positive-Gram embedding,
the paired weighted T/W contraction with only dimension-logarithmic
Schur multiplier loss, and the input*r reconstruction avoiding all
negative-bulk amplification. No novelty is asserted from search alone.

14. What has and has not advanced
--------------------------------
There are now several checked mechanisms beyond a plan: a randomized
stability-free residual certificate; an H-weighted forcing that controls
Riccati graph conditioning; absolute polylogarithmic exact RADI
convergence/rank bounds; a bounded whole-line rational filter and inverse
map for relative PSD projection independent of negative spectral scale;
and a direct thin-Gram contraction on an explicit sign-split core.

The original arbitrary AB relative spectral task remains unsolved. Its
real threshold H=Q-tG^-1 cannot simply be fed to the unshifted signed
solver: its small-positive-index signed factorization is not supplied
at cheap cost. A wide signed saddle representation has the large latent
dimension, and compressing it by an uncharged Gram would lose the target.
The useful-theoretical-advance claim therefore still requires a complete
costed signed-product theorem and a serious prior-art audit, while a
broad product breakthrough requires the additional threshold bridge.

15. Closest primary: rational low-rank matrix-function updates
-----------------------------------------------------------
The serious comparator is not just a PSD-projection paper. It is
Beckermann, Cortinovis, Kressner and Schweitzer, Low-rank updates of
matrix functions II: Rational Krylov methods, SIAM J. Numer. Anal.59
(2021),1325--1347, https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1362553 ; primary preprint
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.11501 . I read the relevant primary Sections
3.1--3.2,4.2 and5, rather than inferring the theorem from an abstract.

Their Theorem3.3 proves exactness of the low-rank-update approximation
for rational functions with the prescribed poles. For Hermitian A and
D=UU^T, the Hermitian simplification in their Eq4 gives

 r(A+D)-r(A)=Q[r(Q^T(A+D)Q)-r(Q^TAQ)]Q^T,                (U1)

when Q spans the rational Krylov space of A,U containing those poles.
Their Theorem4.5 gives the familiar four-times rational-approximation
error under an analyticity assumption. For our continuous bounded f,
the same FOUR spectral-theorem errors follow directly from (U1), with
no appeal to analytic continuation across the kink at zero.

Take A=-B<=0 and S=A+UU^T. Both f(A) and f(Q^TAQ) vanish. If r
approximates f uniformly on the whole real line within eta, then

 ||f(S)-Q f(Q^TSQ)Q^T||<=4eta.                           (U2)

The positive index of Q^TSQ is at most rank(U)<=r, so after the psi
map the result is rank<=r and approximates S_+. This removes Gaussian
range conditioning, the chi factor, and the second full filtered action
from the direct Section10 construction. It uses only negative PSD-base
resolvents on the r update columns.

Therefore a claim that generic low-rank rational-update geometry is new
would be false. The potentially new corollary is narrower: the bounded
whole-line f/psi trick, condition-free positive normalization, precision
budget, and input-linear raw-factor implementation with an error relative
to ||S_+|| independent of the entire negative spectral scale. Whether
this corollary was already explicitly available remains unresolved.

Their Section5 discusses matrix-sign updates, including a squaring
transformation and spectral-set convergence issues. It does not state
the complete relative signed-PSD raw-factor theorem considered here.
This is a scope observation, not a priority certificate.

16. A fully costable classical-update composition (candidate theorem)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
This section removes the raw signed-resolvent oracle from the signed-PSD
theorem by using a negative PSD base and a small positive update. It
does not remove the real-threshold obstacle in the original AB task.

Input: raw tall X(N,m), supplied symmetric J(m,m), S=X J X^T, and
positive index q<=r. A coarse OSE gives implicit S=Z K Z^T, supported
core dimension k<=m, and g_lo I<=G=Z^TZ<=g_hi I with constant ratio.
The OSE/setup has its usual input-plus-core cost. A raw G action on a
thin block costs O(input_size*blockwidth+m^2*blockwidth); no full tall
Z is materialized. The exact supported core inertia supplies q. If q=0,
return the exact zero PSD projection.

Step1, positive normalization and a well-scaled positive update. Section11
gives ahat in [a/2,4a], a=||K_+||, using square roots only and ~m^3
core work. Set v=4g_hi ahat, so

  2||S_+||<=v<=16(g_hi/g_lo)||S_+||<=64||S_+||.

Compute a rank-q PSD core P approximating K_+ within tau in norm, where
tau=eps*ahat/(1000 sqrt(m)). This is a SMALL explicit-core instance of
Section10: apply the bounded rational filter to the identity using exact
core inverses, diagonalize only the resulting bounded matrix to absolute
accuracy, clip to q positive values, and apply psi. It costs ~m^3 with
logarithmic precision factors independent of ||K_-||/||K_+||. It does
not need a raw-input Gram or an exact eigenbasis of K.

Define

  Ncore=P-K+2tau I >=tau I,
  S'=Z(P-Ncore)Z^T=S-2tau ZZ^T.

Thus n_+(S')<=q and S'=U U^T-B, with U=Z factor(P) and
B=Z Ncore Z^T>=0. Only U's q columns are physically lifted. Ncore is
stored as a small matrix and never applied to all raw tall columns.
The positive update is well scaled:

  ||U||^2<=g_hi(a+tau)=O(||S_+||).

Moreover PSD projection is nonexpansive in Frobenius norm, so

 ||S_+-S'_+||<=||S-S'||_F
             <=2tau g_hi sqrt(k)=O(eps)||S_+||.          (U3)

The constants above leave a large error-budget margin. Scale S',U,B by
v so the positive spectrum is <=1/2 and ||U|| is bounded by an absolute
constant (increase the fixed normalization constant if needed).

Step2, cheap PSD-base resolvents. Let A=-Z N Z^T, N=Ncore/v>0,
and let z=it be one of the imaginary poles of Section10. For a right
hand side Zb, its resolvent solution remains in range(Z); writing it
as Zy reduces the system to

  (G+z N^-1)y=-N^-1 b.                                  (U4)

Set c0=(g_lo+g_hi)/2 and precondition by Pz=c0 I+zN^-1.
Then ||Pz^-1||<=1/c0 and

 ||Pz^-1(G-c0I)||<=(g_hi-g_lo)/(g_hi+g_lo)=rho0<1.

Fixed Richardson iteration therefore converges geometrically, uniformly
in t and the spectrum of N. The inverse of Pz is a SMALL known-core
operation, charged O(m^3) per pole; every Richardson action is a raw
G action on q columns. Since lambda_min(N)>=tau/v=eps/poly(m), all
right-hand-side, solution and tolerance bounds have only polynomial
dependence on m,1/eps. There is no negative spectral magnitude factor.

In exact arithmetic this supplies arbitrarily small ABSOLUTE resolvent
action errors using logarithmically many raw passes. It is not a theorem
about floating-point cancellation inside a raw factor action.

Step3, use direct rational-update formulas to avoid an inexact-Krylov
basis-conditioning gap. A finitely accurate Richardson solve does not
create the EXACT rational Krylov space in (U1); merely invoking that
identity on an approximately computed Q would be unjustified.
Instead use the classical rational-update / Woodbury formula, explicitly
credited to Bernstein--Van Loan in BCKS's introduction and Section3.3.
For each pole z,

 Vz=(A-zI)^-1 U, Dz=I+U^T Vz,

 (S'-zI)^-1-(A-zI)^-1=-Vz Dz^-1 Vbarz^*.                (U5)

The crucial inexpensive conditioning bound is

 Dz^-1=I-U^T(S'-zI)^-1U,
 ||Dz^-1||<=1+||U||^2/|Im z|.                           (U6)

Thus the small feedback core is only polynomially ill conditioned at
the selected t_min=poly(eps); no eigenvalue of B or S' enters the bound.
The resulting rational-update matrix has rank at most q times the
number of poles, and its factors use only the q-column PSD-base solves
in (U4). The rational function's constant cancels in the difference.

Uniform error budget. Normalize ||U||<=1 for notation and put
t_min=min |Im z|. If ||Vtilde_z-Vz||<=d for every pole and
d<=1/[2(1+t_min^-1)], the resolvent-identity inverse perturbation bound
and (U6) give

 ||Ttilde_z-Tz||<=32(1+t_min^-1)^4 d,
 Tz=-Vz Dz^-1 Vbarz^*.

Let Wcoef=1+sum|c_z| for the partial-fraction coefficients of r. Choose

 d<=eta/[100 Wcoef (1+t_min^-1)^4].                      (U7)

All poles in Section10 lie between eta/256 and 512/eta in magnitude.
Half-shifted dyadic nodes have |1-t_j^2|>=Omega(1/Q), so elementary
partial fractions have Wcoef<=poly(log(1/eta),1/eta). Hence (U7)
requires only logarithmically many Richardson iterations in m,1/eps.
Conjugate pairing gives an actual real symmetric represented update.

The exact scalar rational approximation contributes two errors eta:
r(S')-r(A) is within 2eta of f(S'), because f(A)=0. Inexact updates
add at most eta by (U7). Thus the represented low-rank matrix R obeys
||R-f(S')||<=3eta. QR its computed factors and diagonalize only the
bounded small core. Clip to [0,2/5] and retain at most q positive
eigenvalues. This changes R by at most 3eta, giving Fhat with

  ||Fhat-f(S')||<=6eta, rank(Fhat)<=q.

Then v psi(Fhat) approximates S'_+ within (25/2)v eta. Taking
eta=eps/10000 and combining (U3) leaves total error below eps||S_+||,
with room for small-core Jacobi and scalar-coefficient preparation.
No unfiltered S'-Ritz action or ill-conditioned rational basis is used.

Total ordinary-arithmetic build cost:

  ~O(input_size*q+m^3),                                 (U8)

where every hidden factor is logarithmic in m,1/eps,1/delta. The number
of poles and temporary factor columns is O(q log^2(1/eps)), before
the harmless dimension/accuracy allocations. Orthogonalization costs
N*O(q^2 polylog), dominated by input_size*q polylog while q<=m;
if the temporary width reaches m, the full supported core branch fits
the same polylogarithmic budget. Known-core inverses, all projected
products and bounded core diagonalizations fit m^3 polylog. Expansion
of all N^2 entries adds O(N^2 q) if explicitly required.

This is a substantial concrete composition, but its status is still a
CANDIDATE costed theorem until the parent independently audits the
setup and finite coefficient/linear-solve budget. It is a corollary of
strong classical rational-update machinery plus our filter/normalization
lemmas, not a claim to have invented Woodbury, rational Krylov or
semidefinite projection. The signed unshifted scope is essential.
