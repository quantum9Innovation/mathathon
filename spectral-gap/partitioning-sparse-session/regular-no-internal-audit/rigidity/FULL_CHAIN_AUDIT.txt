1. Exact theorem and admissible graph model
------------------------------------------
The true frame is fixed, integer, and connected, with 1<=k<=K and EVERY
row sum the same integer d in [3,D]. Internal degrees may be zero or one.
Positive class sizes obey balance and necessary internal parity. All
active internal regular and cross biregular factors are independently
uniform simple graphs. The supplied K,D are fixed constants.

Under exactly that model, every equitable partition into at most K
nonempty classes is, with high probability, an equitable coarsening of
the planted partition. The partition is therefore the unique finest
partition within that bounded class family. The theorem is not a claim
of a globally finest equitable partition without a bound on class count.

Row regularity is actually used to cancel root entropies; merely bounded
unequal row sums are not substituted into this extension. Random and
independent cross factors are also used in the exact product formula.
This does not inherit the arbitrary-cross scope of earlier different
strong-mixing arguments. The one-class case is legitimately included:
it is the ordinary uniform simple d-regular model for d>=3.

Connected balance gives alpha_min>=1/[K*D^(K-1)]. Along a frame path each
class-size ratio is a ratio of positive integers at most D; every class
is at most D^(K-1) times the smallest. A connected consistent frame fixes
its positive ratios, and only finitely many frames occur under K,D.
Consequently uniform eventual simplicity constants, support constants,
entropy gaps, and admissible-size thresholds are available. The same
argument handles k=1 directly with its single proportion equal to one.

2. Exact factorial formula with internal degrees zero and one
------------------------------------------------------------
For each labeled vertex the ordered star records the root label and the
target label on EVERY distinguishable stub. Assigning type T_i to its
N_i vertices contributes N_i!/product_s T_i(s)!. This already assigns
the root labels, so there is correctly no second root multinomial.

For an active internal factor, categories (x,y) and (y,x) must pair.
For x<y their bijections number m_ii(x,y)!, while category (x,x) has
(m_ii(x,x)-1)!! pairings and requires even count. Divide by the total
(N_i*a_i-1)!!. If a_i=1 this is exactly the ordinary perfect-matching
count. If a_i=0 there is NO internal factor or internal table.

Each cross category has m_ij(x,y)! bijections to its reversed category,
and the denominator is (N_i*d_ij)!. Equal pool sizes follow from balance.
Factor independence gives the displayed product formula. Conversely,
every labeled-stub configuration and root labeling determine all ordered
stars uniquely. Thus the count is exact, including loops and parallel
edges before conditioning. A loop supplies two neighbor stubs and two
to the adjacency diagonal; the equation and count use the same convention.

The pooled pair laws have the required marginals: first marginals are
root frequencies because every vertex has the same number of each typed
stub; the second follows from the reversed table. This applies before
symmetrization and for nonuniform proportions.

Deleting ANY physical slot determines its vector with coefficient one.
Thus a legal field star has exactly q^d possibilities, q=p^r, regardless
of the presence of an internal slot and regardless of R's rank or Jordan
form. Characteristic dividing d is irrelevant: dividing by d later is
an operation on real entropy, not finite-field linear algebra.

3. Uniform remainder, type count, and normalization
--------------------------------------------------
The exact main terms are
  sum_i N_i H(star_i)
   -sum_(i:a_i>0)(N_i*a_i/2)H(pair_ii)
   -sum_(i<j)N_i*d_ij H(pair_ij).
Call this unnormalized exponent Psi. The rigidity and composition use
Phi=Psi/n, so their negative constant gap becomes n times that constant
in the exponential first moment. The composition retains this factor n.
No half-factor at internal edges or cross-edge orientation is missing.

I checked the uniform factorial inequalities including zero counts.
Writing f(u)=u logu-u, the factorial error is nonnegative and at most
log(u+1)+1. For even u, the double-factorial error relative to f(u)/2
has absolute value at most twice that amount. The root factor needs one
upper error, the cross factor at most q^2, and the internal factor at
most q(q-1)/2+2q+2<=2q^2 for q>=2.

With q_*=p^K this yields
  F=K+[K(K+3)/2]*q_*^2.
The number of types is at most (n+1)^(K*q_*^d). Thus for p=2 and d<=D,
the conservative shared exponent is correctly
  B=K*2^(K*D)+K+[K(K+3)/2]*2^(2K).
The prefactor (3D)^F safely bounds exp(F)*(Dn+1)^F/(n+1)^F.
All constants are independent of candidate R and n.

For the integer one-hot count the same category formula applies; its
combinatorics does not depend on a field. A legal omitted unit vector is
unique if it exists, so at most r^d stars occur. A fixed generous alphabet
bound supplies the same uniform polynomial remainder. In the r=1 case,
the defect is zero; using the larger q_* bound also avoids any q=1
error-coefficient issue. No positive lower bound on candidate masses is
needed for counting boundary or zero-frequency types.

4. All-slot cancellation and simplicity conditioning
-----------------------------------------------------
Within-type permutations preserve pooled edge laws, root laws, and the
local sum constraint while entropy concavity increases star entropy.
No slots of different old neighbor types are permuted. The averaged law
need not be an empirical integer type; it is used only for an inequality.

Lossless deletion of each of d slots and averaging gives exactly
  H(star_i)=H(root_i)+(d-1)/d sum_s H(Y_s|root_i)
                         -(1/d)sum_t J_it.
After substitution, edge reversal and common row sum d cancel every
root entropy. The remaining identity is
  -Phi=(d-2)/d *sum_(i<=j)w_ij I_ij
                     +(1/d)sum_i alpha_i sum_t J_it >=0,
where w_ii=alpha_i*a_i/2 and w_ij=alpha_i*d_ij for i<j.
The identity includes internal double counting correctly and works when
all internal factors are absent. It proves the configuration-model
polynomial moment for every candidate matrix over the finite field.

The simplicity means are correct: internal loops have mean (a-1)/2 and
internal unordered double pairs have mean (a-1)^2/4 for a>=2. An active
cross factor has double-pair mean (u-1)(v-1)/2. A zero internal factor
contributes zero, and a degree-one internal factor is always simple.
Cross factors with a degree-one side have no possible multiple edges;
in particular a (1,1) matching is always simple.

For each fixed factorial moment order, disjoint bad patterns have the
product limit. Consistent overlapping DISTINCT patterns have edge
excess at least one; shared-edge double pairs produce at least three
parallel edges on two vertices. Their contributions are O(1/min N_i).
Bonferroni truncation for the zero event gives the stated Poisson
exponential. With finitely many independent factors the product
simplicity probability tends to exp(-Lambda)>0, where the source
correctly omits the invalid negative (a^2-1)/4 value at a=0.

The bound Lambda<=K^2*D^2 is conservative and valid. Simple factors have
constant labeled-stub multiplicity, including degrees zero and one.
Conditioning therefore gives the intended independent uniform SIMPLE
factors, and costs only a constant in a nonnegative expectation.
Importantly this moment is UNCONDITIONAL over the simple graph draw,
including rare graphs with very large modular nullity.

5. Real gap and macroscopic exclusion
------------------------------------
The real proof has no internal-slot assumption. For omission t the
auxiliary retained law keeps the root law and makes retained neighbors
independent from their root-independent marginals. Its divergence is
kappa_it=J_it+sum_(s!=t)I_is. For one fixed old type i,
  alpha_i sum_t kappa_it<=2*d*(d-1)/(d-2)*L.
The bound uses alpha_i sum_s I_is<=2*sum_edges w_ij I_ij and compares
the J and edge coefficients separately. It does not sum local incident
edges as if globally disjoint.

Using a fixed marginal mean mu_t in
  f_t=||R_X-sum_(s!=t)e_(Y_s)-mu_t||^2
gives actual expectation V_t and product expectation
sum_(s!=t)V_s+B_i, with the SAME nonnegative B_i for every omission.
The sum of differences is (d-2)sum_s V_s+d*B_i. Individual differences
may have either sign; the proof correctly uses their sum and bounds
their absolute values.

Every OCCUPIED row R_X is a nonnegative degree-count row of mass d.
The retained sum plus mu_t is also nonnegative of mass d under either
law. Thus 0<=f_t<=2*d^2 even on illegal auxiliary tuples. Pinsker and
Cauchy--Schwarz give
  Phi<=-alpha_min*(d-2)^3*delta^2/[4*d^6*(d-1)]
      <=-alpha_min*delta^2/(8*D^6).
The bound is valid without injectivity or distinct rows of R.

Some old root variance is at least delta. A positive-degree old type
has an incoming neighbor by balance, possibly itself, so the local bound
detects that variance even when k=1. Equality implies all old root laws
are points; matching marginals and the legal integer sum force QT=TR.
The converse point maps have deterministic laws and zero entropy.

This is an INTEGER/real variance calculation, not modular variance.
Modular solutions are used only for enumeration; integer-verified
one-hot solutions receive the rigidity estimate. The sources keep this
distinction intact.

At fixed defect eta, the same exact count gives polynomial(n) times
exp[-alpha_min*eta^2*n/(8*D^6)]. Every equitable partition of a graph of
maximum degree D has a bounded nonnegative integer quotient, so a
finite union over all r<=K and all such R covers every candidate.
Disconnected R and arbitrarily small candidate classes are covered.

6. Arbitrary-support exposure and near-point closure
----------------------------------------------------
For bad S, every outside neighbor has at least two edges from S. With
s=|S|, outside-neighbor count w, internal edges a, boundary edges b, and
e=a+b, the identities 2a+b=d*s and b>=2w give
  e-s-w>=(d-2)*s/2.
The integer ceiling is valid. Loops count once among edges a and twice
in the degree sum. Every active typed stub pool has size at least rho*n.
For s<=rho*n/(4D), each prescribed-pair denominator is at least rho*n/2.

Counting S,W, selected W stubs, and their pairings gives the stated
binomial factors, 2^(Dw), and (2Ds)^e. All S stubs are fixed. Invalid
typed prescriptions and pairs not touching S can be overcounted safely.
The rearrangement into
  exp(s+w)*2^(Dw)*(4D/rho)^e
    *(s/n)^(e-s-w)*(s/w)^w
is correct, including the w=0 convention. The inequality
(s/w)^w<=exp(s/euler) and the displayed constant C absorb all e,w sums.
Thus E Z_s<=C^s(s/n)^ceil((d-2)s/2) for arbitrary support composition.

The chosen eta makes successive u_s ratios at most one-half for s>=2.
The geometric sum is at most 2*C^2*(2/n)^(d-2). Singletons cannot be bad
in the simple positive-degree graph. Dividing by the positive simplicity
probability yields failure O_(K,D)(n^(-(d-2))). Although the displayed
eta uses d, taking a minimum over the finite d in [3,D] makes a single
eta(K,D); the algorithm does not need to know or compute it.

This ONE graph event excludes small row support of AV=VR over any
field, any vector dimension, and any R: an outside unique edge would
give a nonzero V row on the left and zero on the right. No coefficient
or candidate-matrix union bound is missing.

For Y near an arbitrary point map JT, including non-onto T, changed

Evaluating the VERIFIED INTEGER equation there gives QT=TR exactly.
Consequently V=Y-JT satisfies AV=VR with support S, and the common
support event forces S empty. This closes tiny extra classes near

7. Unknown-frame enumeration, cost, cap, and memory
---------------------------------------------------
The candidate family contains at most sum_(r<=K)(D+1)^(r^2) matrices,
a fixed number. The planted (J,Q) is included on EVERY graph draw.
Vectorization gives the binary operator I_r tensor A-R^T tensor I_n.
Its kernel has exactly 2^h_R matrices. One-hot labels remain distinct
over F_2^r, so every integer candidate appears in that kernel. Candidate
row sums different from observed d are harmless: integer verification
rejects any occupied incompatible row.

Dense binary elimination costs O_K(n^3) bit work and O_K(n^2) bits of
storage. Combining all basis vectors to generate one kernel element
costs at most O_K(n^2) bit work without optimizations. Row tests,
nonemptiness flags and integer neighbor checks fit that loose estimate.
Nonemptiness can be checked with K Boolean flags; if class sizes are
counted instead their O(log n) counters also fit the O(n^2) allowance.
Neighbor-count counters have bounded bit length for fixed D.

The full kernel list is not stored. The linear system/basis plus the
current vector and best accepted partition use O_K(n^2) bits. A cap
counter uses O_(K,D)(log n) additional bits, swallowed by polynomial
space for fixed supplied bounds. Bounded-cost enumeration/verification
chunks and counter overhead fit the generous stated bit-work bound.

Using the UNCONDITIONAL moment, including expensive rare draws, gives
  E[work]<=C(K,D)[n^3+n^2(n+1)^B]
          <=C'(K,D)*n^(B+3).
This does not condition work on the rigidity event or on eventual
correctness. A deterministic polynomial budget n^(B+5), accounting
for its bookkeeping, interrupts with probability O_(K,D)(n^-2) by
Markov. On interruption the algorithm explicitly ABSTAINS; it does
not certify the best candidate from an incomplete enumeration.

On complete enumeration and the structural event, every accepted Y=JT
has nonempty classes, so T is onto and r<=k. Since the planted candidate
has r=k, the maximum is k. An onto map between k old types and k labels
is a permutation. This recovers the partition and k, and exact neighbor
counts recover Q up to the same unavoidable class renaming. Thus the
truncated algorithm has worst-case polynomial work and high-probability
correct recovery, with a large explicit exponent rather than a practical
runtime claim.

8. Random-lift and projection interpretation
-------------------------------------------
For a fixed connected SIMPLE d-regular base H, Q=A_H has zero diagonal
and a single cross stub on each base edge. Its balanced fibers have
equal sizes N, and the independent uniform bijections on base edges are
exactly independent uniform perfect matchings. This is the standard
random N-lift distribution, up to vertex names within each fiber.

The resulting graph is automatically simple: no base loop can create
a loop, no two base edges join the same type pair, and a matching has
no parallel edges. Every N, including odd N, is admissible. There is
no hidden even-N condition and no simplicity cost in this specialization.

The theorem recovers the fiber SETS, up to their names, and their simple
quotient, hence H up to graph isomorphism. If H is supplied with fixed
names, composing the quotient labeling with any isomorphism to H gives
a covering projection; different label choices differ by base
automorphisms. It does not recover or assign canonical coordinates
within each fiber. No unproved assertion about a transitive deck group
or a regular covering map is being used.

Another covering projection with at most K fibers induces an equitable
partition, so it is a coarsening of the recovered fiber partition.
At the original k fibers it has the same fibers up to permutation.
Coarse partitions of a base such as the cube are therefore allowed and
do not contradict the finest bounded-class claim. No spectral old/new
gap, real eigenspace simplicity, or exclusion of exact new eigenvalue
atoms is needed. Growing base size, arbitrary deterministic lifts and
noisy edits are not covered.
