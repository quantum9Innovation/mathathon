1. Exact arbitrary-output enumeration, including loops
------------------------------------------------------
At n labeled vertices pair dn labeled half-edges uniformly. A loop
contributes two to the diagonal of C_n. For fixed q define n_a,
nu_a=n_a/n, and let M_ab count stubs rooted at q-label a whose partner
has q-label b. Then

  M=M^T>=0,        M_aa even,        sum_b M_ab=dn_a.

For a target r, every vertex i requires its d ORDERED partner colors
to sum to r_i. There are exactly p^(d-1) such tuples at each vertex,
also when p divides d. Let X^(t) be the color-count vector of a
uniform tuple with sum t, and Z_a the sum of independent X^(r_i)
over vertices of input color a. If

  W(M)=product_{a<b}M_ab! product_a(M_aa-1)!!,
  T=(dn-1)!!,

then assigning all partner colors to labeled stubs and pairing them
gives exactly

  P(C_n q=r)=p^((d-1)n)/T
                 *sum_M W(M) product_a P(Z_a=M_a).         (A1)

Each pairing determines its partner-label assignment uniquely. On
off-diagonal color classes, compatible pairs are arbitrary bijections
between the two prescribed stub sets. Within a color class, they are
pairings of distinct labeled stubs, giving (M_aa-1)!!. These include
loops correctly but never pair a stub with itself. No random-entry
independence assumption is present.

An equivalent coefficient formula replaces the row probability by
[z^(M_a)] product_{i:q_i=a}P_(r_i)(z), with P_t the sum of all d-tuple
monomials of field sum t. This proves arbitrary-target enumeration
directly, not by shifting the zero-target matrix ensemble.

For unconditioned d-tuples let Y_a be the count vector of dn_a iid
uniform field labels, and put

  B(M)=W(M) product_a (dn_a)!/product_b M_ab!.

These multinomials assign arbitrary partner colors to all stubs in
each input color class. Thus

  sum_M B(M)=T,
  W(M) product_a P(Y_a=M_a)=p^(-dn)B(M).                  (A2)

In particular B(M)/T is the EXACT baseline distribution of the
pairing's color table, not a surrogate multinomial approximation.


2. Conditioned tuple laws: covariance and maximal difference lattice
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix d>=3. In a uniform tuple of prescribed sum t, any one or two
specified positions are independent uniform labels: at least one
remaining position completes their sum. Hence the count vector X^(t)
has the same mean and covariance for EVERY t:

  mu=(d/p)1,
  Sigma=(d/p)I-(d/p^2)11^T.                               (A3)

The count vector of an unconditioned d-tuple has exactly the same
mean and covariance. Drop count coordinate zero. Sigma is then
strictly positive on the retained p-1 coordinates; all laws have
fixed finite support. This is why an arbitrary, even highly
inhomogeneous, target sequence has the same leading Gaussian law.

The necessary difference-lattice constraints for X^(t) are total
zero and weighted sum zero modulo p. To prove they are sufficient,
take a character theta whose phase sum_j theta_(a_j) is constant
over all tuples with sum t. Compare their first two entries
(a,b) and (a+u,b-u). The other d-2 positions can complete any chosen
pair and stay unchanged, because d>=3. Therefore, modulo 2pi,

  theta_(a+u)-theta_a=theta_b-theta_(b-u)                  (A4)

for every a,b,u. For fixed u this difference is independent of the
starting point. The increments form an additive character of Fp.
Thus theta_b=theta_0+2pi ell b/p for one ell in Fp.

After deleting coordinate zero, there are precisely p characters
annihilating support differences. Positive covariance gives full
real rank; finite-index lattice duality now identifies the lattice
as exactly the weighted-sum-zero sublattice, of index p in Z^(p-1).
The unconditioned d-tuple count lattice has index one, since replacing
one tuple coordinate 0 by any label gives the coordinate generators.
There is no further parity, t, p|d, or n-residue restriction.


3. Uniform relative row local limit for ANY target sequence
----------------------------------------------------------
Let t_1,...,t_N be arbitrary, Z=sum_i X_i^(t_i), independently.
All variables have (A3) and a common maximal-character set by (A4).
For retained-coordinate m with

  m-Nmu=O(sqrt(N)log N),

the source claims

  P(Z=m)=(1+o(1))p P(Y=m)                                (A5)

on weighted-sum congruence sum_b b m_b=sum_i t_i, and zero otherwise,
uniformly over the whole target sequence. I independently checked
the uniformity as follows.

Write Psi_N(z)=sum_i log E exp(z.X_i^(t_i)). Every derivative through
any fixed order is uniformly O(N) in a fixed neighborhood: only p
fixed finite-support laws occur. At zero its Hessian is N Sigma.
The Hessian stays bounded above and below by constant multiples of
N for small real z. The quantitative inverse function theorem then
gives a tilt with mean m, uniformly over the sequence, satisfying

  z=N^(-1)Sigma^(-1)(m-Nmu)+O(||m-Nmu||^2/N^2),
  ||z||=O(log N/sqrt(N)).

Undoing this tilt contributes

  exp[-(m-Nmu)^T Sigma^(-1)(m-Nmu)/(2N)
             +O(log^3 N/sqrt(N))].                       (A6)

Under the tilt the covariance sum is N Sigma+O(N||z||). Each
support atom remains bounded below uniformly, since the family of
laws is finite and z is small. Outside fixed neighborhoods of the
common maximal characters, every tilted characteristic function
has modulus <=rho<1, independently of its target t_i. Thus their
product is bounded by rho^N for ANY sequence.

Near each maximum, scaling frequency by sqrt(N) gives the same
Gaussian determinant, with uniformly o(1) relative error on a log N
frequency window and Gaussian domination for the rest. At character
ell, the exact phase of X^(t_i) is exp(2pi i ell t_i/p). Fourier
extraction adds exp(-2pi i ell sum_b b m_b/p). All p maxima therefore
contribute identically precisely on the stated congruence class.
Off that class the probability is exactly zero from support alone.

The same tilted calculation for the unconditioned tuple count has
one maximum, the same quadratic term, and the same covariance
determinant. Comparing it with (A6) proves (A5) as a RELATIVE estimate.
An absolute-error local CLT would have been insufficient here; the
source supplies and uses the stronger relative form correctly.

The real means in this argument lie in the interior of the support
convex hull uniformly for all t. This follows from positive covariance,
positive weights on the finite support, and the finite list of targets.
It justifies small tilts for all displayed nearby means. Fourier
inversion also implies genuine support at all central affine lattice
points eventually; no semigroup-normality assumption is needed.


4. Uniformly negligible actual and baseline type tails
------------------------------------------------------
The central proof uses a moderate type window

  R_n={M: max_ab |M_ab-dn nu_a nu_b|<=sqrt(n)log n}.

Its tail estimate is independently valid for arbitrary output r.
For a conditional row pi_a(b)=m_ab/nu_a, m=M/(dn), evaluate the
nonnegative row polynomial at pi_a(b)^((d-1)/d). The AM--GM row
inequality of Section 7 gives a value <=1 for EVERY vertex target.
Combining coefficient evaluation with uniform Stirling bounds gives

 P(C_nq=r,type=M)
  <=(n+1)^A exp[-nH(nu)-(d-2)n D(m||nu nu^T)/2].          (A7)

All constants hold uniformly at zero entries, by limits or the
zero-exponent convention. No local limit theorem is used in (A7).

For balanced input counts n_a=n/p+O(sqrt(n log n)),
nH(nu)=n log p-O(log n). Outside R_n, Pinsker bounds the relative
entropy below by c log^2 n/n. Since d-2>0, summing (A7) over
polynomially many tables gives

  sup_r P(C_nq=r,M notin R_n)=o(p^(-n)).                  (A8)

For the exact baseline B(M)/T, Stirling instead gives a polynomial
factor times exp[-dn D(m||nu nu^T)/2]. Thus

  P_baseline(M in R_n)=1-o(1).                            (A9)

Both estimates are uniform over the balanced input and survive
enlarging or shrinking the window by a bounded additive amount,
or changing its fixed positive multiplicative constant. The
exp(-c log^2 n) factor dominates every fixed power of n.

For M in R_n, row a differs from d n_a/p by O(sqrt(n)log n): the
additional center difference is d n_a(nu_b-1/p)=O(sqrt(n log n)).
Since every n_a is asymptotic to n/p, all rows satisfy the relative
row local limit window in Section 3, uniformly and simultaneously.


5. Symmetric compatible-table lattice and the p^(p-1) factor
-----------------------------------------------------------
Write Phi_a(M)=sum_b b M_ab modulo p, and S_a=sum_(i:q_i=a) r_i.
For symmetric M with exact row sums dn_a,

  sum_a Phi_a(M)=d sum_b b n_b.                            (A10)

Necessity of the global target sum is immediate. To show there are
no further restrictions IN THE CENTRAL WINDOW, use the explicit
bounded integral shifts

  Z_j=2(e_j-e_0)(e_j-e_0)^T,          j=1,...,p-1.

They are symmetric, have zero row sums, and have even diagonal.
Direct multiplication by the vector of field labels gives

  Phi(Z_j)=2j(e_j-e_0).                                   (A11)

Because p is odd, these vectors are a basis of the total-zero
hyperplane in Fp^p. Bounded integer combinations with coefficients
0,...,p-1 realize every difference between congruence vectors
satisfying (A10). The bound on the entries depends only on p.
For central tables entries are order n, so adding these shifts
preserves nonnegativity at all sufficiently large n. All actual
parity requirements, including EVEN DIAGONALS, are preserved.

The exact formula

  B(M)=product_a(dn_a)! /
       [product_(a<b)M_ab! product_a 2^(M_aa/2)(M_aa/2)!]

implies for each such bounded zero-row-sum shift Z,

  log[B(M+Z)/B(M)]
     =-(1/2)sum_ab Z_ab log(dn/p^2)+O(log n/sqrt(n))
     =O(log n/sqrt(n)).                                  (A12)

This uses positive central entries and bounded factorial shifts;
the first-order common term vanishes because sum_ab Z_ab=0.
The uniform input displacement from exact counts n/p changes the
error only by O(sqrt(log n/n)), already covered in (A12).

For completeness, a bounded shift might change membership at the
window boundary. Compare a window shrunk by its maximum entry shift
with the original and an enlarged window. By (A9) the intervening
boundary mass is o(1). Shifting from one congruence class to another
and using (A12) proves that their central baseline masses differ by
o(1), uniformly in the balanced input. There are exactly p^(p-1)
allowed classes and their total mass is 1-o(1). Thus every allowed
class has mass

  p^(-(p-1))+o(1).                                       (A13)

This calculation neither assumes parity independence nor invokes
an uncomputed Gaussian lattice constant. It also shows why p|d or
p|n creates no obstruction: (A11) is independent of either division.


6. The balanced all-target leading constant
-------------------------------------------
Apply the row ratio (A5) in (A1) for every one of the p nonempty
input-color rows. The product contributes p^p. Use (A2) to convert
the remaining unconditioned row counts to exact baseline mass.
For a globally admissible target the central part is

  (1+o(1))p^(-n+p)
       P_baseline(M in R_n, Phi(M)=S).

By (A13) this equals (1+o(1))p^(-n+1). The tail is o(p^(-n))
by (A8). If the global sum is incompatible, C_n1=d1 makes the
probability exactly zero. Hence uniformly over balanced q and ALL r,

  P(C_nq=r)=(1+o(1))p^(-n+1)

on the sum hyperplane, exactly as claimed. All errors keep p,d fixed.
The net normalization is p^p/p^(p-1)=p. No missing factor depending
on n, row parity, or target type remains.


7. All-target noncentral entropy tails
---------------------------------------
For clarity I also reconstructed the two remaining noncentral
ingredients instead of relying only on the delegated audit.
For a probability vector pi and alpha=(d-1)/d set

  F_t(pi)=sum_(a_1+...+a_d=t) product_j pi(a_j)^alpha.

For each tuple, AM--GM bounds its product by the average over j of
product_(i!=j) pi(a_i). Sum over the constrained tuple: every omitted
coordinate is determined uniquely, and each remaining product sums
to one. Therefore F_t<=1, for EVERY t.

If equality holds and S=supp(pi), every d-1 supported labels must
complete to a supported label. Fixing d-2 labels gives a reflection
preserving S. Varying one of those fixed labels and composing two
reflections gives translation invariance by any difference of two
support points. In a prime field nontrivial support therefore fills
Fp. Since d>=3, any two labels can occur in one constrained tuple;
AM--GM equality forces their probabilities equal. Thus equality
requires uniform pi, or a point mass at a with t=da. This reasoning
does not assume p does not divide d.

The uniform quadratic expansion is also correct. Pairwise independence
of conditioned tuple positions cancels all quadratic cross terms,
leaving

  log F_t(u+z)=-(d-1)p ||z||^2/(2d)+O(||z||^3),           (A14)

uniformly over the finitely many t. A largest-atom tuple gives the
positive lower bound F_*>=p^(-(d-1)), where F_*=max_t F_t.
The logarithm of F_* is therefore uniformly bounded even at the
boundary of the probability simplex.

Applying coefficient evaluation at each row as in (A7), but retaining
its F_* factor, and summing input vectors of fixed empirical profile
cancels the factor exp[-nH(nu)]. The resulting summed rate is

  Psi(m)=-(d-2)D(m||nu nu^T)/2
              +sum_a nu_a log F_*(m_a/nu_a).             (A15)

It is continuous on the full compact symmetric matrix simplex,
including zero rows. It is nonpositive, and its only zeros are
u u^T and the p point masses e_a e_a^T: the first loss forces
m=nu nu^T and then the equality cases above classify nu.

Near uniformity put Q=sum_a nu_a||pi_a-u||^2. Symmetry implies
sum_a nu_a pi_a=nu, giving Q>=||nu-u||^2. Row-constant orthogonality
also gives ||m-u u^T||^2<=(1+1/p)Q. Equation (A14) consequently
implies a strictly negative quadratic bound for (A15) in both
||m-u u^T|| and ||nu-u||. Away from a fixed point-mass neighborhood,
compactness extends it over the remaining domain.

Every factorial error and every number of types is polynomial in n
for fixed p,d. Moving sup_r inside the finite sum over type matrices
only enlarges it; no enumeration of the p^n possible targets is made.
Choosing a sufficiently large fixed B thus bounds the sum of maximum
atoms outside ||nu-u||^2<=B log(n+1)/n, away from point masses,
by any prescribed inverse power of n. This is the required all-target
middle estimate with all target uniformity charged.


8. Nearconstant atoms, uniformly over the ENTIRE target vector
--------------------------------------------------------------
Write q=a1+z, support(z)=S of size k. Translation only shifts the
output by da1, so the maximum atom is unchanged. Fix any output r
for Cz and put T={v notin S:r_v!=0}. If |T|>dk the event is
impossible. Otherwise |T|<=dk.

Expose stubs of S. Call a hit constrained when its vertex belongs
to S union T or has been touched before. The constrained-stub
candidate set has size at most d(2d+1)k; the remaining pairing
pool has at least dn/2 stubs when k<=n/4. Thus the conditional
probability of a constrained step is <=K_d k/n, K_d=2(2d+1),
uniformly over the past and the fixed r.

Let I count internal S-pairs, J hits from S to T, F fresh hits
outside S union T, and R their repeat hits. On Cz=r, every outside
zero-output vertex that is touched must be hit at least twice,
so F<=R. Stub counting gives

  dk=2I+J+F+R,
  2(I+J+R)-dk=J+R-F>=0.

Thus at least dk/2 of at most dk exposure steps are constrained.
Counting their binary exposure words gives the uniform bound

  sup_r P(Cz=r)<=2^(dk+1)(K_d k/n)^(dk/2).                 (A16)

There is NO binomial penalty for choosing T: the target is fixed
before taking probability, and the same numerical bound holds for
every choice of target. Taking a supremum afterward is therefore
valid. This is the delicate all-target point; replacing T by a random
or subsequently optimized exposure set would have been different.

Summing p binom(n,k)(p-1)^k inputs and choosing a sufficiently small
fixed eta gives O(n^(1-d/2)) by a geometric-series ratio, since
d/2-1>0. This goes to zero for all d>=3. Loops and arbitrary field
cancellations were allowed throughout the exposure calculation.


9. Three-region assembly and parity
-----------------------------------
Constants have exactly zero error because C_n a1=da1. Partition
nonconstant inputs into a sufficiently small fixed nearconstant
region, the central empirical window, and the rest. Shrink eta so
h(eta)+eta log(p-1)<log p and it is disjoint from the central region.

  * Near constants, the actual maximum atoms sum to o(1) by (A16).
    Their reference contribution is also o(1), since their number
    is exponentially smaller than p^n.

  * In the middle, the actual maximum atoms sum to O(n^-2), or a
    stronger chosen inverse power, by Section 7. The reference
    contribution is p times the iid-uniform empirical-profile
    probability of missing its sqrt(log n/n) window; a fixed-p
    Hoeffding bound makes this o(1).

  * At the center, Section 6 gives error o(p^(-n+1)) per input,
    uniformly over all target vectors. There are at most p^n inputs,
    so their total error is o(1), with p fixed.

These regions cover every input. The middle theorem applies whenever
the input alone misses its central window, since the joint input-and-
edge distance event then holds for every edge table. The supremum
is always taken before the sum over inputs. No estimate uniform in
a growing prime has entered the proof.

Every underlying pairing has dn stubs, and all source steps require
only dn even. The symmetric table translations preserve exact row
sums and even diagonals at every admissible n. Therefore the argument
has no hidden restriction to even n or to a residue class modulo p.


10. What transfers to uniform simple graphs and to RSBM moments
---------------------------------------------------------------
For independent degree-a configuration blocks and a prescribed simple
b-regular cross matrix B, the all-target mixing theorem gives the
same two-block replacement calculation as in the source synthesis.
For fixed odd p not dividing b the reference calculation yields

  E_config |ker_Fp(A_B-(a-b)I)|<=2p+o(1),                 (A17)

uniformly over all such B and every admissible aN. The lower reference
count also gives equality 2p+o(1), though only the upper bound is
needed. Both-constant and both-nonconstant parts each tend to p;
mixed patterns vanish because rank_Fp B is proportional to N by
disjoint-support row selection. The affine replacement error is o(1).

Let S denote simplicity of both internal blocks, with probability
s_N->s_a=exp(-(a^2-1)/2)>0. Every configuration has the planted p
vectors. For K=|ker_Fp(A_B-(a-b)I)|,

  E_simple K
   =p+E_config[(K-p)1_S]/s_N
   <=p+[E_config K-p]/s_N.

Consequently

  limsup_N sup_B E_simple K<=(1+s_a^(-1))p.               (A18)

This is a bounded RAW expectation with a coefficient independent of
p, and is legitimate because the argument conditions a nonnegative
variable directly. It is stronger than transferring only a probability
event by contiguity. The coefficient 2 in (A17) is not claimed to
transfer unchanged, and no simple-model all-target mixing theorem
is inferred merely by this conditioning inequality.

Integer minors transfer extra real nullity to extra modular nullity;
subtract the planted p in Markov and then let arbitrarily large fixed
odd primes not dividing b follow the N limit. This proves real
planted-line simplicity uniformly in prescribed B for all a>=3,
including a=4 at odd block size. The source synthesis's qualitative
conclusion is therefore supported.

For a fixed suitable prime, (A18) also bounds the expected number of
enumerated modular candidates by a constant. This permits O(N)
expected verification work after obtaining the field-kernel basis.
Near-quadratic or other fast total runtimes require a separate sparse
linear-algebra implementation and its correctness/complexity proof;
they are not certified by this mixing audit alone. The a=2,b=1 case
is outside these d>=3 mixing inputs and needs its separate cubic
moment proof.
