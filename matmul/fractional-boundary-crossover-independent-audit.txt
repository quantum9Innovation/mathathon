1. NUCLEAR TAIL CONSTANT AND EXACT ONE-RANK REGION
------------------------------------------------

Independently reconstructed Woodbury with m=w^T(A+tI)^-1w. For Q=I-ww^T,
the projected trace is (-m'-m^2)/(1+m). The spectral inequality
R_t^2<=R_t/t and m<=1/t give

  tr(QD_tQ)<=m(1/t-m)/(1+m)<=1/t-m.

The fractional integral of the last quantity is exactly w^T A^alpha w:
write 1/t-m=int lambda/[t(lambda+t)]dmu(lambda) and use the beta
integral. Scalar concavity then gives w^T A^alpha w<=a^alpha<=h^alpha.
All integrands are positive; the exchanges are justified by Tonelli.

The scalar d0=w^TDw is positive. In w plus w-perp blocks, subtracting
F=(Dw)(Dw)^T/d0 leaves precisely the lower-right Schur complement.
It is PSD and has trace at most tr(QDQ). Thus the nuclear residual
bound has constant exactly one as stated. The spectral rank-one
truncation has no larger trace residual, so lambda_2(D)<=h^alpha.
For h^alpha<=exp(-L), the STRICT count is at most one. A=0 and B=ww^T
achieves one since L>0. This proves the exact cutoff tau>=1/alpha,
including equality. The construction does not require optimality of
the nuclear constant for another regime.

4. VARYING DENSITY AND FINITE ENDPOINT NORMALIZATION
--------------------------------------------------

The two-sided log density has total mass one and mean M_delta*h0.
For delta=1+1/L in [1,2], c_delta is in [1/3,2/5], the mean after the
upper truncation is <=h_L/96, and kappa_delta is bounded above and

log(h0)=-tau L+O(log(L+1)), uniformly as used.

For t<=h0, direct integration against the lower power density gives
m(t) comparable to h0^(-eta)*t^(eta-1); the upper-density contribution

On every bounded interval I, downward step approximations preserve

The final strict inequality holds even for exponentially small h_L,

5. MOMENT LIMIT, COUNT DEFICIT, AND GRAPH CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------

Integrating the two comparison powers explicitly gives the displayed

The norm bound ||D||<=1 and the uniform fixed-[0,2] rational count upper

A fixed deficit of n_L(1) below positive C(1,tau), together with
monotonicity of n_L and continuity of C, persists on an interval to

assuming a fixed-operator moment/count Tauberian theorem. If C(1,tau)
is zero the universal upper suffices. Substitution v=1 gives all three
