1. RESULT AND EXACT CREDIT
===========================

Write f(x)=x log(x)-x+1 and b(x)=(x-1)^2/2, x>0. Let t_j,w_j be
the m-node Gauss-Legendre nodes and weights on [0,1], and define

 r_m(x)=sum_(j=1)^m w_j (x-1)/[1+t_j(x-1)],
 delta=2^(-k),  r_(m,k)(x)=delta^(-1) r_m(x^delta),
 F_(m,k)(x)=x r_(m,k)(x)-x+1.

The approximation r_(m,k), its operator concavity, root/SDP lifts,
and its all-x pointwise error bound are EXISTING Fawzi--Saunderson--
Parrilo constructions. The following is a global weighted corollary
of those existing ingredients, derived in this note:

 THEOREM. For m>=1 and k>=2,

  sup_(x>0) |f(x)-F_(m,k)(x)|/b(x)
       <= 64 m (m 2^(-k))^(2m).                         (1.1)

 Moreover F_(m,k) is nonnegative operator convex, normalized by
 F_(m,k)(1)=F'_(m,k)(1)=0. The approximation error f-F has the
 sign of x-1. Consequently, for every 0<eta<1, there are explicit
 normalized operator convex algebraic functions f_eta^- and
 f_eta^+ with

  0<=f_eta^- <= f <= f_eta^+ = f_eta^- + eta*b          (1.2)

 globally on (0,infinity). Their matrix perspectives have lifts
 with O(sqrt(log(1/eta))) PSD blocks of order 2N for N-dimensional
 matrix arguments. Their standard Petz divergences have the same
 NUMBER of stages, but the direct established joint-matrix lift
 uses blocks of order N^2, specified in Section 6 below.

 In the explicit Gauss-plus-dyadic-root construction the stage count
 s=m+k can be chosen to satisfy

  s <= sqrt(2 log(1/eta)/log(2)) + O(log log(1/eta)).     (1.3)

This escapes a rational-degree lower because r_(m,k) and F_(m,k)
are algebraic functions of x when k>0. It establishes neither an
arbitrary SDP lower bound nor a reduction of the standard Petz
block size from N^2 to N.

2. GLOBAL WEIGHTED ERROR: COMPLETE ELEMENTARY DERIVATION
=========================================================

The pointwise input is FSP Proposition1. Put u=|log x|. Its bound is

 |log(x)-r_(m,k)(x)|
 <=4 delta^(-1) sinh^2(delta*u/2) tanh(delta*u/4)^(2m-1).

Since 2x/(x-1)^2=1/[2sinh^2(u/2)], this yields

 |f(x)-F_(m,k)(x)|/b(x)
 <=2 delta^(-1) [sinh(delta*u/2)/sinh(u/2)]^2
                                      *tanh(delta*u/4)^(2m-1).
                                                               (2.1)
The ratio at x=1 is its continuous limit. Both elementary bounds

 sinh(v)<=v exp(v), v>=0,    tanh(v)<=v, v>=0,

will be used. For 0<u<=1, sinh(u/2)>=u/2 gives

 (2.1)<=2 delta exp(delta*u)*(delta*u/4)^(2m-1)
       <=8 exp(1/4) delta^(2m)/16^m
       <=64 m (m delta)^(2m).                            (2.2)

For u>=1, use

 1/[2sinh^2(u/2)] = 2exp(-u)/(1-exp(-u))^2 <=8exp(-u).

Multiplying the pointwise log error directly gives

 (2.1)<=8 delta u^2 exp(-(1-delta)u)*(delta*u/4)^(2m-1)
       <=32 delta^(2m)/16^m *u^(2m+1)exp(-3u/4).       (2.3)

For p=2m+1, the all-u maximum of u^p exp(-3u/4) is
 [4p/(3e)]^p. As m>=1 implies p<=3m and e>2,

 [4p/(3e)]^p <= (2m)^(2m+1).

Therefore (2.3)<=64m (m delta/2)^(2m), which implies
(1.1). This proof treats the full positive half-line; no truncation,
condition number, or support restriction occurs.

3. EXPLICIT PARAMETER CHOICE AND STAGE COUNT
============================================

For a target two-sided error epsilon in (0,1), let L=log(1/epsilon)
and choose

 m=max(1,ceil(sqrt(L/(2log2)))),
 k=max(2,ceil(log_2(m)+(L+log(64m))/(2m log2))).          (3.1)

Then

 64m (m 2^(-k))^(2m)<=epsilon.

As L tends to infinity, m=sqrt(L/(2log2))+O(1) and

 k=sqrt(L/(2log2)) + (log L)/(2log2)+O(1).

Thus m+k=sqrt(2L/log2)+O(log L). This is an upper bound for this
specific construction; its leading constant is not claimed optimal
among all algebraic approximants or all semidefinite lifts.

For (1.2), apply (3.1) with epsilon=eta/2. This changes L by O(1).
Small values of L are covered by the displayed max operations.

4. OPERATOR CONVEXITY, ERROR SIGN AND THE ONE-SIDED RESCALE
===========================================================

The positive-weight Gauss formula r_m is operator monotone and
operator concave: each kernel (x-1)/[1+t(x-1)] has these properties.
Composition with x^delta, 0<delta<=1, preserves operator concavity
because the outside function is operator monotone. Thus r_(m,k)
is operator concave, as proved already in FSP Proposition3.

The Gauss rule is invariant under t->1-t. Directly in the kernel
formula this gives

 r_m(1/x)=-r_m(x),  r_(m,k)(1/x)=-r_(m,k)(x).           (4.1)

Hence F_(m,k) is the transpose x*g(1/x) of
g(x)=-r_(m,k)(x)+x-1. Since g is operator convex, its transpose is
operator convex by the noncommutative perspective theorem. Also
r_(m,k)(1)=0, r'_(m,k)(1)=1, so F(1)=F'(1)=0. Its ordinary
convexity implies F>=0 globally.

For completeness the Gaussian error sign is elementary. At fixed
y>0 the integrand a(t)=(y-1)/[1+t(y-1)] satisfies

 a^(2m)(t)=(2m)!(y-1)^(2m+1)/[1+t(y-1)]^(2m+1).

The positive Gaussian error formula therefore shows that
log(y)-r_m(y) has the sign of y-1. Take y=x^delta and scale by
delta^(-1); then f(x)-F(x) has the sign of x-1 too. This is also
stated in FSP Proposition6.

Assume the bound |f-F|<=epsilon*b. Define

 f^- = F/(1+epsilon).

We need two exact scalar facts:

 b(x)<=f(x)<=2b(x)  for 0<x<=1,
 0<=f(x)<=b(x)      for x>=1.                           (4.2)

The b comparisons follow by differentiating f-b, whose derivative
is log x+1-x<=0 and whose value at1 is0. The global upper f<=2b
follows from (x-1)^2-f=x(x-1-log x)>=0.

For x<=1 we have F>=f by the error sign, and
F<=f+epsilon*b<=(1+epsilon)f. For x>=1 we have F<=f.
Thus 0<=f^-<=f globally. Further,

 f-f^-=[epsilon*f+(f-F)]/(1+epsilon).

On x<=1 use f-F<=0 and f<=2b; on x>=1 use f<=b and
f-F<=epsilon*b. Both give

 0<=f-f^-<=2epsilon*b/(1+epsilon).                     (4.3)

With epsilon=eta/2, set f^+=f^-+eta*b. These are normalized
operator convex functions, and (1.2) follows. No subtraction of a
convex quadratic is used, so convexity is retained on both sides.

5. DIVERGENCE GUARANTEES, INCLUDING JOINTLY NONCOMMUTING INPUTS
==============================================================

Use the standard Petz convention

 S_h(A||B)=<B^(1/2), h(L_A R_B^(-1)) B^(1/2)>_HS,

where L_A(Z)=AZ and R_B(Z)=ZB. These two positive self-adjoint
superoperators commute, so scalar inequalities transfer by functional
calculus and a positive quadratic form. Consequently

 0<=S_(f^-)(A||B)<=S_f(A||B)<=S_(f^+)(A||B),
 S_(f^+)-S_(f^-)=eta*S_b(A||B)=eta*Chi2(A||B)/2,        (5.1)

where

 S_f(A||B)=Tr[A(logA-logB)]-TrA+TrB,
 Chi2(A||B)=Tr[(A-B)B^(-1)(A-B)].

All statements here are for A,B positive definite. For equal trace1,
the affine corrections vanish and
Chi2=Tr[A^2 B^(-1)]-1. Petz divergences of normalized operator
convex functions are jointly convex, so both bounds are jointly
convex and suitable as convex epigraph constraints.

The same scalar inequalities also hold for the noncommutative matrix
perspectives, giving Loewner brackets there. These perspectives and
their traces are DIFFERENT functions from standard Petz/Umegaki
divergence on noncommuting inputs. Their smaller lifts cannot be
silently transferred to (5.1).

6. PRECISE LIFTS AND MATRIX ORDERS
==================================

For positive N-by-N matrices X,Y, write
P_r(Y,X)=X^(1/2) r(X^(-1/2) Y X^(-1/2)) X^(1/2).
The operator epigraph -P_r(Y,X)<=T has the existing FSP root-Gauss
lift. A count-preserving explicit formulation uses Hermitian root variables:

 Z_0=Y,  Z_i Hermitian (1<=i<=k),
 [ Z_(i-1)   Z_i ; Z_i   X ] >=0       (1<=i<=k),
 [ Z_k-X-U_j  -sqrt(t_j)U_j ;
   -sqrt(t_j)U_j   X-t_j U_j ] >=0      (1<=j<=m),
 sum_j w_j U_j = -delta*T.                            (6.1)

All blocks are order2N. For i<k, Z_i>=0 follows from its occurrence
as the top-left diagonal of the NEXT root block; no separate PSD
constraint is added. A root block implies Z_i<=Z_(i-1)#_(1/2)X
even if the last Z_k is indefinite. Monotonicity of the geometric mean
therefore gives Z_k<=X#_delta Y. Replace Z_k in every Gauss block by
that ACTUAL final mean: only its top-left corner increases, preserving
PSD feasibility. The Gauss Schur bounds then prove the desired output
inequality at the actual mean. Conversely choose all actual nested
means, which satisfy the root blocks, and use the actual Gauss values.
This proves epigraph equivalence without an extra order-N final
positivity block or a false converse for arbitrary mean underestimates.

There is a relevant primary 2020 corrigendum:
 https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/hf323/corrigendum_lieb.pdf
It corrects the false full-hypograph equivalence for matrix geometric
means in FS2017. The implication from the 2N block to V<=X#Y is
valid, and the actual mean satisfies the block. These are precisely
the two facts used above. The corrigendum expressly proves that
FSP2019 entropy/logarithm lifts are unaffected. The issue is not
limited to indefinite V; V>=0 alone would not repair the false full
hypograph equivalence, because squaring is not operator monotone.

The normalized F perspective is obtained from (6.1) by changing
matrix arguments and adding the affine correction; (4.1) identifies
the transpose. The bracket rescaling is an affine output rescaling.
The b perspective adds one block of order2N, by the usual Schur
complement for (A-B)B^(-1)(A-B).

For STANDARD PETZ/Umegaki let

 X=A tensor I, Y=I tensor bar(B),
 phi(M)=vec(I)^* M vec(I).

The matrices X,Y commute and their order is N^2. Functional calculus
and the identity phi(C tensor D)=Tr[C D^T] show that

 S_F(A||B)=-phi(P_r(Y,X))-TrA+TrB.                     (6.2)

Applying (6.1) in dimension N^2 gives m+k blocks of order2N^2.
The existing FSP Section3.3 smaller representation reduces the m
Gauss blocks to orderN^2+1; the k root blocks remain order2N^2.
Thus the established representation counts are

  S_(f^-): m blocks orderN^2+1, k blocks order2N^2,
  S_(f^+): those blocks plus one block order2N.

The extra block for Chi2 uses a Hermitian W with

 [ W A-B ; A-B B ] >=0,

and its scalar epigraph is TrW. These claims count PSD constraints
and their matrix orders; they make no arithmetic or iteration-cost
claim for solving an arbitrary optimization instance.

7. COMPARISON WITH THE PARENT POSITIVE RATIONAL THEOREM
========================================================

The parent entropy a=1,b=2 positive rational theorem uses
m_R=log^2(1/eta)/(2pi^2)+O(log(1/eta)) poles and O(m_R) blocks
of order2N for standard Petz divergence. The companion lower proves
that leading POLE resource is optimal even for unrestricted real
rational functions of type[m_R+1/m_R].

Here the algebraic approach uses O(sqrt(log(1/eta))) stages, but
standard Petz blocks grow to orderN^2. These two constructions give
an accuracy versus matrix-order tradeoff. There is no contradiction
to the rational lower and neither construction dominates in every
combination of N and eta. The root route is directly small-block for
scalar relative entropy, fixed-reference matrix entropy, and maximal
operator perspectives, as distinguished in FSP Table1.

The error is absolute measured against Chi2, which can be arbitrarily
large. No global multiplicative error relative to entropy itself,
uniform bounded absolute error for all states, or condition-free
solver complexity follows from (5.1). With Chi2<=M, the enclosure
width is at most eta*M/2. A rigorous convex optimization certificate
can therefore be obtained on sets where M is controlled, subject to
ordinary feasibility/optimization-error handling.

The underlying construction and square-root acceleration are fully

all-x Chi2-weighted estimate (2.1)--(2.3), its explicit parameters,
and the sign-aware convex two-sided bracket (4.3). A targeted source
search did not locate this exact global statement, but that does NOT

all-x estimate and should be described as such unless independent
literature review establishes a meaningful missing application.

FF2023 Theorem5 instead discusses standard Petz lifts with order2N
blocks and rational quadrature size; it does not state this algebraic
global root result. Its fixed-degree minimax problem does not cover
the algebraic function class. Source:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2305.12405

Potentially larger open structural target: retain the algebraic
root acceleration AND O(N)-order blocks for jointly variable standard
Petz entropy on arbitrary noncommuting A,B. The root superoperator
is (L_B R_A^(-1))^delta, which involves both left and right fractional
powers and cannot simply be replaced by an N-dimensional matrix
geometric mean. Such a replacement generally changes standard Petz
quantities to maximal-perspective/fidelity-type quantities. No valid
small-block elimination is proved in this note.

A concrete reduction clarifies this structural target. Already the
single-node, single-root case m=1,k=1 has

 r_(1,1)(x)=4(sqrt(x)-1)/(sqrt(x)+1),
 F_(1,1)(x)=3x+1-8h(x),  h(x)=x/(1+sqrt(x)),
 h(x)+x*h(1/x)=sqrt(x).

Consequently its standard Petz divergence obeys the exact identity

 S_F(A||B)+S_F(B||A)
   =4TrA+4TrB-8Tr[sqrt(A)sqrt(B)]
   =4||sqrt(A)-sqrt(B)||_HS^2.                         (8.1)

An epigraph lift of S_F gives a hypograph lift of S_h by the affine
relation S_F=3TrA+TrB-8S_h. Applying that lift to both argument
orders and adding gives a hypograph lift of quantum AFFINITY
Tr[sqrt(A)sqrt(B)]. Thus a constant-stage O(N)-order exact lift of
even this simplest algebraic Petz generator would yield such an
affinity lift. This is a reduction, NOT an impossibility result.
The known general Lieb-function construction uses N^2-order blocks:
 H. Fawzi, J. Saunderson, Lieb's concavity theorem, matrix geometric
 means, and semidefinite optimization, LAA513(2017),240--263.
 https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03401
 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2016.10.012
This again identifies exactly where the fidelity/affinity distinction
matters. It does not assert that smaller affinity lifts are impossible.

Another potentially stronger target is a genuine lower for arbitrary
SDP extensions under an explicitly specified approximation model.
The entropy pole lower cannot supply it, and (1.1) is a concrete
reason to keep these resource models separate.
