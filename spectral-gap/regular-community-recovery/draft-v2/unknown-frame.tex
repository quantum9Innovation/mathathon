\section{Recovery of an unknown degree frame}\label{sec:unknown-frame}

We first give the most general partition-recovery statement. The number
of classes, their sizes, and their degree frame are unknown to the
algorithm. Class sizes need not be equal. The conclusion identifies
the planted partition among all bounded-class equitable partitions,
rather than only among candidates with the same supplied frame.

Fix constants $K\ge1$ and $D\ge3$. A true frame is a fixed matrix
$Q=(d_{ij})\in\Z_{\ge0}^{k\times k}$, with $1\le k\le K$,
$a_i=d_{ii}\ge0$, and $d_i=\sum_jd_{ij}\le D$.
Its off-diagonal support is connected, with the one-type convention.
Assume either of the following two conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Every row sum is the same $d$, with $3\le d\le D$.
       Internal degrees may be zero or one.
 \item $k\ge2$ and every internal degree $a_i\ge2$.
       Total degrees may differ across classes.
\end{enumerate}
The nonempty planted classes
$V_i$ have sizes $N_i$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-balance}
 N_id_{ij}=N_jd_{ji},\qquad a_iN_i\text{ even},\qquad
 n=\sum_iN_i.
\end{equation}
Independently sample a uniform simple $a_i$-regular graph in each
class and a uniform simple $(d_{ij},d_{ji})$-biregular graph between
each active pair. Zero-degree cross factors are omitted. Write $J$
for the $n\times k$ planted indicator matrix. Always $AJ=JQ$.
The graph need not be globally regular.

A partition into $r$ classes is represented by a one-hot matrix
$Y\in\{0,1\}^{n\times r}$: each row is a standard basis vector and
each column is nonzero. It is equitable if $AY=YR$ for a nonnegative
integer matrix $R$. A coarsening of the planted partition has the form
$Y=JT$, where the $k\times r$ one-hot map $T$ is onto.

\begin{theorem}[Unknown-frame recovery]\label{thm:unknown-frame}
In this model, with probability $1-o(1)$ every equitable partition
into at most $K$ nonempty classes is a coarsening of the planted
partition. The plant is therefore the unique finest such partition,
up to class names. A deterministic algorithm, given $A$ and only
the fixed bounds $K,D$, recovers the planted partition, $k$, its
class sizes, and $Q$, with high probability, in polynomial bit time.
The algorithm has both an expected polynomial-time version and a
worst-case polynomial truncation. No quotient eigenvalue, frame rank,
spectral separation, or numerical gap is supplied or assumed.
\end{theorem}

The proof below also gives failure $O_{K,D}(n^{-(d_{\min}-2)})$
for the rigidity assertion, where $d_{\min}=\min_i d_i\ge3$.
The constants are uniform over the finite family of permitted frames.
Connectedness and the balance identities compare sizes along a path:
every adjacent ratio is a ratio of positive integers at most $D$.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-rho}
 \alpha_i:=N_i/n\ge\rho:=\frac1{KD^{K-1}}.
\end{equation}
This supplies uniformly positive class proportions without requiring
the algorithm to know them.

\subsection{Vector intertwiners and aggregate fibers}
For the counting argument, temporarily sample every independent typed
factor by its uniform distinguished-stub configuration, allowing loops
and repeated edges. We return to the simple law by conditioning below.
For $r\le K$, fix an arbitrary matrix $R\in\F_2^{r\times r}$.
Its entries need not be degree counts for this moment argument.
Use the additive alphabet $\mathcal A=\F_2^r$ of size $q=2^r$,
and let
$Z_R(A)=|\{Y\in\F_2^{n\times r}:AY=YR\}|$.
For each old class $i$, a profile records its root vector $x$ and
the vector at the partner of every distinguished stub, retaining
the stub's old neighbor type. Its local equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-star}
 \sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_{ij}}z_{j\ell}=xR.
\end{equation}
Deleting any one physical slot determines it uniquely, with coefficient
one. There is no condition on the rank of $R$ or on field inverses.

Let $m_{ii}(x,y)$ be internal directed-stub tables, symmetric with
even diagonal; let $m_{ij}=m_{ji}^T$ be cross tables of total
$M_{ij}=N_id_{ij}$. For each feasible collection of these tables,
let $\Omega$ be the fiber of all ordered arrays of legal profiles
at all labeled vertices. With
$W(m)=\prod_{x<y}m_{xy}!\prod_x(m_{xx}-1)!!$, the exact count is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-fiber}
 \E Z_R(A)=\sum_{\text{table collections}}|\Omega|
  \prod_{i:a_i>0}\frac{W(m_{ii})}{(a_iN_i-1)!!}
  \prod_{i<j:d_{ij}>0}\frac{\prod_{x,y}m_{ij}(x,y)!}{M_{ij}!}.
\end{equation}
This is proved by assigning requested partner labels to ordered stubs
and pairing each class to its reverse, as in the full factor count
\eqref{eq:fmp-exact-types}. All root assignments and local types are
already in $\Omega$.

Choose uniformly from a nonempty fiber. Permuting vertex positions
within an old block, and slots within each old neighbor type at each
vertex, preserves the fiber. Let $P_i$ be its common class-$i$
profile law, $\mu_i$ its root law, and $\nu_{ij}$ the one-slot
root/neighbor pair law. These pair laws are exactly the normalized
fixed tables. Their marginals are $\mu_i,\mu_j$, and
$\nu_{ij}=\nu_{ji}^T$. Subadditivity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-fiber-entropy}
 \log|\Omega|\le\sum_iN_iH(P_i).
\end{equation}
Every old type has an active incident factor, since its total degree
is at least three. Such a table determines its root marginal even
when its internal factor is absent.
Define the per-vertex entropy exponent
\[
 \Phi=\sum_i\alpha_iH(P_i)
 -\sum_i\frac{\alpha_i a_i}{2}H(\nu_{ii})
 -\sum_{i<j}\alpha_id_{ij}H(\nu_{ij}).
\]
All inactive terms are omitted. First use the second model condition,
with $a_i\ge2$. Let $J_i\ge0$ be conditional total
correlation of the retained neighbors after deleting one internal
slot, and let $I_{ij}$ be mutual information in $\nu_{ij}$.
Deleting the determined slot, expanding conditional entropies, and
using $\alpha_id_{ij}=\alpha_jd_{ji}$ give exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-entropy}
 -\Phi=\sum_i\alpha_i(a_i/2-1)I_{ii}
       +\sum_{i<j}\alpha_id_{ij}I_{ij}
       +\sum_i\alpha_iJ_i\ge0.
\end{equation}
The root-entropy terms cancel with these unequal-size weights.
This calculation works in characteristic two, at $a_i=2$, and for
zero or rank-deficient $R$. For the first model condition,
Section~\ref{sec:all-slot} proves the same modular nonpositivity
by averaging over all omitted slots instead of choosing an internal one.

For an alphabet of size $q$ put $t=q(q+1)/2$.
The elementary zero-safe bounds
\[
 r\log r-r\le\log(r!)\le r\log r-r+1+\log(r+1)
\]
and $(r-1)!!=r!/[2^{r/2}(r/2)!]$ for even $r$ imply
\begin{align*}
 \frac{W(m)}{(T-1)!!}
 &\le e^{t+1}(T+1)^{t+1}e^{-TH(m/T)/2},\\
 \frac{\prod_{x,y}m(x,y)!}{T!}
 &\le e^{q^2}(T+1)^{q^2}e^{-TH(m/T)}.
\end{align*}
For the first inequality, the off-diagonal factorial and diagonal
double-factorial leading terms sum to
$\frac12\sum_{x,y}m(x,y)\log m(x,y)-T/2$;
subtract the denominator's leading term and bound the $t+1$
remainders. The second follows by the same factorial estimate on
the $q^2$ numerator entries and a lower bound on the denominator.
Both bounds include zero entries and use only alphabet cardinality.

Let $e_Q$ count active cross factors and
$\ell_Q=|\{i:a_i>0\}|$. These bounds cost power at most $t+1$
of $Dn+1$ for each internal factor and $q^2$ for each cross factor.
Counting aggregate tables costs powers $\ell_Qq^2$ and $e_Qq^2$,
respectively. The exponential factors cancel by the corresponding
entropy identity and \eqref{eq:unknown-fiber-entropy}.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-moment}
 \E Z_R(A)\le C_{K,D}(n+1)^{H_K},\qquad
 H_K=2K^2 4^K+K,
\end{equation}
since the actual power
$\ell_Q(t+1+q^2)+2e_Qq^2$ is at most $H_K$ for $q\le2^K$,
$k\le K$, and $e_Q\le k(k-1)/2$.
The constant absorbs fixed degree coefficients and simplicity
conditioning. This aggregation improves a bound obtained by counting
all local profile types, whose exponent would depend exponentially
on $D$.

For completeness, fixed-degree ordinary and bipartite simplicity
criteria give a positive limiting probability for all independent
factors. Each simple internal factor has $(a_i!)^{N_i}$ stub
realizations; each simple cross factor has
$(d_{ij}!)^{N_i}(d_{ji}!)^{N_j}$. Conditioning therefore gives exactly
the required independent uniform-simple laws. By
\eqref{eq:unknown-rho} and finiteness of the frame family the
conditioning cost is uniform. Thus \eqref{eq:unknown-moment} holds
unconditionally over the target simple graph draw.

\subsection{A strict one-hot gap with internal degree at least two}
For integer equitable candidates use the real unit-vector alphabet
$e_1,\ldots,e_r$, and the integer identity
$\sum e_{\text{neighbor}}=R_{\text{root},*}$. The same exact fibers
and entropy calculation apply. Let $\mu_i$ be the candidate-label
law in old class $i$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-distance}
 \epsilon=\sum_i\alpha_i(1-\max_x\mu_i(x)).
\end{equation}
This is precisely the least fraction of changed rows in replacing
the candidate by any blockwise point map $JT$. The map may merge
old blocks or leave candidate labels unused.

Use the second model condition in this subsection.
Write $L=-\Phi$. For a fixed class $i$, let $P_i^-$ be the retained
star after deleting one internal slot. Define $T_i$ to keep its root
law and conditional internal-neighbor laws, make retained internal
slots independent given the root, and make each cross slot independent
with root-independent neighboring law $\mu_j$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-KL}
 \kappa_i:=\KL(P_i^-\Vert T_i)
 =J_i+\sum_{j\ne i}d_{ij}I_{ij},\qquad
 \alpha_i\kappa_i\le L.
\end{equation}
For this fixed $i$, incident cross edges occur once in the edge sum
of \eqref{eq:unknown-entropy}; no sum of endpoint KL bounds is being
charged against one edge twice.

Let $m_i(X)$ be the conditional mean of an internal unit-vector
neighbor given the root, and $v_i$ its expected conditional variance
trace. Let $V_j=1-\|\mu_j\|_2^2$. Infer the omitted vector $U$ from
the root row of $R$ minus the retained neighbor sum, and put
$f=\|U-m_i(X)\|_2^2$. Under $P_i^-$, its expectation is $v_i$.
Under $T_i$, conditional independence gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:unknown-variance}
 \E_{T_i}f-\E_{P_i^-}f
 &=(a_i-2)v_i+\sum_{j\ne i}d_{ij}V_j\notag\\
 &\quad+\E\left\|R_{X,*}-a_im_i(X)
                   -\sum_{j\ne i}d_{ij}\E e_{X_j}\right\|_2^2
 \ge\sum_{j\ne i}d_{ij}V_j.
\end{align}
An occupied row $R_{X,*}$ is nonnegative of total mass $d_i$.
The retained neighbor sum plus $m_i(X)$ has that same mass.
Thus $0\le f\le2d_i^2$ on both distributions, even if total degrees
differ across old classes. Pinsker and \eqref{eq:unknown-KL} imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-local-gap}
 L\ge\frac{\alpha_i}{2d_i^4}
          \left(\sum_{j\ne i}d_{ij}V_j\right)^2.
\end{equation}
Since $V_j\ge1-\max_x\mu_j(x)$, some $j$ has $V_j\ge\epsilon$.
Connectedness and $k\ge2$ supply a neighbor $i$ of that class.
Using \eqref{eq:unknown-rho} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-gap}
 \Phi\le-\frac{\rho}{2D^4}\epsilon^2.
\end{equation}
The proof retains internal conditional dependence and therefore
covers $a_i=2$. If $\Phi=0$, all root laws are points; matching
consistency and the local integer equations give exactly $QT=TR$.
Conversely every such compatible point map has deterministic legal
stars and zero entropy.

Restrict the fibers to fixed root-label counts in every old block.
Their invariances survive. The common strict entropy bound supplies
an exponential loss for candidates at distance at least any fixed
$\eta>0$ from every point map. There are only polynomially many
root-count collections and aggregate edge tables, and finitely many
candidate integer frames with $r\le K$ and row sums at most $D$.
Thus all such macroscopic candidates are absent except with
exponentially small probability. There is no lower bound on the
sizes of candidate classes in this argument.

\subsection{Small row supports and exact compatibility}
Let a set $S$ be bad if no exterior vertex has exactly one incident
edge into $S$, counting multiplicities. In the typed configuration
model, for $s=|S|\le\eta n$, a witness consists of $S$, its exterior
neighbor set of size $w$, and all prescribed stub pairs incident to
$S$. If $a$ edges lie in $S$ and $b$ leave it, put $e=a+b$.
With $d_{\min}=\min_i d_i\ge3$,
\[
 2a+b\ge d_{\min}s,\qquad 2w\le b,\qquad
 e-s-w\ge\frac{d_{\min}-2}{2}s.
\]
Every relevant stub pool has size at least $\rho n$.
For $s\le\rho n/(4D)$, a prescribed partial configuration with
$e$ pairs has probability at most $(2/(\rho n))^e$.
Choose $S,W$, stubs at $W$, and pairs in at most
\[
 \binom ns\binom nw\,2^{Dw}(2Ds)^e
\]
ways. Invalid types may be overcounted. Using
$(s/w)^w\le e^{s/e_{\mathrm{Euler}}}$ when $w>0$, and the value one
at $w=0$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-support}
 \E\#\{\text{bad }S:|S|=s\}
 \le C_{K,D}^s(s/n)^{\lceil(d_{\min}-2)s/2\rceil}.
\end{equation}
For sufficiently small fixed $\eta$, the sum from $s=2$ to $\eta n$
is $O_{K,D}(n^{-(d_{\min}-2)})$, by the consecutive-term ratio
$C e^{(d_{\min}-2)/2}((s+1)/n)^{(d_{\min}-2)/2}<1/2$.
Singletons cannot be bad in a simple graph of positive degree.
Conditioning on simplicity therefore proves this same probability
bound for any nonempty small bad set in the target model.

On the complementary graph event, simultaneously for every field,
every finite square matrix $R$, and every matrix $V$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-no-support}
 AV=VR,\quad |\supp_{\mathrm{row}}V|\le\eta n
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad V=0.
\end{equation}
Otherwise an exterior vertex with exactly one edge into the nonzero
row support would give one nonzero row on the left and zero on the
right. This statement requires no union over $R$ or matrix entries.

Let an integer-verified one-hot $Y$ be within $\eta n$ rows of $JT$.
Choose $\eta<\rho/[2(D+1)]$. Changed closed neighborhoods occupy at
most $(D+1)\eta n$ vertices globally, so every old class has a vertex
whose row and all neighbor rows agree with $JT$. At that vertex,
\[
 (QT)_i=(AJT)_v=(AY)_v=(YR)_v=(TR)_i.
\]
Hence $QT=TR$ as an integer identity. Thus $V=Y-JT$ satisfies
$AV=VR$ and has precisely the changed rows as its nonzero support.
Equation~\eqref{eq:unknown-no-support} forces $Y=JT$.
Combining this with the macroscopic bound proves that every
equitable candidate is exactly a coarsening. It also rules out tiny
new candidate classes near point maps with unused labels.

\subsection{Enumeration without knowing the frame}
First check that the input is a simple graph of maximum degree at most
$D$, or report failure. Enumerate $r=1,\ldots,K$ and all nonnegative integer $r\times r$
matrices $R$ with row sums at most $D$. There are at most
$\sum_{r=1}^K(D+1)^{r^2}$ candidates, a fixed constant.
For each, solve the binary linear system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-operator}
 AY-YR=0,\qquad Y\in\F_2^{n\times r},
\end{equation}
whose matrix under column vectorization is
$I_r\otimes A-R^T\otimes I_n$.
Enumerate its kernel in Gray order. Check one-hot rows, nonempty
columns, and the original integer equation. Keep an accepted
partition with the largest number of classes. The vectors
$e_1,\ldots,e_r$ are distinct over $\F_2^r$, including $r=1$, so
every desired integer partition appears. Zero or rank-deficient
candidate matrices create no exception to the moment proof.

Binary elimination costs $O_K(n^3)$ and each update and exact check
costs $O_{K,D}(n)$ elementary operations. Equation~\eqref{eq:unknown-moment}
therefore gives expected work $O_{K,D}(n^{H_K+1}+n^3)$, unconditionally
over the simple graph law, including rare large kernels.
Cap each kernel's candidate count at $n^{H_K+3}$ and report failure
if any cap is exceeded. The finite union and Markov give cap
probability $O_{K,D}(n^{-3})$ and bounded work
$O_{K,D}(n^{H_K+4})$. Memory is $O_K(n^2)$ plus a running candidate.
These are theoretical bounds for fixed $K,D$.

The planted pair $(J,Q)$ always appears at $r=k$.
On the rigidity event, any accepted $Y=JT$ with nonempty columns
has an onto map $T$, hence $r\le k$. The maximum is $k$, and an
onto map between $k$ old and $k$ new classes is a permutation.
The selected partition is planted, and exact neighbor counts recover
its frame. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:unknown-frame}.

\begin{corollary}[Unknown base fibers in random lifts]\label{cor:random-lift}
Let $H$ be any fixed connected simple $d$-regular graph with $d\ge3$
and at most $K$ vertices. From its random $N$-lift, given only the
lifted graph and fixed bounds $K,D\ge d$, the algorithm recovers the
base fibers and the base graph up to isomorphism with high probability,
in expected polynomial bit work and with a polynomial truncation.
Every $N$ is admissible and no old/new eigenvalue gap is required.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Take $Q=A_H$, equal sizes $N_i=N$, absent internal factors, and
independent uniform perfect matchings for the base edges. These are
exactly the lift model and are automatically simple. Theorem~\ref{thm:unknown-frame}
applies. The finest partition is among those with at most $K$ classes;
the unrestricted singleton partition is outside this bounded family.
Coarse equitable partitions of the base lift to allowed coarsenings
and do not contradict recovery of the base-fiber partition.
\end{proof}

The degree-three threshold has a real boundary: a uniform simple
degree-two graph has many cycle components with high probability.
Each component permits an equitable component split, so the one-class
plant is not finest when two candidate classes are allowed. The
all-slot mutual-information coefficient and small-support excess
both vanish at degree two. Section~\ref{sec:lift-boundary} gives the
exact statistical obstruction for lifts of a supplied cycle base.

The independent random cross factors are essential to the exact count.
The theorem does not inherit the prescribed-cross quantifiers of the
separate strong-mixing theorem. Nor does it infer real spectral
simple multiplicities from rigidity of integer partitions.
