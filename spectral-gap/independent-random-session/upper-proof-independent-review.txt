1. Universal denominator.
For length q, the multiplicity of the factor (1-jx) in any pattern
denominator is at most floor(q/(j+1)), independent of generator count.
The degree G of their common denominator is at most q log q.
For a pattern, the numerator degree after clearing denominators is
G+e-sum_j d_j = G+number_of_used_colors, hence at most G+q.
The removed invariant vector contributes -xg(x), also of permitted degree.
The constant word has normalized trace 1-x and causes no exception at q=1.

2. Interpolation and Taylor remainder.
The proof fixes B=q(1+log q), polynomial degree ceil(B), and a reciprocal
grid beginning at M=ceil(32B^2). The grid gap is at most M^-2.
Since ceil(B)<=2B and M>=8ceil(B)^2, Markov's derivative inequality
transfers the grid bound to a uniform bound by at most a factor two.
The denominator stays between exp(-1) and one on [0,1/M].
The first two derivatives of the rational trace are bounded by
C B^4 ||h|| and C B^8 ||h||. The all-N patch for N<M uses the
bounded normalized trace and the same Taylor coefficients, and yields
C B^8 ||h||/N^2 without using the rational formula at N<q.
Every constant in this argument is independent of coefficient dimension
and generator count.

3. Smooth extension.
At Chebyshev index j the error is at most C[j(1+log j)]^8/N^2.
Cauchy-Schwarz with sum_j (1+log j)^16/j^2 finite absorbs this into
the L2 norm of nine angular derivatives. Smooth Chebyshev series converge
in every finite derivative norm. Thus they can be passed through the
published compactly supported distributions. The zeroth Fourier term
has exact zero remainder, because nu_0(1)=1 and nu_1(1)=-1 under
normalization by DN rather than actual dimension D(N-1).

4. Spectral support and cutoff.
The published exact support inclusion for nu_1 does not require a
uniform dimension-dependent bound on the intermediate proof constants.
For each finite coefficient system it says its support lies in the free
spectrum interval. nu_0 is the free spectral distribution. Both therefore
annihilate a smooth cutoff that vanishes in a neighborhood of that interval.
The cutoff can be chosen with nine-derivative angular norm at most
C(K/epsilon)^9. Markov's inequality on its nonnegative unnormalized trace
multiplies the normalized second-order error by DN, giving C D/N.

5. Graph identification.
One independent generator for every undirected base edge gives coefficient
dimension D=|V(base)|. Restricting permutation matrices to constants-
orthogonal vectors is exactly the new fibre space. The full unitary norm
is bounded by the base adjacency norm, by the quadratic form on block
norms. The free graph is a forest covering the base and each component
is its universal cover. For d-regular connected bases the free norm is
2sqrt(d-1). No dependence on the number of edges remains in the error.

Scope:
The result applies uniformly to each deterministic base before sampling.
It does not prove simultaneous adaptive coefficient control. It establishes
a sufficient base-size range D=o(N). The separate diamond-necklace proof
shows that range is sharp for a uniform statement over simple cubic bases.
No exact mixed-product bound, fractional matrix function, or signing theorem
is used. Historical priority for this parameter refinement remains open.
