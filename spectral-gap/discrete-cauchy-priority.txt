UNIFORM DISCRETE CAUCHY COUNTS: AN INDEPENDENT PATH-GRID PROOF
November 15, 2026. Prepared by signing_frontier for the root research session.

Scope and status
----------------
This note proves the requested uniform upper bound directly. It does not
infer a uniform estimate from convergence at fixed eigenvalue index.
The only sharp asymptotic input is the classical weighted Carleman
integral-operator theorem of Laptev (1974), itself attributed by Laptev
in this symmetric exponent-one case to Widom (1966), Theorem 3.3.
The elementary finite-grid comparison below was derived in this session;
its historical priority is unverified. The separate main session has a
more general periodization argument for arbitrary measures satisfying a
low-energy mass bound. Neither proof requires a discrete-asymptotic
theorem for a growing Nyström grid.

Definitions and conclusion
--------------------------
Fix 0<alpha<1. For n>=2 let

  lambda_k = 4 sin^2(k pi/(2n)),       1<=k<=n-1,
  K_alpha(s) = sinh(alpha s/2)/sinh(s/2),  K_alpha(0)=alpha,
  (H_{n,alpha})_{ij} = n^{-1} K_alpha(log lambda_i-log lambda_j).

For a positive compact operator T, N(epsilon;T) counts eigenvalues
strictly greater than epsilon, with multiplicity. Then

  limsup_{epsilon -> 0}
    sup_{n>=2} N(epsilon;H_{n,alpha}) / log^2(1/epsilon)
       <= 1/(2 pi^2).                                      (A)

The proof actually supplies, for fixed alpha, a single continuum
operator S and constants independent of n such that

  N(epsilon;H_{n,alpha})
    <= N(epsilon/(4 c_alpha log 2); S)
          + O_alpha(1+log^+(1/epsilon)),                    (B)

where S has kernel sqrt(xy)/(x+y) on L^2((0,sqrt(2)),dx), and

  c_alpha = max{sin(pi alpha), tan(pi alpha/2)}.

Here and below all eigenvalue comparisons are spectral or quadratic-form
comparisons. Entrywise inequalities alone are never used to infer a
Loewner inequality.

1. Reduction to alpha=1/2 works on arbitrary finite atomic grids
----------------------------------------------------------------
With Fourier convention hat f(xi)=integral_R f(s) exp(-i xi s) ds,

  hat K_alpha(xi)
    = 2 pi sin(pi alpha)/(cosh(2 pi xi)+cos(pi alpha)).       (1)

This standard transform is the rescaled sinh(at)/sinh(pi t) entry of
NIST DLMF Table 1.14.1. Its ratio to hat K_{1/2} equals

  sin(pi alpha) cosh(2 pi xi)
      / [cosh(2 pi xi)+cos(pi alpha)].

The ratio lies between min{sin(pi alpha),tan(pi alpha/2)} and
c_alpha. In particular the symbols are nonnegative and integrable.
Fourier inversion gives, for any real nodes t_i and complex coefficients
v_i, the exact quadratic form

  sum_{i,j} conjugate(v_i) v_j K_alpha(t_i-t_j)
    = (2 pi)^{-1} integral_R hat K_alpha(xi)
          |sum_j v_j exp(i xi t_j)|^2 dxi.

Consequently the kernel matrices satisfy K_alpha <= c_alpha K_{1/2}
in Loewner order, including on the atomic grids in the question. Thus

  H_{n,alpha} <= c_alpha H_{n,1/2}.                         (2)

It is enough to prove (B) for alpha=1/2 and rescale epsilon.

2. A fixed continuum compression dominates the entire low block
----------------------------------------------------------------
Put h=1/n and

  x_k = sqrt(lambda_k) = 2 sin(k pi/(2n)),
  m = floor(n/2),     a=sqrt(2).

The half-power identity is

  K_{1/2}(log lambda_i-log lambda_j)
       = sqrt(x_i x_j)/(x_i+x_j).                          (3)

For 1<=k<=m, x_k<=a and x_1>=2h. Moreover the derivative of
2 sin(pi t/(2n)) on 0<=t<=n/2 is at least pi/(sqrt(2)n)>2h.
Thus consecutive low nodes have spacing >2h. In particular the left
cells

  I_k=[x_k-h,x_k]  (1<=k<=m)

are pairwise disjoint subsets of (0,a), up to endpoints of measure zero.
Define on L^2(0,a)

  f_k(x) = sqrt(x_k)/(sqrt(h) sqrt(x)) 1_{I_k}(x).

Their disjoint supports imply orthogonality. Their squared norms are

  d_k^2 = (x_k/h) log[x_k/(x_k-h)] <= 2 log 2.              (4)

Indeed r=x_k/h>=2, and r log[r/(r-1)] decreases for r>1:
its derivative is log(1+1/(r-1))-1/(r-1)<0.

Let S be the positive integral operator with kernel sqrt(xy)/(x+y)
on L^2(0,a). Positivity follows from its Laplace representation;
it is trace class, with trace a/2. Set

  B_{ij}=<f_i,S f_j>,
  H_low=(h sqrt(x_i x_j)/(x_i+x_j))_{1<=i,j<=m}.

Using 1/(x+y)=integral_0^infinity exp[-t(x+y)] dt gives

  B_{ij}
    = h sqrt(x_i x_j) integral_0^infinity
         exp[-t(x_i+x_j)] phi(ht)^2 dt,
  phi(z)=(exp(z)-1)/z,  phi(0)=1.

All cells have the SAME LENGTH h, so the multiplier phi(ht) is common
to every coordinate. Since phi(z)>=1 for z>=0, for any v in C^m

  v^*(B-H_low)v
    = h integral_0^infinity [phi(ht)^2-1]
         |sum_j v_j sqrt(x_j) exp(-t x_j)|^2 dt >=0.        (5)

The integrals converge, since x_j>=2h. This establishes a genuine
positive-semidefinite difference; an entrywise comparison would not
suffice.

Normalize g_k=f_k/d_k and let J:C^m -> L^2(0,a) send e_k to g_k.
Then J is an isometry and B=D J^* S J D, where D=diag(d_k). Thus,
by the min-max principle, or singular values of S^(1/2) J D,

  lambda_j(H_low) <= lambda_j(B)
      <= ||D||^2 lambda_j(S) <= (2 log 2) lambda_j(S).      (6)

The last step is an eigenvalue bound under bounded congruence; it
does not assert the generally false order DTD<=||D||^2 T.

3. The high block has only O(log(1/epsilon)) eigenvalues uniformly
----------------------------------------------------------------
All remaining x_k lie in [a,2]. For real x,y in that interval write

  1/(x+y)
    = sum_{ell=0}^{q-1} (-1)^ell (x-a)^ell/(a+y)^{ell+1}
         + R_q(x,y),
  |R_q(x,y)| <= r^q/(2a),
  r=(2-a)/(2a) <1.

Multiplying by sqrt(xy)<=2 and by h gives a rank-at-most-q matrix
approximant to the high block H_high with entry errors at most
h r^q/a. Since its size is at most n, its operator-norm error is at
most r^q/a, independently of n. Hence

  lambda_{q+1}(H_high) <= r^q/a,
  N(t;H_high) = O(1+log^+(1/t)),                           (7)

uniformly in n. The approximating matrix need not be symmetric: the
singular-value approximation inequality suffices for this positive
matrix's eigenvalues.

4. Pinching, Laptev's constant, and the order of quantifiers
------------------------------------------------------------
For a positive two-block operator, H<=2 diag(H_low,H_high). Therefore

  N(epsilon;H_{n,1/2})
     <= N(epsilon/2;H_low)+N(epsilon/2;H_high)
     <= N(epsilon/(4 log 2);S)
            + O(1+log^+(1/epsilon)).                      (8)

The continuum operator S is fixed, independent of both n and epsilon.
Laptev's theorem for x^beta y^gamma/(x+y)^b on L^2(0,1) gives

  N(epsilon) ~ log^2(1/epsilon)/(2 pi^2 rho),
  rho=beta+gamma-b+1>0.

Here b=1, beta=gamma=1/2, rho=1. Changing (0,1) to (0,a)
multiplies the operator's eigenvalues by a, which leaves this leading
coefficient unchanged. Combining (2) and (8) proves (B) and (A).

Crucially, the same comparison (8) holds FOR EVERY n. We take sup_n
first, and only then let epsilon decrease. No bound on n in terms of
epsilon, no convergence rate of a quadrature rule, and no interchange
of a fixed-index limit with a growing-index limit occurs.

5. Consequence for every edge location of a unit-weight path
------------------------------------------------------------
The already established relative derivative sandwich is C_alpha<=D_alpha.
In a path eigenbasis, after removing harmless coordinate signs,

  (D_alpha)_{ij}=sqrt(z_i z_j)
                    K_alpha(log lambda_i-log lambda_j),
  0<=z_i<=2/n.

Writing D_alpha=R H_{n,alpha} R with R_ii=sqrt(n z_i), one has
||R||^2<=2, and hence lambda_j(C_alpha)<=2 lambda_j(H_{n,alpha}).
Thus (A) proves the same upper coefficient 1/(2 pi^2) uniformly over
all path sizes AND all existing edge locations. Together with the
previous centered-path lower bound, this establishes that coefficient
for the supremum over unit-weight paths. This paragraph invokes the
prior relative-derivative reduction and lower bound; the new argument
above proves the previously missing uniform matrix upper estimate.
