PARTIAL-SPECTRUM FRESH SEARCH
2026-11-14. Dedicated worker note; other workers' files are unchanged.

STATUS

The original arbitrary-AB rank-r relative spectral target remains open.
This note proves a concrete generalization of the raw signed resolver,
then composes it with the other workers' bounded filter and positive
normalization. The resulting solver handles an ARBITRARY supplied
symmetric core J, INCLUDING SINGULAR J. It needs no J inverse, sign
split, exact eigenbasis, or negative-PSD-base factorization. Its output
error is relative to the POSITIVE part, independent of the negative
spectral scale:

  S=X J X^T, q=n_+(S),
  C>=0, rank(C)<=q, ||C-S_+||<=eps||S_+||,
  conventional ~O(input_size*(q+1)+m^3),
  fast         ~O(input_size*(q+1)^(omega-2)+m^omega).

The input is tall X(N,m), N>=m, plus explicit symmetric J(m,m).
Here input_size=Nm+m^2. All suppressed accuracy/confidence dependencies
are logarithmic. The algorithm learns q from its supported coarse core;
it need not be supplied. Expansion of the N-by-N answer costs an
additional O(N^2 q), or the appropriate fast rectangular product.
When q=0 the algorithm returns zero after preprocessing.

This is an exact-real-arithmetic theorem, not a floating-point stability
claim. It combines the raw worker's contraction/Frobenius mechanism with
the inertia worker's bounded filter and normalization. No invention of
Woodbury, rational filters, Graeffe bounds, or rational low-rank updates
is claimed, and a global novelty claim is not established. The new
algebra here removes the involution/nonsingularity restriction on J.
The original real-threshold bridge is not supplied by this extension.


1. COARSE SUPPORT, INCLUDING RANK DEFICIENCY

Put G=X^T X. Use a sparse constant-distortion OSE, with logarithmic
confidence overhead, to construct Y=Pi X in ~O(Nm) arithmetic and
L(k,m), full row rank, with

  G0=Y^T Y=L^T L,
  (1-eta)G0 <= G <= (1+eta)G0, eta=1/4.                 (1)

The small Gram, support factorization, and all core maps cost ~m^omega,
or ~m^3 conventionally. The true G is NOT formed. The sparse embedding
can use standard OSNAP parameters, not an optimal-constant 2026 result:
Nelson--Nguyen, OSNAP, https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.1002 .

On (1), X=ZL for Z=X L^dagger, with

  (1-eta)I <= Z^T Z <= (1+eta)I.                        (2)

Z is an analytical factor, carried as an implicit map; it is never
applied to all k columns unless the cost permits that width. Set

  K=L J L^T,       S=Z K Z^T.                           (3)

K is symmetric even when J is singular. Because Z has full column rank,
n_+(S)=n_+(K). Thus exact small-core inertia supplies the output width q.

There is a useful all-outcomes rank cap. Always row(L)=row(Pi X) is a
subspace of row(X). Hence K is a congruence compression of the form J
restricted to row(X), whose positive index equals n_+(XJX^T). Therefore

  qhat=n_+(K) <= n_+(S)                                 (4)

even on a failed embedding outcome. Runtime dimensions can use qhat;
on the successful event qhat=q. If k=0 or qhat=0, return zero. A failed
embedding may invalidate the approximation but cannot inflate this width.


2. SIGNED IMAGINARY SOLVES WITH GENERAL, POSSIBLY SINGULAR J

For any nonreal z, I-zJG is invertible: its nonzero eigenvalues are
those of the Hermitian G^(1/2) J G^(1/2), and are therefore real.
Define

  A_z=I-zJG,       A0_z=I-zJG0,       b=J X^T h.

Woodbury, with no J inverse, gives

  (I-zS)^-1 h = h+zX w_*,  w_*=A_z^-1 b,               (5)
  A0_z^-1=I+z J L^T (I-zK)^-1 L.                       (6)

The rank deficiency of J causes no division by zero in these formulas.
Since K is symmetric, I-zK is invertible for every nonreal z.

Let E=G-G0. The Loewner bound gives an analytical symmetric D with

  E=L^T D L,       ||D||<=eta.                          (7)

Use fixed-step preconditioned Richardson, starting from w_0=0:

  w_(l+1)=w_l+A0_z^-1(b-A_z w_l).                       (8)

Writing e_l=w_l-w_*, the coarse physical error obeys the EXACT identity

  L e_(l+1)=z K(I-zK)^-1 D L e_l.                      (9)

Indeed e_(l+1)=z A0_z^-1 J L^T D L e_l, and

  L A0_z^-1 J L^T
    =K+zK(I-zK)^-1K=K(I-zK)^-1.                        (10)

For purely imaginary z, ||zK(I-zK)^-1||<=1, independently of every
eigenvalue magnitude and condition number of J, K, G, or S. Thus

  ||X e_l|| <= sqrt((1+eta)/(1-eta))*eta^l ||Xw_*||.

Also ||zXw_*||=||[(I-zS)^-1-I]h||<=||h||. Consequently the actual
physical resolvent approximation h+zXw_l has error at most

  sqrt((1+eta)/(1-eta))*eta^l ||h||.                    (11)

This is the same scale-free physical bound as for an involution core,
now proved without J^2=I or det(J)!=0. Off-axis sectors also work with
contraction eta*|z|/|Im z|, exactly as in the raw worker's theorem.


3. ONE SMALL PREPARATION, ALL SHIFTS AND THIN RIGHT-HAND SIDES

Compute one exact Frobenius form K=P C P^-1, including P and P^-1.
The primary NPV theorem supplies this in ~O(k^omega):
Neiger--Pernet--Villard, Computing Krylov iterates in the time of matrix
multiplication, ISSAC 2024, Corollary 5.2,
https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07345v2#S5.SS1 .

For a block R=[r_j] and distinct supplied shifts z_j, formula (6) uses

  a_j=L r_j, d_j=P^-1 a_j,
  f_j=(I-z_j C)^-1 d_j,
  A0_zj^-1 r_j=r_j+z_j J L^T P f_j.                    (12)

A companion-block shifted solve takes O(block_size) arithmetic by the
explicit recurrence in raw-factor-fresh-search.txt Section 5. All other
maps in (12), including multiplication by dense J, are thin small-core
products. For b right-hand-side columns, 1<=b<=m, their cost is
O(m^2 b^(omega-2)), versus O(Nm b^(omega-2)) for one raw G action.
Both are ordinary O(m^2 b) and O(Nm b) when omega=3.

Initial X^T H, multiplication by J, and final XW fit the same bound.
Thus b supplied imaginary-shifted solves to per-column error nu||h_j||
cost

  ~O(input_size*b^(omega-2)*log(1/nu)+m^omega).         (13)

There is no per-shift dense inverse or per-shift m^omega setup. With
several poles, process the same b-column RHS separately at each pole,
reusing (12); a polylogarithmic number of poles adds only that factor.
A spectral block-action bound requires per-column accuracy nu/sqrt(b),
which changes only logarithms. All shifts/RHS may depend on the coarse
sketch; the same Loewner event controls them.


4. POSITIVE SCALE FROM THE EXPLICIT COARSE CORE

This is the inertia worker's Graeffe normalization, recorded here to make
the general-core algorithm complete. Let a=lambda_max(K)>0. Choose a
power of two d>=log_2 k, d=O(log k), and form K^d by O(loglog k)
small-core squarings. The nonzero eigenvalue magnitudes mu_j decrease;
the elementary coefficients e_j of K^d satisfy

  mu_j^d/j <= e_j/e_(j-1) <= (k-j+1)mu_j^d.

Obtain e_j by a small-core characteristic polynomial. Thus

  a_j=(e_j/e_(j-1))^(1/d) in [mu_j/2,2mu_j].           (14)

Only repeated positive square roots are needed. The ratios a_j decrease
by Newton log-concavity. Binary-search the first index j* at which
n_+(K-a_j I/4)>0. If mu_l=a, then j*<=l, and

  a/2 <= a_(j*) <4a.                                  (15)

With g_lo=1-eta, g_hi=1+eta, let v=4g_hi*a_(j*). Then (2) gives

  2||S_+|| <= v <=16(g_hi/g_lo)||S_+|| <64||S_+||.      (16)

The O(log k) inertia calls, characteristic polynomial, and squarings
cost ~m^omega or ~m^3. Zero coefficients after the nonzero support are
omitted. There is no loop depending on ||K_-||/||K_+||.


5. WHOLE-LINE BOUNDED FILTER AND POSITIVE-PART INVERSION

Normalize T=S/v and use f(x)=x_+/(1+x^2). Its positive support has
dimension q, ||f(T)||<=2/5, and

  |x|/(1+x^2)=(2/pi)int_0^infty
                         x^2/[(x^2+t^2)(1+x^2)]dt.   (17)

The inertia worker's logarithmic trapezoid construction gives a proper
rational r with r(0)=0, imaginary poles only, and

  sup_(x real)|r(x)-f(x)|<=nu/2,
  degree O(log^2(1/nu)).                               (18)

For completeness, t=exp(y) turns the integrand into
|x|/[2(1+x^2)]*sech(y-log|x|), of amplitude <=1/4. Tails outside
[c nu,C/nu] contribute O(nu); Poisson summation gives exponentially
small trapezoid alias error at spacing O(1/log(1/nu)). Algebraic nodes
t_j=2^((j+1/2)/Q), Q a power of two of order log(1/nu), use only
positive square roots and scalar products. The fixed scalar weight
log(2)/pi can be approximated by geometric/arctangent series at
logarithmic cost if it is not admitted as an input-independent constant.

Partial fractions use

 x^2/[(x^2+t^2)(1+x^2)]
   =[1/(1+x^2)-1/(1+x^2/t^2)]/(1-t^2),                 (19)

and each reciprocal quadratic is the average of two imaginary
resolvents. Half-shifted nodes avoid t=1. The partial-fraction coefficient
mass is polynomial in 1/nu (in fact the near-1 harmonic sum is much
smaller); there is no exponential residue penalty. Formula (13) therefore
applies r(T) to b columns to operator-action accuracy nu with only
logarithmic extra Richardson precision.

Cancel the zero-at-zero identity terms algebraically before accumulation.
The actual finite linear map F0 then has range in range(X), annihilates
ker(X^T), and satisfies ||F0-f(T)||<=nu on the good coarse event. It need
not be exactly symmetric; only its second compressed action is symmetrized.


6. TWO FILTER PASSES, RANK q OUTPUT, ALL EXTRACTION COSTS

For 0<qhat<k, draw Omega(N,qhat+1), independent of the sketch. On the
good event qhat=q. A crude Gaussian inverse/norm bound gives, with
failure at most delta/3,

  ||Omega||*||(U_+^T Omega)^dagger|| <= B,
  B=(100N(m+1)/delta)^3.                               (20)

Here U_+ spans the true positive subspace. Choose

  nu=eps/[2000(B+1)].                                  (21)

First form Y=F0 Omega and an exact orthonormal basis Q of range(Y).
Since f(T) has rank q,

  ||(I-QQ^T)f(T)||<=nu B.                              (22)

Apply the SAME filter again to Q. Set R=sym(Q^T F0 Q), approximate its
bounded symmetric spectral decomposition, clip to [0,2/5], and retain at
most qhat eigenvalues. The resulting PSD core Rhat has rank <=qhat and
differs from Q^T f(T)Q by O(nu). Hence

  Fhat=Q Rhat Q^T,
  ||Fhat-f(T)||<=2nu B+5nu.                            (23)

The scalar inverse on [0,2/5] is

  psi(z)=[1-sqrt(1-4z^2)]/(2z), psi(0)=0.

It has positive Catalan power coefficients. Telescoping powers proves
the OPERATOR Lipschitz bound ||psi(A)-psi(B)||<=(25/12)||A-B|| for
PSD A,B with norm <=2/5. Since psi(f(x))=x on [0,1/2], output

  C=v Q psi(Rhat)Q^T.                                 (24)

Equations (16), (21), and (23) give ||C-S_+||<=eps||S_+||, with
rank(C)<=q. Crucially there is no Q^T S Q action after rangefinding;
the uncontrolled large negative spectral scale never multiplies the
range error. A PSD factor of (24) uses only the retained small core.

If qhat=k, K is positive definite and S is PSD on its support: compute
an exact small Cholesky factor K=H H^T and lift ZH=X(L^dagger H),
using k-column products. This returns S exactly on the good event and
fits the qhat-sensitive bound. It avoids a gratuitous k+1-column branch.

For b<=m, fast rank-revealing QR/Gram-Cholesky on an N-by-b block and
its compressed products cost O(N b^(omega-1)+b^omega), absorbed by
O(Nm b^(omega-2)+m^omega). Conventional QR costs O(Nb^2), absorbed
by input_size*b+m^3. The bounded small symmetric spectral truncation
costs ~b^omega with standard fast Hermitian-core linear algebra, or
~b^3 with maximum-pivot Jacobi; the latter uses only field operations
and square roots. A primary source for the fast bounded Hermitian-core
step is Sobczyk, Deterministic Complexity Analysis of Hermitian
Eigenproblems, ICALP 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21550 : its
Real-RAM diagonalization bound is O(b^omega log b+b^2 polylog(b/nu)).
The input to that step is only the explicitly formed, bounded small R,
so it hides neither a raw Gram nor the negative/positive spectral ratio.
The same arithmetic bounds cover (24)'s thin lift.

All iteration counts are capped in advance by dimension, eps, delta.
For a uniform failed-outcome cap, reject and return zero if the compressed
R has Frobenius norm >2sqrt(qhat+1); this cannot occur on the good event
at the chosen tolerance. The bounded-core eigensolver's absolute stopping
budget then depends only on dimension and nu, not a failed sketch's
possibly huge numerical output. Exact algebraic rank/QR routines have
dimension-only operation bounds. Combined with (4), runtime width never
exceeds the true positive index, on all outcomes.

Storage can reuse one N-by-(qhat+1) filtered block and thin coefficient
buffers, plus raw input and ~m^2 core data. The Frobenius normal-form
routine's temporary storage is conservatively charged at its arithmetic
bound; no sharper in-place space claim is made.


7. WHY THIS DOES NOT BRIDGE A NONZERO THRESHOLD

The new J generality is substantive but limited. For the product dilation
S=[0 AB;(AB)^T 0], its positive index is normally m, so Section 6 costs
the full-width baseline. Replacing S by S-tI does not preserve a known
m-dimensional supported core: a raw identity augmentation has physical
dimension N and many latent sign directions, while a support-coordinate
core contains the UNKNOWN true Gram/inverse mass. General singular J
alone supplies neither that core nor a cheap real-threshold inverse.

Likewise, knowing that an implicitly represented threshold has at most
r positive eigenvalues is not the same as supplying a tall-factor signed
representation of it with the small core already computable. This note
does not replace one promise by the other.


8. PARTIAL-ALGEBRA CHECK: SHORT KRYLOV IS NOT ELIMINATED BY SCALAR AD

One useful distinction survives the audit. Let
F(G)=[v,Gv,...,G^(d-1)v] with one fixed seed. For an arbitrary scalar
linear functional of these outputs, its derivative with respect to G is
a sum whose right factors all belong to K_d(G,v). Thus its unsymmetrized
gradient has rank <=d; restricting G to symmetric inputs gives rank <=2d.
The derivative with respect to a raw Gram factor X is consequently also
thin. In contrast, traces/determinants used in the earlier exact-scalar
shortcuts can have a full-rank G-gradient and recover the whole Gram.

This is an elementary chain-rule calculation, not a lower bound or an
algorithm. It explains why the exact full-statistic AD reductions do not
dispose of the short, one-seed raw-factor Krylov target. A Newton/Hensel
inverse-series scheme still needs inverse action on NEW residual directions
to double precision; knowing the inverse series on one seed does not
supply it. No input-plus-core construction of that partial sequence was
obtained here.

The current concrete result is therefore the general/singular-core signed
positive-part solver, together with an honest unchanged original gate.


9. NUMERICAL SANITY CHECK OF THE NEW GENERAL-CORE IDENTITY

A direct JavaScript complex-arithmetic check used 135 cases, m=3,5,8,
both full-rank and rank-deficient X, and dense singular cores
J=R diag(0,3,-2,3,-2,...)R^T. X had a support Gram within 0.1 of
the chosen coarse Gram. Imaginary shifts varied by a factor 10000.
After six Richardson steps, the maximum error divided by the uniform
bound (11) was 0.011671. The maximum normalized discrepancy in the
exact recurrence (9) was 9.1e-15. This checks noncommuting placement
and singular-core handling numerically; the proof is (5)--(11), not the
finite diagnostic. No finite-word guarantee follows from these cases.
