FRACTIONAL POTENTIALS: DENSE ATOMS AND AN ATOMLESS OBSTRUCTION
============================================================

Owner: /root/fractional_spline_characterization.
Date: 2026-11-15 UTC. New research deduction; independent audit pending.
Only this file is owned. No existing paper or audit has been edited.

This note proves two structural facts beyond the isolated-cusp theorem:
  (i) an atomic-mass necessary condition holds even for dense atoms and
      in the presence of an arbitrary diffuse measure;
  (ii) the atomic condition alone is NOT a characterization for general
       fractional potentials: an absolutely continuous probability
       measure, with C-infinity density away from zero, can give an
       infinite matrix Hoelder constant for every s<1/(1+alpha).

The pure-atomic polynomial-weight GAP is NOT closed. A matching universal
upper above 1/(1+alpha), or weak-type at equality, is also NOT proved.
No novelty certificate or seminal-significance claim is made.

1. DEFINITIONS AND IMPORTED INPUTS
=================================

Fix 0<alpha<1 and I=[0,1]. Put F(x)=|x|^alpha. For a finite complex
Borel measure mu supported in [0,1], define

  f_mu(x)=integral |x-c|^alpha dmu(c),          x in R.

All matrices below are finite Hermitian. For 0<s<=1 define

  H_s^I(f)=sup_(spec(A),spec(B) subset I,A!=B)
       Tr|f(B)-f(A)|^s / Tr|B-A|^(alpha*s).

Norms without a subscript are operator norms. The single-cusp companion
sharp-fractional-holder.tex / fractional-channel-classification.tex gives

  ||F(B)-F(A)|| <= K_alpha ||B-A||^alpha,

and finite H_s(F) over unrestricted real spectra. For every fixed s,
H_s(F) is the supremum over positive rank-one unit updates B-A=vv*,
||v||=1. Only these previously reviewed inputs are used below.

The integrated operator estimate is immediate from the Bochner integral:

  ||f_mu(B)-f_mu(A)||
       <=K_alpha ||mu||_TV ||B-A||^alpha.                 (1.1)

Scalar calculus gives the sharper scalar constant ||mu||_TV, because
||x-c|^alpha-|y-c|^alpha|<=|x-y|^alpha.

2. ATOMIC TANGENTS DO NOT REQUIRE ISOLATION
==========================================

Lemma 2.1. At EVERY atom c of a finite complex measure mu,

  f_mu(c+h)-f_mu(c)=mu({c})|h|^alpha+o(|h|^alpha)          (2.1)

as h tends to zero through arbitrary real values. No isolation of c,
no absolute continuity of the rest of mu, and no analytic remainder
are assumed.

Proof. Remove the atom: nu=mu-mu({c})delta_c, so nu({c})=0. For each
fixed y!=c the kernel |x-y|^alpha is differentiable at x=c. Thus

  [|c+h-y|^alpha-|c-y|^alpha]/|h|^alpha ->0,

since 1-alpha>0. Its absolute value is at most1 uniformly in y,h.
Dominated convergence with respect to the finite variation measure
|nu| proves that the integral is o(|h|^alpha). The atom contributes
exactly the first term of (2.1). For a complex measure, |nu|({c})=0,
so the exceptional point causes no problem. QED.

The same conclusion holds after adding any scalar remainder with
o(|h|^alpha) increments at c. Pointwise scalar o remainders are enough
for the LOWER mechanism below; they do not justify a matching upper.

3. A LOCAL TANGENT PACKING LOWER BOUND
=====================================

Lemma 3.1. Suppose f is operator alpha-Hoelder on a neighborhood of I,
and at finitely many distinct interior points c_j it has expansions

  f(c_j+h)=f(c_j)+F_j(h)+o(|h|^alpha),                     (3.1)

where F_j are homogeneous degree-alpha functions with finite global
single-cusp constants and the positive rank-one reduction. Then for
every choice p_j>0 with sum p_j=1,

  H_s^I(f)>=sum_j p_j^(alpha*s) H_s(F_j).                 (3.2)

Proof. Fix arbitrary finite positive rank-one unit-update witnesses
B_j-A_j=v_jv_j*, ||v_j||=1 for each F_j. Set

  C=diag(c_j I), H_j=(A_j+B_j)/2,
  v=direct_sum sqrt(p_j) v_j,
  T_-=diag(p_j H_j)-vv*/2,
  T_+=diag(p_j H_j)+vv*/2.

The global endpoints A_t=C+tT_-, B_t=C+tT_+ have ONE positive rank-one
increment t vv* of norm t. Their spectra lie in I for sufficiently
small t>0. To evaluate f, apply the finite separated-block transform:
for fixed T=T_+ or T_-, take the anti-Hermitian S with

  S_ij=-T_ij/(c_i-c_j), i!=j; S_ii=0.

Then U_t=exp(tS)=I+O(t), and

  U_t^*(C+tT)U_t=C+t diag(T_jj)+O(t^2).

The operator-Hoelder bound changes the functional-calculus remainder
by O(t^(2alpha)); undoing the unitary costs O(t) because f is bounded
on the fixed compact interval. By finite spectral calculus, (3.1) gives

 f(c_j I+tT_jj)=f(c_j)I+t^alpha F_j(T_jj)+o(t^alpha).

The o remainder is uniform over the finitely many scalar eigenvalues
in this FIXED witness. Thus

 t^-alpha[f(B_t)-f(A_t)]
      ->direct_sum p_j^alpha[F_j(B_j)-F_j(A_j)]           (3.3)

in operator norm. The matrices are finite at this point, so the power
trace converges. The input denominator is exactly t^(alpha*s). Let
the independent local witnesses approach their global suprema to get
(3.2). No supremum/interchange of an infinite trace is used. QED.

Theorem 3.2 (universal atomic necessity). Let a_j=mu({c_j}) be the
nonzero atoms of a finite complex measure at interior points of I.
For every 0<s<=1, with q=s/(1-alpha*s),

  H_s^I(f_mu)>=H_s(F)*(sum_j |a_j|^q)^(1-alpha*s).       (3.4)

The right side may be infinite. In particular H_s^I(f_mu)<infinity
forces its interior atomic masses to belong to ell_q. This includes
dense atoms and arbitrary singular-continuous or absolutely continuous
parts of mu.

Proof. Apply Lemma3.1 to any finite selected set using Lemma2.1 and
(1.1). H_s(a_jF)=|a_j|^s H_s(F). Maximizing (3.2) over the masses
gives (3.4) for each finite set, and increasing finite sets gives the
full sum. The exponent alpha*s<1 throughout the stated range. QED.

For pure-atomic a_j=j^-p, p>1, this proves H_s=infinity for
s<=1/(p+alpha). At equality the sum in (3.4) is harmonic and diverges.
The simple sufficient condition sum |a_j|^s<infinity still only proves
finiteness for s>1/p. The interval between these indices is OPEN here.

4. A DIMENSION-FREE OFF-BLOCK LIPSCHITZ ESTIMATE
==============================================

Lemma 4.1. Let K be a compact interval at distance at least d>0 from
the support of a finite measure nu, and let A,B have spectra in K.
Then

 ||f_nu(B)-f_nu(A)||
          <=alpha*d^(alpha-1)||nu||_TV ||B-A||.          (4.1)

Proof. For c outside K, either A-cI,B-cI>=dI or cI-A,cI-B>=dI.
The positive fractional resolvent difference gives

 ||P^alpha-Q^alpha||
 <=[sin(pi alpha)/pi]||P-Q|| integral_0^infinity
                                        t^alpha/(d+t)^2 dt
 =alpha*d^(alpha-1)||P-Q||.

Integrate this estimate against |nu|. The bound is independent of
dimension, measure location on either side of K, or fine structure of
nu's support. QED.

This explicit uniformity allows all later blocks of a measure to be
chosen AFTER one witness pair has already been fixed.

5. AN ABSOLUTELY CONTINUOUS COUNTEREXAMPLE WITH NO ATOMS
======================================================

Theorem 5.1. For every fixed alpha in(0,1), there is a positive
probability measure mu supported in [0,1], with mu((0,1))=1, absolutely continuous with
a C-infinity density on R\{0}, such that f_mu is globally scalar and
operator alpha-Hoelder and belongs to C-infinity(R\{0}), but

        H_s^[0,1](f_mu)=infinity
                   for EVERY 0<s<1/(1+alpha).            (5.1)

The lower witnesses already have positive rank-one perturbations and
positive semidefinite endpoints. The SAME measure works for every s
in the displayed range. Thus even an empty atomic mass sequence does
not imply finiteness in this class.

Proof. Choose pairwise disjoint compact carrier intervals

 J_k=[2^-k, 2^-k+2^(-k-3)],  k>=1,

and expanded intervals K_k with J_k inside the interior of K_k,
K_k subset(0,1), and distance(K_k, union_(l!=k)J_l)=d_k>0.
For example expand J_k by one tenth of its length at each endpoint.
All these choices precede the rest of the construction. Put

  w_k=2^-k,       M_k=2^(k^2),
  a_k=w_k/M_k,    e_k=a_k M_k^-alpha
                         =w_k M_k^(-1-alpha).

Choose M_k distinct centers c_(k,j) in the interior of J_k. For this
one block let

  phi_k(x)=a_k sum_(j=1)^M_k |x-c_(k,j)|^alpha,
  C_k=diag(c_(k,j)), v_k=(1,...,1)/sqrt(M_k),
  T_(k,-)=I/(2M_k)-v_kv_k*/2,
  T_(k,+)=I/(2M_k)+v_kv_k*/2.

The diagonal entries of T_(k,-),T_(k,+) are respectively0 and1/M_k.
The local scalar witness is simply A_j=0,B_j=1. The finite block
blow-up in Lemma3.1 consequently gives

 t^-alpha[phi_k(C_k+tT_(k,+))-phi_k(C_k+tT_(k,-))]
                       ->e_k I_(M_k) as t->0.             (5.2)

Choose t_k>0 so small that both endpoint spectra lie in K_k and

 norm of the difference in (5.2) from e_k I <=e_k/8,
 alpha*d_k^(alpha-1)*t_k^(1-alpha)<=e_k/8.                 (5.3)

This is possible by (5.2) and alpha<1. Importantly, (5.3) uses only
the universal bound for the TOTAL mass of all other blocks, at most1,
and does not depend on how any future block is distributed inside J_l.
Set A_k=C_k+t_kT_(k,-), B_k=C_k+t_kT_(k,+). Then

  B_k-A_k=t_k v_kv_k*, ||B_k-A_k||=t_k.

Fix a nonnegative C-infinity bump psi of integral1 supported in[-1,1].
Replace each atomic mass a_k at c_(k,j) by its convolution with
psi_(delta_k)(x)=delta_k^-1 psi(x/delta_k). Choose delta_k>0 so small
that every bump remains inside J_k and

                  2w_k delta_k^alpha<=t_k^alpha e_k/8.   (5.4)

No dependence on a Schatten exponent enters any choice above. Let
mu_k be this smooth measure of total mass w_k, and put mu=sum_k mu_k.
It is a positive probability measure. The density sum is locally finite
away from zero, hence C-infinity there. In particular mu has NO atoms.

For every real x, scalar Hoelder continuity gives

  |f_(mu_k)(x)-phi_k(x)|<=w_k delta_k^alpha.

Functional calculus therefore gives the correction estimate

 ||[f_(mu_k)(B_k)-f_(mu_k)(A_k)]-[phi_k(B_k)-phi_k(A_k)]||
                <=2w_k delta_k^alpha<=t_k^alpha e_k/8.    (5.5)

The remaining measure nu_k=mu-mu_k has mass at most1 and support
outside K_k at distance at least d_k. Lemma4.1 and (5.3) give

       ||f_(nu_k)(B_k)-f_(nu_k)(A_k)||
                              <=t_k^alpha e_k/8.         (5.6)

Combining (5.3),(5.5),(5.6),

 ||t_k^-alpha[f_mu(B_k)-f_mu(A_k)]-e_k I_(M_k)||
                                      <=3e_k/8<e_k/2.

Thus its M_k singular values are all at least e_k/2. For every fixed
s>0, its normalized power trace is consequently at least

   2^-s w_k^s M_k^[1-(1+alpha)s].                        (5.7)

The denominator is exactly t_k^(alpha*s), since the input increment
has positive rank one. For s<1/(1+alpha), logarithms of (5.7) grow as
[1-(1+alpha)s]k^2*log2-s*k*log2+O(1), tending to infinity. This proves
(5.1) for the SAME mu and witness sequence for all such s.

The scalar and operator alpha-Hoelder bounds follow from (1.1) and
mu's unit mass. For the remaining smoothness assertion, at any point
x!=0 only finitely many block measures are nearby. Each of their
potentials is C-infinity, since convolution of the locally integrable
polynomial-growth kernel |x|^alpha with a compactly supported smooth
function is smooth (differentiate the smooth function). The remaining
blocks have support a fixed positive distance from a neighborhood of x;
all kernel derivatives there are uniformly bounded against their
finite total mass. Termwise differentiation is then justified for
every derivative order. Hence f_mu belongs to C-infinity(R\{0}). QED.

All witnesses are finite matrices, although their dimensions M_k grow.
No infinite-dimensional functional calculus, unverified trace limit,
or cancellation of a large background was needed. Parameters t_k and
delta_k may be extremely small. This is an existence/counterexample
theorem, not a charged algorithm or finite-word stability statement.

6. COMPARISON WITH A GOOD ATOMLESS MEASURE
========================================

For Lebesgue probability measure on[0,1], the potential on I is

  f_good(x)=[x^(alpha+1)+(1-x)^(alpha+1)]/(alpha+1).

It has H_s^I finite for EVERY 0<s<=1. This can be proved directly,
without a general smooth-function characterization. For PSD A,B<=I,
put E=B-A and D=B^alpha-A^alpha. The identity

  B^(alpha+1)-A^(alpha+1)=B D+E A^alpha

and the power triangle imply

 Tr|B^(alpha+1)-A^(alpha+1)|^s
 <=Tr|D|^s+Tr|E|^s
 <=[H_s^+(alpha)+1] Tr|E|^(alpha*s),

since ||E||<=1. Apply the same estimate to I-A,I-B and add. Therefore

 H_s^I(f_good)<=2(alpha+1)^(-s)[H_s^+(alpha)+1]<infinity.  (6.1)

Both mu_good and the measure in Theorem5.1 are atomless probability
measures with smooth densities in the OPEN interval(0,1). Their
fractional potentials can nevertheless have radically different
small-Schatten behavior. Absence of atoms and qualitative smoothness
away from the endpoint do not provide a sufficient criterion.

7. PRECISE OPEN UPPER GATES
==========================

The following stronger claim remains a CANDIDATE, not a theorem here:

  H_s^I(f_mu)<=C_(alpha,s)||mu||_TV^s
               for every finite mu when s>1/(1+alpha).    (7.1)

If true, Theorem5.1 would prove its universal threshold sharp. The
critical strong or weak estimate at s=1/(1+alpha) is also open here.
The finite atomic lower has q=s/(1-alpha*s), which crosses1 precisely
at this threshold. This motivates(7.1) but is NOT an upper proof.

For the original PURE-ATOMIC polynomial sequence, the fixed-s gap
1/(p+alpha)<s<=1/p also remains open in this note. The atomless example
does not pretend to be a counterexample within that narrower class.

A parent-supplied promising route concerns Hankel operators: a periodic
cusp model has a positive moment Hankel matrix with diagonal of order
j^(-1-alpha). Translation averaging can be factored through a positive
twirl, whose trace-s moment is bounded by diagonal pinching when
s>1/(1+alpha). This may prove a uniform Hankel estimate for mixtures.
The transfer to the FULL normalized nonlinear matrix supremum is NOT
automatic: arbitrary spectral measures and updates allow expanding
background-to-update ratios. A fixed-model Hankel estimate is not a
proof of(7.1), and that bridge has not been supplied here.

8. PRIMARY COMPARISON AND HONEST NOVELTY SCOPE
===========================================

Freshly read primary statements:

  E. McDonald, F. Sukochev, Lipschitz estimates in quasi-Banach Schatten
  ideals, Math. Ann.383 (2022),571--619,
  https://arxiv.org/html/2009.08069
  Theorem4.3.2 gives disjoint-support smooth-function coefficient
  control; Theorem4.5.3 gives a Besov sufficient operator-Hoelder
  condition and Theorem4.6.2 its weak counterpart. These are genuine
  antecedents. Substitution of the present cusp mixtures is not a
  proof of the conjectured(7.1): a nonzero individual cusp already has
  borderline fine-scale behavior not covered by naive finite Besov
  norm substitution. No new general Besov or wavelet principle is claimed.

  J. Huang, F. Sukochev, D. Zanin, Operator theta-Hoelder functions
  with respect to ||.||p, JLMS105 (2022),2436--2477,
  https://arxiv.org/html/2110.09708
  Read Introduction, Theorem1.2, Section2.3 and its necessary Besov
  discussion. Qualitative C-infinity smoothness off zero alone is not
  their hypothesis: quantitative weighted derivative bounds define
  S_(d,alpha). The bad measure above violates some of those bounds;
  there is no contradiction with that established sufficient theorem.

  A. Aleksandrov, V. Peller, Functions of operators under perturbations
  of class S_p, JFA258 (2010),3675--3724,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3623
  The primary abstract and the HSZ primary discussion identify broad
  necessary Besov conditions and finite-rank perturbation theory.
  These antecedents prevent a first-discovery claim that scalar
  Hoelder regularity does not ensure all quasi-Schatten estimates.

The precise present additions are the finite-measure atomic tangent

potential counterexample with one measure bad for all subcritical s.
A bounded search has not certified their novelty. Counterexample
assembly by disjoint blocks, smooth approximation, and uniform control
of remote contributions is classical methodology. This is not asserted
to be a seminal result or a characterization of the whole class.
