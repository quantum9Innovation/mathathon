Candidate result: full Conjecture 2, including its fixed prefactor
----------------------------------------------------------------
Let 0<c<d<=1 and gamma=d-c. There is C(c,d)<infinity such that, for
every integer M>=1, there is a positive Stieltjes rational function

  R_M(x)=sum_{j=1}^M w_j/(x+t_j),  w_j>0, t_j>0,

with R_M(x)<=x^{-c} for all x>0 and

  sup_{0<x<=1} x^d [x^{-c}-R_M(x)]
       <= C(c,d) exp[-2*pi*sqrt(gamma*M)].                    (C2)

The poles can be taken distinct; zero weights/padding can handle small
M. In particular, R_M has type [M-1/M], so this implies the exact full
Conjecture 2 of Faust--Fawzi2023 under alpha=-c, beta=-d. This is a
fixed multiplicative constant at the endpoint exponent, not merely
a logarithmic-rate statement.

Proof ingredient 1: Ganelius product (classical)
-----------------------------------------------
For each fixed r>0 and all sufficiently large n, there are explicit
a_1,...,a_n in (0,1) such that, for

  B_n(s)=prod_{j=1}^n (s-a_j)/(s+a_j),

  sup_{0<=s<=1} s^r |B_n(s)| <= A_r exp[-pi*sqrt(r*n)].       (G)

A_r is independent of n. The finitely many smaller n can be absorbed
into A_r. We use r=gamma/4. If desired, the explicit formula is TOS
Definition2.5: n0=n-ceil((pi/4)*sqrt(n*r)), phi(u)=exp(pi*sqrt(u/r));
a_j=phi(j-1)/phi(n0) for1<=j<=n0; a_(n0+1)=phi(n0-1/2)/phi(n0);
a_j=1-(j-n0-1)/[5(n-n0-1)] for n0+2<=j<=n. Use only large n with
n0>3 and n-n0-1>0, as in TOS AppendixB.

Proof ingredient 2: double-node multipoint Gaussian error
--------------------------------------------------------
Let rho be a finite positive measure on [0,1], with infinite support,
and set

  g(x)=integral_[0,1] d rho(t)/(x+t), x>0.
  Omega(t)=prod_j(t+a_j)^2,
  d nu(t)=d rho(t)/Omega(t).

Take the n-node Gaussian quadrature for nu, with nodes t_i in(0,1)
and weights A_i>0, and define

  r_n(x)=sum_i A_i Omega(t_i)/(x+t_i).

It is a positive Stieltjes rational function of type[n-1/n], agrees
with g and g' at every a_j, and is globally a lower bound for g.

Indeed, Omega(t)-Omega(-x) is divisible by t+x, and the quotient has
degree2n-1, so Gaussian exactness yields

  g(x)-r_n(x)=Omega(-x)[S_nu(x)-Gauss_n(S_nu)(x)].

Let q_n be the monic degree-n orthogonal polynomial for nu. The usual
Gaussian Cauchy-transform identity gives

 S_nu(x)-Gauss_n(S_nu)(x)
     = [q_n(-x)]^{-2} integral q_n(t)^2/(x+t) d nu(t).        (E)

For completeness, multiply the integrand by q_n(t)^2 and use Gaussian
exactness on [q_n(t)^2-q_n(-x)^2]/(t+x), a polynomial of degree2n-1;
the Gaussian samples q_n(t_i)^2 all vanish. This proves(E), including
its positive sign.

The polynomial q_n(t)/q_n(-x) minimizes

   integral p(t)^2/(x+t) d nu(t)

over all polynomials deg p<=n with p(-x)=1: the difference of any
competitor from q_n/q_n(-x) has the form(t+x)s(t), deg s<=n-1;
the cross term vanishes by orthogonality. Select competitor

  p(t)=prod_j(t-a_j)/prod_j(-x-a_j).

Combining with(E) gives the central double-product bound

  0<=g(x)-r_n(x)
       <= B_n(x)^2 integral_[0,1] B_n(t)^2/(x+t) d rho(t).   (DP)

There are TWO occurrences of the Ganelius product. Bounding only
the one at x loses the factor sqrt(2) and misses the conjecture.

Apply(DP) to the singular part
--------------------------------
For 0<c<d<=1, gamma=d-c, set r=gamma/4 and

  g_c(x)=integral_0^1 t^{-c}/(x+t)dt.

This is a finite positive Markov measure because c<1. By(G),

  B_n(s)^2 <= A_r^2 exp[-2*pi*sqrt(r*n)] s^{-2r}.

Therefore, for 0<x<=1,

 x^d(g_c-r_n)(x)
  <= A_r^4 exp[-4*pi*sqrt(r*n)]
       x^{d-2r} integral_0^1 t^{-c-2r}/(x+t)dt
  <= A_r^4 [pi/sin(pi*(c+2r))]
       exp[-4*pi*sqrt(r*n)] x^{d-c-4r}
  = C_0(c,d) exp[-2*pi*sqrt(gamma*n)].                      (S)

Here p=c+2r=(c+d)/2 lies STRICTLY between0and1 even when d=1,
so integral_0^infty t^{-p}/(x+t)dt=pi*x^{-p}/sin(pi*p) is valid.
The identity d-c-4r=0 is why the quarter-weight r=gamma/4 works.

Treat the analytic tail with positive quadrature
------------------------------------------------
The Stieltjes representation of the full negative power is

  x^{-c}=kappa_c[g_c(x)+h_c(x)],
  kappa_c=sin(pi*c)/pi>0,
  h_c(x)=integral_1^infty t^{-c}/(x+t)dt
        =integral_0^1 s^{c-1}/(1+x*s)ds.

Let H_k(x) be the k-point Gaussian quadrature for the measure
s^{c-1}ds on[0,1]. It is a positive Stieltjes rational sum with poles
strictly below-1, and is a global lower bound to h_c for x>0.
For 0<=x<=1 expand1/(1+x*s) around s=1/2. Its ratio has magnitude

  |x(s-1/2)/(1+x/2)|<=1/3.

Truncation after degree2k-1 has uniform remainder<=3/2*3^{-2k}.
Both the measure and its quadrature weights have total mass1/c.
Gaussian exactness therefore gives

  0<=h_c(x)-H_k(x)<= (3/c)*3^{-2k}, 0<=x<=1.               (A)

This inequality also follows from standard Gauss-Jacobi theory; the
Taylor argument makes the constant and degree accounting explicit.

Fixed-prefactor degree accounting
---------------------------------
For total budget M choose

  k=ceil(pi*sqrt(gamma*M)/log3), n=M-k,

for all sufficiently large M that n>=1. Set

  R_M=kappa_c(r_n+H_k).

It has M positive poles and type[M-1/M]; the low poles lie in(-1,0),
the high poles in(-infty,-1), so there is no overlap. Equations(S,A)
give

 sup x^d[x^{-c}-R_M(x)]
  <=kappa_c[C_0 exp(-2*pi*sqrt(gamma*n))
                   +(3/c)exp(-2*pi*sqrt(gamma*M))].

As k<=pi*sqrt(gamma*M)/log3+1,

 sqrt(M)-sqrt(n)=k/[sqrt(M)+sqrt(n)]
              <=pi*sqrt(gamma)/log3+1.

Thus this is at most a fixed C(c,d)*exp[-2*pi*sqrt(gamma*M)].
For the finitely many smaller M, use the M-node Gaussian rule for
t^{-c}(1+t)^{-2M}dt and rescale its positive weights by(1+t_i)^{2M}.
It has M distinct positive poles, is a global positive lower bound,
and has weighted error<=1 on(0,1]. Increase C for these finite cases.
This proves(C2), provided the displayed
classical product theorem is accepted. Every other step is proved
above without asymptotic remainders.

Direct positive construction for FULL Conjecture 1

This bypasses the stated Conjecture2 implication and proves positivity
and the double zero at1 directly, including a=1 and b=2.

Fix1<=a<b<=2, write gamma=b-a and kappa=a*gamma/b. Let

 H_a(x)=f_a(x)/(x-1)^2
       =K_a integral_0^infty t^a/[(1+t)^2(x+t)]dt,           (H)
 K_a=sin(pi*(a-1))/[pi*a*(a-1)] for a>1, K_1=1.

The formula extends at x=1 continuously and is FF's representation
after a simple change of variables. Since a<b<=2, always a<2, so
K_a>0 and both tails converge. Split(H) as

 H_a(x)/K_a=G_-(x)+G_+(x),
 G_-(x)=integral_0^1 t^a/[(1+t)^2(x+t)]dt,
 G_+(x)=integral_0^1 s^{1-a}/[(1+s)^2(1+x*s)]ds.

Uniform elementary lower bound on the relative weight
-----------------------------------------------------
Because f_b''(x)=x^{b-2},

 H_b(x)=integral_0^1 (1-u)[1+u(x-1)]^{b-2}du
       >=(1/2)*min(1,x^{b-2}) for ALL x>0.                (W)

This includes b=2 with equality H_2=1/2. Thus no excluded endpoint
case in FF Theorem11 is needed.

Generalized quadrature bound with extra moment conditions
--------------------------------------------------------
Let rho(t)dt be a positive density on[0,1]. Choose n positive
Ganelius nodes a_j and let

 Omega(t)=t^ell prod_{j=1}^n(t+a_j)^2, ell=0 or1.

Assume nu(dt)=rho(t)dt/Omega(t) is finite. Take m=n+k Gaussian
nodes for nu, where k>=1. Define the positive rational quadrature

 Q(x)=sum_{i=1}^m A_i Omega(t_i)/(x+t_i).

The degree condition2n+ell-1<=2m-1 holds. The same Gaussian Cauchy
error identity and Christoffel minimum as above apply. Test with

 p(t)=[prod_j(t-a_j)](t-1/2)^k
                 /{[prod_j(-x-a_j)](-x-1/2)^k}.

This proves

 |integral rho(t)/(x+t)dt-Q(x)|
 <= x^ell B_n(x)^2 integral_0^1 B_n(t)^2
       [(t-1/2)/(x+1/2)]^{2k} rho(t)t^{-ell}/(x+t)dt.      (GX)

In particular, the extra ratio is <=1 for x>0,t in[0,1], and is
at most(2x+1)^{-2k}. The error sign is(-1)^ell: ell=0 is a lower
approximation, ell=1 an upper approximation, but all weights remain
strictly positive in both cases.

One global quadrature: bounded overhead and elementary preprocessing
===================================================================
This is the preferred, sharper candidate proof. It replaces the
two separately constructed compact quadratures by ONE quadrature on
(0,infinity). In particular it needs no analytic-tail node reserve.

Set gamma=b-a, kappa=a*gamma/b, fix integer n_-,n_+>=1, and let

  m=n_-+n_++1.

Take Ganelius nodes a_j in(0,1), j=1..n_-, with parameter r_-=a/4;
and Ganelius nodes b_j in(0,1), j=1..n_+, with parameter r_+=gamma/4.
Define

 B_-(s)=prod_j(s-a_j)/(s+a_j),
 B_+(s)=prod_j(s-b_j)/(s+b_j),
 B(s)=B_-(s)*B_+(1/s),
 D_-=A_(a/4)^2 exp[-pi*sqrt(a*n_-)],
 D_+=A_(gamma/4)^2 exp[-pi*sqrt(gamma*n_+)].

The irrelevant sign in B_+(1/s) can be dropped. For every s>0,
|B(s)|<=1. Moreover the classical product theorem gives

 B(s)^2<=D_-*s^{-a/2} for0<s<=1,
 B(s)^2<=D_+*s^{gamma/2} for1<=s<infinity.                 (ENV)

The single positive modified measure
-------------------------------------
Let rho(t)=t^a/(1+t)^2 and

 Omega(t)=t(t+1) prod_j(t+a_j)^2 prod_j(t+1/b_j)^2,
 nu(dt)=rho(t)dt/Omega(t).

Omega has degree2m and is strictly positive for t>0. At zero,
nu has density asymptotic to a positive constant times t^{a-1},
which is integrable for a>=1. At infinity it has density asymptotic
to t^{a-2m-2}. Thus ALL moments through degree2m-1 are finite,
because the last moment behaves as t^{a-3} and a<2.

It is not necessary for the degree2m moment to be finite. The
following elementary Gaussian construction uses only2m-1 moments.
Solve for the monic polynomial q_m of degree m satisfying

 integral q_m(t)t^j dnu(t)=0, j=0,...,m-1.

The m-by-m moment matrix is positive definite, so q_m exists and
is unique. It has m distinct roots t_i in(0,infinity): otherwise,
multiplying q_m by the product of its fewer than m sign-changing
positive roots gives a polynomial of degree<=2m-1 of one nonzero
sign on the support, contradicting orthogonality. Let ell_i be its
degree m-1 Lagrange polynomials and set A_i=integral ell_i dnu.
Polynomial division by q_m proves Gaussian exactness through degree
2m-1, and A_i=integral ell_i^2 dnu>0 proves positive weights.

Define the actual approximant of H_a by

 R_m(x)=K_a sum_{i=1}^m A_i Omega(t_i)/(x+t_i).            (R)

It has m distinct negative poles and strictly positive residues.

Exact error identity and the special test polynomial
-----------------------------------------------------
Polynomial division of Omega(t)-Omega(-x) by t+x, followed by the
Gaussian identity(E), gives

 [H_a(x)-R_m(x)]/K_a
   =Omega(-x)/q_m(-x)^2
               integral q_m(t)^2/(x+t) dnu(t).            (GE)

All integrals are finite. Although integral q_m(t)^2 dnu(t) may
diverge, integral q_m(t)^2/(x+t)dnu(t) uses at most the finite
degree2m-1 moment. The Christoffel minimum argument remains valid:
its cross term is integral q_m(t)s(t)dnu(t), deg s<=m-1.

Use the degree-m test polynomial

 q0(t)=(t-1) prod_j(t-a_j) prod_j(t-1/b_j).

The two ratios simplify as

 |Omega(-x)|/q0(-x)^2
   = [x*|x-1|/(x+1)^2] * B(x)^2,

 q0(t)^2/Omega(t)
   = [(t-1)^2/(t(t+1))] * B(t)^2.

Thus(GE) and Christoffel minimization prove the key global bound

 |H_a(x)-R_m(x)|/K_a
 <= [x*|x-1|/(x+1)^2] B(x)^2
       integral_0^infinity B(t)^2
            t^{a-1}(t-1)^2/[(1+t)^3(x+t)]dt.             (GLOBAL)

Everything in this estimate is positive. The actual error has sign
sign(x-1), since Omega(-x)=x(x-1) times a square. In particular
R_m interpolates H_a at x=1.

Uniform bound for x<=1
----------------------
For x<=1 the outside factor is at most x. For t<=1 the integrand
apart from B(t)^2 is at most t^{a-1}/(x+t); for t>=1 it is at most
t^{a-2}/(x+t). Apply(ENV) to x and to the two pieces of the integral:

 |H_a-R_m|/K_a
 <=D_-^2 x^{1-a/2} integral_0^1 t^{a/2-1}/(x+t)dt
   +D_-D_+ x^{1-a/2}
                   integral_1^infinity t^{a-2+gamma/2}/(x+t)dt
 <=D_-^2 *pi/sin(pi*a/2)
   +D_-D_+ /[2-a-gamma/2].                              (LEFT)

The denominator2-a-gamma/2=2-(a+b)/2 is positive because a<b<=2.
Also1-a/2>0. The first beta integral is valid because0<a/2<1.
Use(W), H_b>=1/2 here, to get the same relative estimate times2K_a.

Uniform bound for x>=1
----------------------
The outside factor is at most1. Apply(ENV) to x and split at t=1:

 |H_a-R_m|/K_a
 <=D_-D_+ x^{gamma/2} integral_0^1 t^{a/2-1}/(x+t)dt
   +D_+^2 x^{gamma/2}
                   integral_1^infinity t^{a-2+gamma/2}/(x+t)dt.

For the first term, use1/(x+t)<=1/x and integral_0^1 t^{a/2-1}dt=2/a.
Divide by(W), H_b>=x^{b-2}/2: its remaining x power is
gamma/2-1+2-b=1-(a+b)/2<0, hence it is bounded by1.

For the second term put p=2-a-gamma/2=2-(a+b)/2, which lies in(0,1).
The beta integral over(0,infinity) gives pi*x^{-p}/sin(pi*p).
Its x power after division byH_b is gamma/2-p+2-b=0. Therefore

 |H_a(x)-R_m(x)|/H_b(x)
 <=2*K_a[(2/a)D_-D_+ +(pi/sin(pi*p))D_+^2], x>=1.         (RIGHT)

Together(LEFT,RIGHT) prove

 sup_{x>0} |H_a(x)-R_m(x)|/H_b(x)
       <=C(a,b)[D_-^2+D_-D_++D_+^2].                    (FIN)

All constants are fixed, independent of n_-,n_+ and m.

Node count and exact conjectured bound
---------------------------------------

 n_-=ceil[L^2/(4*pi^2*a)],

Then each term on the RHS of(FIN) is at most a fixed constant times

 m<= L^2/[4*pi^2*kappa]+3.                               (BESTCOUNT)

For each large integer m one may instead allocate

exponent is their mean. Thus directly

 epsilon^[m]_(a,b) <= C(a,b)*exp[-2*pi*sqrt(kappa*m)]

for all m>=1 after enlarging C over finitely many small m. This is
the FULL exact fixed-prefactor Conjecture1 in its positive and,
by the involution already given, negative parameter ranges.

Allowed positive rational quadrature
-------------------------------------
From(R), set T_i=1/(1+t_i) in(0,1),
u_i=K_a A_i Omega(t_i)/(1+t_i)>0. Then

 fhat_m(x)=(x-1)^2 R_m(x)
           =sum_i u_i (x-1)^2/[1+T_i(x-1)].

The denominator roots are distinct, so the T_i are distinct and can
be sorted. The factor(x-1)^2 gives exact double Hermite constraints
at1; indeed the construction also matches H_a(1)=1/2, so fhat''(1)=1.

Elementary scalar preprocessing, with costs explicitly separated
------------------------------------------------------------------
R_m is the unique type[m-1/m] rational Hermite interpolant of H_a at
the following total2m data:

   x=0 once, x=1 once,
   x=a_j twice for all low Ganelius nodes,
   x=1/b_j twice for all high Ganelius nodes.

At zero H_a(0)=1/a and at1 H_a(1)=1/2. All other values/first
derivatives are explicit from x^a, or x*log(x) whena=1. Repeated
Ganelius nodes, if any, mean the corresponding higher Hermite data.

The interpolation statement follows directly from(GE), with the
zero at x=0 also justified by Gaussian exactness on Omega(t)/t,
and the zero at1 by exactness on Omega(t)/(t+1), both polynomials
of degree2m-1. For distinct positive nodes the other zeros are double.
Uniqueness follows because the difference numerator of two type
[m-1/m] rationals has degree<=2m-1 but at least2m zeros counted with
multiplicity. Both denominators have no nonnegative roots.

Consequently, construction can proceed without moment integration:
form the2m linear interpolation equations for numerator and monic
denominator coefficients and solve by dense elimination. This costs
O(m^3) scalar arithmetic operations, O(m^2) storage, and O(m)
elementary power/log evaluations for distinct nodes. Obtain the m
positive t_i as the negatives of the denominator roots, and the
positive residues as P(-t_i)/Q'(-t_i). Node/residue extraction is a
degree-m real-root problem, explicitly charged separately; it is
not assumed free. The theorem proves exact positivity of that result.

This is an exact-real arithmetic preprocessing bound. A certified
finite-precision root extraction/conditioning analysis has not yet
been completed here, so no bit-complexity or stable machine-precision
claim is made. The mathematical theorem and node count do not depend
on choosing this particular numerical implementation.

Explicit SDP consequences, with the quantum block-size caveat
============================================================
Scalar epigraph: introduce scalars y_i, require tau>=sum u_i*y_i and

  [[1+T_i(x-1), x-1], [x-1,y_i]] >=0

for each of the m nodes. Thus exactly m PSD blocks of size2 and m
auxiliary scalars represent tau>=fhat_m(x), x>0.

Matrix perspective: for positive definite n-by-n Hermitian A,B, set
P_f(A,B)=B^(1/2) f(B^(-1/2) A B^(-1/2)) B^(1/2).
Its rational approximation is

 P_fhat(A,B)=sum_i u_i(A-B)[(1-T_i)B+T_i A]^{-1}(A-B).

Introduce Hermitian Y_i and require T>=sum_i u_i Y_i together with

 [[(1-T_i)B+T_i A, A-B], [A-B,Y_i]] >=0.

This is an explicit representation with exactly m PSD blocks of
size2n. Functional calculus and congruence give the certified matrix
inequalities -epsilon P_fb <= P_fa-P_fhat <= epsilon P_fb.
This claim is independently proved from the scalar theorem.

Petz quantum f-divergence / Umegaki relative entropy: on the n^2
dimensional Hilbert--Schmidt space let L_rho(Z)=rho*Z,
R_sigma(Z)=Z*sigma, and b=vec(rho-sigma). Each allowed kernel has

 S_fT(rho||sigma)
  = b^* [T I tensor rho+(1-T) sigma^transpose tensor I]^{-1} b.

This follows by diagonalizing rho and sigma on the left and right;
its summands are |<u_i,v_j>|^2*(rho_i-sigma_j)^2 /
[(1-T)*sigma_j+T*rho_i]. Therefore the epigraph of S_fhat has m
Schur-complement blocks of size(n^2+1), with the displayed affine
top-left matrix, off-diagonal b, and a scalar bottom-right y_i,
plus tau>=sum u_i y_i. This supplies an explicit, polynomial-size
SDP without needing an unpublished representation theorem.

For a=1,b=2 and density matrices of trace1, scalar functional
calculus for the relative modular operator gives

 |D(rho||sigma)-S_fhat(rho||sigma)|
  <=(epsilon/2)[Tr(rho^2 sigma^{-1})-1].

For target scalar error epsilon, the proven construction gives
m<=log^2(C/epsilon)/(2*pi^2)+3, with fixed C, rather than the
log^2(1/epsilon)/pi^2 leading node count implied by FF's Theorem4.
The actual end-to-end SDP solution cost depends on n and on the
chosen solver; the theorem is an explicit approximation-size result,
not a claim of quadratic-time quantum entropy optimization.

but explicitly offers it without proof, citing forthcoming Fawzi2023

Its author publication page inspected2026-11-14 still does not list

One global quadrature also gives elementary preparation for Conjecture2
=====================================================================
The first Conjecture2 proof above split the Markov integral and used
an analytic-tail quadrature. This variant keeps one global quadrature
and uses only values/derivatives of x^{-c} as interpolation data.

Fix0<c<d<=1, gamma=d-c, r=gamma/4, and take n Ganelius nodes a_j.
LetM=n+k and

  Omega(t)=(1+t)^{2k} prod_j(t+a_j)^2,
  nu(dt)=t^{-c}dt/Omega(t).

The moments through2M-1 are finite: the last tail is t^{-c-1} and
the zero endpoint is t^{-c}. Take its M-node Gaussian quadrature
and rescale weights by Omega(t_i), as before. After multiplying by
K_c=sin(pi*c)/pi this is a positive Stieltjes rational R_M, and
0<=R_M(x)<=x^{-c} for allx>0 because Omega(-x)>=0.

Test Christoffel minimization with q0(t)=prod_j(t-a_j), degree n<=M.
For0<x<=1 this yields

 x^d[x^{-c}-R_M(x)]/K_c
 <=x^d(1-x)^{2k} B_n(x)^2
         integral_0^infinity B_n(t)^2 t^{-c}/[(1+t)^{2k}(x+t)]dt.

Put D=A_r^2 exp[-pi*sqrt(gamma*n)]. On(0,1], B_n(s)^2<=D*s^{-gamma/2}.
Splitting the last integral at1, its first part is bounded as before
byD^2*pi/sin(pi*(c+d)/2). In the second part, discard B_n(t)^2<=1,
use(1+t)^{-2k}<=2^{-2k},1/(x+t)<=1/t, and x^{(c+d)/2}<=1. Thus

 sup_{0<x<=1} x^d[x^{-c}-R_M(x)]
  <=K_c[D^2*pi/sin(pi*(c+d)/2) +(D/c)2^{-2k}].            (C2GLOBAL)

For target exponentL choose

 n=ceil[L^2/(4*pi^2*gamma)],
 k=max(1,ceil[L/(4*log2)]).

Then(C2GLOBAL)<=C(c,d)e^{-L}, with

 M<=L^2/(4*pi^2*gamma)+L/(4*log2)+O(1).

This again proves the FULL fixed-prefactor Conjecture2, with an
explicit positive lower approximant of type[M-1/M]. It is the
Hermite interpolant of x^{-c} at each a_j twice and at1 with
multiplicity2k. These2M data use only elementary powers and their
derivatives. Dense scalar preparation costsO(M^3) arithmetic and
O(M^2) storage plus the charged degree-M pole extraction, as in the
preferred Conjecture1 construction.

Conservative finite-precision preparation route (separate audit needed)
=====================================================================
This section gives a polynomial bound rather than claiming that the
monomial Hermite system is stable at machine precision. Its matrix
perturbation constants should receive an independent numerical-analysis
audit before presenting a polished bit-complexity theorem.

For the preferred global Conjecture1 construction, normalize

 Omega(t)=t(t+1) prod_j[(t+c_j)/(1+c_j)]^2,

where the m-1 numbers c_j are the low a_j and reciprocal high1/b_j.
This scalar normalization does not change the resulting quadrature.
Set s=t/(1+t) and

 eta(ds)=(1-s)^[-(2m-1)] nu(dt),
 D(s)=prod_j[(s+c_j(1-s))/(1+c_j)]^2.

An exact change of variables yields

 eta(ds)=s^{a-1}(1-s)^{1-a}ds/D(s), s in(0,1).            (ETA)

The transformed m Gaussian nodes are s_i=t_i/(1+t_i). If B_i are
the Gaussian weights for eta, then the original weights are
A_i=B_i(1-s_i)^{2m-1}. The actual positive pole residues in H_a are

 v_i=K_a B_i s_i D(s_i)/(1-s_i).                          (RES)

Indeed, transforming any polynomial of degree<=2m-1 in t gives a
polynomial of that degree in s after multiplication by(1-s)^{2m-1};
this proves both directions of the quadrature correspondence.

For fixed a,b, explicit Ganelius nodes have |log c_j|<=C_(a,b)*sqrt(m).
Each factor(s+c_j(1-s))/(1+c_j) lies between
min(1,c_j)/(1+c_j) and1. Therefore

 exp[-C_(a,b)m^{3/2}]<=D(s)<=1, 0<=s<=1.

Thus eta lies between the fixed Jacobi weight
w_a(s)=s^{a-1}(1-s)^{1-a} and exp[C m^{3/2}]w_a(s). Both Jacobi
exponents exceed-1. All moments and the total mass are bounded by
exp[C m^{3/2}], while the m-by-m monomial Gram matrix H0 has
lambda_min(H0)>=exp[-C'm]. To see the latter bound, restrict the
integral to[1/4,3/4], where w_a is bounded below, and use shifted
Legendre polynomials to bound coefficient norm by C^m times L2 norm.
The harmless powers of m can be absorbed into C'^m. The same lower
bound holds for H1=integral s*v(s)v(s)^T deta(s) and H0-H1, with
v(s)=(1,s,...,s^{m-1})^T.

Consequences:
  * Every s_i and1-s_i is at least exp[-C m^{3/2}], by the generalized
    Rayleigh quotients for H1,H0 and H0-H1,H0.
  * Every B_i>=lambda_min(H0)/m>=exp[-C'm], using the Christoffel
    identity B_i=1/[v(s_i)^T H0^{-1}v(s_i)].
  * Every pair of nodes is separated by exp[-C m^{3/2}]. Indeed, if
    V has columns v(s_i), then H0=V diag(B_i)V^T, B_i<=eta([0,1]),
    so sigma_min(V)>=sqrt(lambda_min(H0)/eta([0,1])). Since
    ||v(s_i)-v(s_j)||<=m^{3/2}|s_i-s_j| and also is at least
    sqrt(2)*sigma_min(V), the stated gap follows.

Consequently standard Cholesky plus symmetric generalized-eigenvalue
perturbation bounds imply that moments accurate to

  P=C_(a,b)m^{3/2}+C_(a,b)L+O(log m) bits

suffice for relative pole/residue errors O(exp(-L)), with positivity
preserved by interval certification. The endpoint gaps control the
map t=s/(1-s); (RES) and the root gap control weights and residues.
This is a conservative bound, not an optimized condition estimate.

One can compute these moments with polynomial work. Under the
logistic map s=exp(y)/(1+exp(y)), the2m moment integrands extend to
any fixed strip |Im y|<=d<pi. On, for example, d=pi/4 their envelope
is bounded by

 exp[C m^{3/2}] min(exp(a*y),exp(-(2-a)*y)), y real,

uniformly for moment degree0,...,2m-1. This follows factor by factor
from |exp(y+iv)+c|>=cos(d/2)(exp(y)+c), including the rational D.
The usual trapezoid strip bound plus tail truncation gives P-bit
absolute moment accuracy with

 N=O_(a,b)((P+m^{3/2})^2)

real samples. Evaluating all2m moments and all denominator factors
at these samples costs O(m*N) P-bit scalar operations, plus N
elementary exponential/power evaluations. Gaussian recovery costs
O(m^3) scalar linear algebra plus polynomial-precision symmetric
eigenvalue work. Thus there is a fully polynomial preparation route
in m and L; for m=O(L^2) its conservative sample arithmetic count is
O(m^4+mL^2). Elementary function bit costs are to be charged in the
usual way for fixed polynomial-time-computable a,b.

Finally, relative perturbation of positive poles and residues by eta
changes their Stieltjes sum by at most O(eta) times that positive sum,
uniformly for ALL x>0. Since H_a/H_b is bounded (finite endpoint
limits and continuity), and R_m/H_b is bounded by the established
approximation theorem, eta=O(exp(-L)) preserves the target relative
error after a fixed factor. It preserves the positive SDP structure.

The preferred O(m^3) elementary Hermite route may be much faster in
practice; this conservative moment route supplies a way to charge
certification and high precision rather than treating pole extraction
or exact Gaussian quadrature as free.
