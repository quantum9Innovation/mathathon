\paragraph{Status.}
This note proves an oracle reduction, with an explicit raw output factor.
It does not prove that the required oracles can be implemented by a
gap-independent, nearly-linear number of shifted graph solves.  Its
purpose is to isolate that access problem without losing the
log-determinant rank budget or charging fractional powers as free.

Let $A\succ0$, $B=A+uu^T$, $0<\alpha<1$, and
\[
 \Delta=B^\alpha-A^\alpha,\qquad
 C=A^{-\alpha/2}\Delta A^{-\alpha/2},\qquad
 b=\alpha\log(1+u^TA^{-1}u).
\]
Thus $C\succeq0$ and $\log\det(I+C)=b$.  The argument applies to
any positive semidefinite correction with this log-determinant budget,
including a common-kernel graph problem restricted to its support.

\begin{lemma}[Log-determinant controls ridge dimension]
For $0<\mu\le1$,
\[
 d_{\rm eff}(C;\mu):=\tr C(C+\mu I)^{-1}\le b/\mu.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every $x\ge0$,
\[
 \frac{\mu x}{x+\mu}\le\frac{x}{1+x}\le\log(1+x).
\]
The first inequality uses $\mu\le1$, and the second follows by
integrating $(1+t)^{-1}$ on $[0,x]$.  Sum over the eigenvalues of $C$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact access model.}
Charge $S$ for all setup.  After setup suppose that two operations are
available: one independent sample $\omega\sim N(0,A^{-\alpha})$ costs
$T_{\rm cov}$, and one action $z\mapsto\Delta z$ costs $T_\Delta$.
These assumptions concern exact arithmetic.  In particular,
$T_{\rm cov}$ includes applying $A^{-\alpha/2}$ to a Gaussian vector,
or the full cost of any equivalent covariance sampler; $T_\Delta$
includes the full cost of both fractional matrix-function actions.
They are not assumed to be ordinary shifted SDD solves.

\begin{theorem}[Raw-factor conditional construction]
For $0<\varepsilon,\delta<1/2$, the exact access model above admits
a randomized construction of an explicit $X\in\R^{n\times r}$ such
that, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\[
 0\preceq\Delta-XX^T\preceq\varepsilon A^\alpha.
\]
One may take
\[
 r=O\bigl((1+b/\varepsilon)\log(1/\delta)\bigr)
\]
or $r=n$, whichever is preferable.  Its complete charged cost is
\[
 S+r(T_{\rm cov}+T_\Delta)+O(nr^2+r^3),
\]
with $O(nr+r^2)$ storage.  No graph spectral gap or condition number
appears in the reduction itself.  Every such dependence in either
oracle remains charged through $T_{\rm cov}$ and $T_\Delta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $\mu=\varepsilon/40$, and let
\[
 p=\max\{3,\lceil2b/\mu\rceil+1\},\qquad \ell=2p-1.
\]
First suppose $\ell<n$.  Independently draw $t=\lceil\log_2(1/\delta)\rceil$
blocks $\Omega_j$ with $\ell$ columns from the stated covariance,
concatenate them to $\Omega$, and form
\[
 Y=\Delta\Omega,\qquad M=\Omega^TY,\qquad
 X=Y(M^\dagger)^{1/2}.
\]
Zero eigenvalues of the small Gram matrix can be dropped.
This explicitly constructs the raw factors without applying
$A^{\alpha/2}$ to a whitened output factor.

In distribution, $\Omega=A^{-\alpha/2}G$, where $G$ is standard
Gaussian.  Consequently
\[
 A^{-\alpha/2}Y=CG,\qquad M=G^TCG,
\]
and hence
\[
 A^{-\alpha/2}XX^TA^{-\alpha/2}
       =CG(G^TCG)^\dagger G^TC=:\widehat C.
\]
For every $G$, including singular cases, the last matrix is
$C^{1/2}P C^{1/2}$, where $P$ projects orthogonally onto
$\operatorname{range}(C^{1/2}G)$.  Therefore
$0\preceq\widehat C\preceq C$ exactly.  Enlarging $G$ by more columns
can only increase this orthogonal projector and decrease the residual
in Loewner order.

Write the eigenvalues of $C$ as $c_1\ge\cdots\ge c_n$.  The
Gaussian Nystr\"om estimate of Frangella, Tropp, and Udell
\cite[Corollary 2.3]{FTU} gives for one block
\[
 \mathbb E\|C-\widehat C_j\|
 \le 3c_p+\frac{4e^2}{p}\sum_{i\ge p}c_i.
\]
Here $p\ge2d_{\rm eff}(C;\mu)+1$.  The definition of effective
dimension implies $c_p\le\mu$: otherwise each of the first $p$
eigenvalues contributes more than $1/2$.  Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{i\ge p}c_i\le\mu d_{\rm eff}.
\]
To see this, put $d=d_{\rm eff}$ and $s=\sum_{i\ge p}c_i$.
If $c_p=x$, then
\[
 d\ge \frac{(p-1)x}{x+\mu}
             +\frac{s}{x+\mu},
\]
because the first $p-1$ eigenvalues are at least $x$ and the remaining
ones are at most $x$.  Thus
$s\le\mu d-x(p-1-d)\le\mu d$.
This gives
\[
 \mathbb E\|C-\widehat C_j\|
 \le(3+2e^2)\mu<\varepsilon/2.
\]
Markov's inequality bounds the probability of a block residual above
$\varepsilon$ by $1/2$.  With independent blocks, all block residuals
exceed $\varepsilon$ with probability at most $2^{-t}\le\delta$.
The concatenated residual is no larger than each individual residual,
so this proves the assertion.  This amplification requires no
unavailable residual-norm test or selection of a successful block.

There are $r=\ell t$ samples and actions.  Forming $M$, computing
its pseudoinverse square root, and multiplying $Y$ by that root cost
$O(nr^2+r^3)$, including the explicit factor construction.  If the
prescribed block width reaches $n$, take $n$ Gaussian columns once;
their range is the full support almost surely, giving $XX^T=\Delta$
exactly in the same cost model.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What this does and does not resolve.}
The structural rank can therefore be converted into an explicit
one-sided factor without a further exponential dependence on
$b=\alpha\log(1+\tau)$.  A trace-only argument would instead give
$\tr C\le e^b-1$, which loses this feature for large leverage.
The matrix arithmetic is linear in $n$ when the target rank is fixed,
but quadratic in the target rank; it is not an $O(nr)$ factor algorithm.

The missing graph primitive is substantial.  A shifted SDD solver
supplies $(L+tI)^{-1}z$ to relative shifted-energy error.  It does not
by itself supply the fractional Gaussian sample or a product by
$L'^\alpha-L^\alpha$ in the norm used above.  Replacing the second
oracle by the existing absolute quadrature theorem requires absolute
accuracy at most $\varepsilon\lambda_{\min}^+(L)^\alpha$ in a
worst-case norm conversion, reintroducing the forbidden scale.
Likewise, an inexact $\Delta$ action generally destroys the exact
Nystr\"om Loewner inequality unless an additional certified argument
is supplied.  Thus this is a reduction with fully charged oracle
costs, not a proof of the requested nearly-linear graph construction
and not an impossibility theorem for explicit graphs.
