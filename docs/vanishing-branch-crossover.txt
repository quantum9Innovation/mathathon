1. DEFINITIONS AND RESULTS
--------------------------
Fix alpha in (0,1), beta=1-alpha, and set

  g_+(x)=(-i x)^alpha,  g_-(x)=(i x)^alpha,
  b=1/(2 pi^2 alpha beta),  C=2b=1/(pi^2 alpha beta).

All powers use the principal branch and both maps vanish at zero.
For Hermitian finite matrices define the POWER constant

  H_s(f)=sup_(A!=B) tr|f(B)-f(A)|^s / tr|B-A|^(alpha s).

For fixed positive integer r, let N_(f,r)(L) be the supremum of
the number of singular values of f(B)-f(A) STRICTLY above exp(-L),
over finite Hermitian pairs with ||B-A||=1 and rank(B-A)<=r.
All input matrix dimensions are permitted.

THEOREM U (uniform l_s coefficient law).

  lim_(s down to 0) sup_((kappa,lambda)!=(0,0))
    | s^2 H_s(kappa g_+ +lambda g_-)
           / (|kappa|^s+|lambda|^s) - C | =0.        (U)

Thus the earlier integer channel count is the fixed-coefficient special
case of an asymptotic l_s power formula. The coefficient can now depend
arbitrarily on s, including superexponential approach to an exceptional
analytic line. No assertion of uniformity in alpha is made.

THEOREM A (uniform accuracy law).
For every fixed positive integer r,

  lim_(L to infinity) sup_(theta in C, |theta|<=1)
    | L^(-2) N_(g_+ +theta g_-,r)(L)
       -r b {1+[1+L^(-1)log|theta|]_+^2} | =0.    (A)

Here log 0=-infinity and its positive-part term is zero. In particular,
if -log|theta_L|/L ->tau in [0,infinity], including tau=infinity, then

  L^(-2) N_(g_+ +theta_L g_-,r)(L)
      -> r b [1+(1-tau)_+^2].                       (A')

Both statements allow completely arbitrary coefficient phases.
The moments and counts have DIFFERENT transition functions: if
theta_s^mod=exp(-u/s+o(1/s)), then

  s^2 H_s(g_+ +theta_s g_-) -> C(1+exp(-u)),        (U')

whereas a branch of size exp(-tau L) contributes no leading accuracy
count at threshold exp(-L) once tau>=1.

The lower construction actually proves a simultaneous, coefficient-
uniform threshold profile. This is the key strengthening needed for U;
integrating supremal counts at separately selected matrices would not
prove a moment lower.

2. THE UNIFORM SIMULTANEOUS PROFILE LEMMA
---------------------------------------
Fix delta>0 and K>0. Put

  b_delta=(1/(2 pi^2))[1/alpha+1/(beta+delta)] < b.

For all sufficiently large L there is ONE finite Hermitian pair
(A_L,B_L), depending on alpha,delta,K,L but NOT theta, whose increment
is rank one with norm t_L in (0,1], such that for every |theta|<=1
and every x in [0,K],

  L^(-2) # {sigma_j(Delta_(theta,L))>exp(-xL)}
    >= b_delta [x^2+(x-u_(theta,L))_+^2]
                        -C_(alpha,delta,K) L^(-1/2),             (P)

where Delta_(theta,L)=(g_+ +theta g_-)(B_L)
                         -(g_+ +theta g_-)(A_L),
      u_(theta,L)=-L^(-1)log|theta| in [0,infinity].

For theta=0 the second summand means zero. The same pair works for
all coefficient phases and all thresholds x in the stated interval.
It is harmless if the right side of P is negative near x=0.

PROOF OF P.
Use the centered construction of the frozen companion Sections 3--5:

  rho(x)=c0 exp(x) (x<=0), c0 exp(-delta x) (x>=0),
  c0=(1+1/delta)^(-1), D=M_(exp x), w=sqrt(rho)|I,
  H=[[0,D],[D,0]], v=(w,0), A=H-vv*/2, B=H+vv*/2.

Take I to cover [-(K+1)L/alpha,(K+1)L/(beta+delta)],
enlarged to cell endpoints. The actual Woodbury weight omega_I is
bounded above and below by positive constants uniformly in I, and
the exact singular pencil is W K_theta W*, with

  K_theta=diag(1,-theta),  ||K_theta||<=1.

Use cells of length ell=sqrt(L) and polynomial localization degree
ceil(C1 L), with C1 fixed sufficiently large depending on K.
The checked full-line localization, including all input variables
outside I, gives

  W K_theta W* = Acal K_theta Acal* + R_(theta,L)+Z_(theta,L),
  rank R_(theta,L) <= C_(alpha,delta,K) L^(3/2),
  ||Z_(theta,L)|| <= C_(alpha,delta,K) exp(-(K+3)L).             (1)

Crucially these constants are UNIFORM in theta. The factors W and
Wtilde=W-small_error are uniformly bounded; ||K_theta||<=1 is the
only coefficient bound used in the product error estimate. No large
norm of the finite-rank approximation is charged.

On a single periodic cell the exact middle is

  Acal K_theta Acal*
    =2 M_u U Hhat diag(S,-theta S) Hhat* U* M_u,
  S=Q M_(v_I^2) Q >= (inf_cell v_I)^2 Q^2.                    (2)

Here U,Hhat are unitaries and Q has multiplier whose squared modulus
at frequency 2pi k/ell is (pi^2/2)sech(2pi^2 k/ell).
The two middle singular channels have factors 1 and |theta|.
The inverse bounds on the two OUTER positive spatial weights M_u
give a singular-value lower for each ordered singular value of their
direct sum. There is no assumption of positivity of K_theta and no
Schatten pinching or deletion of a weight in an indefinite integral.

Writing g_delta(y)=alpha(-y)_+ +(beta+delta)y_+, a cell starting at
y_j therefore supplies the multiset lower bounds

  c exp(-g_delta(y_j)-C ell) exp(-2pi^2 |k|/ell),
  c |theta| exp(-g_delta(y_j)-C ell) exp(-2pi^2 |k|/ell),
                    k in Z,                                    (3)

with c,C independent of theta. Unlike the fixed-coefficient proof,
do NOT replace the two amplitudes by their minimum.

For a fixed absolute threshold multiplier, e.g. 4 exp(-xL), the
cell count is at least

  (ell/pi^2)[xL-g_delta(y_j)-C ell-C0]_+ -1
   +(ell/pi^2)[xL+log|theta|-g_delta(y_j)-C ell-C0]_+ -1.        (4)

If theta=0 simply omit the second channel; keeping its possible
negative -1 error only weakens the inequality. Both effective depths
lie in [0,KL] after taking positive parts. Riemann summation and

  int_R [h-g_delta(y)]_+dy
      =(h_+^2/2)[1/alpha+1/(beta+delta)]

give, UNIFORMLY for 0<=x<=K and |theta|<=1,

  N_model(4 exp(-xL))
   >= b_delta L^2[x^2+(x-u_(theta,L))_+^2]
                         -C_(alpha,delta,K) L^(3/2).           (5)

Indeed the cell-minimum loss O(ell) in either depth loses at most
O_K(L ell) from the squared phase volume; the Riemann error is of
that order, and the integer count errors are O_K(L/ell). This is
uniform even when the second depth approaches zero. The interval I
contains the support of both positive triangular envelopes.

The count perturbation inequality and (1) discard O(L^(3/2))
singular values. Its exponentially small error is less than exp(-xL)
uniformly on x in [0,K], so the factor 4 in (5) provides a strict
margin for the actual threshold. This proves the infinite-space
bounded-interval version of P.

Finally approximate D uniformly by finite step functions, keeping w
fixed. At this fixed L choose the approximation so BOTH corrections
Delta g_+ and Delta g_- change by less than exp(-(K+3)L).
Functional calculus gives such an approximation, and then
||Delta f_theta-new -Delta f_theta-old||<=2 exp(-(K+3)L)
simultaneously for every |theta|<=1. The finite cell-vector spans
reduce both endpoints, and the corrections are zero on their
orthogonal complements. Thus these are genuine finite matrices.
The remaining strict threshold margin proves P for that one finite
pair, for all theta and x simultaneously. The increment is still
vv*, of norm t_L=int_I rho in (0,1]. This proves the lemma.

3. PROOF OF THE UNIFORM MOMENT THEOREM U
---------------------------------------
For 0<s<=1 the exact signed rank-one reduction and the trace s-power
triangle give

  H_s(kappa g_+ +lambda g_-)
      <= (|kappa|^s+|lambda|^s) H_s(g_+),                      (6)

because g_- corrections are adjoints of g_+ corrections.
The reviewed one-branch theorem gives s^2 H_s(g_+)->C.
This proves the required UNIFORM upper.

For the lower, scalar homogeneity, multiplication of f by a scalar
phase, and the exchange g_+(-x)=g_-(x) reduce every nonzero pair of
coefficients to f_theta=g_+ +theta g_- with |theta|<=1. These operations
preserve H_s divided by the coefficient power sum. Put L=1/s and
u=-s log|theta|, allowing u=infinity.

For the SAME finite pair in P, layer cake over the positive logarithmic
thresholds, the denominator t_L^(alpha s)<=1, and P give

  s^2 H_s(f_theta)
   >= b_delta int_0^K exp(-x)[x^2+(x-u)_+^2]dx
                       -C_(alpha,delta,K) sqrt(s).             (7)

No issue is caused by singular values above one: their contribution
is nonnegative, and only the positive logarithmic thresholds are used
for this lower. The integral identity

  int_0^infinity exp(-x)[x^2+(x-u)_+^2]dx
       =2(1+exp(-u))=2(1+|theta|^s)                           (8)

holds also at u=infinity. Moreover (x-u)_+^2<=x^2 for u>=0, so
the omitted tail is bounded UNIFORMLY by 2 T_K, where
T_K=int_K^infinity exp(-x)x^2 dx ->0.
Divide (7) by 1+|theta|^s>=1. It follows uniformly in theta that

  s^2 H_s(f_theta)/(1+|theta|^s)
   >=2b_delta-2b_delta T_K-C_(alpha,delta,K) sqrt(s).            (9)

First let s->0 at fixed delta,K, then K->infinity, then delta->0.
Together with (6), this proves U. Thus no compactness/subsequence
argument, and no interchange of a supremum and an integral, is needed.

4. PROOF OF THE UNIFORM ACCURACY THEOREM A
-----------------------------------------
Write b_d=1/(4 pi d alpha beta), with fixed d<pi/2, so b_d down to b.
The one-branch Gaussian localization theorem supplies unit-input
rank-r corrections at error exp(-h) using rank at most

  r b_d h^2+O_(alpha,d,r)(h^(7/4))+O_(alpha,d,r)(1).             (10)

All envelopes are independent of the background spectrum. For
f_theta and target exp(-L), use separate branch quadratures with
depths

  h_+=L+C_d,
  h_-=[L+log|theta|]_+ +C_d,                                  (11)

where C_d is a sufficiently large FIXED constant to pass the
large-depth threshold and make the two weighted errors sum to at
most exp(-L). When theta=0 omit the second quadrature. Its inclusion
would only cost a constant number of nodes. The second weighted error
bound follows from |theta|exp(-h_-)<=exp(-L-C_d), including the
case L+log|theta|<0.

Since 0<=[L+log|theta|]_+<=L, the resulting rank bound is UNIFORM:

  N_(f_theta,r)(L)
    <=r b_d {L^2+[L+log|theta|]_+^2}
                            +O_(alpha,d,r)(L^(7/4)+1).        (12)

For the matching lower take K=1 in P, evaluate at x=1, and scale
BOTH finite endpoints by 1/t_L after preservation. Homogeneity makes
every output singular value increase, and the input norm becomes
exactly one. For r use r direct copies of this same pair. Thus

  N_(f_theta,r)(L)
    >=r b_delta {L^2+[L+log|theta|]_+^2}
                              -O_(alpha,delta,r)(L^(3/2)).    (13)

Equations (12)--(13) sandwich the normalized count UNIFORMLY in
theta. Let L->infinity at fixed d,delta, then d->pi/2 and delta->0.
This proves A. The sequential law A' follows immediately.

If the stated sequence in A' permits |theta_L|>1 but still
-log|theta_L|/L->tau>=0, the same law holds: set
M_L=max(1,|theta_L|), divide the function by M_L, and if necessary
exchange the branches. Then log M_L=o(L), so the changed threshold
depth L+log M_L is asymptotic to L, and the smaller normalized branch
has the same limiting tau (tau=infinity already forces |theta_L|<1).

5. STATUS AND SCOPE
-------------------
The uniform laws are deductions from the checked exact nonlinear
factorization and classical weighted-Cauchy localization, retaining
the two separate amplitudes. Laptev1974 remains a direct antecedent
for exact Cauchy spectral asymptotics and this localization mechanism;
Arazy--Zelenko2006 is an antecedent for rank-one/Hankel/Riesz-channel
connections. Neither mechanism is claimed new here.

These statements are stronger than the fixed-function exceptional-line
classification: they resolve arbitrarily fast or slow approach to its
exceptional lines, quantitatively. A separate primary-literature search
has not certified their novelty. They imply neither finite-word numerical
stability nor a general quadratic-time matrix algorithm.

The remaining sections prove the mixed-pole spectral compression and
the resulting joint dimension law. No inference of a sharp dimension
law is made merely from a count upper and lower.

6. GENERAL q+1-STATE SINGULAR-SPECTRUM REALIZATION
-----------------------------------------------
LEMMA G.
Let A=A*, ||v||=1, B=A+vv*, and let z_1,...,z_q be distinct nonreal
poles such that z_j!=conj(z_i) for every i,j. They may occupy BOTH
open half-planes. For any scalar node coefficients d_j there exist
Hermitian A', B'=A'+v'v'^* of size at most q+1, with ||v'||=1, such
that

  Q=sum_j d_j[R_B(z_j)-R_A(z_j)],
  Q'=sum_j d_j[R_B'(z_j)-R_A'(z_j)]

have identical singular values after zero padding.

PROOF.
Let mu be the probability spectral measure of (A,v), and set

  m(z)=int (t-z)^(-1)dmu(t),
  w(t)=prod_j (t-z_j)(t-conj(z_j)) >0 for real t.

Use ordinary (q+1)-point positive Gaussian quadrature for dmu/w,
exact on polynomials through degree 2q+1; if its support has at most
q+1 points use the support exactly. Reweight the Gaussian weights
a_k by b_k=a_k w(t_k). The polynomial w has degree 2q and
w/(t-z_j) has degree 2q-1. Consequently the reweighted positive
measure mu'=sum b_k delta_(t_k) has mass exactly one and matches
m at every z_j and every conj(z_j). Realize it by

  A'=diag(t_k),  v'=(sqrt(b_k)),  B'=A'+v'v'^*.

Put U_j=R_A(z_j)v and V_j=R_A(conj(z_j))v. Both column Grams are

  (U*U)_ij=[m(conj(z_i))-m(z_j)]/[conj(z_i)-z_j],
  (V*V)_ij=[m(z_i)-m(conj(z_j))]/[z_i-conj(z_j)].                (14)

The no-conjugate-pair hypothesis says EXACTLY that every denominator
in (14) is nonzero, including mixed-half-plane indices. Hence both
Grams are reproduced. The rank-one Woodbury formula gives

  R_B(z_j)-R_A(z_j)
      =-U_j V_j*/[1+m(z_j)],                                  (15)

and those denominators are matched too. They are nonzero because
B is Hermitian and z_j is nonreal (also Im m(z_j) has the sign of
Im z_j). Thus Q=U D V* and Q'=U'DV'^* have the same two Grams
and diagonal D. Their singular values therefore coincide: the
Gram identities induce isometries between the left column spans
and, separately, the right column spans. These are not asserted to
be one common Hermitian congruence. The mass identity gives the
required EXACT unit increment norm in the model. This proves G.

The modified-measure positive quadrature is classical (Gautschi2001,
Theorem 3.1 and Section 6(ii)). The content needed here is the
singular-spectrum realization using BOTH Grams, extended from one
half-plane to any pole set without conjugate collisions. No claim
of a new Gaussian quadrature construction is made.

7. REMOVING CONJUGATE COLLISIONS AT ARBITRARILY SMALL ERROR
--------------------------------------------------------
For a fixed finite two-branch quadrature, merge repeated poles within
each half-plane. Its poles are nonreal, but often conjugate pairs.
Leave the negative-half-plane poles unchanged and shift ALL positive-
half-plane poles horizontally by a common nonzero real h, chosen
arbitrarily small. Originally the poles are purely imaginary, so
there are now no exact conjugate pairs. Distinct poles within either
half-plane stay distinct.

This change preserves approximation UNIFORMLY in the Hermitian
background. For T=T* and real h,

  ||R_T(z+h)-R_T(z)|| <= |h|/|Im z|^2.

Consequently, with D_(A,B)(z)=R_B(z)-R_A(z),

  ||D_(A,B)(z+h)-D_(A,B)(z)||
       <= 2|h|/|Im z|^2.                                     (16)

If Q=sum d_j D_(A,B)(z_j), the perturbation costs at most
2|h|sum_(shifted j)|d_j|/|Im z_j|^2, a FINITE number times |h|
depending only on this quadrature, never on A or B. The same h
therefore controls the quadrature perturbation for both the original
and every compressed Hermitian endpoint pair. Choose h sufficiently
small to spend any prescribed fraction of the target error.

At each depth all quantities remain finite. Close conjugate pairs
can make the Gram divided differences ill-conditioned; the lemma
is an exact-realization/existence statement, with no finite-word
conditioning or stability assertion.

COMPRESSION CONSEQUENCE.
Fix d<pi/2 and |theta|<=1. At a target depth h_0->infinity, use
separate branch quadratures to approximate f_theta=g_+ +theta g_-.
As in (11), their two leading node counts use depths h_0 and
[h_0+log|theta|]_+. Fixed additive depth constants make the initial
combined error at most exp(-h_0)/4. Perturb the positive poles as
above at a cost at most exp(-h_0)/4, uniformly for all backgrounds.
Apply G to the resulting q-pole set and reproduce the perturbed
quadrature's singular values exactly in at most q+1 states.

For the compressed endpoints, the ORIGINAL branch quadratures have
the same error bound by the uniform unit-input envelope, and (16)
bounds their perturbation too. Thus both actual corrections are
within exp(-h_0)/2 of their own matched perturbed quadrature.
After zero padding,

  |sigma_j(Delta)-sigma_j(Delta')| <=exp(-h_0),
  dim A' <= b_d {h_0^2+[h_0+log|theta|]_+^2}
                         +O_(alpha,d)(h_0^(7/4)+1),
  ||B'-A'||=1.                                               (17)

The error could be replaced by any fixed multiple of exp(-h_0)
without changing the leading node count. All bounds in (17) are
uniform for |theta|<=1. The tiny pole shift may depend on the actual
quadrature. The endpoint background may be arbitrarily large.

This argument also compresses an unperturbed conjugate-symmetric
quadrature in q_total+1 states up to arbitrarily small singular error.
It does NOT claim exact equality of the original Hermitian correction
by a common compression, nor does it match resolvent derivatives at
exact conjugate pairs. The pole perturbation is indispensable for
the stated simple Gram proof.

8. JOINT DIMENSION / VANISHING-COEFFICIENT THEOREM
------------------------------------------------
For |theta|<=1 write f_theta=g_+ +theta g_-, and let H_(s,n) mean
the same power-constant supremum restricted to size at most n.
Suppose s->0, n_s s^2->t in (0,infinity), and

  u_s=-s log|theta_s| ->u in [0,infinity],

again allowing u=infinity. Define

  p_u(x)=b[x^2+(x-u)_+^2],  x>=0,
  p_infinity(x)=b x^2,
  F(t,u)=int_0^infinity exp(-x) min(t,p_u(x)) dx.

THEOREM D.

  s^2 H_(s,n_s)(f_(theta_s)) -> F(t,u).                       (D)

The formula is phase independent. If a is the unique positive
solution p_u(a)=t, direct integration gives:

  if u=infinity, or t<=b u^2:
     a=sqrt(t/b),
     F(t,u)=2b[1-(1+a)exp(-a)];

  if u<infinity and t>b u^2:
     a=[u+sqrt(2t/b-u^2)]/2,
     F(t,u)=2b[1+exp(-u)-(2+2a-u)exp(-a)].                    (18)

At t=b u^2 the expressions agree. At u=0 this is the earlier
two-channel Phi_(2b)(t); at u=infinity it is Phi_b(t).
For fixed u, F(t,u)->2b(1+exp(-u)) as t->infinity, consistently
with U. The finite-dimensional coefficient is exactly one-branch
when t<=b u^2.

PROOF OF THE UPPER.
Use the exact signed rank-one reduction within dimension n_s.
For its normalized rank-one correction Delta, (12) and the dimension
cap give, at x=sL>=0,

  s^2 N_Delta(exp(-x/s))
   <=min(n_s s^2, b_d[x^2+(x-u_s)_+^2]
                                  +O_d(s^(1/4)x^(7/4)+s^2)). (19)

The harmless O(s x) from fixed additive depth constants is absorbed
by a slightly enlarged integrable remainder. For clarity (19) can
equivalently carry O_d(s x+s^2+s^(1/4)x^(7/4)). Its coefficients
are uniform in theta_s and the background.

There is a common output norm bound M depending only on alpha.
Singular values above one contribute at most n_s(M^s-1), whose
product with s^2 tends to zero. Layer cake, dominated convergence
in (19), and u_s->u give the upper F_d(t,u) with b replaced by b_d.
First let s->0 and then d->pi/2. This proves the upper F(t,u).

PROOF OF THE LOWER: NO EXTREMAL-PROFILE DEFICIT ARGUMENT IS NEEDED.
Fix delta>0, d<pi/2, and a>0 with

  p_(u,d)(a):=b_d[a^2+(a-u)_+^2] < t.                        (20)

Choose K>a+1 and use the simultaneous finite pair from P at L=1/s.
Normalize both endpoints by 1/t_L to make its input norm exactly
one; this can only increase all its output singular values. Apply
(17) at depth h_0=a/s. Its dimension is at most

  s^(-2)b_d[a^2+(a-u_s)_+^2]+o(s^(-2)) < n_s               (21)

eventually. This holds also if u=infinity; then the second effective
depth is eventually zero. The compressed increment has exact norm one.

For 0<x'<x<a,

  exp(-x'/s)-exp(-a/s)>exp(-x/s)

when s is small. Hence singular perturbation in (17), P, and
x' up to x give the compressed lower profile

  liminf s^2 N_(Delta'_s)(exp(-x/s))
      >= b_delta[x^2+(x-u)_+^2],  0<x<a.                    (22)

For x>=a, monotonicity and values x' up to a give the constant lower
profile b_delta[a^2+(a-u)_+^2]. Fatou over the positive logarithmic
thresholds therefore yields

  liminf s^2 H_(s,n_s)(f_(theta_s))
   >= int_0^infinity exp(-x)
        b_delta{min(x,a)^2+[min(x,a)-u]_+^2} dx.              (23)

First let delta->0. Increase a to the unique solution of
p_(u,d)(a)=t, and then let d->pi/2. The limiting capped profile is
min(t,p_u(x)), proving D. All the matrices at each s are finite;
their dimensions before compression are allowed to depend on s.
No limit exchange involving a supremal count is used.

The explicit formulas (18) follow by integration by parts:
F(t,u)=int_0^a exp(-x)p_u'(x)dx. For a>u this is
2b[int_0^a x exp(-x)dx
       +exp(-u)int_0^(a-u) y exp(-y)dy].
For a<=u omit the second integral.

The same theorem implies the genuinely UNIFORM version on every
fixed compact range 0<t_0<=n s^2<=t_1<infinity:

  sup_(|theta|<=1, t_0<=n s^2<=t_1)
   |s^2 H_(s,n)(f_theta)-F(n s^2,-s log|theta|)| ->0.          (D-uniform)

Indeed a violating sequence has a subsequence with n s^2 converging
in [t_0,t_1] and u=-s log|theta| converging in the compact extended
interval [0,infinity]. The just-proved sequential theorem contradicts
it. F is continuous also at u=infinity: for t in this compact range
the one-branch expression already holds identically whenever
u>=sqrt(t_1/b). Arbitrary coefficient pairs reduce by the same scalar
normalization as in U, multiplying this formula by the maximum
coefficient magnitude to the s power.

If n_s s^2->infinity, the universal upper U and embeddings of examples
of size floor(T/s^2), followed by T->infinity, give
s^2 H_(s,n_s)(f_(theta_s))->2b(1+exp(-u)).
If n_s s^2->0, then s^2 H_(s,n_s)->0 by the elementary n_s M^s
upper. No additional relative small-dimension statement is required
for D.

9. JOINT DIMENSION / ACCURACY COUNT COROLLARY
--------------------------------------------
Suppose |theta_L|<=1, -log|theta_L|/L->tau in [0,infinity],
n_L/L^2->t in (0,infinity), and r is a fixed positive integer.
The count restricted to dimension n_L obeys

  L^(-2) N_(f_(theta_L),r,n_L)(L)
       -> min(t,r b[1+(1-tau)_+^2]).                          (24)

Upper: combine (12) with the dimension cap.
Lower for r=1: use P at its original scale L, normalize its input,
and compress at depth aL, with 0<a<1 and
b_d[a^2+(a-tau)_+^2]<t. For any 0<x<a, original singular values
above exp(-xL) remain above exp(-L) after the O(exp(-aL)) error.
Thus the surviving count is at least
(b_delta[x^2+(x-tau)_+^2]+o(1))L^2. Increase x,a to the smaller
of 1 and the inverse of p_(tau,d) at t, then let delta->0 and
d->pi/2. This gives min(t,p_tau(1)). For fixed r use r direct copies
with each block dimension at most floor(n_L/r); their operator input
norm is one and their increment rank is r. This gives (24).

These joint laws remain part of the same proposed quantitative
perturbation theorem, rather than independent claims of a new
localization method or a new general matrix multiplication algorithm.
