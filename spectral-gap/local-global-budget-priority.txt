historical-novelty certificate and not a replacement for the separate
proof audit. The operator determinant, cutset resistance, Kron update,

states the proposed sharp strict-count law

 lim_(epsilon->0) epsilon W_bridge,d,alpha(epsilon)
                   = alpha log[2d/(d+2)],       d>=3,

The defensible possible contribution is this precise graph extremal
law and its sharp realization, if the separate proof audit accepts all
steps. In particular alpha log(6/5) is the proposed sharp cubic BRIDGE
DOUBLING coefficient, not an assertion for arbitrary nonbridge edits
or for a one-edge insertion.

1. Symmetrized perturbation determinants: close analytic predecessor
-------------------------------------------------------------------
F. Gesztesy and M. Zinchenko, Symmetrized perturbation determinants and
applications to boundary data maps and Krein-type resolvent formulas,
Proc. London Math. Soc.104 (2012), 577--612; arXiv:1007.4605.
Primary source:
 https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.4605
 https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/pdr024

Inspected the introduction, positive/nonnegative-type definitions,
Hypothesis 2.4, Theorem 2.8 (equation 2.57), its proof's determinant
and trace-differentiation mechanism, and Remark 2.9. Not a full-paper
read of the one-dimensional boundary-map applications.

Theorem 2.8 establishes the logarithmic derivative of a symmetrized
Fredholm perturbation determinant as a trace of resolvent differences,
including suitable form perturbations. Its assumptions require a
positive-type shifted pair, domain compatibility, and a trace-class
symmetrized resolvent difference. This is a much broader established
framework than ordinary finite matrix determinants.

The local note's fractional determinant identity should be credited as
a rank-one consequence/extension of this classical determinant and
trace machinery, rather than presented as an independent new operator
theory theorem. For a positive lower bound delta, apply the usual
relative determinant trace formula to (A+delta)^alpha and
(A+delta+bb*)^alpha. The logarithms of those powers are alpha times
the logarithms of the endpoints. The resulting log determinant is
alpha log(1+<b,(A+delta)^-1 b>). The note's useful extra verification
at gap zero is trace-norm convergence of its positive resolvent Gram
integral, dominated by an integral of trace alpha tau. This makes the
regularization limit legitimate. The cited theorem does not itself
print this exact gap-zero rank-one fractional formula, so a short proof
remains appropriate, with classical credit.

2. Spectral-shift invariance and finite-volume weak convergence
-------------------------------------------------------------
A. Pushnitski, The spectral shift function and the invariance principle,
J. Funct. Anal.183 (2001), 269--320.
Primary author copy:
 https://nms.kcl.ac.uk/alexander.pushnitski/papers/paper12.pdf
 https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9911182

Read introduction items 1--7, especially formulas (1.2), (1.3),
(1.8)--(1.9), and the statement of the extended invariance framework.
The full 50-page proof was not audited. The trace formula and invariance
principle make transformation of ordinary perturbation trace data by
functional calculus classical. They reinforce the credit in item 1;
they do not by themselves supply the targeted generalized eigenvalues.

F. Gesztesy and R. Nichols, An abstract approach to weak convergence of
spectral shift functions and applications to multi-dimensional
Schrodinger operators, arXiv:1111.0096.
Primary sources:
 https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.0096
 https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/33473

Inspected the abstract, varying-Hilbert-space setup, relevant resolvent
convergence discussion, Hypothesis 3.9, Theorem 3.13, Corollaries
3.11 and 3.14--3.15, and the proof of the weak-convergence implication.
No full audit of the multidimensional Schrodinger applications.

Theorem 3.13 gives weak convergence of spectral-shift measures weighted
by (1+lambda^2)^-1 against bounded continuous tests. Thus convergence
of local perturbation data through Fredholm determinants is established
prior theory. It is a close conceptual comparison, not just a keyword
match. Two obstacles prevent treating it as the complete target theorem:

* The relevant logarithmic weight at the zero spectral endpoint is
  proportional to 1/lambda, which is not a bounded test. Weak convergence
  does not automatically preserve that mass. Its possible loss is the
  very deficit being studied.
* A spectral-shift function of (A+bb*,A) concerns differences of ordinary
  endpoint eigenvalue counts. The target counts eigenvalues of the
  relative fractional congruence C_alpha. Converting the former into
  the latter, with an epsilon-dependent count and a sharp construction,
  is not a conclusion of the inspected weak-convergence theorem.

The finite-support generalized Ritz argument in the local notes is a
direct way to bridge that second obstacle. Polynomial locality,
continuous functional calculus and min--max are standard. Once fixed-j
lower semicontinuity of the local correction eigenvalues is established,
the budget subtraction is an elementary positive-tail/Fatou argument.
This is not a new abstract compactness principle.

3. Tree entropy: determinants, local limits and resistance already meet
---------------------------------------------------------------------
R. Lyons, Identities and inequalities for tree entropy, Combin. Probab.
Comput.19 (2010), 303--313.
Primary author copy:
 https://rdlyons.pages.iu.edu/pdf/trent-published.pdf
 https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.3035

Read the abstract and setup, Theorem 2.1, the discussion of its
Fuglede--Kadison representation, and Theorem 3.3 with its proof and
adjacent remarks. Not a full audit of the forest and domination results.
The paper links normalized spanning-tree determinants under random weak
graph limits to effective-resistance integrals. This is genuine prior
work combining the three themes of the target. It should be acknowledged.

It does not immediately imply the proposed law: its principal quantity
is a determinant normalized per vertex, its limiting objects are
unimodular random rooted networks, and it is a one-operator entropy.
The target is an unnormalized trace-class determinant for a fixed
distinguished edge and a noncommuting fractional relative pair. There
is no uniform-root/unimodularity hypothesis in the edge-rooted target.
Lyons explicitly warns that a pointwise version of one resistance
representation fails without unimodularity. Do not identify the two
formulas merely because both involve logarithms and resistance.

4. The wired resistance constant is classical network theory
------------------------------------------------------------
R. Lyons and Y. Peres, Probability on Trees and Networks, corrected and
updated author edition dated 19 August 2026.
Primary author PDF:
 https://rdlyons.pages.iu.edu/prbtree/book.pdf

Browser opening exceeded its size limit; downloaded the full PDF to
/tmp and read selected extracted sections. Actual coverage: Chapter 2,
Nash--Williams inequality/criterion and proof, pp.36--37; Chapter 9,
wired/free resistance definitions, p.314; Exercises 9.25--9.29, p.339,
and the author's solution of Exercise 9.25. The rest of the book was
not read in this audit.

Nash--Williams applied to distance cutsets in a bridge half with root
degree <=d-1 gives resistance to infinity at least 1/(d-2). The
series/parallel formula then gives wired bridge resistance >=2/d.
The book's regular-tree exercise and its solution already give the
exact adjacent-vertex wired resistance 2/d. Thus neither the constant
2/d nor equality on the regular tree is a new electrical result.
What would be additional here is its conversion into a sharp fractional
relative COUNT coefficient alpha log[2/(1+2/d)].

5. Kron response updates and the lower construction
--------------------------------------------------
F. Dorfler and F. Bullo, Kron reduction of graphs with applications to
electrical networks, IEEE Trans. Circuits Syst. I60 (2013), 150--163.
Primary author copy:
 https://motion.me.ucsb.edu/pdf/2011d-db.pdf
 https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.2950

Inspected the introduction, Theorem III.8 on resistance invariance,
and Section III.D, especially Theorem III.14 and its rank-one reduced
network formula (38). No full-paper proof audit.

Eliminating interior graph vertices by Schur complement, preserving
terminal resistance, and obtaining a rank-one terminal update from a
single interior edge change are explicitly established results. The
lower note's star inversion and exact impedance change by a multiple
of 11^T are special circuit instances of this established algebra.

The specific attainability obligation is more than a static response
matrix identity: one must approximate the desired star behind optimal
access resistances using simple unit-weight bounded-degree graphs,
allocate all residual logarithmic budget into m nearly equal reverse
pivot factors, and transfer those factors to the energy-relative
fractional spectrum with ordinary unit vertex masses. The newly read
sharpness note addresses this with repeated colored tree blocks and
many expander-reservoir ports, uniformly controlling contact matrices.
No inspected Kron theorem directly asserts those combined properties.
Its separated-mass asymptotic input should retain the classical graded
matrix / Schur-pivot credits documented in the earlier priority audit.

Scope conclusions for the current proof notes
--------------------------------------------
The strongest upper statement in the notes is one-sided:
 limsup epsilon_n N_(C_n)(epsilon_n)
       <= alpha log 2-alpha log(1+tau_wired).
It forces at least the whole local determinant budget. It does not by
itself prove a direct-sum spectral decomposition, equality of escaped
mass, or a flat distribution of all remaining eigenvalues. The new
multiport lower construction is what could establish the sharp
supremal coefficient. Keep those logically separate.

The regular-tree resistance, perturbation determinant identity,
spectral-shift invariance, resolvent convergence, generalized Ritz
principle and positive-budget subtraction are classical ingredients.
The inspected literature does not hand over the complete sharp
finite unweighted bounded-degree bridge response law as a single
existing theorem or a directly stated corollary. This limited finding
supports continuing the proof audit; it does not establish historical
priority or justify calling the result seminal.
