1. Setup and exact evaluation-functional identity
------------------------------------------------
Let a selected family contain q blocks of b nonnegative eigenvalues
Lambda_i and independent square Gaussian starts Omega_i. Cross-block
spectra are disjoint. Use left evaluation and right polynomial
coefficients throughout. A0=diag(Lambda_i), Gamma=col(Omega_i), and
the full raw q-block Krylov matrix is generically invertible, as proved
in the earlier all-depth audit.

Let R_q be its terminal orthogonal block-Lanczos polynomial and let
V_last be the last orthonormal block. The already-checked identity is

  R_q(z)^{-1}=V_last^T(zI-A0)^{-1}Gamma=:W_q(z)             (1)

for every z outside spec(A0). Define the b-by-qb row operator

  L_z=R_q(z)V_last^T(zI-A0)^{-1}.

For 0<=t<q, resolvent division and V_last's orthogonality to all
A0^j Gamma with j<q-1 give

  L_z A0^t Gamma=z^t I.                                   (2)

Indeed (zI-A0)^{-1}A0^t equals z^t(zI-A0)^{-1} minus lower powers;
every lower-power term vanishes after V_last^T. The surviving term
is R_q(z)W_q(z)=I. Thus L_z is EXACT scalar evaluation of every
right-coefficient polynomial of degree<q represented by this raw basis.

Let G_i be the q-node fundamental polynomials. Choosing the data
supported only at block i in (2) yields

  (L_z)_i=G_i(z)Omega_i^{-1}.                              (3)

Consequently, for eta outside the selected spectra,

 R_q(z)R_q(eta)^{-1}
   =L_z(zI-A0)(eta I-A0)^{-1}Gamma
   =sum_i G_i(z)(zI-B_i)(eta I-B_i)^{-1}.                 (4)

This derivation preserves all noncommuting orders. It is preferable to
the alternative residue-cancellation argument because no pole bookkeeping
or Sylvester uniqueness argument is required. The latter argument is
also valid: disjoint block spectra prevent cancellation of a pole between
different blocks, and the sum of the resulting polynomials is I.

2. Relative scalar ratio removes the spectral condition number
--------------------------------------------------------------
Let the full target head be nonnegative, and initially assume its minimum
h is positive. Consider scalar tail points 0<=z<h. Let the relative
cross-gap for the fixed partition be

  g=min_(i!=j,lambda in Lambda_i,eta in Lambda_j)
                |lambda-eta|/max(lambda,eta)>0.

For every fresh root value eta and selected other value lambda,

  |z-lambda|/|eta-lambda|
      <=lambda/|eta-lambda|<=1/g.                         (5)

Diagonal similarity therefore gives

 ||(zI-B_i)(eta I-B_i)^{-1}||<=kappa(Omega_i)/g,
 ||R_q(z)R_q(eta)^{-1}||
      <=q*G_other(z)*max_i kappa(Omega_i)/g.               (6)

The relevant gap is relative to the pair's larger eigenvalue, not to
the largest eigenvalue of the whole head. Thus neither lambda_1/h nor
an additive full-diameter gap is introduced.

The theorem is PSD/tail-sided. An arbitrary affine shift of an indefinite
matrix changes these relative gaps, so condition removal must not be
asserted for generic indefinite or interior targets from (5) alone.

3. Fresh independent rows and the path probability bound
--------------------------------------------------------
For a root k omitted from the selected q-block family, put

  Ntilde=Omega_k R_q(B_k)R_q(z)^{-1}.

R_q(z) is invertible because z is strictly below every selected node.
Conditional on all other blocks, row l is

  omega_(k,l)^T R_q(eta_(k,l))R_q(z)^{-1}.

Its covariance is at least a^2 I with

  a=g/[q*G_other(z)*max_i kappa(Omega_i)],                 (7)

by inverting (6). The rows remain independent. F_k(z)=Ntilde^{-1}
Omega_k exactly. No root Gaussian is hidden in R_q or the conditional
covariance floor. Since sum_i G_i(z)=I, G_other(z)>=1/q, so the
normalization in (7) is positive on the nonsingular event.

Define Z=a*||Ntilde^{-1}||. The same row-distance lemma gives

  Pr{Z>x|other blocks}<=min(1,b^{3/2}/x),
  E[sqrt(Z)|other blocks]<=2b^{3/4}.                       (8)

For D total blocks and confidence beta, use the single common norm event

  ||Omega_i||<=L0=2b*sqrt(D/beta),
  ||Omega_i^{-1}||<=U0=4D*b^{3/2}/beta,

with failure at most beta/2. Do NOT condition the moment calculation on
this event. On it, the deterministic pointwise recursion is

 G(S,z)<=max_(k in S)
       [(|S|-1)*U0*L0^2/g] Z_(S,k,z) G(S\k,z).            (9)

There is no sqrt(q) stack-norm factor and no factor three in (9).
For a fixed nested deletion path the half moment of the product of Z's
is at most (2b^{3/4})^{D-1}, by tower conditioning on remaining blocks.
For a deterministic set of N scalar points, union the N*D! path events
ONLY after the final Markov inequality. Their simultaneous product bound
is [2N D!(2b^{3/4})^{D-1}/beta]^2 except beta/2. Combining it with
the norm event proves

 max_(z in grid) G_D(z)
  <=4N^2 beta^{-2}(D!)^2(D-1)!
                  [64D^2 b^5 beta^{-2}/g]^{D-1}.           (10)

Constants check: U0 L0^2=16D^2 b^{7/2}beta^{-2}; multiplying the
squared half-moment factor4b^{3/2} gives64D^2 b^5 beta^{-2}.
No independence between paths, points, or the norm event is required.

4. Uniform tail interval without a D log(n) penalty
--------------------------------------------------
For h>0 and D>=2, put t=D-1 and N=4t^2+1. Use the grid

  z_j=j*h/N, j=0,...,N-1.

The upper endpoint is deliberately omitted, so every point is strictly
below all head nodes and R_q(z_j) is invertible for every subproblem.
Every x in [0,h] is nevertheless within h/N of a grid point.

For a matrix polynomial F of degree<=t, scalar Markov inequality on
[0,h], applied to fixed unit bilinear forms u^T F(x)v, gives

  sup_x ||F'(x)||<=2t^2/h * sup_x||F(x)||.

The mesh therefore yields sup_[0,h]||F||<=2 max_j||F(z_j)||.
This is deterministic; it needs no union over uncountably many x or
over vectors u,v. Since N<=5D^2, (10) gives the safe uniform bound

 Pr{max_i sup_(0<=z<=h)||F_i(z)||
       <=[20000D^12 b^5/(beta^4*g)]^{D-1}}>=1-beta.         (11)

Its log-prefactor is O(D log(Db/beta)). In particular a factor D log(n)
from a naive union over all tail eigenvalues is unnecessary.

If the head minimum is zero, the interval collapses to {0}. A separate
fixed-input limit can handle it: at z=-epsilon the scalar ratio in (5)
is at most1/g+epsilon/Delta_abs, tending1/g. The same proof with this
vanishing perturbation, continuity of the fundamental polynomials and
Fatou gives the limiting pointwise probability bound. One cannot instead
claim to approach zero from nonnegative points strictly below it.
For a first clean theorem, retaining positive head minimum is sufficient.

5. Graph angle, good starts, and exact algorithmic scope
------------------------------------------------------
For a top target head of dimension Db and Gaussian start, the usual graph
argument gives

 tan angle(head,K_D(H,Omega))
   <=sqrt(D)||Omega_tail||_F max_i||Omega_i^{-1}||
                    max_i sup_(0<=z<=h)||F_i(z)||.          (12)

Allocate separate confidence to the tail Frobenius bound; its contribution
is polynomial in n,b,1/beta OUTSIDE the exponent D-1. Consequently
the log good-start parameter is

  O(D log(Db/(beta*g))+log(n/beta)).                       (13)

There is no polynomial or logarithmic dependence on lambda_1/h.

For padding to a non-multiple head size k, the original raw initial
space may have MORE than k columns. This is covered by the published
good-start definition, which asks for existence of a suitable orthonormal
k-column subspace inside that range. The zero-dummy graph below gives
such a subspace by orthonormalizing [I;X]; its parameter is1+||X||^2.

Take B=[Omega,H Omega,...,H^{D-1}Omega], including all its columns.
Regardless of its rank or width, K_s(H,B)=K_(D+s-1)(H,Omega) exactly.
Chen et al.'s Definition3.1/Imported Theorem3.2 explicitly allow this
wide simulated start. Thus the new graph estimate transfers to the
standard spectral/Frobenius LRA theorem without assuming a monotonicity
property of arbitrary Rayleigh--Ritz extraction on nested spaces.

Up to universal constants, the resulting depth is

 D+theta^{-1/2}[D log(Db/(beta*g))+log(n/(beta*theta))].    (14)

The corresponding gap-dependent statement uses a suitable larger head
and replaces theta^{-1/2} by the inverse square root of the applicable
external relative gap. Standard full reorthogonalization, storage and
extraction costs remain charged. This is a convergence improvement for
the same algorithm; it does not improve a raw factor-action's cost.

6. Relative forward b-gap: contiguous components and dummy padding
----------------------------------------------------------------
For descending positive target nodes lambda_1>=...>=lambda_k and k>b,
define precisely the forward relative gap

  gamma=min_(1<=i<=k-b)(lambda_i-lambda_(i+b))/lambda_i>0.

Cut consecutive nodes whenever their adjacent relative gap is at least
gamma/b. Every remaining component has at most b nodes. Otherwise b
consecutive uncut edges give

 lambda_(i+b)>(1-gamma/b)^b lambda_i
                >=(1-gamma)lambda_i,

contradicting the definition of gamma. This proof uses the forward gap,
not an arbitrary nearest-neighbor gap with varying denominators.

Greedily pack whole consecutive components into blocks of size<=b.
Consecutive completed block sizes sum to more than b, so the number of
blocks satisfies D<=2ceil(k/b)-1. Every cross-block pair has symmetric
relative gap at least gamma/b: the closest pair across the contiguous
boundary already has that property, and separating them further only
decreases their smaller/larger eigenvalue ratio.

Pad each block to b by duplicating one of its own eigenvalues and adding
independent fictitious Gaussian rows. The relative cross-gap remains
at least gamma/b; no spectrum rescaling or condition number is incurred.
For a requested ORIGINAL head vector y, prescribe dummy head values0.
Interpolation in the augmented system then generates a vector [y;Xy]
on the original head/tail coordinates and zero dummy coordinates.
Projection to the original coordinates belongs to K_D(H,Omega), because
the same raw polynomial coefficients act there. Its graph error is no
larger than in the augmented system.

The final original-space graph/good-start property is independent of
the fictitious Gaussian draws. If it holds with probability1-beta for
the joint draw, it holds with at least that probability marginally for
the original start. Thus padding is an analysis device, not an operation
the algorithm must implement. D<=2ceil(k/b)-1 preserves k-linear scalar
matvec scaling up to logarithms. For k<=b use the ordinary large-block
Gaussian result instead of defining an empty forward-gap minimum.

Substituting g>=gamma/b into (14) proves a condition-free forward-b-gap
bound with an additional D log(Db^2/beta) overhead. It does NOT prove
the exact displayed Chen rate with no such additional logarithms.

7. Explicit counterexample to kappa-free monomial conditioning
-------------------------------------------------------------
Let b=1 and C=diag(1,1/2,...,2^{-(k-1)}). Its minimum consecutive
relative gap is1/2. For a standard Gaussian g, form the raw monomial
matrix K=[g,Cg,...,C^{k-1}g]. The monic polynomial

  p(x)=product_(j=0)^(k-2)(x-2^{-j})

has coefficient vector c with ||c||>=1. All but its final node
evaluations vanish, so

  sigma_min(K)<=||Kc||/||c||
      <=|g_k|*2^{-(k-1)(k-2)/2}.                          (15)

With probability at least0.99, |g_k|<=10, by Markov on g_k^2.
Thus sigma_min is exp(-Omega(k^2)) with high probability. A literal
replacement of Delta_k/kappa_k by Delta_k=1/2 in Chen Theorem4.1
would give a fixed-confidence lower bound polynomial(k)^{-1} times
(1/12)^{6(k-1)}=exp(-O(k)), which (15) contradicts for all large k.
All nodes are distinct and positive and ||C||=1, so the example obeys
that theorem's input hypotheses.

This disproves the PROPOSED strengthened monomial singular-value bound,
not the published theorem that retains kappa. It does not refute the
algorithmic convergence conjectures: orthogonalized subspace geometry
can be well behaved even when this monomial coordinate matrix is very
ill conditioned. The relative-tail interpolation proof measures the
former directly.
