VERDICT
-------

PASS for the relative interpolation theorem below, including the
conditional covariance argument, elimination-path moment bound,
uniformization, clustering/whole-component packing, auxiliary padding,
projection back to the actual Krylov space, and the good-start bridge.
No fatal algebraic or probability flaw was found after checking these
steps independently. No hardware benchmark or numerical experiment is
used as mathematical evidence.

The main caveats are substantive to the statement:

* The relative bound is for scalar evaluation at or below the smallest
  nonnegative target node, not for arbitrary points in the spectral
  interval. It is well suited to PSD top-eigenspace/low-rank analysis.
* A zero minimum target node needs a separate limiting argument. Merely
  approaching it from below while asserting z>=0 is invalid. A factor-2
  relative comparison on negative points repairs this without introducing
  a spectral condition number.
* A naive separate confidence allocation beta/N to N pointwise theorems
  is unnecessary. Union over points only at the final path Markov step;
  then N appears as N^2 outside the depth power. The uniform polynomial
  grid removes even that dependence on the number of tail eigenvalues.
* The resulting low-rank rate has extra D log(Db/beta) terms compared
  with the literal quantitative formula conjectured by Chen et al.
  It establishes relative-gap/no-condition-number convergence up to
  logarithmic overhead, not their complete displayed conjecture verbatim.
* The argument proves a good Krylov starting SPACE, not a condition-number
  free lower bound for the minimum singular value of the raw monomial
  Krylov matrix. The latter is a different claim.

1. PRECISE RELATIVE INTERPOLATION STATEMENT CHECKED
--------------------------------------------------

Let D>=2 and b>=1. Fix real diagonal b-by-b matrices Lambda_1,...,
Lambda_D with nonnegative entries and pairwise disjoint spectra.
Let Omega_i be independent standard Gaussian b-by-b matrices and
B_i=Omega_i^-1 Lambda_i Omega_i. Let F_i be the ACTUAL right-coefficient
fundamental polynomials of degree <=D-1 with LEFT evaluation

  F_i(B_j)=delta_ij I_b.

Put

  m=min(all entries of all Lambda_i),
  g=min_(i!=j,lambda in spec Lambda_i,eta in spec Lambda_j)
        |lambda-eta|/max(lambda,eta).

Cross-block disjointness and nonnegativity make every denominator in
this minimum positive and imply 0<g<=1. In particular, there cannot
be zero in two different blocks. For 0<beta<1 the bound checked is

  Pr{ max_i sup_(0<=z<=m) ||F_i(z)||_2
          <=[20000 D^12 b^5/(beta^4 g)]^(D-1) } >=1-beta.       (R1)

This probability event can be chosen jointly with

  ||Omega_i||<=2 b sqrt(D/beta),
  ||Omega_i^-1||<=4 D b^(3/2)/beta   (all i).                 (R2)

For D=1 take F_1=I_b and omit g. The singleton statement holds
deterministically. Statements involving g should not be written with
an undefined empty minimum in that case.

If spectra are normalized, positive multiplicative scaling preserves g
and the interval [0,m]. An arbitrary affine shift does not preserve this
relative parameter. No normalization is needed for the identities below;
the norm-of-recurrence-coefficients bound from the absolute-gap proof is
not used in the relative argument.

2. RESOLVENT RESIDUES REALLY ARE FUNDAMENTAL POLYNOMIALS
------------------------------------------------------

Fix q>=1 blocks S. Write A=diag(Lambda_i:i in S), Gamma=stack(Omega_i),
and let V_last be the last b-column orthonormal Krylov block. The
terminal degree-q right-coefficient polynomial R satisfies

  R(B_i)=0  (i in S),
  W(z)R(z)=I_b,
  W(z)=V_last^T(zI-A)^-1 Gamma.

These identities hold almost surely on the common generic full-rank
event for every subset. They hold without any bound on ||A||.
For block row V_i of V_last, define

  A_i=V_i^T Omega_i.

The ordering in the partial fractions is

  W(z)=sum_(i in S) A_i (zI-B_i)^-1.

Indeed (zI-Lambda_i)^-1 Omega_i=Omega_i(zI-B_i)^-1.
Since W and R are square, W R=I implies R W=I wherever these expressions
are initially defined, and then as a rational identity. Define

  G_i(z)=R(z) A_i (zI-B_i)^-1.

Their sum is identically I_b. The possible poles of different summands
are disjoint. Each B_i is diagonalizable, including when Lambda_i has
repeated eigenvalues, so its resolvent has only simple poles at its
distinct eigenvalues. At a pole belonging to block i no other G_j has
a pole; the identity sum G_j=I therefore forces its residue to vanish.
All poles of G_i are removable. A rational function without finite poles
is polynomial, and its O(|z|^(q-1)) growth at infinity gives degree <=q-1.
The same argument may be applied entry by entry.

There is no unjustified evaluation of a matrix-polynomial product here.
Instead use the polynomial identity

  R(z) A_i=G_i(z)(zI-B_i).

Its LEFT evaluation at B_j is

  0=B_j G_i(B_j)-G_i(B_j) B_i.

If i!=j, disjoint spectra make the Sylvester map X -> B_j X-X B_i
invertible; for example, diagonalizing each B turns it into entrywise
multiplication by nonzero eigenvalue differences. Hence G_i(B_j)=0.
Left evaluation of sum_i G_i(z)=I at B_j then gives G_j(B_j)=I.
By interpolation uniqueness, G_i is exactly F_i^S. This handles q=1
as well, when its sole polynomial is I_b.

3. RELATIVE TRANSFER IDENTITY AND DETERMINISTIC BOUND
----------------------------------------------------

For eta outside the spectra in S, R(eta)^-1=W(eta). Consequently

  R(z)R(eta)^-1
    =sum_(i in S) F_i^S(z)(zI-B_i)(eta I-B_i)^-1.             (R3)

All factors have the stated order. The identity follows from
R(z)A_i=F_i^S(z)(zI-B_i), not from multiplying left matrix evaluations.

Suppose first m>0 and 0<=z<m, and let eta be any eigenvalue in the fresh
root block. Every lambda from S satisfies lambda>=m>z. Therefore

  |z-lambda|/|eta-lambda|
     <=lambda/[g max(lambda,eta)]<=1/g.

Diagonal similarity then gives

  ||(zI-B_i)(eta I-B_i)^-1||<=kappa(Omega_i)/g.

Writing

  G_S(z)=max_(i in S)||F_i^S(z)||,
  kappa_S=max_(i in S)kappa(Omega_i),

equation (R3) yields

  ||R(z)R(eta)^-1|| <= q G_S(z) kappa_S/g.                 (R4)

This is the required comparison. No lambda_max/lambda_min ratio occurs.
Also G_S(z)>=1/q, since sum_i F_i^S(z)=I. Thus its use as a random
denominator in the next conditional estimate is legitimate.

4. NORMALIZED FRESH GAUSSIAN ROWS
--------------------------------

Let T have at least two blocks, k in T, S=T\{k}, and q=|S|.
Construct R using ONLY the Gaussian blocks in S, with a fixed measurable
orthogonalization rule. For a deterministic z outside all target nodes,
define

  Ntilde_(T,k)(z)=Omega_k R(B_k)R(z)^-1.

Conditional on the blocks of S its row l is

  omega_(k,l)^T R(eta_l)R(z)^-1,

where eta_l is the corresponding diagonal entry of Lambda_k. These rows
are independent centered Gaussian rows. The inverse of the multiplying
matrix is R(z)R(eta_l)^-1, so (R4) proves the covariance lower bound

  Cov(row l)>=h_(T,k)(z)^2 I_b,
  h_(T,k)(z)=g/[q G_S(z) kappa_S].

The quantity h is fixed by the conditioning and positive almost surely.
There is no dependence on Omega_k hidden in R, G_S, or kappa_S.

Moreover, constant-right-factor evaluation gives

  F_k^T(z)=Ntilde_(T,k)(z)^-1 Omega_k.

Define Z_(T,k)(z)=h_(T,k)(z)||Ntilde_(T,k)(z)^-1||. The independent-row
lemma gives, before conditioning on any success event,

  Pr{Z>u | Omega_S}<=min(1,b^(3/2)/u),
  E[Z^(1/2) | Omega_S]<=2 b^(3/4).                         (R5)

This is exactly the same valid conditioning mechanism as in the audited
absolute-gap proof. Substituting norms into the last expression for F
gives the pointwise deterministic recursion, on (R2),

  G_T(z)<=[q U_0 L_0^2/g] Z_(T,k)(z) G_S(z)

for each k, hence with a maximum over k on the right. The factor is q,
not q^(3/2), because no stacked-Gaussian resolvent norm is needed here.
The singleton base value is one.

5. JOINT POINTWISE AND UNIFORM PROBABILITY BOUNDS
------------------------------------------------

For all D blocks put L_0=2b sqrt(D/beta), U_0=4D b^(3/2)/beta.
The union of the D norm and D inverse-norm failures costs beta/2, as
in the absolute proof. On this one shared event,

  U_0 L_0^2=16 D^2 b^(7/2) beta^-2.

For any fixed evaluation point z and elimination path pi, later Z
factors are measurable in the blocks remaining after the current root
is removed. Iterating conditional expectation in (R5) therefore gives

  E[(product_path Z(z))^(1/2)]<=(2b^(3/4))^(D-1).

For N fixed evaluation points, union over all pairs (point,path) only
at the final Markov step. With additional failure at most beta/2,

  max_(z,pi) product_path Z(z)
       <=[2 N D! (2b^(3/4))^(D-1)/beta]^2.

Expanding the recursion and combining with the shared Gaussian event
gives the exact intermediate bound

  max_grid G_D(z)
    <=4 N^2 beta^-2 (D!)^2 (D-1)!
        [64 D^2 b^5 beta^-2/g]^(D-1).                     (R6)

In particular, N^2 is OUTSIDE the depth power. There is no need to pay
beta/N in the Gaussian norm event or at each level of elimination.

For m>0 let r=D-1 and take N=4r^2+1 deterministic grid nodes

  z_j=j m/N,  j=0,...,N-1.

The upper endpoint m is deliberately omitted. Thus every grid point is
strictly below every target eigenvalue and all R(z_j) are invertible.
Every x in [0,m] is within m/N of a grid node, including x=m.

For any real b-by-b polynomial F of degree <=r, scalar Markov's
inequality applied to u^T F(z)v for every fixed pair of real unit vectors
gives

  sup_[0,m]||F'(z)|| <=[2r^2/m] sup_[0,m]||F(z)||.

Using a nearest grid point and the fundamental theorem of calculus,

  sup||F|| <= max_grid||F|| + [2r^2/N] sup||F||,

so sup||F||<=2 max_grid||F||. This also holds simultaneously for the
finitely many fundamental matrix polynomials, without another probability
allocation or any net over vectors u,v.

Since N<=5D^2, (R6) implies

  max_i sup_[0,m]||F_i||
    <=8 N^2 beta^-2 (D!)^2 (D-1)!
        [64D^2 b^5 beta^-2/g]^(D-1)
    <=[12800 D^11 b^5/(beta^4 g)]^(D-1)
    <=[20000 D^12 b^5/(beta^4 g)]^(D-1).

The simplification uses (D!)^2<=D^[4(D-1)], (D-1)!<=D^(D-1), and
8N^2 beta^-2<=200D^4 beta^-2<=(200D^4 beta^-2)^(D-1).
All hold for D>=2. This proves (R1) jointly with (R2) when m>0.

6. ZERO MINIMUM TARGET NODE
--------------------------

When m=0 the asserted interval is just {0}; a positive-width grid is
unavailable. Approaching 0 from negative points does not preserve the
previous assumption z>=0, so the comparison must be checked again.

For D>=2 there is at least one positive target node; otherwise cross-
block disjointness would fail. Let nu be the smallest positive target
node and take z=-epsilon with 0<epsilon<=nu. If lambda>0, then

  |z-lambda|=lambda+epsilon<=2lambda,
  |z-lambda|/|eta-lambda|<=2/g.

If lambda=0 then the fresh eta>0, since zero cannot occur in two blocks,
and epsilon/eta<=1<=2/g. Thus (R4)--(R6) hold at every such negative
point with g replaced by g/2, uniformly in epsilon. Taking N=1 gives

  G_D(-epsilon)
    <=4 beta^-2(D!)^2(D-1)!
       [128D^2 b^5 beta^-2/g]^(D-1)
    <=[512D^7 b^5/(beta^4 g)]^(D-1)

with probability at least 1-beta jointly with (R2).

Fix any deterministic sequence epsilon_j down to zero with epsilon_j<=nu.
The actual F_i are continuous scalar-variable polynomials almost surely.
The limsup of the joint good events is contained in

  (R2) intersect {G_D(0)<=[512D^7 b^5/(beta^4 g)]^(D-1)}.

Its probability is at least the limsup of the individual probabilities,
hence at least 1-beta. This does not take an intersection of infinitely
many probability-(1-beta) events. Since 512D^7<=20000D^12, the same
advertised constant in (R1) covers m=0. D=1 remains the separate base.

7. FORWARD RELATIVE b-GAP TO WHOLE-CLUSTER BLOCKS
-----------------------------------------------

Let lambda_1>=...>=lambda_k>=0 be fixed target values, b<k, and assume
the following ratios are defined and have positive minimum:

  gamma=min_(1<=i<=k-b) (lambda_i-lambda_(i+b))/lambda_i>0.

Thus 0<gamma<=1. Put tau=gamma/b. Make maximal contiguous components
by connecting i to i+1 exactly when

  (lambda_i-lambda_(i+1))/lambda_i < tau,

using the obvious zero-zero connection for repeated zero values. All
repeated values must remain in one component. Every component has at
most b entries: if it had b+1, for its first index i the b strict
adjacent inequalities would give

  lambda_(i+b)>lambda_i(1-tau)^b>=lambda_i(1-gamma),

contradicting the definition of gamma. A component of b+1 zeros would
likewise contradict positivity/well-definedness of the forward gap.

Different components are separated by a boundary relative gap >=tau.
For arbitrary values lambda>=eta on opposite sides of that boundary,
1-eta/lambda is at least the relative gap at the boundary. Thus every
cross-component pair is separated by at least tau in the symmetric
relative parameter |lambda-eta|/max(lambda,eta).

Pack components whole, in order, into bins of capacity b by the next-fit
rule. If there are D bins, each adjacent pair of bins has total size
strictly greater than b: the first component of the later bin did not
fit in the former. Pairing bins shows

  D<=2 ceil(k/b)-1.

There is no bin-splitting of components, so spectra of distinct bins are
disjoint and their relative gap is at least gamma/b. Any deficient bin
can be padded to size b by copies of an eigenvalue already in that bin.
This preserves cross-bin disjointness and the relative gap. The padding
adds at most Db-k synthetic target coordinates. For b>=k, use one padded
bin and the ordinary D=1 base case instead of a forward-gap minimum.

This padding step is what overcomes the equal-size-partition obstruction
in the original theorem. One must not pretend that every cluster of
size <=b can already be partitioned into exactly k/b disjoint full blocks.

8. AUXILIARY PADDING DOES TRANSFER TO THE ORIGINAL KRYLOV SPACE
--------------------------------------------------------------

Let M be the original PSD matrix in a fixed orthonormal eigenbasis and
augment it by a diagonal block of the prescribed target eigenvalue copies,
obtaining Mhat. Augment the original n-by-b Gaussian start Omega with
independent standard Gaussian rows, obtaining Omegahat. Let P delete
the synthetic coordinates. Then

  P Mhat^j Omegahat=M^j Omega  for every j>=0,
  P K_D(Mhat,Omegahat)=K_D(M,Omega).

The augmented head has dimension Db and admits the full blocks above;
the augmented tail consists of the original n-k tail rows, all at values
in [0,m]. The relative interpolation theorem therefore gives an augmented
graph with a controlled norm. To obtain a graph over the ORIGINAL k head
coordinates, set all synthetic head coordinates to zero and then project.
Every vector [y;Xy] of the resulting original graph lies in the actual
original depth-D Krylov space, and its graph norm is no greater than the
augmented bound.

This is an existence argument for the original space. Neither padding
nor the target spectrum needs to be constructed by the actual algorithm.
For probability, the augmented success event implies a property depending
only on the original Omega: that its Krylov space contains such a graph.
Marginalizing the auxiliary Gaussian rows preserves its lower probability
bound. No conditioning on an auxiliary success event is used to alter
the distribution of the original start.

With head confidence beta/2, the joint event supplies

  G<=[320000D^12 b^5/(beta^4 g)]^(D-1),
  max_i||Omega_i^-1||<=8D b^(3/2)/beta.

The original tail has Frobenius norm <=sqrt(2b(n-k)/beta) except with
probability beta/2. Thus the original depth-D Krylov space contains
a graph over its k target coordinates with norm at most

  C_beta=[8D b^(3/2)/beta] sqrt[2D b(n-k)/beta]
            [320000D^12 b^5/(beta^4 g)]^(D-1).             (R7)

Use g>=gamma/b for the forward-gap construction. In particular, the
bracket in (R7) is at most 320000D^12 b^6/(beta^4 gamma).
The tail-empty case is trivial and should be stated separately.

9. GOOD START, CONVERGENCE, AND EXACT CLAIM SCOPE
-----------------------------------------------

If the actual original K_D contains the graph [I_k;X] with ||X||<=C_beta,
then

  Q=[I_k;X](I_k+X^T X)^(-1/2)

has k orthonormal columns in that space and

  ||(U_k^T Q)^-1||^2=||I_k+X^T X||=1+||X||^2<=1+C_beta^2.

Therefore the simulated starting matrix

  B=[Omega,M Omega,...,M^(D-1)Omega]

is a (k,L)-good start with L=1+C_beta^2. This does not require its
columns to be independent or well conditioned; its range is what matters.
The deterministic identity

  K_s(M,B)=K_(s+D-1)(M,Omega)

then transfers any applicable good-start convergence theorem to the
actual standard small-block iteration.

For M=A A^T, Chen et al. Section 3.1 states the relevant good-start
criterion and a low-rank approximation theorem with additional depth
O(log(nL/epsilon)/sqrt(epsilon)). Its Section 3.4 gives an outer-gap
variant. The new calculation yields

  q=O( [D log(Db/(beta gamma)) + log(n/epsilon)]/sqrt(epsilon) ),
  D<=2 ceil(k/b)-1,                                      (R8)

with no spectral condition-number ratio. Source and precise imported
algorithm/output guarantees:
https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06486v2#S3

The constant-probability version has the expected k/b scale up to
logarithms and is insensitive to within-cluster gaps. The same calculation
works for a larger head rank ell when invoking an outer-gap theorem.
The exact dependence retained in (R8) is safer than claiming the literal
quantitative conjecture: D log(Db/beta) is not in general absorbed by
D log(1/gamma)+log(n/(beta epsilon)). For example, the relative gap can
stay constant through an arbitrarily long geometrically decreasing
spectrum. The raw monomial conditioning conjecture is not a consequence
of this argument. Priority and comparison with all prior relative-gap
convergence results still require a literature review.

FINAL ASSESSMENT
----------------

The new relative comparison is mathematically credible and survives this
independent attempt to find a fatal flaw. The explicit theorem (R1),
with joint event (R2), follows by the audited algebra and probability
steps above. Whole-component packing and analysis-only padding then
provide a real extension beyond supplied equal-size target partitions.
The resulting actual block-Krylov convergence claim should be stated
with the logarithmic overhead visible, and kept separate from raw
monomial singular-value, finite-precision, and algorithm-runtime claims.

A. Different but valid proof of the normalized resolvent identity

The actual source, lines 393--412, proves the identity using the exact
evaluation functional rather than the rational-pole argument reviewed
above. That proof is also correct. For 0<=t<q,

  (zI-A)^-1 A^t Gamma
    =z^t(zI-A)^-1 Gamma
       -sum_(r=0)^(t-1)z^(t-1-r) A^r Gamma.

Multiplication by V_last^T kills every lower-degree term because
r<=q-2. With L_z=R(z)V_last^T(zI-A)^-1 and R(z)W(z)=I this gives

  L_z A^t Gamma=z^t I.

Since the raw Krylov matrix is invertible, L_z is its unique polynomial
evaluation functional. Its i-th horizontal b-column block is exactly
F_i^S(z)Omega_i^-1. Multiplying by (zI-A)(eta I-A)^-1 Gamma yields
the actual displayed normalized identity in the correct order. No
affine-relative-gap invariance is needed: the identity can be derived
for the unscaled operator and its norm comparison uses the original
positive eigenvalues. The mention of affine scaling cancels only in
the resolvent ratios, not in the definition of g_rel itself.

B. Actual relative theorem and joint event

shared Gaussian norm event, final union over N d! pairs, and omitted-
endpoint Markov grid reviewed above. The intermediate coefficient 64,

C. Actual packing, projection, and good-start statements

Lines 502--539 correctly keep whole components together, apply next-fit
packing, pad only within a bin, and transfer augmented graphs by setting
dummy head coordinates to zero before projection. Marginalizing the
dummy Gaussians is valid because the conclusion is a property of the
original Krylov space. The augmented dimension is at most a constant
multiple of n: D b<=2b ceil(k/b)-b<2k+b<=3k for b<k<=n, so n_aug<4n.
Thus its appearance inside a logarithm is harmless. More precisely the
tail row count remains n-k, as in (R7).

The actual good-start claim in lines 542--563 needs no raw singular-value
bound and no extra output-specific conditioning. Choose the k orthonormal
graph columns Q constructed in Section 9 of this audit. They lie in the
entire initial orthogonalized Krylov space and have inverse head overlap
squared at most 1+T^2. That proves precisely the property required by the
imported theorem. The exact span identity holds even when the initial
Krylov matrix has dependent columns; orthogonalization preserves its
range. Each final iteration is still one multiplication of the original
small block through the ordinary Krylov process, so multiplying depth by
b is the appropriate matrix-vector count up to fixed conventions for
applying M M^T. The source correctly states the logarithmic overhead
instead of claiming the literal Chen quantitative conjecture.

D. Raw monomial counterexample

Lines 579--592 are correct. The polynomial is monic, so its coefficient
Euclidean norm is at least one. Its first k-1 evaluations vanish, and

  |p(2^(-(k-1)))|
     =product_(j=0)^(k-2)(2^(-j)-2^(-(k-1)))
     <=2^(-(k-1)(k-2)/2).

Normalizing its coefficient vector gives the stated deterministic upper
bound on sigma_min(K). For any fixed constants C>0,a>=0,c>1, a proposed
lower bound C k^(-a)c^(-k) would force

  |g_k|>=C k^(-a)c^(-k) 2^((k-1)(k-2)/2),

whose right side tends to infinity. A Gaussian tail or simply the
second-moment Markov bound shows that the lower-bound success probability
tends to zero. The minimum adjacent relative gap is exactly 1/2. Thus
this refutes the specified condition-number-free exponential-in-depth
raw-monomial form, while leaving the proved source theorem with its
condition number untouched. It does not refute the good-start result.
