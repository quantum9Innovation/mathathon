SPECTRAL CONCENTRATION FOR BALANCED PERMUTATION MULTIPLICATION
===========================================================

Research derivation, November 2026. This note closes a useful spectral
concentration bound for the explicit estimator in balanced-permutation-
amm.txt. It uses classical matrix concentration for a combinatorial
permutation sum, the exact second-order covariance, and truncation.
It is not a claim of a new asymptotic AMM or OSE tradeoff. In particular,
it does not yet exploit the sharp higher mixed moments SG-11/14/16.

1. PRECISE ESTIMATOR AND GUARANTEE
================================
Fix dense real A of size n-by-m and B of size m-by-p, chosen before the
randomness. Let m>=4 and m=sq, with 2<=s<=m. For each of d independent
uniform balanced partitions of [m] into s buckets of q coordinates, let
H_c be its s-by-m incidence matrix. Put

  rho=(q-1)/(m-1), gamma=1/(1-rho),
  J=11^T/m, Pi=I-J,
  W_c=(H_c^T H_c-rho*11^T)/(1-rho),
  Wbar=(1/d)sum_c W_c, C=A Wbar B.

Thus E W_c=I and W_c1=1 exactly. Each W_c is PSD of rank s, and
rank Wbar<=min(m,1+d(s-1)). The rank-one constant channel can be shared.
The sparse bucket incidence H_c is unsigned. Its exact Gram factor has
a deterministic dense mean correction, so this is a sparse-plus-low-rank
implicit sketch, NOT a matrix with d literal nonzeros per column and
NOT a purely nonnegative subspace embedding.

Let r>=max(1,sr(A),sr(B)), where sr(X)=||X||_F^2/||X||^2 and zero
factors are handled by returning zero. Take 0<epsilon,delta<1 and assume

  s-1 >= r/epsilon.                                      (1)

There is a universal constant C_0 such that choosing

  d = ceil(C_0 epsilon^-1 log^3(C_0(2s+n+p)/(delta epsilon))) (2)

gives

  Pr{||C-AB|| > epsilon ||A|| ||B||} <=delta.             (3)

One entirely explicit conservative choice in the proof is C_0=2^20.
The implicit sufficient inequality (17) below is much less wasteful
and can be used instead of the conservative closed formula.

For s=ceil(1+r/epsilon), the output width is

  O(r epsilon^-2 log^3((r+n+p)/(delta epsilon))).          (4)

Factor construction, including all factor entries, takes

  O(d m(n+p)+d s(n+p)),                                  (5)

and naive explicit expansion adds O(np[1+d(s-1)]). The arithmetic
consists of bucket sums, deterministic centering and scaling, and the
final factor product. Randomness is d independent uniform permutations.
There are no independent random entry signs. General matrix-multiplication
algorithms can replace the final naive product if appropriate.

The divisibility condition is removable by appending zero columns/rows
to ambient dimension M=s*ceil(m/s)<m+s when s<m. Norms and stable ranks
of the padded A,B are unchanged. Run the theorem at M. When s>=m,
return the exact factorization A,B instead. Thus neither divisibility
nor a prime ambient dimension forces an asymptotic dimension loss.

For an r-dimensional fixed subspace, apply the theorem with A=U^T,
B=U for an orthonormal basis U. Its Gram guarantee is an epsilon-OSE
with the same row count (4) and implicit application cost. The actual
matrix is sparse plus a rank-one correction. No standard sparse-OSE
nonzero-count claim follows from the application-time statement.

An ordinary O(N^2) fixed-stable-rank regime
------------------------------------------
There is a simpler one-color theorem, without the logarithmic dimensions
in (2). For fixed r>=max(1,sr(A),sr(B)) and 0<epsilon,delta<1, choose

  s=ceil(1+3(r^2+2r)/(epsilon^2 delta)), d=1.             (4a)

Use zero padding or the identity fallback as described above. Then the
same estimator satisfies (3). Its width is at most s, construction is
O(m(n+p)+s(n+p)) after at most doubling the ambient input dimension,
and explicit expansion costs O(nps). Consequently for n=p=N, m<=N,
and fixed r,epsilon,delta, total ordinary arithmetic is O(N^2), including
every output entry. The input matrices can have full algebraic rank;
the promise is bounded stable rank, not exact low rank.

Proof. Normalize ||A||=||B||=1. Taking the trace of the exact covariance
identity (7) and discarding its negative term gives

  E||A T B||_F^2
   <= a||A_0 B_0||_F^2+b||A_0||_F^2||B_0||_F^2
   <= a r+b r^2
   <=[4r+3r^2]/(s-1)
   <=3(r^2+2r)/(s-1).                                   (4b)

The coefficient bounds a<=4/(s-1), b<=3/(s-1) are established in
Section 2. Markov's inequality and ||X||<=||X||_F prove (3) with (4a).
If s>=m, the exact identity fallback has width m<=s, is always accurate,
and preserves all displayed costs. Otherwise the padded ambient M is
less than 2m, with A,B norms and stable ranks unchanged.

This is an additional bounded-stable-rank absolute-error case. It does
not establish error (1+theta)sigma_(r+1)(AB) for arbitrary factors or
accuracy relative to ||AB|| under cancellation. Classical stable-rank
AMM already supplies comparable or stronger tradeoffs. No asymptotic
state-of-the-art improvement is claimed for this corollary.

2. MATRIX COVARIANCE, INCLUDING THE NEGATIVE CORRECTION
====================================================
Write T=W-I. Then T is symmetric, T1=0 and T_ii=0. Let

  c=(m-s)/(s-1), v=c/m,
  u=-v/(m-2), w=2v/[(m-2)(m-3)],
  a=v-2u+2w, b=v-2u+w, kappa=2(v-4u+3w).

The covariance of two off-diagonal T entries is v for equal unordered
pairs, u if the pairs share one vertex, and w for disjoint pairs.
These identities follow from permutation symmetry, E T^2=c Pi,
and T1=0, as derived in balanced-permutation-amm.txt.

For any symmetric G with G1=0, direct summation of the three overlap
classes gives the exact covariance map

  E[T G T]=a G+b tr(G)Pi-kappa Pi diag(G)Pi.              (6)

For completeness, the diagonal entry of its left side is

  (v-u)tr(G)+(-v+2u)G_ii.

For i!=j the corresponding entry is

  (v-2u+2w)G_ij+(u-w)[tr(G)-2G_ii-2G_jj].

These two entrywise formulas are equivalent to (6), using G1=0 and
the displayed relations among v,u,w. Thus (6) is not inferred merely
from a trace or Frobenius second moment.

Normalize ||A||=||B||=1 for the proof, and put A_0=A Pi, B_0=Pi B.
The error of one color is X=A T B=A_0 T B_0. In particular EX=0.
Since A_0 1=0 and 1^T B_0=0, (6) gives

  E[XX^T]
   =a(A_0B_0)(A_0B_0)^T+b||B_0||_F^2 A_0A_0^T
        -kappa A_0 diag(B_0B_0^T) A_0^T,                 (7)

and the analogous transposed identity for E[X^T X]. The last term is
negative semidefinite, since kappa>=0. Also

  b = c(m-1)/[m(m-3)] <=3/(s-1),
  a = b[1+2/((m-1)(m-2))] <= (4/3)b.

Because ||A_0||,||B_0||<=1 and ||A_0||_F^2,||B_0||_F^2<=r,
both covariance matrices in (7) are bounded above by V I, where

  V=3(r+2)/(s-1).                                      (8)

Under (1), V<=9epsilon. This operator variance bound, rather than its
trace, is what avoids the rank-squared second-moment width.

3. A PERMUTATION BERNSTEIN BOUND FOR THE BUCKET FACTORS
====================================================
We invoke the following primary-source statement, without changing its
assumptions or introducing independent permutation entries:

Mackey--Jordan--Chen--Farrell--Tropp, Matrix concentration inequalities
via the method of exchangeable pairs, Ann. Probab.42(2014),906--945.
https://lmackey.github.io/papers/matstein-aop14.pdf
Corollary10.3, printed p933 (PDF p28), says: for a deterministic Hermitian
array F_ij with sum_ij F_ij=0, ||F_ij||<=R, and uniform permutation pi,

  Pr{lambda_max(sum_i F_(i,pi(i)))>=t}
   <=D exp[-t^2/(12 sigma^2+4sqrt(2)Rt)],
  sigma^2=||(1/m)sum_ij F_ij^2||,                         (9)

where D is the Hermitian matrix dimension. The complete primary PDF
and the statement were inspected, including its proof setup in Section10.

Let U be any m-by-r' matrix satisfying U^T1=0, ||U||<=1 and
||U||_F^2<=r. It need not have orthonormal columns. For a fixed balanced
bucket assignment b(j), put a_j=e_(b(j))-1_s/s. If u_i^T is row i of U,
form the rectangular array a_j u_i^T and its self-adjoint dilation.
All dilated entries have norm at most ||a_j||||u_i||<=1. Their total
sum is zero. The permuted sum is exactly the dilation of H U, because
sum_i u_i=0. Moreover

  (1/m)sum_ij dilation(a_j u_i^T)^2
   =diag( (||U||_F^2/s)(I_s-J_s),
               (1-1/s)U^T U).

When s>=r, the variance parameter in (9) is at most one. Since
12+64sqrt(2)z <=128z for z>=1, (9) implies the convenient bound

  Pr{||H U||>=16z} <=(s+r')exp(-2z), z>=1.              (10)

Indeed 256z^2/(12+64sqrt(2)z)>=2z. Dilation converts its largest
eigenvalue to the rectangular norm, so no extra factor of two is needed.

Apply (10) to U=A_0^T and U=B_0. Their column dimensions are n and p,
their spectral norms are at most one, and their squared Frobenius
norms are at most r. Although these column dimensions can be large,
the variance parameter uses their Frobenius norm, not column rank.
Writing D=2s+n+p, a union bound shows that with failure at most
D exp(-2z), both ||A_0 H^T|| and ||H B_0|| are at most16z.
Since gamma<=2 and X=gamma A_0 H^T H B_0-A_0B_0,

  Pr{||X||>513z^2} <=D exp(-2z), z>=1.                  (11)

The bound has no ambient factor m/s. It also has no assumed entrywise
incoherence. Its logarithmic dimensional factor is allowed to depend
on the physical output dimensions n,p.

4. TRUNCATION WITHOUT A HIDDEN AMBIENT-DIMENSION BIAS
==================================================
Fix a candidate integer d>=1 and set

  h=log(8192 D d/(delta epsilon)), R=513h^2.             (12)

Here h>=1. For each independent color let X_c=A T_c B and define
Y_c=X_c 1_(||X_c||<=R), B_trim=E Y_c. Truncation is an analysis device;
the algorithm returns the ORIGINAL, untruncated average C.

Equation (11) and the tail-integral identity give

  ||B_trim|| <=E[||X||1_(||X||>R)]
   <=513D exp(-2h)(h^2+h+1/2)
   <=1283D h^2 exp(-2h)
   <=1283D exp(-h) <=delta epsilon/(4d).                (13)

For the penultimate inequality use h^2 exp(-h)<=1 for h>=1.
The tail integral is computed by substituting t=513z^2:
int_R^infinity Pr(||X||>t)dt
 <=1026D int_h^infinity z exp(-2z)dz
 =513D exp(-2h)(h+1/2).
The additional R Pr(||X||>R) gives the first h^2 term in (13).

Also a union bound over the d colors gives

  Pr{some X_c is truncated} <=dD exp(-2h)<=delta/4.      (14)

Use self-adjoint dilations, and center Z_c=Y_c-B_trim. The resulting
independent Hermitian matrices have mean zero, norm at most 2R, and
variance bounded by V I. Indeed, after dilation,

  E[(Y-EY)^2]=E Y^2-(EY)^2 <=E Y^2<=E X^2<=V I.       (15)

This Loewner argument is the reason truncation does not introduce an
extra variance factor. It does not assume the untruncated X is symmetric.

Corollary5.2 in the same primary PDF, printed p922 (PDF p17), gives
matrix Bernstein with denominator 3sigma^2+2Rt. Applying it to the
d centered dilations, with t=d epsilon/2, yields

  Pr{||(1/d)sum_c Z_c||>epsilon/2}
   <=(n+p)exp[-d epsilon^2/(12V+8R epsilon)].           (16)

Consequently it suffices that

  d >=[12V/epsilon^2+4104h^2/epsilon]
                    log(4(n+p)/delta).                (17)

On the complement of the two bad events in (14),(16), the original
average satisfies
||(1/d)sum X_c||<=epsilon/2+||B_trim||<epsilon.
Its total failure is at most delta/2, hence at most delta. Scaling A,B
back proves (3). Neither trimming nor norms of realized sketches need
to be computed by the algorithm.

5. AN EXPLICIT DEGREE CHOICE, PADDING AND APPLICATION COST
=======================================================
Assume (1), let C_0=2^20, and put

  L=log(C_0 D/(delta epsilon)),
  d=ceil(C_0 epsilon^-1 L^3).

Since D>=1 and epsilon,delta<1, L>=log C_0>13.8 and
d<=2C_0 epsilon^-1 L^3. Therefore

  log d <=log(2C_0)+log(1/epsilon)+3log L<3L,
  h=log(8192D/(delta epsilon))+log d<4L,
  log(4(n+p)/delta)<=L.

Together with V<=9epsilon, the right side of (17) is at most
epsilon^-1(108+65664 L^2)L, which is below
C_0 epsilon^-1 L^3. Thus the explicit d satisfies the sufficient
concentration inequality. The constants deliberately favor a fully
checkable elementary proof; (17) gives a sharper actual parameter test.
Comparisons, repeated multiplication/doubling and fixed-constant series
can choose the integer parameters without unit-cost transcendental
primitives. This scalar overhead is absorbed by the displayed bounds.

Choose s=ceil(1+r/epsilon). If s<m but s does not divide m, append zeros
to M=s ceil(m/s). Then m<=M<m+s<=2m. Stable ranks and operator norms
of A,B are unchanged by this padding. The proof uses M in place of m
and physical n,p unchanged. If s>=m, use W=I and return A,B exactly.
In that branch m<=1+r/epsilon, which is also within the row bound (4).

The Gram representation Wbar=J+(lambda/d)sum_c Pi_(H_c) shares the
constant channel once. A fixed Householder reflection in bucket space
removes the redundant centered bucket row per color, giving a factor
with exactly 1+d(s-1) rows, unless capped by the identity fallback.
Applying the reflection is a vector dot product plus rank-one update,
O(s) arithmetic per input/output column. Bucket sums cost one addition
per input entry per color. This proves (5) with no hidden dense row
orthogonalization, and counts all factors and explicit-output costs.

The actual Gram factor has dense entries from the common mean correction.
Only its incidence part has one bucket assignment per color and input.
Calling the complete S a d-sparse-per-column embedding would be false.

6. RELATION TO THE ORIGINAL GOAL AND EXISTING RESULTS
==================================================
This is a proved concentration guarantee for the actual balanced
permutation estimator. It is stronger than its previous Frobenius/
Markov baseline and gives a stable-rank spectral AMM guarantee with
polylogarithmic confidence overhead. It is an absolute product error
epsilon||A||||B||, not a (1+theta)sigma_(r+1)(AB) low-rank guarantee.
No relative-product conclusion follows for arbitrarily cancelling A,B.

The construction is oblivious and requires a supplied stable-rank upper
bound to choose s, or a separate estimator for that parameter. The theorem
does not obtain a new stable-rank bound merely by using an optimistic r.

The dimension/accuracy tradeoff r epsilon^-2 times logarithms and the
epsilon^-1 application-degree dependence are familiar from signed
sparse embeddings and AMM. The possible contribution is this concrete
balanced-permutation, sparse-plus-rank-one construction and its exact
variance/cancellation analysis. No global state-of-the-art improvement
or historical novelty is certified by this derivation. A comparison with
the actual assumptions and guarantees of OSNAP, modern SparseStack, and
stable-rank AMM is still required before any priority claim.

The imported SG-11/14/16 exact mixed envelopes remain relevant if one
seeks sharper logarithms, constants or dependency behavior than these
classical concentration inequalities provide. This proof does not
claim to have summed their full sharp high-order trace expansion.
