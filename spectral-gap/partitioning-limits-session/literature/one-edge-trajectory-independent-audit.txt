1. Exact cut envelope.
   The binary crossing signatures are the correct complete sufficient
   parameter for additive edge-weight changes. Within a signature class,
   all cut costs change identically. The entire optimizer-set description
   is correct, as are at most two fixed representatives for one edge and
   2^r representatives for r edges. This is existential; the statement
   correctly avoids claiming the constrained optima can be computed fast.

2. Flat orthonormal input cuts.
   For n=2^m, characters independent of the first bit give exactly n/2-1
   nontrivial balanced mutually orthogonal sign vectors. They agree at
   u=(0,...,0), v=(1,0,...,0), so they are all orthogonal to e0=b/sqrt2.
   Selection of k is allowed by the stated n condition.

3. Rotation.
   The displayed formula is one orthogonal plane rotation. Its q_j
   component parallel to z is replaced by gamma*z+beta*e0, while e0
   becomes gamma*e0-beta*z. Consequently v0,v1,...,vk are orthonormal,
   b0^2=2-c sum d_l, b_j^2=c d_j, and b has no remaining component.
   All rotated vectors remain orthogonal to constants.

4. Genuine graph edge, uniformly bounded positive conductances.
   M=A-KP has eigenvalues -1,d_j,1,0, hence norm at most1 when d1<.01.
   K=2n makes the offdiagonal entries of KP equal -2, so adding M puts
   every offdiagonal entry in [-3,-1]. Zero row sums then give the
   complete weighted graph exactly. An increase t in [0,w_e] of the
   selected existing edge is exactly its doubling, with only that weight
   changing. The resulting weights all stay in [1,6].

5. Global simplicity of the Fiedler value.
   Restricted to constants-perp, the baseline lowest eigenvalue K-1 is
   simple at v0; the next active pole is K+d_k. In the interval between
   these poles, m(mu) is strictly increasing and goes from -infinity to
   +infinity, since all active couplings are strictly nonzero. For EVERY
   finite t>0, 1+t m=0 therefore has a unique simple root in that first
   interval. The characteristic determinant factorization plus interlacing
   show it is the lowest eigenvalue, with algebraic multiplicity one.
   Unchanged eigenvalues at K+1 cannot interfere. Positivity preserves
   constants as the only zero eigenvector. Thus the global assertion
   'simple for every t>=0' is established, not merely at selected times.

6. Sample parameters.
   d1<.01 gives c sum d_l<.001 and positive sum <=ck=.1. Hence
   m(K-d_j)<-1, so 0<t_j<1<=w_e. Since d_j strictly decrease, K-d_j
   strictly increase, and -1/m is strictly increasing while m<0.
   Therefore t1<...<tk. k=1 has no cross-sample ordering obligation.

7. Uniform coordinate dominance and parameter order.
   Dividing the resolvent vector by its jth coefficient gives exact
   off-diagonal coefficient ratios 2 sqrt(d_l d_j)/(d_l+d_j).
   Their geometric sum is bounded by 4 sqrt(r)/(1-sqrt(r)), as claimed.
   At fixed k,n,r, the v0 coefficient ratio is O(sqrt(d1/c)) uniformly
   in j and tends to0. Also every v_l-q_l tends to0 in supremum norm,
   and the total coefficient mass on l>=1 is uniformly bounded. Thus
   one sufficiently small positive d1 controls all finite j simultaneously
   and gives the stated supremum-norm error. No invalid exchange of a
   growing n limit with d1 is made: d1 may depend on n,k,eta,r.

8. Normalization and bisections.
   Supremum error <eta/sqrt(n) implies all signs exactly equal q_j,
   coordinate magnitudes in [(1-eta)/sqrt(n),(1+eta)/sqrt(n)], and norm
   in [1-eta,1+eta]. Normalizing gives exactly the stated ratios and
   Euclidean distance <=2eta. All sampled sign cuts are balanced. Their
   normalized SIGN vectors are exactly orthogonal, and their Hamming
   distance is n/2 under either orientation. The actual Fiedler vectors
   are approximately orthogonal, not asserted to be exactly orthogonal.

9. Vanishing total relative operator norm.

   This argument is gap-free for energies; no eigendirection stability
