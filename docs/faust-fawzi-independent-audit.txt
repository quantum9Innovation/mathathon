VERDICT
-------
PASS for the core existence proofs of BOTH complete conjectures, with
the exact displayed root-exponential exponent and a fixed prefactor.
The preferred single global quadrature construction is valid, including
the endpoint parameters a=1 and b=2. No use of a nonexistent degree-2m
moment is needed. The old split proof is consistent with this verdict,
but the preferred global proof was the principal object of this audit.

The proof is conditional only on the correctly quoted classical
modified-Ganelius product theorem. All other key steps were checked
directly below. This is an independent internal mathematical review,
not a formal verification or external human peer review.

The exact-real interpolation coefficient computation and the explicit
SDP representations also pass at their stated, limited scope. A full
numerical stability or bit-complexity theorem was NOT audited here.
Neither the core proof nor this audit certifies novelty as of 2026.

Two minor presentation corrections are listed in section 10. Neither
affects the two conjectures or the main global quadrature proof.

1. MATCH WITH THE ACTUAL PUBLISHED CONJECTURES
---------------------------------------------
The positive-parameter target is 1<=a<b<=2 and

  epsilon_m <= C_(a,b) exp[-2*pi*sqrt(a*(b-a)*m/b)],

where epsilon_m is the best relative error for f_a against f_b among
the allowed positive m-kernel sums. This matches FF Conjecture 1,
including its fixed prefactor. The negative-parameter statement is
obtained with a=1-alpha, b=1-beta, where -1<=beta<alpha<=0.

FF Conjecture 2 asks for a type [m/m] rational approximation to x^alpha
on (0,1], weighted relative to x^beta, with -1<=beta<alpha<0. Writing
c=-alpha and d=-beta gives 0<c<d<=1. The candidate's positive lower
approximation of type [m-1/m] is stronger than the required rational
degree restriction and has the required error exp[-2*pi*sqrt((d-c)*m)].

The source statements establish upper bounds. Neither FF's conjectures
nor the candidate proof assert the sharp fixed multiplicative constant
or a matching exact asymptotic equivalent.

2. CLASSICAL PRODUCT INGREDIENT: CORRECT NORMALIZATION
-----------------------------------------------------
TOS Definition 2.5 supplies n positive nodes in (0,1). Their Theorem 2.6
states, for fixed 0<r<1,

  sup_(0<=s<=1) s^r |prod_(j=1)^n (s-a_j)/(s+a_j)|
       <= A_r exp[-pi*sqrt(r*n)],

with A_r independent of n. There is no extra doubling of n in this
formula. The candidate uses r_-=a/4 in [1/4,1/2), and r_+=(b-a)/4 in
(0,1/4], so Theorem 2.6 already covers every parameter used. For
Conjecture 2, r=(d-c)/4 lies in (0,1/4).

Squaring the product bound gives exponent -2*pi*sqrt(r*n). The two
occurrences of that squared bound in the error comparison produce the
full exponent. All Ganelius constants remain fixed for fixed a,b (or
c,d); no uniformity as the parameters approach each other is claimed.

The explicit node formula in the note agrees with Definition 2.5.
Its small-n exceptions can be avoided by using it only after a fixed
parameter-dependent threshold, then absorbing finitely many cases.

3. GAUSSIAN QUADRATURE WITH MOMENTS ONLY THROUGH 2m-1
---------------------------------------------------
The preferred Conjecture 1 construction defines

  rho(t)=t^a/(1+t)^2,
  Omega(t)=t(t+1) prod_(low)(t+a_j)^2 prod_(high)(t+1/b_j)^2,
  m=n_-+n_++1,
  dnu=rho(t)dt/Omega(t).

Omega has degree 2m. At zero dnu has density comparable to t^(a-1),
and at infinity to t^(a-2m-2). Hence the moments through degree 2m-1
are finite for 1<=a<2. The degree-2m moment generally diverges.

The following construction needs no such last moment:

  (i) Solve for a monic polynomial q_m of degree m orthogonal to
      degrees 0,...,m-1. Its moment matrix uses degrees at most 2m-2;
      the right-hand side uses degrees at most 2m-1. The matrix is
      positive definite because nu has a strictly positive density
      on (0,infinity).

 (ii) q_m has exactly m distinct roots in (0,infinity). If its positive
      sign-changing roots numbered k<m, their product p_k would make
      q_m*p_k have one fixed nonzero sign on (0,infinity), and degree
      at most 2m-1. Its finite integral would contradict orthogonality.
      This also excludes complex roots, negative roots, multiple roots
      and roots at the endpoint, since all m degrees are exhausted by
      the m distinct positive sign changes.

(iii) Let ell_i be the degree-m-1 Lagrange polynomial of a root t_i,
      and A_i=integral ell_i dnu. For a polynomial P of degree at most
      2m-1, division P=q_m*s+r, deg s<=m-1, deg r<=m-1, proves exactness
      of the rule sum A_i P(t_i). Applying exactness to ell_i^2 shows
      A_i=integral ell_i^2 dnu>0.

Thus an ordinary positive m-node Gaussian rule exists with exactly the
finite moment data available. No integral of q_m^2 without an additional
denominator is used anywhere in the argument.

4. MULTIPOINT ERROR IDENTITY AND VARIATIONAL COMPARISON
------------------------------------------------------
For x>0 put S_nu(x)=integral dnu(t)/(x+t). Since

  [q_m(t)^2-q_m(-x)^2]/(t+x)

is a polynomial of degree 2m-1, Gaussian exactness gives

  S_nu(x)-sum_i A_i/(x+t_i)
    = integral q_m(t)^2/(x+t) dnu(t) / q_m(-x)^2.       (E)

This integral is finite, even though integral q_m^2 dnu can diverge:
its tail uses only the finite degree-2m-1 moment. Also q_m(-x)!=0.

For any polynomial p of degree at most m with p(-x)=1, write

  p(t)-q_m(t)/q_m(-x)=(t+x)s(t), deg s<=m-1.

The cross term in integral p(t)^2/(x+t)dnu(t) vanishes by orthogonality.
The remaining added term is nonnegative. Thus q_m/q_m(-x) minimizes
this weighted integral over all such p. Every cross term is finite.

Polynomial division of [Omega(t)-Omega(-x)]/(t+x), whose degree is
2m-1, followed by (E), yields the candidate's exact GE identity for
H_a and R_m. If Omega(-x) is negative, take its absolute value before
using the variational comparison; the note does this correctly.

For completeness, the Stieltjes identity for H_a can itself be checked
without another approximation theorem. Differentiate twice in x the
kernel (x-1)^2/(x+t). Its second derivative is 2(t+1)^2/(x+t)^3.
Multiplication by rho and integration therefore gives x^(a-2) after
the beta integral and the candidate's K_a normalization. The value
and first derivative at x=1 vanish, exactly as for f_a. For a=1 the
same calculation uses K_1=1. This proves the displayed representation.

5. GLOBAL FACTORIZATION AND THE TWO UNIFORM WEIGHTED BOUNDS
----------------------------------------------------------
Let B(t) be the combined low-node product and reciprocal high-node
product, with irrelevant signs removed. Use

  q0(t)=(t-1) prod_(low)(t-a_j) prod_(high)(t-1/b_j),

which has degree m. Direct cancellation gives

  |Omega(-x)|/q0(-x)^2
     = x|x-1| B(x)^2/(x+1)^2,

  q0(t)^2/Omega(t)
     = (t-1)^2 B(t)^2/[t(t+1)].

This proves GLOBAL exactly as displayed in the candidate note. In
particular, the actual sign of H_a-R_m is the sign of x-1, apart from
the double interpolation zeros. This does not jeopardize positivity
of the residues, which follows from the positive Gaussian weights.

Write gamma=b-a and

  D_-=A_(a/4)^2 exp[-pi*sqrt(a*n_-)],
  D_+=A_(gamma/4)^2 exp[-pi*sqrt(gamma*n_+)].

The product estimate gives B(s)^2<=D_- s^(-a/2) for s<=1 and
B(s)^2<=D_+ s^(gamma/2) for s>=1. The unused factor of the combined
product has absolute value at most one in each range.

For x<=1, the outside GLOBAL factor is at most x. Splitting its
integral at t=1 gives the two terms

  D_-^2 x^(1-a/2) integral_0^1 t^(a/2-1)/(x+t)dt,

  D_-D_+ x^(1-a/2) integral_1^infinity
                        t^(a-2+gamma/2)/(x+t)dt.

The first is at most D_-^2*pi/sin(pi*a/2). In the second use x+t>=t,
x^(1-a/2)<=1, and p=2-(a+b)/2>0 to get D_-D_+/p. All convergence
conditions hold at a=1,b=2 as well as throughout the open range.

For x>=1, the outside GLOBAL factor is at most one. The low integral
is bounded by 2/(a*x). After division by H_b(x)>=x^(b-2)/2, its
remaining x power is 1-(a+b)/2<0. The high integral is bounded by
pi*x^(-p)/sin(pi*p), with 0<p=2-(a+b)/2<1. Its remaining x power
after division by H_b is exactly zero. This proves RIGHT.

The weight inequality used here is elementary:

  H_b(x)=integral_0^1 (1-u)[1+u(x-1)]^(b-2)du
       >= (1/2) min(1,x^(b-2)).

It holds also for b=2, where H_b=1/2 exactly. Thus the proof does
not invoke FF's best-approximant characterization at an excluded
endpoint. LEFT and RIGHT imply FIN with a constant depending only
on a,b and no polynomial factor in the node counts.

6. NODE COUNT, ALL-m STATEMENT, KERNEL FORM AND INVOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------
Choosing

  n_-=ceil[L^2/(4*pi^2*a)],
  n_+=ceil[L^2/(4*pi^2*gamma)]

makes D_-^2, D_-D_+ and D_+^2 each at most a fixed multiple of e^(-L).
The total node count satisfies

  m <= b*L^2/[4*pi^2*a*gamma]+3.

For every sufficiently large prescribed integer m, use instead
n_-=floor[gamma*(m-1)/b], n_+=m-1-n_-. Both a*n_- and gamma*n_+
equal a*gamma*m/b plus a uniformly bounded error. The difference of
the corresponding square roots is bounded, and tends to zero.
Consequently FIN has the exact requested exponent with a fixed
prefactor for every sufficiently large m. For the finitely many
remaining m, the zero allowed-kernel approximation has finite relative
error: H_a/H_b is bounded at zero, one and infinity. Increasing the
fixed constant handles these cases. FF permits zero weights.

Set T_i=1/(1+t_i) and u_i=K_a*A_i*Omega(t_i)/(1+t_i). The T_i are
distinct and strictly between zero and one; every u_i is positive.
Multiplication by (x-1)^2 gives exactly the allowed kernel family,
with a double zero at one. GE also gives H_a(1)=R_m(1)=1/2, so the
second derivative agrees there as claimed.

Finally, f_alpha(x)=x*f_(1-alpha)(1/x). Applying this identity to the
rational kernels sends T to 1-T without changing its positive weight.
The relative error is preserved after the substitution x to 1/x.
This proves the full negative-parameter part of Conjecture 1,
including alpha=0 and beta=-1 when those endpoints are admissible.

7. CONJECTURE 2: GLOBAL PROOF AND FIXED PREFACTOR
------------------------------------------------
For 0<c<d<=1 set gamma=d-c and

  Omega(t)=(1+t)^(2k) prod_(j=1)^n(t+a_j)^2,
  M=n+k,
  dnu(t)=t^(-c)dt/Omega(t).

The moments through degree 2M-1 are finite at both endpoints, so the
same finite-moment Gaussian argument applies. All rescaled residues
are positive. Omega(-x)>=0, and (E) proves 0<=R_M(x)<=x^(-c) for all
x>0. The competitor q0=prod(t-a_j), of degree n<=M, is legitimate
in the same variational minimum.

With D=A_(gamma/4)^2 exp[-pi*sqrt(gamma*n)], the low-t part is bounded
by D^2*pi/sin(pi*(c+d)/2). The beta integral is finite since
0<(c+d)/2<1, even when d=1. In the high-t part use B(t)^2<=1,
(1+t)^(-2k)<=2^(-2k), and (x+t)^(-1)<=t^(-1). The remaining integral
is 1/c, and the remaining factor x^((c+d)/2)<=1. This gives exactly
C2GLOBAL, including its D*2^(-2k) cross term.

For n=ceil[L^2/(4*pi^2*gamma)] and k=max(1,ceil[L/(4*log 2)]), both
error terms are O(e^(-L)) and

  M <= L^2/(4*pi^2*gamma)+L/(4*log 2)+O(1).

For large M choose L as the positive root of A L^2+B L+C0=M, with
fixed rounding bound C0. Then

  L=2*pi*sqrt(gamma*M)-O_(c,d)(1).

Thus inversion introduces only a fixed prefactor, with no unbounded
subleading loss in the exponent. Padding by zero weights, or selecting
a lower positive rule with the desired number of nodes, gives every
integer budget. The older split proof independently reaches the same
rate by reserving O(sqrt(M)) tail nodes; its count arithmetic is also
consistent with a fixed prefactor.

8. EXACT-REAL PREPARATION: WHAT IS AND IS NOT PROVED
--------------------------------------------------
GE supplies the Conjecture 1 interpolation data at 0 once, 1 once,
and the low and reciprocal high positive nodes twice each. There are
2m data counted with multiplicities. The value at zero follows also
by exactness on Omega(t)/t; the value at one by exactness on
Omega(t)/(t+1). These are polynomials of degree 2m-1.

The resulting rational function has type [m-1/m], exactly m distinct
negative poles and nonzero residues, so its numerator and denominator
are coprime and the denominator has degree exactly m. The coefficient
interpolation equations with a monic denominator have a unique solution.
To justify uniqueness even against algebraic solutions with a denominator
zero at an interpolation node, cross-multiply with the true solution:
the difference polynomial has degree at most 2m-1 and 2m zeros with
multiplicity, so it vanishes identically; coprimality and monic
normalization then give the identical coefficient pair.

Thus dense elimination costs O(m^3) arithmetic after O(m) elementary
power/log evaluations and O(m^2) coefficient assembly/storage. The
Ganelius nodes themselves use O(m) elementary exp/sqrt evaluations.
Coincident interpolation nodes can be interpreted with higher Hermite
data, as the note says. Conjecture 2 uses the same argument, with a
2k-fold interpolation condition at one and only elementary power data.

Pole and residue extraction is a further degree-m real-root problem.
The note explicitly charges it separately. Therefore the honest claim
is an O(m^3) coefficient computation plus elementary function evaluations
and root extraction, not a bare O(m^3) field-arithmetic bound for all
numerical SDP coefficients. The exact positive existence theorem is
independent of any implementation of those last operations.

The appended conservative bit-cost argument is outside this audit's
PASS verdict. Its compactification identity and weight transformation
were checked algebraically and are correct; its full error budget,
quadrature implementation and certified root extraction still deserve
a separate numerical-analysis review before a bit-complexity theorem.

9. EXPLICIT SDP CONSEQUENCES
----------------------------
The scalar 2-by-2 Schur blocks are exact because their top-left entries
are positive for x>0. Summing their positive weighted bottom-right
entries yields precisely the epigraph of the allowed rational sum.

For positive definite A,B the matrix-perspective formula follows by
substituting C=B^(-1/2)AB^(-1/2) and applying congruence by B^(1/2).
Each term is

  (A-B)[(1-T_i)B+T_i A]^(-1)(A-B).

Its 2n-by-2n Schur block is exact. Applying scalar functional calculus
to the relative error and then congruence gives the stated two-sided
Loewner error relative to the perspective of f_b.

For the Petz divergence let Delta=L_rho R_sigma^(-1), whose two positive
operators commute. Write v=vec(rho-sigma). The kernel divergence is

  v^*[T_i I tensor rho+(1-T_i) sigma^transpose tensor I]^(-1)v.

This can be verified either in the common left/right eigenbasis or by
moving the factors R_sigma^(1/2) through the commuting operators. Its
Schur epigraph has size n^2+1, affine top-left block and affine v, as
claimed. For trace-one density matrices the relative f_2 divergence is

  (1/2)[Tr(rho^2 sigma^(-1))-1].

Hence the Umegaki error bound and the leading node count
log^2(1/epsilon)/(2*pi^2) follow. The note correctly makes no SDP
optimization runtime claim from this block count. It also correctly
separates these explicit n^2+1 blocks from the smaller quantum block
representation attributed in FF to a then-forthcoming work.

10. MINOR PRESENTATION CORRECTIONS AND SCOPE
-------------------------------------------
(a) The introductory source discussion says only 0<r<1/4 is needed.
    This is stale after the Conjecture 1 extension: r_-=a/4 can approach
    1/2. Replace it by 0<r<1/2. TOS Theorem 2.6 still applies, so no
    proof step changes.

(b) The initial stronger Conjecture 2 statement requires all residues
    strictly positive, but its finite-small-M paragraph allows R_M=0.
    FF itself permits general rational approximants, so the conjecture
    is unaffected. To preserve the stronger strict-positivity statement,
    use the M-node Gaussian construction for

      dnu=t^(-c)(1+t)^(-2M)dt

    at those finitely many M, without Ganelius nodes. The same error
    identity gives a strictly positive global lower approximation;
    its weighted error on (0,1] is at most one. Enlarging C handles
    every such finite case while retaining M distinct positive poles.

No mathematical repair is required for the main single-global-rule
proof. The two conjecture resolutions survive this independent audit.
Priority, external review, and any claim of broader significance remain
separate tasks. In particular this does not establish omega=2, a general
quadratic matrix algorithm, or a new runtime theorem for SDP solving.
