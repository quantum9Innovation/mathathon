1. CANDIDATE THEOREM
--------------------
Let X be real N-by-m, m<=N, U real N-by-r, 1<=r<=m, and H=XX^T.
Given eps,delta in (0,1), construct Z of width

    q*r,  q=O(log^2(r/eps)),

such that, with probability at least 1-delta,

    ||ZZ^T - [(H+UU^T)^(1/2)-H^(1/2)]||_2 <= eps ||U||_2.

The conventional-arithmetic construction cost is

    ~O(N*m*r + m^3),

with only dimension, eps and delta logarithms suppressed. Storage is
O(N*m + N*r*polylog(r/eps) + m^2*polylog(m/delta)). The factors use only
ordinary arithmetic, positive scalar square roots, and comparisons. H may
be singular, ||H||/||UU^T|| may be arbitrary, and no condition or gap
parameter enters the operation count. The base root H^(1/2) is NOT needed
as an input. The output is a factor for its correction, not the base root.
Explicit expansion adds O(N^2*r*polylog(r/eps)).

The exact rank statement below does not require a raw factor or randomness:
if A,B are PSD and rank(B-A)<=r, then for 0<alpha<1 there is a rank
O_alpha(r*log^2(1/eps)) approximation to B^alpha-A^alpha with spectral
error <=eps||B-A||^alpha. If B>=A, the approximant can be PSD.
Only alpha=1/2 is currently fully costed in the square-root/field model.

2. RESOLVENT INTEGRAL AND A UNIFORM LOW-RANK ENVELOPE
---------------------------------------------------
For PSD A,B and 0<alpha<1,

 B^alpha-A^alpha
   = c_alpha integral_0^infinity
        t^alpha [(A+tI)^(-1)-(B+tI)^(-1)] dt,
 c_alpha=sin(pi*alpha)/pi.

This follows from the scalar Stieltjes formula and spectral calculus;
subtracting the two formulas is justified by the bounds below. With
E=B-A and t>0,

 R_A(t)-R_B(t)=R_A(t) E R_B(t),
 rank(R_A(t)-R_B(t))<=r,
 ||R_A(t)-R_B(t)|| <= min(2/t,||E||/t^2).

For E>=0, the first bound improves to 1/t and every difference is PSD.
At t=a*exp(y), a=||E||>0, the integrand including dt becomes

 F(y)=c_alpha*(a*exp(y))^(alpha+1)*(R_A-R_B).

For |Im y|<=d<pi, both resolvents are analytic and

 ||F(y)|| <= C_(alpha,d)*a^alpha*
                    min(exp(alpha Re y),exp(-(1-alpha)Re y)).

To check the sector estimate, for lambda>=0 and |arg t|<=d,
|lambda+t|>=cos(d/2)*(lambda+|t|). The triangle bound and resolvent
identity therefore supply the two terms above, uniformly over the entire
spectra of A and B. In particular the L1 boundary norm on each strip edge
is O_(alpha,d)(a^alpha), independent of dimension, ||A||, or lambda_min.

The logarithmic trapezoid rule on the infinite line has error
O(a^alpha*exp(-2*pi*d/h)). This can be proved by applying a scalar
strip-contour estimate to u^*F(y)v for arbitrary unit u,v; its constant is
uniform, hence the resulting bound is in operator norm, not entrywise.
Truncating at y in [-K,K] has error
O_alpha(a^alpha*[exp(-alpha*K)+exp(-(1-alpha)*K)]).
Choose K=O_alpha(log(1/eps)), h=Theta_alpha(1/log(1/eps)); then q=O(log^2)
positive nodes suffice. Every node has rank at most r. This proves the
universal rank theorem, including singular PSD endpoints.

For alpha=1/2, the finite real formula is

 Delta_q=sum_j w_j D_j,
 D_j=(H+t_j I)^(-1)-(H+UU^T+t_j I)^(-1),
 w_j=(h/pi)*t_j^(3/2)>0.

Woodbury gives an actual rank-r PSD factor at every node:

 V_j=(H+t_j I)^(-1)U,
 S_j=I+U^T V_j,
 D_j=V_j S_j^(-1)V_j^T.

No base square root and no indefinite threshold inverse occur.

3. FINITE NODES, NORMALIZATION, AND NO NORM ORACLE
------------------------------------------------
For the raw algorithm take a=||U||_F^2, computed in O(Nr). If a=0 return
zero. Otherwise a/r<=||UU^T||<=a. Set an internal accuracy

    rho=eps/(C*sqrt(r))

for a sufficiently large absolute C. Scale by a and apply the preceding
uniform envelope, which only needs ||E||<=a. Its quadrature error is
O(rho*sqrt(a))<=eps||U||/constant after fixing C.

Use h=log(2)/L, L a power of two of order log(1/rho), and
t_j=a*2^(j/L), -LK<=j<=LK, with K=Theta(log(1/rho)). Choose K so
2^(-K/2)<=rho/C0. All nodes and t_j^(3/2) use positive scalar square
roots: form 2^(1/L) by log2(L) repeated roots, then multiply/divide along
the geometric sequence. The fixed constant log(2)/pi can either be in the
arithmetic constant field or approximated to relative O(rho) by ordinary
convergent scalar series in O(log(1/rho)) operations. A relative O(rho)
weight perturbation contributes O(rho sqrt(a)), by the same positive
envelope. No exp/log evaluation on an input-dependent large magnitude is
being treated as a unit-cost primitive.

4. RAW POSITIVE RESOLVENTS WITH AN EVEN NEUMANN TRUNCATION
-------------------------------------------------------
Let G=X^T X. A standard sparse OSE provides G0=L0^T L0 with

    (1-eta0)G0 <= G <= (1+eta0)G0, eta0=1/4,

in ~O(Nm+m^3) conventional work, with failure <=delta. Form only the
sketched Gram, not G. The event holds for every later pole.
For each t>0, set P=G0+tI and F=P^(-1/2)(G-G0)P^(-1/2), ||F||<=eta0.
Cholesky-factor P once, costing O(m^3) per pole. For an EVEN integer ell,
define the common symmetric inverse polynomial

 Q_ell=P^(-1/2)*sum_(k=0)^(ell-1)(-F)^k*P^(-1/2).

Its action on W=X^T U is obtained by ordinary preconditioned Richardson
or the equivalent Neumann recursion, with each G-action through X^T(XW).
No P^(-1/2) is explicitly required. Writing Q_*=(G+tI)^(-1), even ell
gives the exact Loewner bounds

    0 <= Q_*-Q_ell <= eta0^ell Q_*.

Indeed their difference is
P^(-1/2) F^ell (I+F)^(-1) P^(-1/2); F^ell is PSD and commutes with I+F.
The physical approximate inverse

    Rhat_t=t^(-1)(I-X Q_ell X^T)

then satisfies

    R_t <= Rhat_t <= R_t+(eta0^ell/t)I,
    ||Rhat_t|| <= (1+eta0^ell)/t,

because X Q_* X^T<=I. This both proves a uniform norm bound and ensures
Rhat_t is exactly symmetric PSD. A generic two-sided solve error would
not automatically ensure that property.

Compute Vhat_t=Rhat_t U using the raw factors; form
Shat_t=I+U^T Vhat_t, which is SPD; Cholesky-factor Shat_t=C_t C_t^T;
and return the node factor

    Z_t=sqrt(w_t)*Vhat_t*C_t^(-T).

The concatenation Z=[Z_t] is always an actual real PSD factor. No unknown
resolvent or correction is assumed available to the algorithm.

Let eta=eta0^ell<=1. With ||U||<=sqrt(a),

 ||Vhat-V||<=eta sqrt(a)/t, ||V||<=sqrt(a)/t,

Expanding Vhat Shat^(-1)Vhat^T - V S^(-1)V^T and applying the inverse

 ||Dhat_t-D_t|| <=3 eta*a/t^2 + eta*a^2/t^3.              (E1)

With s_j=t_j/a and w_j=(h/pi)t_j^(3/2), the combined error is at most

 C eta sqrt(a)*h*sum_j[ s_j^(-1/2)+s_j^(-3/2)]
   <=C' eta sqrt(a)*(rho^(-1)+rho^(-3)),

since s_min=Theta(rho^2) and h*sum of each geometric series is bounded
by a constant times its largest term. Thus eta<=c*rho^4 suffices for

ell=O(log(1/rho)), independently of ||H||, its nonzero condition number,
or a possible ambient nullspace. Combining with quadrature and coefficient
budgets gives the stated eps||U|| target after choosing constants.

8. IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS AND FAILED UPGRADES
-------------------------------------------
The normalization eps||U|| is not eps||Delta||. The latter cannot have a
dimension/condition-independent rank bound in general. A useful stress
family is a diagonal base whose square-root eigenvalues are separated
geometrically, with a rank-one update chosen so the linearized correction
has almost constant diagonal and tiny off-diagonal entries. Then the
correction can require high relative numerical rank while ||U|| is much
larger than ||Delta||. A rigorous finite version remains to be written if
that observation is used as a theorem; it is only a scoped sanity check
at this stage.

The theorem does not obtain a rank-r (1+theta)-relative approximation to
AB, or a sharp nonzero real spectral cutoff. It is a different actual
matrix-function problem where positivity puts all inverse systems outside
the spectrum. Whether the neighboring advance is sufficiently new/useful
for a standalone result remains an open assessment, not a reason to call
the original research goal complete.

9. MATCHING LOWER BOUND: LOG-SQUARED RANK IS NECESSARY
---------------------------------------------------
This is a new derivation in this search. Priority is NOT yet established.
It proves that the uniform update-norm theorem's rank dependence cannot
be improved from root-exponential to geometric, even for rank-one PSD
updates and positive-definite base matrices.

Fix an integer q>=1, k=q^2, h=1/q, and indices 0<=i,j<k. Put

 a_i=exp(h*i), P=diag(a_i), H=P^2,
 K_ij=2sqrt(a_i*a_j)/(a_i+a_j)=sech(h*(i-j)/2),
 d=exp(-pi^2*q)/(100q),
 u_i=sqrt(2d*a_i), E=u*u^T, C=d*K.

The letter d here is a positive scalar, not the ambient dimension.
P C+C P=E exactly. K is positive definite (a Cauchy kernel, also seen
from its strictly positive Fourier symbol below). Hence P+C is SPD and

    (P+C)^2=H+E+C^2.

Let Delta=(H+E)^(1/2)-P. Operator monotonicity gives

    0<=Delta<=C,
    C-Delta=(H+E+C^2)^(1/2)-(H+E)^(1/2).

Both square roots on the last line have smallest eigenvalue >=1. Their
difference solves the Sylvester equation with RHS C^2. Its semigroup
integral therefore proves

    ||C-Delta||<=||C||^2/2.                              (L1)

9.1 The positive Toeplitz symbol supplies a finite lower bound

The Fourier transform of sech(x/2) is 2pi*sech(pi*omega). Poisson
summation yields the absolutely convergent symbol

 f_h(theta)=sum_(j in Z) sech(h*j/2)*exp(i*j*theta)
    =(2pi/h)*sum_(ell in Z) sech(pi*(theta+2pi*ell)/h).

For every theta in [-pi,pi], the ell=0 term alone gives

    f_h(theta)>=(2pi/h)*exp(-pi^2/h).

The quadratic form of any finite principal Toeplitz section is the
integral of f_h(theta) times its trigonometric polynomial's squared
modulus. Parseval then gives

    lambda_min(K)>=2pi*q*exp(-pi^2*q).                  (L2)

A geometric-series row-sum estimate gives

 ||K||<=1+4/[exp(h/2)-1]<=9q.                           (L3)

This uses a fixed finite k=q^2 section. No asymptotic Toeplitz theorem,
unproved boundary correction, or infinite-dimensional eigenvalue limit
is needed.

9.2 Transfer to the true nonlinear square-root correction

By (L1)--(L3),

 lambda_min(Delta)
   >=2pi*q*d*exp(-pi^2*q)-(81/2)*q^2*d^2
   >=pi*q*d*exp(-pi^2*q),                              (L4)

because q*d=exp(-pi^2*q)/100 and 81/200<pi.
Also

 ||E||=||u||^2=2d*sum_i a_i
      =2d*[exp(h*k)-1]/[exp(h)-1]
      <=2d*q*exp(q).                                  (L5)

Combining (L4)--(L5),

 lambda_min(Delta)
    >= [pi/(10sqrt(2))] *
       exp(-[(3/2)pi^2+1/2]*q) * sqrt(||E||).           (L6)

Thus, for universal positive constants c,C, there is a k=q^2 dimensional
rank-one PSD update with EVERY singular value of its square-root
correction at least

    c*exp(-C*sqrt(k))*||E||^(1/2).

Every approximation of rank <k has error at least that quantity, by the
spectral best-rank theorem. Given sufficiently small eps, choose
q=floor(log(c/eps)/(2C)); then k=Theta(log^2(1/eps)) and (L6) is larger
than eps||E||^(1/2). This proves the necessary Omega(log^2(1/eps)) rank.
Direct sums of r identical blocks give rank(E)=r, unchanged ||E||, and
necessary rank Omega(r*log^2(1/eps)). The upper and lower dependencies
therefore match up to absolute constants for alpha=1/2.

The lower bound is about the required OUTPUT rank at the displayed
uniform error scale; it is not a query-complexity or arithmetic lower
bound. The explicit finite Cholesky/raw-product algorithm in Sections1--6
remains the constructive positive result.

10. A RIGOROUS FULL-RANK FAMILY FOR RELATIVE CORRECTION ERROR
---------------------------------------------------------
The earlier informal warning in Section8 can also be made finite and
elementary. Take k arbitrary, a_i=R^i, R=10^4, P=diag(a_i),
K_ij=2sqrt(a_i*a_j)/(a_i+a_j), and u_i=sqrt(2d*a_i) with d=1/100.
Then the sum of off-diagonal magnitudes in each K row is at most

    4/[sqrt(R)-1]<0.041,

so 0.959I<=K<=1.041I. The same C=dK, E=uu^T, H=P^2 construction and
(L1) give ||C-Delta||<=d^2*(1.041)^2/2<0.000055. Hence

    lambda_min(Delta)>0.00953,
    ||Delta||<0.01041.

Any rank<k approximation therefore has error >0.91||Delta||. This is a
rank-one PSD update of an SPD base, with an arbitrarily high-dimensional
correction requiring full rank for even a 10%-relative spectral error.
Consequently a dimension/condition-independent rank bound in r,eps alone
cannot hold when the scale is ||Delta|| rather than ||E||^(1/2).
Output-sensitive algorithms could still be possible, because this family
has large output numerical rank. This observation does not rule those out.

13. SHARP THEOREM FOR EVERY FRACTIONAL POWER
-----------------------------------------
The all-alpha theorem is now explicit, including the lower bound.
For each fixed alpha in (0,1), there are positive constants c_alpha,
C_alpha such that for all finite PSD A,B with rank(E)<=r, E=B-A,

 s_(j*r+1)(B^alpha-A^alpha)
       <=C_alpha*exp(-c_alpha*sqrt(j))*||E||^alpha,

with the obvious dimension caps. This is the upper bound of Section2,
reparameterized from eps to rank. The worst-case dependence on j is
optimal: there are rank-r PSD updates in dimension r*q^2 for which

 s_(r*q^2)(B^alpha-A^alpha)
       >=c'_alpha*exp(-C'_alpha*q)*||E||^alpha.

Thus the worst-case approximation rank at error eps||E||^alpha is
Theta_alpha(r*log^2(1/eps)), as eps decreases and ambient dimension is
allowed to grow. This is a statement about the whole class, not about
the best rank of every individual correction. PSD updates admit PSD
approximants. Both endpoints may be singular in the upper theorem.

13.1 Exact lower family and constants

Fix q>=1 integer, k=q^2, h=1/q, and 0<=i<k. Let

 a_i=exp(h*i), H=diag(a_i),
 v_i=alpha^(-1/2)*a_i^((1-alpha)/2), E=d*v*v^T.

The Frechet derivative of f(x)=x^alpha at H applied to E is d*K, where

 K_ij=K_alpha(h*(i-j)),
 K_alpha(s)=sinh(alpha*s/2)/(alpha*sinh(s/2)), K_alpha(0)=1.

Indeed divided differences multiply E_ij, and their factors in
(a_i*a_j)^((alpha-1)/2) cancel v_i*v_j exactly.
With the Fourier convention hatK(omega)=integral K(s)exp(-iomega*s)ds,

 hatK_alpha(omega)
    =(2pi/alpha)*sin(pi*alpha)/[cosh(2pi*omega)+cos(pi*alpha)].

This transform is strictly positive. It can be proved by residues or by
expanding sinh(alpha*s/2)/sinh(s/2) into geometric exponentials for
s>0 and summing the elementary Fourier integrals. The corresponding
standard transform pair is in DLMF Table1.14.1, with its 1/sqrt(2pi)
normalization converted to the convention here:
https://dlmf.nist.gov/1.14 .

Poisson summation and the same finite Toeplitz/Parseval argument as in
Section9 yield

 lambda_min(K)>=c0*q*exp(-2pi^2*q),
 c0=pi*sin(pi*alpha)/alpha.                             (A1)

For theta in [-pi,pi], keep the zero image in the positive Fourier
symbol and use cosh(2pi*theta/h)+cos(pi*alpha)
<=2exp(2pi^2/h). This proves the displayed explicit c0 without needing
a minimum-location claim for the periodic symbol.

Let Delta=(H+E)^alpha-H^alpha and L=Df(H)[E]=dK.
Operator monotonicity and operator concavity give 0<=Delta<=L.
There is a useful exact norm remainder bound, because H>=I:

 ||L-Delta||<=alpha*(1-alpha)*||E||^2/2.                 (A2)

Proof: with c=sin(pi*alpha)/pi and R_H(t)=(H+tI)^(-1),

 L-Delta=c integral_0^infinity
      t^alpha R_H(t) E R_H(t) E R_(H+E)(t) dt.

The norm is bounded by c||E||^2 integral t^alpha/(1+t)^3 dt.
The scalar beta integral is pi*alpha*(1-alpha)/(2sin(pi*alpha)).
Multiplication gives (A2). Individual integrands need not be symmetric;
Loewner nonnegativity of their total comes from operator concavity.

Set

 D0=1/[alpha*(1-alpha)], R0=1/[2alpha*(1-alpha)],
 d0=min(1,c0/(4R0)),
 d=(d0/q)*exp(-[2pi^2+2(1-alpha)]q).

The elementary geometric-series estimate gives

 ||E||=d||v||^2<=D0*d*q*exp((1-alpha)q).                (A3)

By (A2)--(A3),

 ||L-Delta||<=R0*d^2*q^2*exp(2(1-alpha)q)
             <=(c0/4)*d*q*exp(-2pi^2*q).

Combine with (A1) and Weyl to obtain

 lambda_min(Delta)>=(c0/2)*d*q*exp(-2pi^2*q).

Normalize using (A3):

 lambda_min(Delta)
   >=c1*exp(-C1*q)*||E||^alpha,
 c1=(c0/2)*D0^(-alpha)*d0^(1-alpha)>0,
 C1=(2-alpha)*[2pi^2+1-alpha].                          (A4)

Every step is finite and its constants depend only on fixed alpha.
Direct sums give the factor r in the necessary rank exactly as before.
Section9's specialized square-root proof has better constants, but (A4)
has the benefit of one uniform argument for all fractional powers.

13.2 Consequence for small Schatten exponents, and a priority caution

The upper singular-value bound implies, for fixed alpha and 0<s<=1,

 ||B^alpha-A^alpha||_(S_s)
   <=C_alpha*r^(1/s)*(C_alpha/s^2)^(1/s)*||E||^alpha.

This follows by summing exp(-c_alpha*s*sqrt(j)); its integral is
O_alpha(s^(-2)). Thus the logarithm of the small-s constant grows like
O_alpha(log(1/s)/s), not O(1/s^2).

Ricard2018 already proves membership and Holder bounds in ALL Schatten
classes, with unspecified constants C_(p,alpha). That is important prior

cutoff derivatives. No novelty conclusion follows merely from the rate

14. A GENERAL COMPLETE-BERNSTEIN COMPRESSION PRINCIPLE
----------------------------------------------------
This is an extension of the MATHEMATICAL approximation theorem; it does
not assume a generic cheap scalar-measure-moment oracle for an algorithm.
Its purpose is to isolate the actual property behind the uniform bound.

Let

 f(x)=c+b*x+f0(x),
 f0(x)=integral_(0,infinity) x/(x+t) mu(dt),
 c,b>=0, mu>=0, integral (1+t)^(-1)mu(dt)<infinity.

This is the standard complete-Bernstein / nonnegative operator-monotone
representation. The constants cancel in differences and the linear
correction bE has rank<=r exactly. We impose the following weak scaling
on f0: for some fixed C>=1 and 0<alpha<=beta<1, all a>0 satisfy

 f0(s*a)<=C*s^alpha*f0(a), 0<s<=1,
 f0(s*a)<=C*s^beta *f0(a), 1<=s<infinity.               (B1)

Then for all PSD A,B with E=B-A, rank(E)<=r and a=||E||>0, there is a
rank O_(C,alpha,beta)(r*log^2(1/eps)) correction approximation satisfying

 ||f(B)-f(A)-Ceps||<=eps*f0(a),

after adding the exact rank-r term bE. For B>=A, its nonlinear part is
PSD. The result includes singular endpoints when f(0) is interpreted by
continuity. Fractional powers and positive mixtures of powers with
exponents in a fixed compact subset of (0,1) satisfy (B1).

14.1 Tail truncation from growth, without analytic density assumptions

Write g(t)=t[(A+tI)^(-1)-(B+tI)^(-1)], so

 f0(B)-f0(A)=integral g(t)mu(dt),
 ||g(t)||<=min(2,a/t), rank(g(t))<=r.

For a PSD update the first constant is 1; using 2 only changes universal
budgets for general endpoints. Since l/(l+t)>=1/2 on t<=l,

 ||integral_(0,l) g(t)mu(dt)||<=2mu((0,l))<=4f0(l).

For t>=u, u/(u+t)>=u/(2t), so

 ||integral_(u,infinity)g(t)mu(dt)||
       <=a*integral_(u,infinity)mu(dt)/t<=2a*f0(u)/u.

By (B1), choose

 l=a*(eps/(C1*C))^(1/alpha),
 u=a*(C1*C/eps)^(1/(1-beta)),

to make both tails <=eps*f0(a)/4. The interval [l,u] has only
O_(C,alpha,beta)(log(1/eps)) dyadic subintervals.

14.2 Positive quadrature with exponentially small interval error

On one interval [v,2v], rescale t=v*z with z in [1,2]. The function g(vz)
is analytic in the Bernstein ellipse of any fixed parameter sufficiently
close to 1; parameter 2 works, since its leftmost real point is 7/8>0.
On that ellipse, both resolvents have norm O(1/v), so

 ||g(vz)||<=C2*min(1,a/v).

Standard polynomial approximation on this fixed ellipse has degree-d
error <=C3*2^(-d)*min(1,a/v), uniformly in A,B and dimension. Apply it
to u^*g(vz)w for arbitrary unit u,w; the bounds are uniform, hence in
operator norm. Gaussian quadrature for the positive finite measure mu
restricted to [v,2v] has positive weights, at most s nodes, and is exact
on scalar polynomials through degree 2s-1. Its matrix integration error
is therefore at most

 C4*2^(-2s)*mu([v,2v])*min(1,a/v).

This also covers finite-support measures: use their exact atoms when
fewer than s nodes exist. Disjointly allocate any atoms at interval
boundaries to one adjacent interval.

Summing interval errors does NOT incur an extra interval-count factor:

 sum_intervals mu([v,2v])*min(1,a/v)
       <=C5*integral min(1,a/t)mu(dt)<=2C5*f0(a).

Thus s=O(log(1/eps)) nodes per interval suffice, and every quadrature
term has rank<=r, with PSD sign for a PSD update. Counting intervals
proves the theorem. Within this growth-controlled class the log-squared
dependence is optimal in general, because fixed fractional powers are
members and Section13 supplies matching lower families.

14.3 Which part is an actual algorithm

For sqrt, all nodes/weights are explicit and Section4 gives the complete
raw-factor arithmetic implementation. The same is true for fixed dyadic
fractional powers, with constants depending on the exponent and with
positive scalar roots generated by repeated square roots. Positive finite
mixtures of these powers add their finite factors and error budgets.

For an arbitrary complete-Bernstein f described only abstractly, Gaussian
quadrature nodes/weights require the measure's scalar moments or another
effective scalar description. The mathematical compression theorem does
not make those data free. The matrix solve cost alone is not a complete
general-f algorithm, and is not presented as one here.

15. WHY THE PRINCIPLE DOES NOT EXTEND TO EVERY OPERATOR-MONOTONE f
----------------------------------------------------------------
There is a SINGLE fixed bounded complete-Bernstein function with only a
polynomial worst-case rank bound at its natural update scale. This rules
out a careless all-CBF root-exponential claim, even with constants allowed
to depend on that fixed f.

Take R=10^4 and

 f(x)=sum_(j>=0) (j+1)^(-2)*x/(x+R^(-j)).               (C1)

The representing measure is finite, of total mass pi^2/6, so (C1) is a
bounded complete-Bernstein function, continuous at zero by dominated
convergence. For each n, set

 lambda_i=R^(-i), 0<=i<n,
 A=diag(lambda_i), u_i=sqrt(lambda_i), E=u*u^T, B=A+E.

Here rank(E)=1 and a=||E||=sum lambda_i is in [1,R/(R-1)]. Every atom
contributes a PSD rank-one correction. Keep just the first n atoms to
obtain a Loewner lower bound on Delta=f(B)-f(A).
For t_j=lambda_j, write

 s_j=1+u^T(A+t_j I)^(-1)u
    =1+sum_i lambda_i/(lambda_i+lambda_j)
    <=j+2+1/(R-1)<=(2+1/(R-1))*(j+1).

The retained contribution is W W^T, with W=T*diag(gamma_j),

 T_ij=sqrt(lambda_i*lambda_j)/(lambda_i+lambda_j),
 gamma_j^2=1/[(j+1)^2*s_j]
          >=1/[(2+1/(R-1))*n^3].

The symmetric T has diagonal 1/2 and off-diagonal row sum at most
2/(sqrt(R)-1)<0.0203. Hence lambda_min(T)>0.4797 and

    Delta>=W W^T >=(0.11/n^3) I_n.                    (C2)

Also f(a)<=pi^2/6. Consequently, with eps_n=0.03/n^3,

    eps_n*f(||E||)<0.05/n^3<lambda_min(Delta).

Every rank<n approximation fails the target eps_n*f(||E||), whereas
log^2(1/eps_n)=O(log^2 n). Thus no bound O_f(log^2(1/eps)) can hold for
all updates for this fixed operator-monotone function. Its slow logarithmic
behavior near zero violates (B1)'s positive lower growth exponent.

This is a scope result paired with a constructive upper algorithm and
sharp fractional compression theorem, not a replacement of those results
by an obstruction. No priority claim is established for (C1)--(C2).

17. QUANTITATIVELY SHARP SMALL-p HOLDER CONSTANTS (ROOT CONSEQUENCE)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Root identified this stronger consequence of the upper/lower rank theorem;
the wildcard worker independently checked its telescoping and parameters.
It is a candidate quantitative refinement of Ricard's theorem, not a
priority-certified new result.

Fix alpha in (0,1). For 0<s<=1 let K_(s,alpha)^+ be the smallest constant,
uniform over ALL finite matrix dimensions and PSD A,B, such that

 ||B^alpha-A^alpha||_(S_s)
    <=K_(s,alpha)^+*||B-A||_(S_(alpha*s))^alpha.         (H1)

Then the constructions above prove

    c_alpha*s^(-2) <= (K_(s,alpha)^+)^s
                  <= C_alpha*s^(-2).                  (H2)

Equivalently

 log K_(s,alpha)^+ = (2/s)log(1/s)+O_alpha(1/s).

In Ricard's source-exponent notation p=alpha*s, this says
log C_(p,alpha)=(2alpha/p)log(1/p)+O_alpha(1/p).
The statement concerns fixed alpha and p decreasing to zero, not alpha
simultaneously approaching 0 or1, nor exact constants for fixed p.

17.1 Upper proof without a finite-rank promise

For a rank-one PSD update t*v*v^T with ||v||=1, Section13's upper bound
and its scalar integral imply

 sum_j s_j(Delta)^s <= C_alpha*s^(-2)*t^(alpha*s).

Indeed sum_(j>=0)exp(-c_alpha*s*sqrt(j))
<=1+2/(c_alpha^2*s^2). The prefactor C_alpha^s is uniformly bounded
above by max(1,C_alpha) for 0<s<=1.

For arbitrary PSD endpoints, E=B-A has spectral decomposition E=E+-E-.
Both paths

 A -> A+E+ = B+E- <- B

are sums of positive rank-one updates, so every intermediate matrix is
PSD. Telescope the two paths, and apply the Schatten s-power triangle
inequality ||sum_j T_j||_(S_s)^s<=sum_j||T_j||_(S_s)^s, valid for 0<s<=1.
The resulting sum of update magnitudes is EXACTLY

    sum_j |lambda_j(E)|^(alpha*s)
       =||E||_(S_(alpha*s))^(alpha*s).

This proves the upper bound in (H2), with no rank factor remaining.
There is no q-norm integration of the original continuum integral: the
argument first gets operator-norm singular-value decay, then sums scalar
singular values, then uses a FINITE telescope.

17.2 Matching lower proof

For the rank-one family of Section13, all q^2 output singular values are
at least c_alpha*exp(-C_alpha*q)*||E||^alpha. Thus its ratio in (H1),
raised to s, is at least

    q^2*c_alpha^s*exp(-C_alpha*s*q).

Choose q=floor(1/s). For all 0<s<=1, q>=1/(2s), s*q<=1, and
c_alpha^s>=min(1,c_alpha). This is at least c'_alpha*s^(-2).
The input update is rank one, so every Schatten norm of E equals ||E||.
No additional norm conversion or dimension-dependent loss occurs.

18. ROOT'S FULL SELF-ADJOINT EXTENSION: INDEPENDENT CONSTANT CHECK
----------------------------------------------------------------
The same quantitative constant order holds for BOTH Ricard maps

    F_even(H)=|H|^alpha,
    F_odd(H)=sgn(H)|H|^alpha,

for arbitrary finite self-adjoint H,K. Root owns the main extension;
the identities and rank/envelope details below were independently checked.

The exact scalar/matrix integrals are

 |H|^alpha
   =[2sin(pi*alpha/2)/pi]*integral_0^infinity
                      t^(alpha-1)*H^2/(H^2+t^2I) dt,

 sgn(H)|H|^alpha
   =[2cos(pi*alpha/2)/pi]*integral_0^infinity
                      t^alpha*H/(H^2+t^2I) dt.

For E=H-K and R_H(z)=(H-zI)^(-1), define

 D_even(t)=H^2/(H^2+t^2I)-K^2/(K^2+t^2I),
 D_odd(t)=t*H/(H^2+t^2I)-t*K/(K^2+t^2I).

The correct first-order-resolvent identities are

 D_even(t)=-t/(2i)*{[R_H(it)-R_K(it)]
                              -[R_H(-it)-R_K(-it)]},
 D_odd(t)=t/2*{[R_H(it)-R_K(it)]
                              +[R_H(-it)-R_K(-it)]}.

Thus both differences have rank<=2rank(E) and, for real t>0,

    ||D(t)||<=min(1,||E||/t).

For the first bound, H^2/(H^2+t^2I) is a PSD contraction, while
tH/(H^2+t^2I) has spectrum in [-1/2,1/2]. For the second, each
resolvent difference is bounded by ||E||/t^2. When t=exp(y) is complex
with |Im y|<=d<pi/2, all bounds gain only a constant depending on d,
because both poles +/-it stay at distance >=|t|cos(d) from the real axis.

After the logarithmic substitution, BOTH integrands are t^alpha D(t),
with uniform envelope min(t^alpha,||E||t^(alpha-1)) and an analytic strip
of fixed width. Hence the same root-exponential low-rank upper theorem
holds, with the factor2 in rank absorbed into constants.

For arbitrary Hermitian E, telescope its ordinary signed rank-one
spectral decomposition along an arbitrary Hermitian path; PSD intermediates
are unnecessary for these two functions. The same s-power triangle
inequality proves their quantitative Holder constants satisfy (H2).
The PSD lower family is simultaneously a lower family for both maps.

For a rectangular T, its self-adjoint dilation is

    D(T)=[0 T; T^* 0].

The odd functional calculus has off-diagonal block U|T|^alpha, where
T=U|T| is the polar decomposition. Both input and output Schatten power
sums double in the dilation, so the factors2 cancel exactly. Thus the
non-selfadjoint homogeneous polar Mazur map has the same Theta_alpha(s^-2)
order for the best constant raised to s. The even dilation also gives
an upper bound for T->|T|^alpha with at most a fixed factor2 at power-s
level; the PSD family again supplies the matching lower order.

The exact sharp leading coefficient of s^-2 is NOT computed. What is
claimed mathematically is matching order and the sharp coefficient2 of
log(1/s)/s in log K, with constants depending on fixed alpha.

19. SEMIFINITE LIFT VIA TRACE-SUPPORT RANK, WITHOUT MATRIX EMBEDDING
------------------------------------------------------------------
This is a useful scope check and a constructive proof route. A uniform
finite-matrix inequality alone must not be assumed to imply the same
inequality for every von Neumann algebra. The following direct lift avoids
that inference for semifinite algebras.

Let (M,tau) be semifinite and define tau-rank(T)=tau(support(|T|)) for
bounded T in M, with the equivalent left-range trace. If P is a projection
of finite trace r, then tau-rank(X P Y)<=r for bounded X,Y in M. Supports
of sums have trace at most the sum of the support traces.

For a perturbation E=tP, every positive/imaginary shifted-resolvent
difference in Sections2/18 therefore has tau-rank<=r or2r, respectively.
The q-node quadrature approximant has tau-rank<=q*r or2q*r and the SAME
operator-norm error, whose proof uses only functional calculus and scalar
uniform resolvent bounds. No finite-dimensional diagonalization is used
by that error proof.

The generalized singular value mu(u;Delta) is bounded by the norm error
of any approximant with tau-rank<=u. It consequently obeys

 mu(u;Delta)<=C_alpha*|t|^alpha*exp(-c_alpha*sqrt(u/r)),

with harmless changes for small u/r. Integrating its s-th power over
u>=0 gives

 ||Delta||_(L_s(M,tau))^s
       <=C'_alpha*s^(-2)*tau(P)*|t|^(alpha*s).

For finite-simple self-adjoint E=sum_j t_j P_j with disjoint finite-trace
projections, telescope the perturbations t_jP_j along a self-adjoint path.
The s-power triangle and the last bound yield the exact quantity

 sum_j tau(P_j)*|t_j|^(alpha*s)
       =||E||_(L_(alpha*s)(M,tau))^(alpha*s).

Thus the same O_alpha(s^-2) power constant holds for every finite-simple
perturbation in every semifinite algebra. The PSD lower family already
supplies the matching lower bound, since finite matrix algebras are
semifinite examples.

The standard completion step takes bounded finite-simple approximants
E_n to E in L_(alpha*s), forms B+E_n, uses continuity of continuous
self-adjoint functional calculus in the local measure topology, and
Fatou for L_s. When endpoints are unbounded, B+E_n is self-adjoint because
E_n is bounded; identification of its measure limit with A uses E=A-B
in the algebra of tau-measurable operators. A final theorem should cite
the exact standard measure-continuity/Fatou facts, or state the bounded
case first and prove this approximation lemma separately.

The route uses finite-simple spectral perturbations, NOT a dyadic layer
cake estimate that would accidentally incur another 1/s factor. It does
not establish the analogous claim for general typeIII/Haagerup Lp-spaces;
that needs its own reduction. The finite-matrix theorem is already complete
without this optional extension.

 Delta >= (1/2)*D(x^alpha)(H)[E]

 lambda_min[K_alpha(h*(i-j))]

 lambda_min Delta

 lambda_min Delta >= c_alpha*exp(-C_alpha*sqrt(k)),

22. EXACT REDUCTION OF THE PSD SCHATTEN CONSTANT TO RANK ONE
-----------------------------------------------------------
For0<s<=1 let

 R_(s,alpha)=sup Tr[((H+vv^*)^alpha-H^alpha)^s],

where the supremum runs over every finite dimension, H>=0, and ||v||=1.
Then the optimal full PSD Holder constant satisfies EXACTLY

 (K^+_(s,alpha))^s=R_(s,alpha).

The >= direction is the rankone example. For the reverse, diagonalize
E=B-A, split positive and negative parts, and join the two increasing
paths A->A+E+=B+E-<-B. Split each positive part into orthogonal
rankone terms t_j u_j u_j^*. Homogeneity bounds every increment's
S_s-power by R_(s,alpha)*t_j^(alpha*s). The exact S_s-power triangle
inequality then gives R_(s,alpha)*Tr|E|^(alpha*s), with no dimension or
number-of-summands penalty. No finite-rank approximation is involved in
this equality. This reduction may be established methodology; it is
recorded for precision, not asserted as new.

24. STRONGER EXPLICIT SMALL-s LOWER COEFFICIENT, ENTIRELY FINITE
----------------------------------------------------------------
The following argument improves the coefficient in Section21 by exp(2).
It uses two classical trace/concavity inequalities and no infinite-rank
completion or asymptotic Hankel theorem. Priority of the consequence is
not certified.

Notation: beta=1-alpha, K=K_alpha as in Section21,

 phi_h(theta)=sum_(j in Z) K(h*j)*exp(i*j*theta)>0,
 hatK(xi)=(2*pi/alpha)*sin(pi*alpha)
                   /[cosh(2*pi*xi)+cos(pi*alpha)].

Poisson summation gives phi_h(theta)>=h^(-1)*hatK(theta/h) on[-pi,pi].

Fixh,gamma>0 and a positive integerk. Take H_i=exp(h*i),

 v_i=sqrt(c)*exp(-gamma*h*i/2),
 c=(1-exp(-gamma*h))/(1-exp(-gamma*h*k)),  i=0,...,k-1.

Thus ||v||=1 and, putting W_i=exp[-(beta+gamma)*h*i/2], the Frechet
derivative is L=alpha*c*W*T_k(phi_h)*W. The actual correction satisfies
Delta>=L/2 by the denominator comparison in Section21.

For0<s<1, the Araki--Lieb--Thirring inequality gives

 Tr[(W*T_k(phi_h)*W)^s]
       >=Tr[W^(2s)*T_k(phi_h)^s].

The finite Toeplitz map T_k is unital completely positive, and x^s is
operator concave, so Jensen's inequality gives

 T_k(phi_h)^s >=T_k(phi_h^s).

Every diagonal entry on the right equals
J_h,s=(1/(2*pi))*integral_(-pi)^pi phi_h(theta)^s dtheta. Therefore

 Tr Delta^s >= (alpha*c/2)^s
       *[1-exp(-(beta+gamma)*h*k*s)]
       /[1-exp(-(beta+gamma)*h*s)] * J_h,s,

 J_h,s >= h^(1-s)/(2*pi)
            *integral_(-pi/h)^(pi/h) hatK(xi)^s dxi.

This is an explicit finite-matrix lower bound for every choice of the
parameters. Now let s->0 and set

 gamma=beta*s/(1-s), h=s^2, k=ceil(s^(-5)).

The two finite geometric-tail numerators tend1; c/(gamma*h)->1;
the denominator divided by(beta+gamma)*h*s tends1. The explicit Fourier
formula has logarithmic decay log hatK(xi)=-2*pi*|xi|+O_alpha(1), so

 s*integral_(-pi/h)^(pi/h) hatK(xi)^s dxi ->1/pi.

This last limit follows directly by z=s*xi and exponential dominated
convergence; the cutoff pi*s/h=pi/s tendsinfinity. Finally gamma^s->1.
Combining all factors proves

 liminf_(s->0) s^2*(K^+_(s,alpha))^s
       >=1/[2*pi^2*(1-alpha)].

The dimension k grows only polynomially in1/s. H is a finite positive
diagonal matrix and E=vv^* has norm1 and rankone in every instance.

A possible exact coefficient1/[2*pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)] remains ONLY a
phase-space heuristic, requiring a genuinely two-sided log-spectral
family and a matching upper. The displayed one-sided lower is proved.

Primary trace-inequality comparator: Audenaert, On the Araki--Lieb--
Thirring inequality, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0701129 , Theorem1 records
Tr[A^r B^r A^r]<=Tr[(ABA)^r] for0<r<=1. Use A=W,B=T_k(phi_h),r=s.

25. TWO-BLOCK LOWER: FULL CANDIDATE LEADING COEFFICIENT
-----------------------------------------------------
The low- and high-scale log spectra can contribute simultaneously even
for ONE positive rankone perturbation. The following estimates are finite
and use classical ALT, operator Jensen, and the exact Schatten-power
triangle. Constants below depend only on fixedalpha.

Setbeta=1-alpha, sigma=1/4, h=s^2, k=ceil(s^(-5)), and let

 H_low,i=sigma*exp(-h*i),
 H_high,i=sigma^(-1)*exp(h*i),  i=0,...,k-1.

Take H=diag(H_low,H_high) and v=(v_low,v_high), where both vector
blocks have squared norm1/2. Their squared entries are proportional to

 |v_low,i|^2=(c_low/2)*exp(-gamma*h*i),
 gamma=(1+alpha)/2,
 |v_high,i|^2=(c_high/2)*exp(-gamma_s*h*i),
 gamma_s=beta*s/(1-s),

with c_u=(1-exp(-u*h))/(1-exp(-u*h*k)). Thus E=vv^* has rankone and norm1.
Write m(t)=v^*(H+t)^(-1)v and use the exact correction integral with
the denominator1+m(t).

LOW BLOCK. For0<t<=1, m(t)<=C_alpha*t^(gamma-1). Indeed the high block
contributes at most1/8. For the low block, c_low<=2*gamma*h for smalls,
and the geometric sum is bounded uniformly by a constant times

 integral_0^infinity exp(-gamma*x)/(sigma*exp(-x)+t) dx
 <=sigma^(-gamma)*t^(gamma-1)*pi/sin(pi*gamma).

For the sum-to-integral comparison, on each x in[hi,h(i+1)], the value
at hi is at most exp(gamma*h) times the value atx. Since t^(gamma-1)>=1,
also1+m(t)<=C'_alpha*t^(gamma-1). Hence the low diagonal block of the
actual correction dominates a fixed positive constant times

 Q_low=integral_0^1 t^gamma R_low(t) E_low R_low(t) dt,

because alpha+1-gamma=gamma. Extending to0..infinity gives a constant
multiple of D(x^gamma)(H_low)[E_low]. Its exact matrix is

 [gamma*c_low*sigma^(gamma-1)/2]
     *W_low*T_k(phi_(gamma,h))*W_low,
 (W_low)_i=exp(-alpha*h*i/2),

because2*gamma-1=alpha. ALT/Jensen as in Section24 and the explicit
Fourier-symbol tail give

 liminf s^2*Tr[(full low integral)^s]>=1/(2*pi^2*alpha),

where fixed positive scalar factors disappear after taking power-s.
The discarded t>=1 integral has geometric singular-value decay uniformly
in h,k: expand R_low(t)v_low in powers of H_low/t, whose norm<=1/4.
Projection onto span{v_low,H_low*v_low,...,H_low^(l-1)*v_low} gives rankl
error<=C_alpha*4^(-l). Thus its S_s-power is O_alpha(1/s). Since
full=Q_low+tail, the exact s-power triangle gives the same leading lower
coefficient for the actual low block.

HIGH BLOCK. For t>=1, m(t)<=1/t<=1, so the high actual block dominates
one-half the t>=1 part of its Frechet derivative for x^alpha. Section24's
geometric weights with gamma_s supply coefficient1/(2*pi^2*beta).
The omitted t<=1 Frechet integral again has rankl error C_alpha*4^(-l),
this time by expanding R_high(t) in powers of t*H_high^(-1). Its
S_s-power is O_alpha(1/s), so it does not affect the leading coefficient.

CROSS BLOCK. LetX be the off-diagonal block of the actual correction.
For t<=1, expand R_high(t)v_high in powers of t/H_high. For t>=1,
expand R_low(t)v_low in powers of H_low/t. Keeping l terms per piece
gives rank at most2l and error<=C_alpha*4^(-l): the integrable norm
envelopes are t^(alpha-1) for0<t<=1 and t^(alpha-2) for t>=1. The scalar
factor(1+m(t))^(-1)<=1 only helps. Hence

 ||X||_(S_s)^s=O_alpha(1/s).

LetD be the block diagonal part of Delta andF=[[0,X],[X^*,0]]. Since
D=Delta-F, exact s-power subadditivity yields

 Tr Delta^s >=Tr D^s-||F||_(S_s)^s
           =Tr Delta_low^s+Tr Delta_high^s-O_alpha(1/s).

Therefore the finite family establishes

 liminf_(s->0) s^2*(K^+_(s,alpha))^s
       >=(1/(2*pi^2))*(1/alpha+1/(1-alpha))
        =1/[2*pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)].

This improves the one-sided Section24 lower. Every reduction and constant
order is explicit; an independent review is requested before promoting
this to the main theorem.

26. GAUSSIAN LOCALIZATION GIVES A SHARP TAPERED-QUADRATURE UPPER
--------------------------------------------------------------
This is a constructive proof plan with full parameter accounting, still
awaiting independent review and priority audit. It improves the uniform
sinc node-count leading constant by a factor2. The hypotheses below
are satisfied uniformly by the normalized positive rankone power-update
log integrand from Section2.

ABSTRACT LEMMA. Suppose F is analytic on |Imz|<pi with values in finite
matrices, rank F(y)<=r for realy, and for every fixed0<d<pi,

 ||F(x+iu)||<=M_d*exp(-v(x)),  |u|<=d,
 v(x)=alpha*(-x)_+ + beta*x_+, alpha,beta>0.

Then for largeL, integral_R F(y)dy has an approximation by positive
weighted real samples with error<=exp(-L), using at most

 r*[L^2/(4*pi*d)*(1/alpha+1/beta)+o_(alpha,beta,d)(L^2)]

rank. If all samplesF(y) are PSD, the approximation is PSD. Letting
d increase topi gives limiting rank coefficient
(1/alpha+1/beta)/(4*pi^2).

CONSTRUCTION. Set ell=L^(3/4), w=L^(1/8), b=2*w*sqrt(L). PartitionR into
intervals I_j=[j*ell,(j+1)*ell]. Smooth each indicator by the normalized
Gaussian

 phi_j(z)=(1/(sqrt(pi)*w))*integral_(I_j) exp(-(z-u)^2/w^2) du.

For realy, phi_j(y)>=0 and sum_(j in Z)phi_j(y)=1. These entire functions
obey, for |Imz|<=d,

 |phi_j(x+iu)|<=exp(d^2/w^2)*phi_j(x).

Letm_j=min_(I_j)v. The Lipschitz constant ofv is fixed, so the boundary
L1 norm of the localized integrand F_j=phi_j*F is

 M_j<=C_d*ell*exp[-m_j+O_(alpha,beta)(w^2)].

Proof: v(x)>=v(u)-kappa*|x-u| and integrate the shifted Gaussian. Its
exponential moment is at most2*exp(kappa^2*w^2/4). No dimension enters.

Retain every interval meeting
[-(L+ell)/alpha,(L+ell)/beta]. Their union contains that interval and
has only O(L/ell) pieces. Omitted-piece integrals sum to at most
C*exp[-L-ell+O(w^2)], by the same Gaussian exponential-moment bound
applied to the two tails of exp(-v). Thus this error is o(exp(-L)).

For each retainedj put

 Q_j=max(ell,L-m_j+3*ell), h_j=2*pi*d/Q_j.

The infinite trapezoid on F_j has error at most
2*M_j/(exp(Q_j)-1), by the strip theorem. Since m_j+Q_j>=L+3*ell,
the sum of all these errors is o(exp(-L)). (Fixed constants in the
O(w^2) term are absorbed by3*ell because w^2=o(ell).)

Keep only lattice nodes in I_j+[-b,b]. Since||F|| is globally bounded,
the discarded lattice Gaussian tail has norm at most
poly(L)*exp[-b^2/(2*w^2)]=poly(L)*exp(-2L). Hereh_j=O(1/ell)<<w, so
the elementary lattice Gaussian tail bound is uniform. Summing all
pieces still gives o(exp(-L)). Small additional constant shifts ofL
can make the total error exactly<=exp(-L) for every sufficientlylargeL.

COUNT. The number of retained samples is at most

 sum_j[(ell+2*b)*Q_j/(2*pi*d)+2].

Since b=o(ell), ell=o(L), and there are O(L/ell) pieces, the leading
term is the Riemann sum for

 (1/(2*pi*d))*integral_R (L-v(x))_+ dx
   =L^2/(4*pi*d)*(1/alpha+1/beta).

The Riemann-sum and +3*ell overheads are O(L*ell)=o(L^2). The b/ell
overhead is also o(L^2). Every coefficient is h_j*phi_j(realnode)>=0.

APPLICATION. For a norm1 positive rankone update, Section2's normalized
power integrand satisfies the abstract lemma with beta=1-alpha and
rankr=1. Thus, uniformly over dimensions and PSD endpoints,

 #{j:s_j(Delta)>exp(-L)}
    <=L^2/[4*pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)]+o_alpha(L^2).

For precision, the d<pi estimate is uniform first; take limsup asL->infty
and thend->pi. Layer-cake integration

 Tr Delta^s=s*integral_0^infinity lambda^(s-1)*N_Delta(lambda) dlambda

and the uniform counting bound yield

 limsup_(s->0) s^2*(K^+_(s,alpha))^s
    <=1/[2*pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)].

For the last step, use the exact rankone reduction in Section22 and
the uniform norm bound forDelta; any bounded initial L-range contributes
o(s^-2). The integral s*integral_0^infinity exp(-sL)*L^2 dL=2/s^2.

Together with Section25, this would prove the precise asymptotic

 lim_(s->0) s^2*(K^+_(s,alpha))^s
       =1/[2*pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)].

The proof has NOT yet been independently reviewed, and no priority
claim is attached to this much stronger candidate. Ordinary uniform
sinc alone does not prove this leading coefficient.

27. COMPLETE FIXED-WIDTH GAUSSIAN LEMMA (PREFERRED UPPER PROOF)
--------------------------------------------------------------
This replaces Section26's unnecessary growing smoothing width. All
constants below are uniform in matrix dimensions and spectra whenever
M_d is. The Gaussian width is exactly1.

LEMMA. Let alpha,beta>0, v(x)=alpha*(-x)_++beta*x_+, kappa=max(alpha,beta).
Let F be matrix-valued and analytic in |Imz|<pi. Suppose that for every
fixed d in(0,pi) one has, uniformly in |u|<=d,

 ||F(x+iu)||<=M_d exp(-v(x)).

Suppose rank F(x)<=r for realx. For every fixed d<pi and all sufficiently
largeL, there are real nodesx_n and positive weightsa_n such that

 ||integral_R F(x)dx-sum_n a_n F(x_n)||<=exp(-L),

 #nodes <= [1/(4*pi*d)]*(1/alpha+1/beta)*L^2
                   +O_(alpha,beta,d)(L^(7/4)).

The thresholdL_0 may depend onM_d. The O constant in node count does not
need to depend onF. Thus approximation rank is at most r times this
count. PSD values give a PSD approximant.

PROOF. Put ell=L^(3/4), b=2*sqrt(L), I_j=[j*ell,(j+1)*ell], and

 phi_j(z)=pi^(-1/2)*integral_(I_j) exp(-(z-u)^2)du.

For realx, phi_j(x)>=0 and sum_j phi_j(x)=1. For |u|<=d,
|phi_j(x+iu)|<=exp(d^2)*phi_j(x). Also

 integral_R pi^(-1/2)*exp(-z^2+kappa*|z|)dz
       <=2*exp(kappa^2/4).

Sincev(x)>=v(u)-kappa*|x-u|, ifm_j=min_(I_j)v, the boundary L1 norms
ofF_j=phi_j F are bounded by

 integral_R ||F_j(x+id)||dx,
 integral_R ||F_j(x-id)||dx
       <=C_0*ell*exp(-m_j),
 C_0=2*M_d*exp(d^2+kappa^2/4).

LetJ contain precisely the integerj whoseI_j meet
T=[-(L+ell)/alpha,(L+ell)/beta]. The unionU of these intervals is a
single interval containingT. There are J_count=O(L/ell) suchj. On the
real line, 1-sum_(j in J)phi_j(x) is the Gaussian convolution of1_(R\U).
Tonelli and the same exponential-moment bound give

 ||integral_R F-sum_(j in J)integral_R F_j||
 <=2*M_d*exp(kappa^2/4)*integral_(R\U) exp(-v(u))du
 <=2*M_d*exp(kappa^2/4)*(1/alpha+1/beta)*exp(-L-ell).

Thus there is no artificial discontinuous cutoff of the analytic F_j.

For each j inJ define

 Q_j=max(ell,L-m_j+3*ell), h_j=2*pi*d/Q_j.

One always hasm_j+Q_j>=L+3*ell. The Banach-valued strip trapezoid
bound, obtained by scalar duality from the usual contour proof, is

 ||integral_R F_j-h_j*sum_(n in Z)F_j(n*h_j)||
      <=2*C_0*ell*exp(-m_j)/(exp(Q_j)-1).

ForlargeL, exp(Q_j)-1>=exp(Q_j)/2. Summing therefore gives at most
C*J_count*ell*exp(-L-3*ell)=O(L*exp(-L-3*ell)).

Now retain only the lattice nodes n*h_j in I_j+[-b,b]. For any realx,

 phi_j(x)<=ell*pi^(-1/2)*exp[-dist(x,I_j)^2],
 ||F(x)||<=M_d.

Alsoh_j<=2*pi*d/ell. ForlargeL, h_j<=1 and b-h_j>=b/sqrt(2). Monotonic
Gaussian-tail comparison gives on either side ofI_j

 h_j*sum_(nodes outside)exp[-dist(node,I_j)^2]
      <=h_j*exp(-b^2)+integral_(b-h_j)^infinity exp(-z^2)dz
      <=C*exp(-b^2/2)=C*exp(-2L).

Consequently total discarded lattice mass over allj is at most
C*M_d*J_count*ell*exp(-2L)=O(L*exp(-2L)). The omitted interval, infinite
trapezoid and lattice-tail errors sum to less thanexp(-L) forlargeL.
Every retained weight h_j*phi_j(n*h_j) is nonnegative (strictly positive
for finite nodes), so positive sampling is preserved.

Finally the retained node count is at most

 sum_(j in J)[(ell+2*b)*Q_j/(2*pi*d)+2].

The elementary bound Q_j<=(L-m_j)_++3*ell holds for everyj. The function
g(x)=(L-v(x))_+ is kappa-Lipschitz. Since (L-m_j)_+=max_(I_j)g,

 ell*sum_(j in J)(L-m_j)_+
      <=integral_R g(x)dx+kappa*J_count*ell^2
      =L^2/2*(1/alpha+1/beta)+O(L*ell).

The +3*ell part adds only O(J_count*ell^2)=O(L*ell). Multiplication by
1+2*b/ell adds an error O(L^2*b/ell). Sinceell=L^(3/4), b=2*L^(1/2),
both errors are O(L^(7/4)). The O(J_count) rounding cost is smaller.
This proves the stated count and lemma.

APPLICATION AND UNIFORM SUPREMUM. For a norm1 positive rankone update,
the exact power log-integrand obeys the lemma with beta=1-alpha, rank1,
and uniformM_d from the sector-resolvent bound. Also0<=Delta<=I:
E<=I implies (H+E)^alpha<= (H+I)^alpha<=H^alpha+I by scalar calculus
forH and operator monotonicity. Thus its counting function is supported
onlambda<=1. For fixed d and allL>=L_0(alpha,d), uniformly,

 N_Delta(exp(-L))<=c_d*L^2+C_d*L^(7/4),
 c_d=1/[4*pi*d*alpha*(1-alpha)].

For0<=L<L_0, use the finite bound atL_0. Therefore

 Tr Delta^s=s*integral_0^infinity exp(-sL)*N_Delta(exp(-L))dL
      <=2*c_d/s^2+O_(alpha,d)(s^(-7/4))+O_(alpha,d)(1).

This estimate holds UNIFORMLY over finite dimensions andH, so taking
the supremum in Section22 is legitimate. Multiplying bys^2, letting
s->0 first, and then lettingd->pi proves

 limsup s^2*(K^+_(s,alpha))^s<=1/[2*pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)].

Together with the finite lower in Section25, the exact leading limit
follows if both proofs pass independent review. This is an audit-ready
statement, not a priority claim.

28. EXACT WORST-CASE COMPRESSION COEFFICIENT FROM THE MOMENT LIMIT
----------------------------------------------------------------
This consequence requires no separate Widom spectral asymptotic. It uses
the sharp Schatten-power limit and the UNIFORM rank counting upper in
Section27. It is offered for root/referee review, not yet incorporated
in the main statement.

Define N_*(L) as the supremum, over all PSDH and unitv in every finite
dimension, of #{eigenvalues ofDelta=(H+vv^*)^alpha-H^alpha > exp(-L)}.
Put c=1/[4*pi^2*alpha*(1-alpha)]. Then the sharp moment theorem implies

 N_*(L)/L^2 ->c  asL->infinity.

PROOF OF THE ONLY NONTRIVIAL DIRECTION. For each s choose a finite
exampleDelta_s whose S_s-power is within o(s^-2) of the supremum. Let
N_s be its counting function and n_s(x)=s^2*N_s(x/s), x>=0. The sharp
moment limit says

 integral_0^infinity exp(-x)*n_s(x)dx ->2*c.

For any fixed d<pi, the uniform Gaussian count bound gives

 n_s(x)<=c_d*x^2+C_d*s^(1/4)*x^(7/4)+O_d(s^2),
 c_d=1/[4*pi*d*alpha*(1-alpha)].

Use this first to control all integrals, then letd->pi. In particular
the integral deficit relative toc*x^2 tends0, up to arbitrarily small
positive slack fromc_d-c. Suppose for some x0>0 and fixedeta>0 there
were a subsequence with n_s(x0)<=c*x0^2-eta. Since n_s is nondecreasing,
for x in[x0-eps,x0] one has the same upper bound; chooseeps>0 small
enough that c*x^2>=c*x0^2-eta/2 throughout that interval. Hence there
is a deficit at leasteta/2 on an interval of positive length. Away
from that interval, the uniform upper bound permits at most
(c_d-c)*x^2+o(1) excess, whose weighted integral can be made smaller
than half this positive deficit by takingd sufficientlynearpi. This
contradicts the moment limit. Therefore n_s(x0)->c*x0^2 for everyx0>0.
Takingx0=1 ands=1/L gives N_*(L)>=N_s(L)=(c-o(1))*L^2. The matching
upper is Section27 with d->pi.

Thus the minimal universal spectral approximation rank, even allowing
arbitrary approximants, has the exact leading coefficientc. For any
fixed positive integer r, direct sums ofr rankone examples give the
lower boundr*c, and the rank-r Gaussian sample construction gives the
matching upper. The threshold convention > versus>= can be absorbed
by an arbitrarily small shift inL.

This is a worst-case supremum over examples. It does NOT assert the
same eigenvalue asymptotic for every fixed update or for the raw-factor
runtime. The original relative product-LRA and broad N^2 goals remain
open.
