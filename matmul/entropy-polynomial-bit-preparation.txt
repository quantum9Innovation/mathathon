1. THEOREM, INPUT MODEL AND MEANING OF THE COMPLEXITY
==================================================

Let b>=1 be an integer accuracy parameter, eta=2^(-b), and

  f(x)=x log(x)-x+1,       f_2(x)=(x-1)^2/2, x>0.

THEOREM. A deterministic algorithm using ordinary integer/rational bit
operations returns positive rational t_i,c_i, i=1,...,m, and the rational
number e=eta/8, such that

  R_0(x)=sum_(i=1)^m c_i/(x+t_i),
  f^-(x)=(x-1)^2 R_0(x)/(1+e),
  f^+(x)=f^-(x)+eta f_2(x),

  0<=f^-(x)<=f(x)<=f^+(x)                 for ALL x>0.       (1.1)

Every output rational has polynomially many bits in b. The number of
bit operations, and total output length, are bounded by a fixed
polynomial in b. The poles are distinct and strictly negative, -t_i.
Moreover, as b tends to infinity,

  m <= log^2(1/eta)/(2*pi^2)+O(log(1/eta)),                 (1.2)

where the logarithm is natural and the implied constant is absolute.
The procedure uses m=2n+1; it need not return the smallest possible m.

Here 'polynomial in b' is precision complexity: b is the requested
number of accuracy bits, equivalently the accuracy input 2^(-b) has
O(b) bits. If b itself is encoded in binary, no claim of a runtime
polynomial in log(b) is made. For an arbitrary rational eta, include
its FULL numerator/denominator encoding length in the complexity.

The same rational data give entropy lower/upper semidefinite lifts
with respectively 2m and 2m+1 blocks of order 2N. Their pointwise
bracket width is EXACTLY eta*chi_P^2/2. This transfers (1.1) by the
parent's nonnegative Petz spectral formula and small-block lift.
No generic SDP optimization runtime, input-independent absolute error,
finite-word matrix stability, or matrix N^2 algorithm follows.

All transcendental scalar calculations and all root isolation below
are paid for. Only entropy is covered: the note does NOT extend its
moment formulas to arbitrary noninteger f_a or assert a uniform
bit theorem for all operator-convex functions.

2. CLASSICAL INPUTS USED, WITHOUT A ROOT ORACLE
============================================

(i) The parent paper's balanced n_-=n_+=n, a=1,b=2 positive Gaussian
construction. For ANY positive interpolation nodes a_j in (0,1), use
the same nodes in the lower and upper tails, paired as a_j and 1/a_j.
Write

  B(s)=prod_(j=1)^n (s-a_j)/(s+a_j),
  M=max_(0<=s<=1) s B(s)^4.

The parent's global comparison gives an ideal m=2n+1 positive
Stieltjes rule R satisfying

 |(x-1)^2 R(x)-f(x)| <= (2*pi+4) M f_2(x).                (2.1)

Its exact error has sign sign(x-1). Therefore, if the right-hand
error coefficient is at most e,

  f_*^-(x)=(x-1)^2 R(x)/(1+e),
  0<=f(x)-f_*^-(x)<=2e/(1+e)*f_2(x),                    (2.2)

and f_*^-<=f<=2f_2 globally. These are the parent's one-sided bounds.

(ii) Ganelius nodes at the FIXED parameter r=1/4. For each sufficiently
large n there are explicit nodes a_j^* with

 max_(0<=s<=1) s^(1/4)|B_*(s)| <= A exp(-pi*sqrt(n)/2),
 M_* <= A^4 exp(-2*pi*sqrt(n)),                          (2.3)

where A is a fixed finite constant. Their explicit formula and the
precise source are recorded in the parent's Ganelius lemma. No need
to know A numerically is used in the stopping rule below.

(iii) Polynomial real-root isolation/refinement is a CLASSICAL paid
subroutine, not a unit-cost primitive. Sagraloff--Mehlhorn, Computing
real roots of real polynomials, Theorems 2 and 3, give deterministic
isolation and refinement for square-free integer polynomials of
degree d and coefficient bitsize tau, with bit cost polynomial in
d,tau,kappa for isolating intervals of width <2^(-kappa). Theorem3
states the bound ~O(d(d^2+d*tau+kappa)). We only require polynomial
complexity. We square-free a rational input and clear denominators
first; exact polynomial gcd and rational linear algebra are paid.

Primary source actually read, including Theorems1--3 on printedpp3--5:
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.4088
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4088
Journal: J. Symbolic Comput.73 (2016),46--86,
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2015.03.004
The algorithm used below only passes explicitly computed RATIONAL
polynomials to this subroutine. No real coefficient oracle is assumed.

Classical Gaussian quadrature, shifted Legendre polynomials, Markov's
polynomial derivative inequality, exact partial fractions, and elementary
scalar series provide the remaining tools. These are not new principles.

3. RATIONAL INTERPOLATION NODES WITHOUT A TRANSCENDENTAL CEILING TEST
==================================================================

For n=16,17,18,... do the following. Enclose

  theta_n=pi*sqrt(n)/8

in a rational interval of width <1/4. This requires only polynomially
many bits in log(n). Try BOTH integers ceil(lower),ceil(upper), if they
differ. At least one is the exact ceil(theta_n), even if theta_n is
extremely close to an integer. Thus there is no claim that the sign of
theta_n-k can be decided to a prescribed absolute precision.

For each candidate k put n_0=n-k and generate the explicit nodes

  a_j^*=exp(2*pi*(sqrt(j-1)-sqrt(n_0))),       1<=j<=n_0,
  a_(n_0+1)^*=exp(2*pi*(sqrt(n_0-1/2)-sqrt(n_0))),
  a_j^*=1-(j-n_0-1)/[5(n-n_0-1)],            n_0+2<=j<=n.

Starting at n=16 makes every such candidate have n_0>3 and
n-n_0-1>0. At least one candidate is precisely the Ganelius choice.
Every candidate nevertheless gives strictly positive nodes below1.

Choose positive DYADIC rational nodes a_j, with

  |a_j/a_j^*-1|<=delta,   delta=eta/[2^16(n+1)].           (3.1)

They can be rounded downward, ensuring a_j<1. Exact coincident nodes
are allowed and are interpreted with multiplicity. This causes no
problem for Gaussian quadrature, the product estimate, or grouped
partial fractions later.

The derivative of the factor (s-a)/(s+a) with respect to log(a) has
absolute value <=1/2, uniformly over s>=0,a>0. Since delta<=1/2,
one factor changes by at mostdelta, and all factors have modulus<=1.
Therefore

  |B(s)-B_*(s)|<=n*delta,
  |s B(s)^4-s B_*(s)^4|<=4n*delta<=eta/2^14.              (3.2)

The formulas also give

  min_j a_j >= (1/2)exp(-2*pi*sqrt(n)),
  log(1/D)=O(n*sqrt(n)),      D=prod_j a_j^2.             (3.3)

All rational nodes have bit height
H=O(b+sqrt(n)+log(n+2)). Indeed their smallest exact value is
bounded below by 2^(-16 ceil(sqrt(n))). Evaluating the elementary
formula to additive error smaller than delta times this lower bound,
then taking a dyadic lower endpoint, proves (3.1) at this height.

None of pi,square root,exp is an oracle: Machin's arctangent series,
dyadic bisection for square roots, and range-reduced Taylor series for
exp compute enclosing rationals in polynomial time in their requested
bits and the displayed argument sizes. All arguments here have magnitude
O(sqrt(n)). The geometric or factorial tails provide explicit error
bounds. Extra O(sqrt(n)) absolute accuracy bits suffice for the relative
accuracy of the smallest node. No inverse-power-of-eta loop is used.

4. A POLYNOMIAL-TIME ACCEPTANCE TEST WITH A FIXED ACCURACY MARGIN
=============================================================

For a candidate rational node set compute an upper bound U for M with

  M<=U<=M+eta/1024.                                      (4.1)

Then ACCEPT if 11U<=eta/8; otherwise try the next candidate or n.
The rational inequality is exactly decidable. A nearly equal comparison
does not trigger unbounded precision refinement: (4.1) is the only
accuracy requested. Since 2*pi+4<11, every accepted set has ideal
error coefficient at most e=eta/8 in (2.1).

Here is a fully charged way to compute (4.1). Apart from the zeros of
B, whose function value iszero, the interior critical points of
F(s)=s B(s)^4 satisfy the rational polynomial equation

 prod_j(s^2-a_j^2)
       +8s sum_j a_j prod_(ell!=j)(s^2-a_ell^2)=0.         (4.2)

This has degree2n and leading coefficient1. Take its square-free part,
isolate its real roots in (0,1), and include the endpoints0,1. Its
integer-cleared coefficient bitsize is polynomial in n,H. Endpoint
roots, if any, can be removed exactly before isolation. Repeated
interpolation nodes merely add irrelevant zero-value critical roots.

For a_min=min a_j, F has the elementary Lipschitz bound

  |F'(s)|<=K_F=1+8n/a_min,       0<=s<=1.                  (4.3)

One proves this by differentiating each factor: its derivative is at
most2/a_min, while every factor has modulus<=1. Isolate each critical
root to width <=eta/(2048 K_F). Endpoint evaluation and directed
rational evaluation on these intervals give upper/lower value bounds
of width <=eta/1024, after a fixed factor2 safety allowance. For example
each factor interval is obtained from its monotone endpoint values in
[-1,1]; its product and fourth power have exactly the width bound
underlying (4.3). Taking the largest upper value proves (4.1).
Root isolation and all evaluations have polynomial bit cost in n,H,b.

Termination with the sharp pole budget follows without knowing A.
For the correct Ganelius candidate, once

  A^4 exp(-2*pi*sqrt(n))<=eta/1024,

(3.2),(4.1) give explicitly
U<=(1/1024+1/16384+1/1024)eta=33eta/16384,
and 11*33/16384<1/8. Thus an accepted n occurs no later than

  n <= log^2(1/eta)/(4*pi^2)+O(log(1/eta)).                 (4.4)

The sequential search has O(b^2) candidates and each has polynomial
cost in b, since n=O(b^2), H=O(b). It is a deterministic polynomial-bit
algorithm even though its proof uses the existence of the fixed
Ganelius constant. Earlier acceptance can only decrease m=2n+1.

5. COMPACTIFY THE IDEAL GAUSSIAN RULE
===================================

Fix an accepted rational node set and m=2n+1. Set, for 0<=T<=1,

  d_a(T)=1+(a-1)T,
  P(T)=prod_j [d_(a_j)(T)d_(1/a_j)(T)]^2,
  w(T)=1/P(T),             D=prod_j a_j^2.

Since

  d_a(T)d_(1/a)(T)=1+(a+1/a-2)T(1-T),
  1<=d_a(T)d_(1/a)(T)<=1/a,

one has the explicit conditioning sandwich

                   0<D<=w(T)<=1.                       (5.1)

Let T_i in (0,1), A_i>0 be the m-node Gaussian rule for w(T)dT,
exact through degree2m-1. This is exactly the compact version of
the ideal rule in (2.1), rather than a different quadrature.
Indeed under t=(1-T)/T the parent's modified measure is

 dnu_infty(t)=dt/[(1+t)^3 prod_j(t+a_j)^2(t+1/a_j)^2]
             =T^(2m-1)w(T)dT.

For each k<=2m-1, t^k T^(2m-1)=T^(2m-1-k)(1-T)^k is a polynomial
of degree2m-1. Thus the transformed nodes and weights A_i T_i^(2m-1)
give an exact m-node Gaussian rule for the parent measure. Uniqueness
identifies it with that rule. Equivalently the transformed orthogonal
polynomial T^m q_infty((1-T)/T) is orthogonal for w on [0,1].

Consequently the ideal entropy kernel is

 R(x)=sum_i u_i/[1+T_i(x-1)],
 u_i=A_i(1-T_i)P(T_i)>0.                                  (5.2)

Writing t_i=(1-T_i)/T_i, c_i=u_i/T_i=A_i t_i P(T_i) gives
the positive Stieltjes form used in (2.1)--(2.2).

6. ELEMENTARY QUADRATURE CONDITIONING BOUNDS
===========================================

The following bounds apply to ANY weight D<=w<=1, not just this P.
They ensure no hidden exponentially many ACCURACY BITS are required.
Exponentially small numerical margins are allowed, but their logarithms
below are polynomial in m and log(1/D).

For a polynomial p of degree<m on [0,1], shifted Legendre expansion
gives the classical Nikolskii bound

  ||p||_infty^2 <= m^2 integral_0^1 |p(T)|^2 dT.             (6.1)

Indeed the squared orthonormal basis values are at most2k+1, whose
sum for k=0,...,m-1 ism^2. Let ell_i be the Lagrange polynomial for
the Gaussian nodes. Gaussian exactness on ell_i^2 and T ell_i^2 gives

  A_i=integral ell_i^2 w >= D/m^2=:a_0,
  T_i A_i=integral T ell_i^2 w.                             (6.2)

Also A_i<=integral w<=1. To bound T_i away fromzero, take
delta_0=1/(2m^2). By (6.1), the interval [0,delta_0] carries at most
half the UNWEIGHTED squared norm of ell_i. Hence

 T_i A_i >= delta_0 D/2 * integral ell_i^2
           >= delta_0 D/2 * A_i,

and the same argument at1 gives

   tau<=T_i<=1-tau,        tau=D/(4m^2).                   (6.3)

Moreover integral ell_i^2<=1/D, so ||ell_i||_infty<=m/sqrt(D).
The classical Markov inequality on [0,1] gives
||ell_i'||_infty<=2m^3/sqrt(D). Since ell_i(T_i)=1 and ell_i(T_j)=0,

  |T_i-T_j|>=sqrt(D)/(2m^3)>=sigma,
  sigma:=D/(2m^3),       i!=j.                             (6.4)

For m=1 the separation assertion is vacuous. Using the weaker rational
sigma avoids a square-root operation in all subsequent precision bounds.

Let mu_k=integral T^k w(T)dT and H=(mu_(i+j))_(i,j=0)^(m-1).
For a monomial coefficient vectorv, (5.1) gives
v^*Hv>=D integral|sum v_j T^j|^2. A shifted Legendre polynomial of
degree k has monomial coefficients
(-1)^(k-j) binom(k,j)binom(k+j,j), of magnitude at most8^k.
The orthonormal degree-k coefficient vector therefore has squared norm
at most(k+1)(2k+1)64^k. Cauchy--Schwarz in the basis change yields

   lambda_min(H)>=h:=D/[2m^3*64^m].                       (6.5)

All bounds are constructive functions of the EXACT positive rational D.
Their negative logarithms are O(m+log(1/D)+log(m+1)).

7. MOMENTS ARE COMPUTABLE IN POLYNOMIAL BIT TIME
==============================================

The polynomial P has degree4n=2m-2 and rational coefficient height
polynomial in n,H. Its rational linear factors vanish only at

  p=1/(1-a_j)>1,       p=-a_j/(1-a_j)<0.

Group coincident poles exactly. For each 0<=k<=2m-1, exact division
and rational partial fractions express T^k/P(T) as a polynomial of
degree at most1 plus rational multiples of (T-p)^(-ell).
The degree4n partial-fraction system is nonsingular after grouping.
Its coefficients have polynomial bit height. Fraction-free elimination
or determinant/minor bounds prove both polynomial operation count and
polynomial numerator/denominator lengths of the answer. No ill-conditioned
floating elimination is being treated as exact here.

Integrals for ell>=2 are exact rationals. For ell=1 the integral is
log|1-p|-log|p|, which for these two pole types is log(a_j) or
-log(a_j). Therefore

                  mu_k=r_(k,0)+sum_j r_(k,j)log(a_j),       (7.1)

with rational coefficients of polynomial bit height, computable exactly.
There can be severe cancellation; it is paid for by computing the
logarithms with B plus the coefficient-height bound plus O(log(m+1))
bits to obtain ABSOLUTE moment error <=2^(-B).

For completeness, logarithms of positive rationals of bit heightH'
are computable with certified absolute error2^(-B) in polynomial
time in H',B. Write q=2^k r with 1<=r<2, using exact comparisons.
Here |k|=O(H'). With z=(r-1)/(r+1) in [0,1/3), use

  log(r)=2 sum_(ell=0)^(N-1) z^(2ell+1)/(2ell+1)+remainder,

whose positive tail is bounded by3*9^(-N). The same series gives
log2, with z=1/3. Taking N=O(B+H'+log(H'+2)) and exact rational
arithmetic, then rounding outward, gives the required enclosure.
All intermediate integer lengths are polynomial. Thus (7.1) is an
explicit polynomial-bit moment procedure, rather than an unspecified
integration or transcendental primitive. These are algorithmic steps;
no claim of a new whole-family software implementation is made.

8. FROM APPROXIMATE MOMENTS TO A RATIONAL POLYNOMIAL WITH KNOWN ROOTS
==================================================================

The monic degree-m orthogonal polynomial is
q(T)=T^m+sum_(j=0)^(m-1) z_j T^j, with

 H z=-(mu_m,...,mu_(2m-1))^T.                              (8.1)

All roots of q are the distinct Gaussian nodes in (0,1); consequently
its coefficient l1 norm is at most2^m. Suppose every moment is replaced
by a rational approximation of absolute error<=epsilon_mu. If
m*epsilon_mu<=h/2, the approximate Hankel matrix is invertible.
Solving that rational system EXACTLY gives a rational monic qhat and

 max_j |zhat_j-z_j| <=4m*2^m*epsilon_mu/h.                  (8.2)

Indeed the matrix perturbation norm is <=m*epsilon_mu, the RHS error
norm is <=sqrt(m)*epsilon_mu, the inverse norm is <=2/h, and
||z||_2<=2^m. Rational solution bit height is polynomial in m and the
moment precision, by the same fraction-free linear algebra argument.

Choose any rational gamma<=min(tau/4,sigma/4) and put

  psi=gamma*(sigma/2)^(m-1)/(4m).

If the RHS of (8.2) is <=psi, then qhat has exactly m distinct REAL
roots, one within gamma of every ideal T_i. This statement is proved,
not assumed of a generic perturbed polynomial: at T_i+/-gamma,

 |q(T_i+/-gamma)|>=gamma*(sigma/2)^(m-1),
 |qhat-q|<=m*psi

on [0,1]. Their endpoint signs therefore agree. The m disjoint
sign-change intervals each contain a real qhat root, and its degree
is m, exhausting all roots. Their separation is >=sigma-2gamma.

Now isolate the roots of the EXPLICIT rational polynomial qhat to
width<=gamma using the integer case of the paid classical subroutine
in Section2. Enlarge every isolating interval by gamma on both sides.
In their sorted order these enclose the true ideal nodes T_i, with
width<=3gamma. The root-label correspondence follows from the disjoint
gamma neighborhoods and their ordering. No exact q root or unknown
coefficient oracle has been supplied for free.

9. CERTIFIED GAUSSIAN WEIGHTS AND AN EXPLICIT SAFE PRECISION
=========================================================

Here are explicit rational choices sufficient for the final rounding.
They are intentionally conservative. Put

  K=(4/sigma)^(m-1), a_0=D/m^2,
  M_0=8/tau+8n/a_min+1,

  delta_T=min{tau/32, sigma/(32m), eta/(2^20 M_0),
                    eta*a_0*sigma/(2^24 m^2 K^2)},
  gamma=delta_T/4,
  psi=gamma*(sigma/2)^(m-1)/(4m),

  epsilon_mu <=min{h/(4m), eta*a_0/(2^24 K^2),
                                      psi*h/(8m*2^m)}.     (9.1)

Choose epsilon_mu as the largest dyadic power not exceeding this
minimum; its negative logarithm differs from that of the minimum by
less than one. The coarser sufficient precision (9.5) is an alternative.

Compute enclosing moments to this error and follow Section8. The
resulting ideal-node boxes have width<delta_T and lie in
[tau/2,1-tau/2]. Their midpoint values T'_i satisfy |T'_i-T_i|<=delta_T.
Form their Lagrange polynomials ell'_i with exact rational coefficients,
and compute the rational approximation

 A'_i=sum_(k=0)^(2m-2) coefficient_k((ell'_i)^2)*muhat_k.

Let

 E_A=K^2*epsilon_mu+16m^2*K^2*delta_T/sigma.                (9.2)

Then [A'_i-E_A,A'_i+E_A] encloses the true positive Gaussian A_i.
To see this, all intermediate node vectors have separation>=sigma/2.
Their Lagrange coefficient l1 norms are <=K. Differentiating the
product/denominator formula for ell_i shows its coefficient l1
Lipschitz constant in the maximum node distance is <=8m^2 K/sigma.
Squaring doubles this to <=16m^2 K^2/sigma. Moment error contributes
at most K^2*epsilon_mu. This proves (9.2) without relying on a
dependency-sensitive interval linear solve. In particular

             E_A<=17 eta*a_0/2^24<eta*a_0/2^19.           (9.3)

All weight intervals are strictly positive, since A_i>=a_0.

The required number of ACCURACY BITS is explicitly polynomial. If
b_D=ceil(log2(1/D)), then a_min^2>=D and (9.1) imply

 log2(1/delta_T)=O(b+m*(b_D+log(m+1))),
 log2(1/epsilon_mu)=O(b+m*(b_D+log(m+1))).                 (9.4)

For example, rounding moments to

 B_mu=ceil(10^6 m^2 [b+b_D+m+10])                          (9.5)

absolute bits is more than sufficient for all inequalities in (9.1).
This follows directly by taking logarithms of their displayed factors:
log2 K=(m-1)log2(4/sigma), log2(1/h)=b_D+O(m+logm),
and log2 M_0<=b_D+O(log(m+1)). There are no unspecified exponentially
many precision bits. Using the exact rational minimum in (9.1), rather
than (9.5), gives a smaller computable bound.

One fully explicit check of sufficiency is as follows. For
X=b+b_D+m+10, use log2(m)<=m and a_min^2>=D to bound
log2(1/tau)<=2X, log2(1/sigma)<=3X, log2 K<=4mX,
log2 M_0<=2X and log2(1/h)<=10X. Taking logarithms in
(9.1) then gives log2(1/delta_T)<=20mX,
log2(1/psi)<=30mX, and, for the largest dyadic choice,
log2(1/epsilon_mu)<=100mX. Thus (9.5) has a fixed verified
polynomial margin, not an unquantified stability constant.

All operations on Lagrange polynomials, qhat, moments and root boxes
have polynomial-size rational operands. The paid root routine's
degree, integer coefficient height and target refinement precision
are therefore polynomial in m,b_D,b. Since accepted n=O(b^2) and
b_D=O(n sqrt(n))=O(b^3), the ENTIRE preparation has polynomial-bit
complexity in b. No optimized exponent for this polynomial is claimed.

10. EXPORT POSITIVE RATIONAL COEFFICIENTS WITH A GLOBAL LOWER BOUND
================================================================

For a node box [T_i^-,T_i^+] define positive rational pole bounds

  t_i^-=(1-T_i^+)/T_i^+,
  t_i^+=(1-T_i^-)/T_i^-.

Bound each positive linear factor d_a(T) on that node box by its
monotone endpoint values, and multiply/square to get rational
P_i^-<=P(T_i)<=P_i^+. With the weight interval from Section9 set

  c_i^-=(A'_i-E_A)t_i^- P_i^-,
  c_i^+=(A'_i+E_A)t_i^+ P_i^+.

Export tbar_i=t_i^+ and cunder_i=c_i^-, both exact rationals. Since
the node boxes are disjoint and ordered, the exported poles are
distinct. All residues are strictly positive. Define the exact rational

  kappa=min_i (c_i^-/c_i^+)*(t_i^-/t_i^+).                  (10.1)

Termwise for EVERY x>0,

 kappa*c_i/(x+t_i)<=c_i^-/(x+t_i^+)<=c_i/(x+t_i).

Thus kappa R<=R_0<=R globally; no interpolation equality is assumed
to survive the rational enclosure.

The quantitative choices above certify 1-kappa<=eta/16. Here are
explicit width bounds. A node box has width<=delta_T and lies in
[tau/2,1-tau/2], where |(log t)'|<=4/tau. Hence

 log(t_i^+/t_i^-)<=4delta_T/tau<=eta/2^21.

Every d_a and d_(1/a) factor has relative variation at most
exp(delta_T/a_min); there are2n factors, each squared. Consequently

 log(P_i^+/P_i^-)<=4n delta_T/a_min<=eta/2^21.

By (9.3), log[(A'_i+E_A)/(A'_i-E_A)]<=4E_A/a_0<=eta/2^17.
It follows that log(c_i^+/c_i^-)<=eta/2^16, and then
-log(kappa)<=eta/2^15. In particular kappa>=1-eta/16 as claimed.
All these conclusions can also be checked by the exact rational
comparison kappa>=1-eta/16 before exporting.

If desired, endpoints can be rounded further to positive dyadics with
a fixed small fraction of the available error budget. That is not
needed for rational output: the displayed endpoints already have
polynomial bit height. Kernel coefficients are then

 Tbar_i=1/(1+tbar_i),   ubar_i=cunder_i/(1+tbar_i),

with the extra rational factor1/(1+e) for f^-.

Finally use e=eta/8 and (2.2):

 f^-<=f_*^-<=f,
 f-f^- <= (f-f_*^-)+(1-kappa)f_*^-
         <=[2e/(1+e)+2(1-kappa)]f_2
         <=(3eta/8)f_2<=eta f_2.

This proves (1.1), and adding EXACTLY eta f_2 gives the stated upper
function and exact-width entropy bracket. Equations(4.4) and m=2n+1
give (1.2). Every output quantity is rational and every scalar root,
moment and transcendental evaluation has been charged.

11. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
========================================

This closes the mathematical polynomial-bit PREPARATION gap for the
entropy family in the parent construction. It preserves the sharp
leading upper pole-count coefficient1/(2*pi^2) proved there; it does
not prove that this coefficient is an optimal entropy lower bound.
No unknown degree-m root operation remains in the model.

The construction is nongalactic in the limited theoretical sense that
