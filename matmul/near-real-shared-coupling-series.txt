NEAR-REAL RAW SOLVES WITH SHARED COUPLING SERIES
==============================================

Working construction, 2026-11-15 UTC. Independent proof review: PASS.
See near-real-shared-coupling-independent-audit.txt and the verification
record for exact reviewed hashes, model and scope.
This note replaces the qk pole-by-band width in the deflated raw resolver
by q+kJ, J=O(log(2mu/epsilon)), by sharing analytic coupling coefficients.
Schur products are shared too; evaluating q separate m-by-k couplings
would lose a substantive part of the gain. The construction remains
conditional on a supplied certified coarse split. It does not settle
the arbitrary-factor rank-one LRA target or the large-band k=Theta(m)
case, and does not assert novelty for Taylor series or Schur complements.

1. MODEL, CERTIFIED SPLIT, AND RESULT
------------------------------------

Use the raw-factor model of near-real-deflated-raw-solves.txt and
raw-factor-fresh-search.txt. The supported coordinates satisfy

  S=Z K Z^*, T=Z^* Z=I+D, ||D||<=eta<=1/8,             (1)

where K is explicit, Hermitian and invertible after zero-core support
removal. Only thin raw products apply Z,Z^*,T. The physical raw factor
has size N-by-m, N>=m, I_X=Nm. The supported core has dimension <=m.
Complex unitary changes of coarse coordinates are allowed; the physical
input may still be real. All complexity is exact arithmetic.

Fix t>0, put B=tK^-1, and SUPPLY an orthogonal/unitary P,Q coordinate
split with dim(P)=k and the following certificates:

  spec(B_QQ) subset (-infinity,1/2) union (2,infinity),
  ||B_PQ||=||B_QP||<=1/16.                             (2)

The cost C_split of constructing/certifying this basis is separate.
There is no free exact eigenvector oracle. An exact invariant split of
K containing every eigenvalue in [t/2,2t] satisfies (2) with B_PQ=0.
The conditions also admit a sufficiently accurate coarse-only split;
no such split construction is imported without its own review.

Supply q poles w_j=t+i y_j, y_j!=0, and q physical right-hand sides h_j.
Let mu=max_j |w_j|/|y_j|>=1 and 0<epsilon<=1. Set

  delta=epsilon/(100mu^2).

Integers L,J>=1 can be chosen with L,J=O(log(2mu/epsilon)) so that

  (3/5)^L<=delta/100, (3/4)^J<=delta/100.              (3)

Repeated multiplication and comparisons construct them without a log
oracle. With R=q+kJ<=m, the algorithm approximates each normalized action
w_j(S-w_jI)^-1h_j to error <=epsilon||h_j|| at total cost

 C_split + ~O(m^omega)
   + O(I_X R^(omega-2)*L)
   + O(mk^2 J+qk^2 J+mkq J+qk^omega).                (4)

Here omega>2 is a feasible multiplication exponent, with the same
elementary blocking convention as the imported raw resolver. No true
Gram is assumed supplied. The required k band columns are queried and
charged; if P fills the supported core these are its entire Gram.
All q pole/RHS pairs must be available together at every raw block action.

The guarantee is per column, hence also Frobenius-relative for the block
of RHS vectors. For a spectral action guarantee on an orthonormal
q-dimensional input basis, use epsilon/sqrt(q), adding only logarithms.
This is not an unconditional spectral-block assertion from independent
per-column bounds. The sole randomness may be the already conditioned
coarse event; the construction below is deterministic on that event.

At the original exponents N=m^(9/8), omega=9/4, polynomial mu and
epsilon^-1, and q,k<=~sqrt(m), all terms in (4) fit ~O(m^(9/4)), provided
C_split does. With q=~sqrt(m), the old q(k+1) width required k=~O(1);
the present width admits k up to ~sqrt(m). These exponent comparisons
retain the source's feasible-omega convention; they do not prove a new
matrix multiplication exponent or a final LRA extraction theorem.

2. SAFE ANALYTIC DISK AND AFFINE COUPLINGS
-----------------------------------------

Write u=(w-t)/t and

  M(u)=(1+u)B-T,
  F(u)=(1+u)B_QQ-T_QQ,
  Gamma(u)=T_PQ-(1+u)B_PQ,
  Lambda(u)=T_QP-(1+u)B_QP.

Then M has off-diagonal blocks -Gamma(u),-Lambda(u). Both couplings
are affine. For complex u, Lambda(u)=Gamma(conj(u))^*, rather than
Gamma(u)^*. Using the wrong pointwise adjoint would destroy analyticity.
Since ||T_PQ||<=eta and (2) holds, on |u|<=1/3,

  ||Gamma(u)||,||Lambda(u)||
       <=1/8+(4/3)/16=5/24<zeta:=1/4.                (5)

Let A0(u)=((1+u)B_QQ-I)^-1. For every real lambda outside [1/2,2],
the scalar inverse has modulus <=3 on |u|<=1/3. If lambda<=0 the
bound is <=1. For 0<lambda<1/2 use
|1-(1+u)lambda|>=1-(4/3)lambda>=1/3; for lambda>2 use
|(1+u)lambda-1|>=(2/3)lambda-1>=1/3. Thus

  ||A0(u)||<=3, ||A0(u)D_QQ||<=3/8,
  ||F(u)^-1||<=3/(1-3/8)=24/5=:beta_H.              (6)

These are uniform ANALYTIC-disk bounds, independent of k, dimensions,
coarse eigenvalue magnitudes and the pole's distance from the real axis.
On the vertical line Re u=0 the sharper pointwise estimates are

  ||A0(u)||<=2, ||F(u)^-1||<=8/3=:beta.              (7)

The finite Richardson operator

  R_L(u)=sum_(a=0)^(L-1)[A0(u)D_QQ]^a A0(u)

is analytic on the disk and obeys

  ||R_L(u)-F(u)^-1||<=beta_H*(3/8)^L.                (8)

The same fixed L is used for all coupling coefficients. This operator
identity, not an RHS-dependent stopping rule, justifies the coefficient
error bound below.

3. SHARED TAYLOR COUPLINGS AND THEIR OPERATOR ACCURACY
----------------------------------------------------

For the near poles |y_j|<=t/4, compute the first J Taylor coefficients
C_0,...,C_(J-1) at u=0 of the matrix function

  U_L(u)=R_L(u)Lambda(u),
  U_hat(u)=sum_(a=0)^(J-1) C_a u^a.                   (9)

Each C_a has (dim Q)-by-k shape. This is kJ coefficient columns total,
independent of q. Use formal-series Richardson from zero, with affine
RHS Lambda(u), truncating after degree J-1 at every operation. All
coarse inverse series have nonzero constant denominators. The procedure
therefore produces EXACTLY the first J coefficients of U_L, rather than
an unrelated approximation to them.

Let U(u)=F(u)^-1 Lambda(u). On |u|<=1/3, ||U||<=beta_H*zeta and
||U_L-U||<=beta_H*zeta*(3/8)^L. Cauchy's coefficient bound, evaluated
at |u_j|<=1/4, has geometric ratio at most 3/4. Consequently

 ||U_hat(u_j)-U(u_j)||
   <=(96/5)*zeta*[(3/8)^L+(3/4)^J]<=zeta*delta.       (10)

Independently, pointwise safe Richardson on each supplied b_Q,j gives
r_hat_j approximating r_j=F(u_j)^-1 b_Q,j with error

  ||r_hat_j-r_j||<=beta*(1/4)^L||b_Q,j||
                   <=delta||b_j||.                  (11)

Condition (3) is more than sufficient for (10),(11). The coefficient
approximation and RHS actions need not arise from one common R_hat.
The proof requires only (10),(11), and uses the SAME U_hat in both
the Schur core and the Q reconstruction.

4. SMALL SCHUR CORES AND THE FULL PHYSICAL ERROR
-----------------------------------------------

Put b_j=Z^*h_j. Exact elimination at a near pole gives

 C_j=(1+u_j)B_PP-T_PP-Gamma(u_j)U(u_j),
 v_P,j=C_j^-1[b_P,j+Gamma(u_j)r_j],
 v_Q,j=r_j+U(u_j)v_P,j.                               (12)

Define C_hat and v_hat by replacing U with U_hat and r with r_hat
in (12), and invert each computed k-by-k C_hat exactly. The required
true T_PP and T_QP are obtained with one raw T-action on the k P
coordinate columns, before the series/RHS iteration begins.

As in the imported resolver, with H=T^(1/2)KT^(1/2) used only for proof,
||M(u_j)^-1||<=2mu/(1-eta)<=3mu=:g. Its P,P block is C_j^-1.
By (10), ||C_hat-C||<=zeta^2*delta, and
g*zeta^2*delta<=3/16 since delta<=1/mu. Thus C_hat is invertible,
||C_hat^-1||<=2g, and its inverse perturbation is at most
2g^2*zeta^2*delta. Set h0=1+zeta*beta<=5/3. Equations (10),(11) give

 ||v_hat_P-v_P||
    <=delta[2g^2*zeta^2*h0+2g*zeta]||b||
    <=(27/8)*mu^2*delta||b||.

For the Q block,

 ||v_hat_Q-v_Q||
    <=delta[1+zeta*g*h0]||b||
        +zeta*(beta+delta)||v_hat_P-v_P||.

With delta<=1, the sum of these block bounds is at most
(279/32)*mu^2*delta||b||<9mu^2*delta||b||. Finally the exact identity

  w(S-wI)^-1h=-h-Z M(u)^-1 Z^*h                       (13)

and ||Z||^2<=9/8 give physical error <11mu^2*delta||h||, safely below
epsilon||h|| under the chosen delta. This estimate permits complex
unitary coordinates and the two different approximations in (10),(11).
No finite-word stability of the small inverse is asserted.

5. ACTUAL FAST COARSE SERIES ACTIONS
------------------------------------

The following detail is essential: applying a dense T0 separately to
each coefficient would not establish the claimed block complexity.
Let

  A00=(B_QQ-I)^-1, T0=A00 B_QQ=I+A00.

These are explicit coarse matrices prepared in ~O(m^omega), and

  A0(u)=(I+uT0)^-1 A00.                              (14)

Compute ONE Frobenius form T0=P0 C P0^-1, including its transformation,
using Neiger--Pernet--Villard, ISSAC 2024, Corollary 5.2:
https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07345v2#S5.SS1
The primary theorem gives O(m^omega*(loglog m)^2) field operations.
Fuse P0^-1 A00 in the preprocessing. This is an explicit COARSE core,
not the true T or a free eigenbasis.

For an arbitrary k-column polynomial RHS W(u) modulo u^J, transform
its kJ coefficient columns by P0^-1 A00, solve

  (I+u C)Y(u)=W_tilde(u) mod u^J,

and transform the kJ columns back by P0. A size-d companion block is
solved by the same rational-series recurrence as raw-factor-fresh-
search.txt, Section 10.2, with variable -u in place of its t. Its last
coordinate is a shifted sum of the d input-coordinate polynomials
divided by one scalar polynomial of constant coefficient one. Invert
that scalar series once per block. Fast scalar convolutions for k RHS
columns and the remaining shift/add recurrences cost ~O(dkJ).
Summing companion sizes costs ~O(mkJ). Both dense transformations cost
O(m^2*(kJ)^(omega-2)), not J separate dense matrix-vector actions.

The pointwise A0(u_j) actions on the q near RHS columns use the SAME
Frobenius preparation, with different scalar u_j in each companion
recurrence. Every required division exists by the safe vertical-line
bound. Their scalar work is O(mq), and their dense transformations
batch across q columns.

All true D_QQ actions, on kJ coefficient columns and q current RHS
columns, are one thin raw T-action followed by projection and identity
subtraction. The total raw width per iteration is at most R=q+kJ.
The input affine coefficients and every column in a current iteration
are already available. This is not retroactive batching of later
adaptive RHS queries. The number of iterations is L, independent of J.

6. SHARED SCHUR PRODUCTS; NO HIDDEN qmk^2 TERM
----------------------------------------------

Write Gamma(u)=Gamma0+u*Gamma1. Precompute the k-by-k coefficients

  W_a=Gamma0*C_a+Gamma1*C_(a-1), 0<=a<=J,
  C_(-1)=C_J=0.                                     (15)

This costs O(mk^2 J) even with ordinary multiplication. Each Schur
correction Gamma(u_j)U_hat(u_j) is evaluated from these W_a by Horner
in O(k^2 J), so all q cores cost O(qk^2 J), followed by O(qk^omega)
small inversions. Computing Gamma(u_j)r_hat_j costs O(mkq).

After all v_hat_P,j are available, reconstruct the coupling term in
all Q outputs as

  sum_(a=0)^(J-1) C_a [u_j^a v_hat_P,j]_(j=1,...,q).  (16)

This costs O(mkq J) and stores only coefficient width kJ and output
width q. It never materializes q separate (dim Q)-by-k U_hat matrices.
This shared polynomial evaluation is necessary: using the old
O(qmk^2) Schur preparation would fail at q,k of order sqrt(m).

7. FAR POLES AND TOTAL COST
---------------------------

For |y_j|>=t/4, the full coarse preconditioner
((1+u_j)B-I)^-1 has norm <=mu_j<=sqrt(17). Its defect contraction is
at most sqrt(17)/8<3/5, without using a band split. Its full inverse
error is at most 9*(3/5)^L, giving normalized physical error at most
11*(3/5)^L||h_j||. The choice (3) is ample. This is the ordinary
far-cone raw Richardson proof, now with an explicit constant cutoff.

A Frobenius form of the full explicit B supplies these differently
shifted preconditioners in one additional ~O(m^omega) setup. It is a
constant number of shared preparations, not q preparations. Pointwise
companion solves cost O(m) per column; dense basis maps and raw T
actions batch with the near-pole columns. Hence arbitrary supplied
poles with the common real center t are covered. If all poles are far,
the split and all coupling-series work may be omitted entirely.

The initial band queries, initial Z^*h_j, final Z lifts and dense
coarse changes of basis fit the raw width term in (4), since k+q<=R
and N>=m. Scalar companion work is ~O(mR) per iteration and is also
dominated. Equations (15),(16) give precisely the remaining coarse
terms in (4), including all output columns. The Frobenius routine's
temporary storage is charged by its arithmetic bound; no stronger
in-place preprocessing claim is made. Thin working buffers have width
R plus explicit coarse/split data and polynomial/small-core matrices.

If q,k<=~sqrt(m), the four postprocessing terms in (4) are respectively
~O(m^2), ~O(m^(3/2)), ~O(m^2), and ~O(m^((omega+1)/2)). At omega=9/4
the last exponent is 13/8. With R<=~sqrt(m), the original raw term is
~m^(17/8)*m^(1/8)=~m^(9/4). This proves the claimed larger-band regime.

8. THE LARGE-BAND ORIGINAL TARGET IS STILL OPEN
-----------------------------------------------

The result removes the multiplicative qk pole cost; it does not remove
the initial k true-band queries or solve a general k-by-k true Schur
bulk without obtaining it. If k=Theta(m), the present width/fallback
still has full-Gram-scale raw work ~I_X*m^(omega-2), exponent 19/8 at
the original N=m^(9/8), rather than the target 18/8. A different
structure or algorithm is required for that case.

For example take coarse K=tI and T=I+D, ||D||<=eta. The entire coarse
space is in the band. The naive coarse Richardson multiplier is
D/(i y/t), with norm eta*t/|y|, so it cannot yield a near-real constant
contraction. Moreover D can have a positive fraction of its singular
values >=eta/2: use a dense orthogonal conjugate of a grid across
[-eta,eta]. Any rank smaller than that fraction leaves operator error
>=eta/2. Low-rank metric-defect compression at inverse-polynomial
target accuracy therefore has no uniform sublinear-rank guarantee.
Such metrics are legitimate: choose Z with Z^*Z=T and pad to any N>=m.
These examples reject those specific shortcuts, not general raw-input
algorithms or the original LRA objective.

The remaining target is still rank-one product LRA at theta=1/m,
N=m^(9/8), including an actual stable filter and final extraction.
The present construction is a positive new resolver step for moderate
coarse-band dimension; a certified inexpensive split and an unrestricted
large-band mechanism remain separate obligations. Classical Frobenius,
formal-series, Cauchy and Schur tools are credited inputs. Historical
priority and finite-word stability remain unverified.
