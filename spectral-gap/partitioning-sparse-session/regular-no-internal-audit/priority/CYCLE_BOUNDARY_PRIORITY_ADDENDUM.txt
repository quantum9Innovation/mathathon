VERDICT
-------
The monodromy reduction, component-cycle correspondence, connectedness
probability1/N, and rising-factorial cycle-count transform are classical.
No dedicated primary theorem asserting the same Bayesian hidden-fiber
success formula was located in this focused pass. That does not certify

1. The cycle/permutation reduction is explicitly in lift literature
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Alon Amit and Nathan Linial, Random Graph Coverings I: General Theory
and Graph Connectivity, Combinatorica22(2002),1-18, primary author PDF:
 https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~nati/PAPERS/rlg_connectivity.pdf

Coverage: full paper read. Page6 gives the unicyclic monodromy reduction
and connectedness probability1/N; Section1.3 supplies tree flattening.
These are classical inputs.

Tomasz Luczak, Lukasz Witkowski and Marcin Witkowski, Hamilton Cycles
in Random Lifts of Graphs, arXiv1306.2057v3,6January2014:
 https://arxiv.org/html/1306.2057v3
 https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.2057

Coverage: Sections1-2, in particular Lemma2 and its complete proof.
Deleting one edge of C_h leaves N disjoint paths in the lift. The final
uniform matching concatenates those paths according to a uniform
permutation. The number of graph components is therefore its cycle
count; component lengths are h times permutation-cycle lengths. Lemma2
uses this to show there are at most2logN components with high probability.
No fiber-posterior or reconstruction theorem is stated there.

2. The exact transform is classical permutation/Ewens enumeration
----------------------------------------------------------------
Alexander Gnedin and Dudley Stark, Random Permutations and Queues,
arXiv2209.12781v1,26September2022; Advances in Applied Mathematics149
(2023),102549:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2209.12781v1
 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2023.102549

Coverage: Sections1-2 and equation(4), with the primary full paper
available. They state the Ewens permutation law and the exact cycle-count
pgf E[z^R]=(theta*z)_N/(theta)_N, with rising factorial notation.
Uniform permutations are theta=1. Thus E[t^R]=(t)_N/N! and its gamma
representation are standard inputs, not a new cycle-count theorem.
The paper studies process/queue questions and does not state a hidden
cycle-lift fiber-recovery bound. Its historical background points to the
classical Ewens sampling formula and standard permutation enumeration.

3. What remains the candidate's reconstruction calculation
---------------------------------------------------------
These sources do not supply the following candidate-specific assertions:
  * an undirected component C_(k*l) has exactly2k locally bijective maps
    onto the KNOWN C_k;
  * maps on R components have (2k)^R choices, and quotienting by ONE
    GLOBAL Aut(C_k) action gives (2k)^(R-1) unordered fiber partitions;
  * the stated hidden-partition prior and matching likelihood make the
    posterior uniform over those unordered partitions.
Those require the fresh mathematical audit. In particular global vertex
names are retained in the observed graph: graph automorphisms swapping
components must not be incorrectly factored out of the inference task.

Conditional on those assertions, the candidate success probability is
simply the following inference from the classical pgf:
  E[(2k)^(1-R)]
   =2k*Gamma(N+1/(2k))/[Gamma(1/(2k))*Gamma(N+1)].
Its asymptotic power follows from the standard fixed-shift gamma-ratio
asymptotic. The average cannot be replaced by inserting the typical
R~logN into the conditional success; the pgf accounts for rare covers
with fewer components. No additional statistical limit theorem is needed.

4. Exact qualifications for any resulting degree boundary
--------------------------------------------------------
The candidate's connected case means conditioning the lift to have one
component, an event of probability1/N. It is a different distribution.
The unordered partition can then be unique for the KNOWN k even though
the unconditioned Bayes success tends to zero. If k is unknown, a long
cycle may cover several different cycle bases; known-base uniqueness
must not be silently promoted to unknown-base identification.

The degree>=3 positive theorem concerns independent lifts of fixed
regular bases and a supplied fixed class bound K. The degree2 negative
result concerns even unlimited computation with known C_k and the
specified uniform hidden-fiber prior. Present this as a sharp boundary
for that precise unconditioned inference problem, after both mathematics
audits pass. It is not a universal impossibility statement for all degree2
graph models, side information, partial recovery, or connected lifts.
