1. CONVENTIONS AND THE EXACT NEW UNIFORM COEFFICIENT

Use row labels a in V=F_p^(1 by r), p odd, q=p^r, and local equation
  sum_(j~i) b_j=aR.
Write
  h_old(R)=dim_Fp{X:BX=XR},
  a(R)=dim_Fp{K:K^T=-K, RK=KR^T}.

The actual cyclic-lattice source proves the more general full-uniform
coefficient
  p^[h_old(R)+a(R)]+o(1).
For cyclic R, a(R)=0. This additional a(R) term is necessary for general
noncyclic R and has not been silently dropped. In particular a scalar
double R=lambda I_2 has a one-dimensional skew-form space; the cyclic
restriction matters for the proposed coefficient p^h_old.

2. INTERACTION TRANSFERS: ALL SIGNS AND TRANSPOSES CHECK

Let C_i(a) be the vector congruence for root label a at old vertex i. A
linear dependency of these congruences is described by COLUMN vectors
h_i(a), with scalar pairing C_i(a)h_i(a). A zero-margin 2-by-2 transfer
on an edge ij gives exactly

  (b-b')[h_i(a)-h_i(a')]
   +(a-a')[h_j(b)-h_j(b')]=0.

Set a'=b'=0, k_i=h_i(0), and U_i(a)=h_i(a)-k_i. Then
  b U_i(a)+a U_j(b)=0.
Taking b successively to be coordinate rows shows each coordinate of
U_i(a) is F_p-linear in a. Thus h_i(a)=K_i a^T+k_i, and substitution
gives K_j=-K_i^T. Connectivity propagates this relation along walks;
an odd cycle forces K_i=-K_i^T. Thereafter it is unchanged across
every edge, so there is ONE common skew-symmetric K.

This argument does not assume beforehand that h_i is polynomial or affine.
It classifies every function h_i:V->V*, including arbitrary nonlinear ones.
That completeness is essential because the lattice congruences have one
coefficient for every root label, not merely r coordinate coefficients.

Collect the remaining terms by root margins n_i(a). The two endpoint K
terms cancel because bK a^T+aK b^T=0. The remaining coefficient is

  a[sum_(j~i)k_j-Rk_i]-aRK a^T.

Root margins at each old type vary independently subject to total zero.
The coefficient must therefore be constant in a; at a=0 it is zero.
Evaluation at a and -a separates the linear and quadratic terms in odd
characteristic, yielding

  sum_(j~i)k_j=Rk_i,
  aRK a^T=0 for every a.

The quadratic condition means RK is skew-symmetric. Since K^T=-K,
this is equivalent to RK=KR^T, with the displayed transpose in exactly
the correct position.

Putting rows k_i^T in X turns the linear condition into BX=XR^T. The
Frobenius adjoint of Y->BY-YR is X->B^T X-XR^T=BX-XR^T. Equal rank of
an operator and its transpose gives this dependency space dimension
h_old(R). Thus all dependencies, necessarily AND sufficiently, have
dimension h_old(R)+a(R). No base semisimplicity is used here.

3. CYCLIC SELF-ADJOINT ALTERNATING FORMS VANISH

Define beta(a,b)=aKb^T. The relation RK=KR^T is precisely
  beta(aR,b)=beta(a,bR)
for the right row action. If v is a cyclic row, every a,b have the form
vf(R),vg(R). Moving polynomial actions across the form gives

  beta(vf(R),vg(R))=beta(v,vg(R)f(R)).

The right side is symmetric in f,g, so beta is symmetric on the whole
space. It is also skew-symmetric, hence zero in odd characteristic.
No nondegeneracy of beta, invertibility of R, or diagonalizability of R
is needed. Companion matrices are cyclic for both row and column actions;
the proof only needs the appropriate action in each occurrence.

Therefore a(R)=0 for every cyclic R, including reducible or nonsemisimple
cyclic matrices. The exact UNIFORM coefficient then equals p^h_old. A
total two-sector formula requires irreducibility separately.

4. MAXIMAL LATTICES, SATURATION AND GAUSSIAN FRACTION

Before edge compatibility, the independent-neighbor law generates the full
integer common-row-margin lattice L0. The legal-star law generates exactly
the sublattice defined by all vector congruences C_i(a)=0, of index
  [L0:L]=p^(k q r).
The additive-character argument in the central packet checks maximality:
on each root row, a character constant on all legal sums is a common
additive character of the neighbor sum plus slot constants. Varying a
third slot forces the Cauchy relation. Thus there are no missing periods.

The compatible integer direction lattice K0 has a direct integral basis:
choose arbitrary zero-total root margins at all old types and arbitrary
nonzero-label interior cells on every undirected edge; fill last rows
and columns from those margins. Its reduction modulo p is the ENTIRE
compatible field direction space. This avoids any denominator involving
q,d, or N and proves the required saturation. The dependency calculation
therefore gives the actual congruence rank on K0,
  rho=k q r-h_old(R)-a(R),
and the exact compatible legal index is p^rho.

The legal and independent iid table laws have the same positive covariance.
Their relative local CLT ratio on the legal affine lattice is therefore
p^(kqr)(1+o(1)). This is a relative estimate, so it remains valid after
multiplication by the nonnegative matching-factorial weights and summation.

The compatible affine lattices are Nv0+(K0 intersect L) and Nv0+K0,
where v0 is the all-zero legal star and is itself compatible. Both are
nonempty for every N. Since the lattices have fixed finite index and full
rank in the compatible real space, their translated positive-Gaussian
Riemann sums have ratio p^(-rho). Offsets may be reduced modulo a fixed
fundamental cell before scaling by 1/sqrt(N); no condition p not dividing
N enters. The local CLT, rather than an assertion that a finite support
equals its whole affine hull, supplies actual support in the central window.

Finally the exact independent-star weighted normalizer is one. Under that
weighted law one can sample a lift and iid uniform root labels; each edge
table has iid uniform matched pairs and lies in the logarithmic central
window with probability 1-o(1). The weighted Gaussian is strictly positive
precision on every compatible tangent, and its factorial powers cancel
the dimension difference. Hence the preceding density fraction applies
to mass tending to one. Combining the two indices gives exactly

  p^(kqr-rho)=p^[h_old(R)+a(R)].

The existing positive weighted precision and analytic rate-function bounds
remove the remainder of the fixed uniform neighborhood as o(1). There is
no unspecified finite lattice constant left in the coefficient.

5. IRREDUCIBILITY AND THE COMPLETE MOMENT

For R a companion of an irreducible g of degree r, its row module is simple.
The common invariant subspace phases are therefore only H=0 and H=V.
The point phases are precisely the base intertwiners, numbering p^h_old;
each has its deterministic point solution plus sparse contribution o(1).
The full uniform phase contributes p^h_old+o(1) by Sections 2-4.

For fixed p,r the point phases are finitely many and their centers differ
on a complete old fiber. Their sufficiently small support neighborhoods
are disjoint, and can be separated from the uniform edge-table neighborhood.
All remaining feasible star laws lie in a compact region with strictly
negative entropy. Uniform Stirling bounds and polynomial type counts
make its contribution o(1). Thus

  E #{Y:A_NY=YR}=2p^h_old+o(1)

along every positive N. This proof uses more than a merely bounded raw
moment. For cyclic reducible R it must not be asserted, since proper
invariant-subspace phases remain.

6. HOM DIMENSION IS GEOMETRIC MODULE MULTIPLICITY

For ANY modular matrix M and an irreducible companion R_g, evaluation at
a cyclic column v of R_g gives an isomorphism

  {Y:MY=YR_g} -> ker g(M),    Y -> Yv.

The inverse sends w to the map R_g^j v -> M^j w; the last companion
relation is exactly g(M)w=0. Hence the dimension is a multiple of r=deg g,
because ker g(M) is a vector space over F_p[t]/(g).

This dimension counts the number of g-primary CYCLIC BLOCKS times r.
It does not generally count the exponent of g in charpoly(M) times r.
For a g^2 block the Hom dimension is r, not 2r. The root proposal's
algebraic-multiplicity shorthand therefore requires its stated good-base
semisimplicity qualification; the actual assembly and transfer files
correctly use dim ker g(B) without that assumption and impose good primes
only at the final transfer step. The random lift need not be semisimple
modulo p anywhere in the argument.

7. GOOD PRIMES AND RATIONAL-TO-MODULAR RANK TRANSFER

Factor the fixed base characteristic polynomial as product f_i^t_i with
distinct monic irreducible integer f_i. Symmetry of B makes it semisimple
over characteristic zero, so F=product f_i annihilates B. Its discriminant
is a nonzero integer. Exclude p=2 and primes dividing disc(F). At every
remaining prime, F mod p is squarefree, the reductions of different f_i
are coprime, and B mod p is semisimple because it is annihilated by this
squarefree polynomial. This ONE finite bad-prime set handles all old
factors. No N-dependent minor exclusion or splitting-prime theorem is needed.

Fix f of degree ell with old exponent t. At a good prime write
  f mod p=product_j g_j,    deg g_j=r_j, sum_j r_j=ell.
Then dim ker g_j(B mod p)=r_j t. The old embedding J repeats coordinates
on fibers, is injective over EVERY field, and obeys A_NJ=JB. It injects
the old companion intertwiners into those of A_N, irrespective of p|N.

If the new real spectrum contains any root of f, rationality of the new
characteristic polynomial makes it divisible by f. One may obtain its
integer coefficients from the primitive old fiber-constant lattice or by
the monic Gauss-lemma quotient; rational coefficients alone suffice for
divisibility. Real symmetry of A_N then implies

  dim_Q ker f(A_N)>=ell(t+1).

All minors of the integer matrix f(A_N) that vanish rationally remain
zero modulo every prime. Consequently its modular nullity is at least
the same number. Since the g_j are pairwise coprime, polynomial CRT gives

  ker f(A_N mod p)=direct-sum_j ker g_j(A_N mod p)

for ANY ambient modular matrix, including matrices with higher Jordan
blocks. Restricting CRT projectors to ker f(A_N) proves this directly;
semisimplicity of the full lift is unnecessary. The sum of deterministic
old dimensions is ell t, so some j must have excess dimension, necessarily
at least r_j(t+1). This is the correct rank-transfer event implication.

Using only divisibility of modular characteristic polynomials here would
be insufficient: new algebraic factors could merge into higher Jordan
blocks. Transfer of the nullity of f(A_N) is exactly what prevents that gap.
Likewise no modular old/new direct-sum splitting is assumed when p|N.

8. SHIFTED MARKOV AND ORDER OF LIMITS

For each fixed good p and factor g_j put h_j=dim ker g_j(A_N). Then
  h_j>=r_j t,
  E p^h_j=2p^(r_j t)+o(1).
On excess, h_j>=r_j(t+1). Subtracting the deterministic old baseline
before Markov gives

  limsup_N P(h_j>=r_j(t+1))<=1/(p^(r_j)-1).

A union bound for f therefore yields at most
  sum_j 1/(p^(r_j)-1)<=ell/(p-1).
For each fixed p its irreducible factors are fixed, so the moment limit
applies before any prime changes. Only AFTER the N-limsup do arbitrarily
large good odd primes tend to infinity. A final finite union over all old
factors proves complete old/new disjointness. No error uniform in growing
p, no Chebotarev or Schur splitting-prime input, and no p not dividing N
assumption is hidden in this last step.

The same reasoning preserves every old multiplicity over the reals but
does not show mutual simplicity among new eigenvalues or characteristic-
polynomial irreducibility. Those would be stronger claims.
