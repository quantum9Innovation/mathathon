1. Exact arbitrary-target formula.
   Conditional ordered d-tuples contribute p^(d−1) possibilities per
   vertex; their count-vector convolution gives precisely Z_a. Matching
   prescribed half-edge labels contributes W(M): M_ab! off-diagonal and
   (M_aa−1)!! on diagonal. Hence exact formula(1) has the stated factor.
   The baseline B(M) counts every pairing once; no missing n_a! factor.

2. Common row mean/covariance.
   For d>=3, any two distinct positions of a d-tuple conditioned on its
   field sum are independent uniform; after deleting coordinate zero,
   covariance is the positive principal minor of
   (d/p)I−(d/p^2)11^T. Neither this step nor the lattice computation
   requires p not dividing d.

3. Difference lattice.
   The phase comparison of (a,b) with (a+u,b−u) is valid for arbitrary
   a,b,u: at least one remaining coordinate exists to complete the fixed
   sum. It forces all increments to depend only on u and hence gives
   precisely the p Fourier characters of the weighted-sum congruence.
   After deleting coordinate zero, the index is p. The unconditioned
   count vector has index1. No additional parity condition on n enters.

4. Relative moderate-deviation local limit.
   In the O(sqrt(N)log N) window the tilt is O(log N/sqrt N).
   Uniform finite support, common positive covariance and finitely many
   row laws justify the uniform inverse-function construction. Cumulant
   errors in the rate are O(log^3 N/sqrt N)=o(1), for BOTH laws, so the
   ratio is relative even when central probabilities have moderate
   Gaussian suppression. Tilted Fourier inversion has p equal-phase
   peaks on the allowed coset, common Gaussian determinant, and uniform
   exponential suppression outside neighborhoods of those peaks.
   This checks the relative-error step; an absolute CLT alone would not.

5. Arbitrary-target type tail.
   The AM--GM estimate F_t(pi)<=1 follows by fixing d−1 entries, which
   determines the remaining entry. Coefficient evaluation at
   pi_b^((d−1)/d) yields the displayed row entropy. Combining with
   log(W/T)=(dn/2)sum m_ab log m_ab+O(log n) gives exactly
   −n H(nu)−((d−2)/2)n D(m||nu nu^T).
   Balancedness contributes only O(log n) to n(log p−H(nu)); outside
   the sqrt(n)log n type window, Pinsker gives a loss c log^2 n.
   Polynomial type count and Stirling factors are therefore negligible
   relative to p^(−n), uniformly over targets.

6. Congruence coset normalization.
   Z_j=2(e_j−e_0)(e_j−e_0)^T preserves row sums and even diagonal,
   and changes Phi by 2j(e_j−e_0). These span every zero-sum congruence
   change for odd p. Central entries are all dn/p^2+O(sqrt(n)log n).
   Exact factorial ratios give B(M+Z)/B(M)=1+o(1); diagonal factors
   contribute the required half derivative, and the leading log term
   cancels by the zero total sum of Z. Enlarged/shrunken windows and
   baseline tails justify the bounded translations near window edges.
   Thus each of p^(p−1) allowed cosets has mass p^(−p+1)+o(1).

7. Final normalization and scope.
   p^((d−1)n) from the exact count, p^p from the p row lattice indices,
   and p^(−dn) from the baseline give p^(−n+p). Multiplying the coset
   mass gives p^(−n+1), as stated. The global sum constraint is necessary
   by regularity and sufficient asymptotically in the central calculation.
   Multiplying the uniform o(p^(−n)) error by at most p^n balanced
   input vectors gives o(1), with p fixed before n tends to infinity.
