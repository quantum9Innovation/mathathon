Mathematical reconstruction
---------------------------
1. Equitable bisections and exact rank.
For a d-regular graph and a sign vector tau, let x_v count opposite-sign
neighbors. Then (A tau)_v=(d-2x_v)tau_v. Therefore A tau=lambda tau is
exactly the (a,b)-equitable condition when a+b=d and a-b=lambda. A real
one-dimensional lambda-eigenspace containing sigma has only the two sign
vectors +/-sigma. Balance must also be checked; the code does check it.
The converse 'unique sign bisection implies nullity one' is not used and
would not follow. Fraction-free exact linear algebra has polynomial bit
complexity for the bounded integer entries here.

2. Signed spanning-tree identity.
S=diag(sigma) gives C=SAS with row sum lambda and
L=lambda I-C=S(lambda I-A)S. This is an incidence Laplacian with edge
weight sigma_u*sigma_v. Deleting any row of the incidence matrix and
applying Cauchy--Binet gives a sum over spanning trees of the edge-weight
products, namely (-1)^(number of crossing edges). The squared incidence
minor is one on trees and zero otherwise, even though weights can be
negative. Principal conjugation by S leaves each minor unchanged. All
cofactors equal the signed tree count Z, and differentiating det(tI-A)
gives P_A'(lambda)=N*Z. Since A is symmetric and lambda is already known
to be an eigenvalue, nonzero derivative is equivalent to multiplicity one.
Thus any one principal cofactor suffices. The sign factor from lambda I-A
to A-lambda I is (-1)^(N-1), harmless for nonvanishing and important for
the actual signed value. The source handles it correctly.

3. Finite-field certificate and reconstruction.
A principal (N-1)-minor nonzero modulo p is nonzero as an integer, so
rank_R M>=N-1. The exact verified nonzero nullvector gives rank_R M<=N-1.
No floating-point rank decision is involved. If modular nullity is one,
the planted sigma reduces to a nonzero modular vector; since all its
coordinates are +/-1, every coordinate is nonzero, and normalization at
vertex0 is valid. For odd p, residues +1 and -1 are distinct. The code
normalizes by v0=1 using M[1:,1:] z=-M[1:,0], then verifies the omitted
row as part of its full integer residual test. Consequently a nonzero
minor alone cannot cause a false accepted candidate.

If p divides d-lambda=2b, then 1 and sigma are independent modular kernel
vectors (both signs occur and p is odd); the principal solve must fail.
Other bad primes can divide all maximal minors. Returning UNDECIDED is
correct and does not assert nonuniqueness. There is no high-probability
success theorem for the three fixed primes.

4. All-real-parameter Bethe-Hessian barrier.
H(r)sigma=(r^2-lambda*r+d-1)sigma. Completing the square gives the exact
strict/equality/supercritical classification in the source. If the scalar
eigenvalue is positive, sigma is orthogonal to every negative eigenspace
of the symmetric H(r); if it is zero, the same orthogonality still holds.
This excludes retaining negative eigenspaces at any collection of real
parameters as a method of including this exact signal direction. It does
not exclude interior-eigenvector algorithms or arbitrary nonlinear graph
algorithms. Examples (2,1) and (4,3) give positive minima 7/4 and 23/4.

5. Nonbacktracking conversion.
For f_theta(u,v)=sigma_v-theta^(-1)sigma_u, summing over w~v,w!=u gives
(lambda-(d-1)/theta)sigma_v-sigma_u=theta*f_theta(u,v). The conjugate
quadratic roots have product d-1 and nonzero imaginary parts in the
embedded regime, hence modulus sqrt(d-1), and f_theta is nonzero. These
are not separated real insider modes.

6. Two-switch brittleness.
The four distinct endpoints each lose a same-sign neighbor and gain an
opposite-sign neighbor, so their residual is -2*sigma_v. Its normalized
Euclidean norm is sqrt(4*4/N)=4/sqrt(N). Degree and simplicity hold under
the stated absent-edge hypotheses. This proves fragility of the exact
equation and makes no inference that robust statistical recovery is
impossible.

7. Cube-lift theorem.
Every lift of the simple cube remains simple and cubic. The three old
coordinate characters (-1)^x_j have balanced fibers, zero pairwise inner
products, two preserving neighbors and one reversing neighbor. Their
unit-normalized fourth-power sums equal 8n/(8n)^2=1/(8n). The common
BH curve is the positive r^2-r+2.

The cube has 12-8+1=5 chords after choosing a spanning tree. Gauge the
tree permutations to identity. Conditional on its permutations, the five
chord permutations remain independent uniform permutations. Components
are their orbits. A fixed k-subset is invariant with probability
binom(n,k)^(-5); summing over subsets of size k and then k<=n/2 gives
sum binom(n,k)^(-4). The k=1 term is n^(-4); all k>=2 together are
O(n^(-7)), so the source's O(n^(-4)) bound is valid. Fixed-radius local
tree convergence follows from O(2^R) exposures and O(4^R/n) collision
probability. This is convergence at a uniform root, not a claim that all
R-balls are trees simultaneously.

The old cube eigenvalues are +/-3 (once each) and +/-1 (three each).
The cited fixed-base random-lift almost-Ramanujan theorem supplies the
new-spectrum bound. The exceptional disconnection probability tends to
zero, so discarding the two trivial bipartite eigenvalues is legitimate
for the in-probability conclusion. This is the only non-elementary input
in the cube spectral assertion; this audit checks its use, not a fresh
proof of that published theorem.

Given the realized graph and any independent vertex relabeling, the
designated index among three distinct partitions remains independent
uniform. An algorithm can exactly output at most one of them, giving
conditional success <=1/3. This is an exact designated-partition
identifiability obstruction in the stated model. It is not an assertion
that no output can weakly correlate with multiple partitions, nor a
counterexample to the uniform RSBM conjecture.

Code and arithmetic audit
-------------------------
Gaussian elimination multiplies the determinant by the unnormalized
pivot at each stage; each row swap reverses the sign. Row normalization
and elimination then preserve the tracked determinant relation. A missing
pivot correctly detects singularity modulo a prime. Back-substitution is
valid because the resulting upper triangular diagonal is one.

For the supplied diagnostics, N<=192 and p<=1019. Each residue is at
most1018, each product at most1,036,324, and each back-substitution dot
sum is below192*1,036,324<2.0e8, far below signed-int64 capacity. Matrix
construction and integer residuals are also far within range. Determinant
tracking uses Python arbitrary-size integers. The API's final degree
bound on integer lambda protects construction for its validated binary
regular graph input. This is an audit of the stated diagnostic sizes and
default primes, not an arbitrary-dimension or arbitrary-dtype arithmetic
library. Input validation is expressed as Python assertions; use normal
execution, as in the supplied runs, rather than python -O if relying on
those validations for untrusted input.

Sampler law
-----------
Uniformly shuffling the regular-graph stub list and pairing consecutive
entries samples uniform configuration pairings. Every simple d-regular
graph on the fixed vertex set has the same number of labeled-stub
realizations, namely (d!)^n. Early rejection is equivalent to rejecting
the complete pairing whenever it is nonsimple. Resampling until accepted
therefore gives the exact uniform simple regular law.

For the bipartite block, fixing the left stubs and uniformly shuffling
the right stubs gives a uniform perfect matching of the labeled stub
sets; every simple d-regular bipartite graph has (d!)^(2n) labeled-stub
matchings. Rejection again yields the uniform simple bipartite law.
The three blocks use independent draws in the ideal random model, and
the final independent vertex permutation makes the ordered balanced
partition uniform. A fixed seeded PRNG produces reproducible examples,
not true independent random variables or evidence of an asymptotic law.
All sampled degrees satisfy the usual nonnegative graphical constraints.
