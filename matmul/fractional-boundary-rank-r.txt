1. THE NORM-ONE RANK-r CLASS
============================
Fix 0<alpha<1, a positive integer r, and tau>=0. For L>0 put

  epsilon_L=exp(-L), h_L=exp(-tau L), c_0=1/(4pi^2 alpha).

Let M_(r,tau)(L) be the supremum of

  N_D(epsilon_L)=#{j:lambda_j(D)>epsilon_L},
  D=(A+E)^alpha-A^alpha,

over all finite matrices satisfying

  A>=0, E>=0, rank(E)<=r, ||E||=1,
  B=A+E<=(1+h_L)I.                                      (1)

Operator monotonicity gives D>=0, so this is also its strict singular-
value count. Define the continuous function

  C_alpha(tau)=(1/alpha-tau^2)/(4pi^2),       0<=tau<=1,
             =(1-alpha*tau)^2/[4pi^2 alpha(1-alpha)],
                                             1<=tau<=1/alpha,
             =0,                             tau>=1/alpha.

Then

  lim_(L->infinity) M_(r,tau)(L)/L^2
      =(r-1)c_0+C_alpha(tau).                            (2)

The same limit holds when rank(E)=r is required. In particular at tau=0
the coefficient is r c_0; at tau>=1/alpha it is (r-1)c_0. The exact
rank-one statement M_(1,tau)(L)=1 for every L>0 and tau>=1/alpha is
inherited from the rank-one theorem. There is no such exact-one cutoff
for the norm-one class when r>=2.

The hypotheses E>=0 and ||E||=1 ensure that at least one eigenvalue of E
is exactly one. The other r-1 eigenvalues need not approach one. This
distinction explains the projection variant in Section 4.

2. CONSTANT FACTORS IN THE STRICT TOLERANCE
==========================================
Write S_tau(L;t) for the supremal rank-one count in the same endpoint
class A>=0, B=A+ww^T<=(1+exp(-tau L))I, ||w||=1, but with strict
threshold t exp(-L), where t>0 is fixed. The rank-one joint theorem
implies

  lim_(L->infinity) S_tau(L;t)/L^2=C_alpha(tau).            (3)

Here is a proof that does not assume uniformity of the theorem in a
varying tau. For every fixed v>0, set L'=vL and tau'=tau/v. The endpoint
bound is exactly unchanged, while exp(-vL)=exp(-L'). Therefore the
supremal count at threshold exp(-vL), divided by L^2, tends to

  v^2 C_alpha(tau/v).                                    (4)

For any fixed 0<eta<1, eventually

  exp(-(1+eta)L)<=t exp(-L)<=exp(-(1-eta)L).

Monotonicity of the strict count in its threshold sandwiches the lower
and upper limiting coefficients between

  (1-eta)^2 C_alpha(tau/(1-eta))
  and (1+eta)^2 C_alpha(tau/(1+eta)).

Let eta decrease to zero and use continuity of C_alpha. This proves
(3), including tau=0 and both transitions. The same proof works uniformly
for t in any fixed compact subinterval of (0,infinity).

The second dependency gives, uniformly over all dimensions and all
0<=X,Y<=2I with rank(Y-X)<=k,

  N_(Y^alpha-X^alpha)(exp(-L)/a)
       <=k c_0 L^2+O_(alpha,k,a)(L+1)                   (5)

for every fixed a>0. Counts in (5) mean singular values when the
difference is not positive. Its rational rank proof only uses the
endpoint bound and rank, and does not require ||Y-X||=1. Indeed a type
(q,q) rational approximant on [0,2] gives rank at most kq and operator
error at most C_alpha exp(-2pi sqrt(alpha q)). This observation is
needed for the residual update below, whose norm can be less than one.

3. PROOF OF (2)
================
For an admissible E choose a unit eigenvector w at its eigenvalue one.
Set P=ww^T and F=E-P. Then F>=0, rank(F)<=r-1, and

  A<=A+P<=B<=(1+h_L)I<=2I.

Telescope the positive corrections as

  D=D_1+D_2,
  D_1=(A+P)^alpha-A^alpha,
  D_2=B^alpha-(A+P)^alpha.                               (6)

Weyl's eigenvalue inequality, or the sum of best-rank approximants,
gives for positive X,Y

  N_(X+Y)(epsilon)<=N_X(epsilon/2)+N_Y(epsilon/2).        (7)

The first term in (6) satisfies the rank-one joint hypotheses, so (3)
bounds its supremal normalized count by C_alpha(tau). Apply (5) with
k=r-1 to the second term. Taking the supremum in (7) proves the upper
coefficient in (2). When r=1 the second term is zero.

For the lower, choose a rank-one unit-norm boundary witness from the
first dependency at each L. It contributes C_alpha(tau)L^2+o(L^2)
strict counts. When that coefficient is zero, A=0,B=ww^T is sufficient.
For each of r-1 further blocks, use a positive unit rank-one fixed-R
lower witness with R=2 from the second dependency, and multiply BOTH
endpoints by 1/2. Each such scaled block has endpoint norms at most one
and update eigenvalue 1/2. Its correction is scaled by 2^(-alpha), so
its count at exp(-L) is the original count at 2^alpha exp(-L). The
fixed-R asymptotic is unchanged by this constant tolerance factor;
equivalently replace L by L-alpha log(2). Each block contributes

  c_0 L^2+o(L^2).

The direct sum satisfies (1), its nonzero update eigenvalues are exactly
1,1/2,...,1/2, and its strict counts add. It has rank(E)=r and norm one.
This proves the lower in both the rank<=r and rank=r classes. All witness
dimensions and the witnesses themselves may depend on L. A choice
within one count of each finite supremum is enough for these limits.

4. MORE THAN ONE SATURATED EIGENVALUE; PROJECTION UPDATES
========================================================
Fix an integer 1<=s<=r, and impose in (1) the further requirement that
the eigenvalue one of E have multiplicity at least s. The sharp
coefficient is

  (r-s)c_0+s C_alpha(tau).                               (8)

To prove the upper, peel off s mutually orthogonal unit eigenvectors.
The s positive unit rank-one corrections have the same boundary bound,
and the last positive residual has perturbation rank at most r-s.
Apply the version of (7) for s+1 summands with threshold epsilon/(s+1),
then (3) and (5). No simultaneous diagonalization of A and E is used.
For the lower, take s independent boundary blocks and r-s independent
scaled fixed-R blocks as in Section 3. The update has rank exactly r
and eigenvalue-one multiplicity exactly s. Thus (8) also holds with
both of those equalities imposed.

In particular for orthogonal projection updates E=P of rank exactly r,
the sharp coefficient is

  r C_alpha(tau).                                       (9)

If a full fixed spectrum is prescribed, with s entries equal to one
and the other r-s entries fixed numbers e_j in (0,1), the coefficient
is still (8). The same upper applies. For each subcritical entry choose
a fixed R_j with 1<R_j<1/e_j, use its positive unit rank-one sharp lower
witness, and scale both endpoints by e_j. Its endpoint norm is then
strictly below one, its update eigenvalue is exactly e_j, and its
correction has the unchanged leading count c_0 after a constant
tolerance rescaling. The direct sum has the prescribed spectrum.

No conclusion about eigenvalues e_j(L) that themselves vary with L is
needed or asserted here.

5. A NUCLEAR RESIDUAL FOR PROJECTION UPDATES
==========================================
Let h>=0 and suppose A>=0, P=P^T=P^2 has rank r, and
B=A+P<=(1+h)I. Put D=B^alpha-A^alpha and Q=I-P. Then there is an
explicit positive rank-at-most-r matrix F such that

  0<=D-F,  ||D-F||_*<=r h^alpha.                         (10)

Choose an orthonormal basis w_1,...,w_r of range(P), set
A_j=A+sum_(i<=j)w_iw_i^T, and telescope D=sum_(j=1)^r D_j, where
D_j=A_j^alpha-A_(j-1)^alpha>=0. Each step is a unit rank-one update
with endpoint at most 1+h. The projected-trace proof in Section 2 of
the first dependency gives

  tr[(I-w_jw_j^T) D_j (I-w_jw_j^T)]<=h^alpha.

Since Q<=I-w_jw_j^T and D_j>=0, taking traces and summing gives

  tr(QDQ)<=r h^alpha.                                    (11)

This proof uses only the already proved rank-one trace bound; no trace
subadditivity theorem is required.

For an explicit F write U=(w_1 ... w_r), K=U^T D U, and let K^dagger
be its Moore--Penrose inverse. Define

  F=D U K^dagger U^T D.                                  (12)

Indeed Y=D^(1/2)U has Gram matrix K, and Y K^dagger Y^T is the
orthogonal projection onto range(Y). Thus 0<=F<=D, rank(F)<=r, and
(D-F)U=0. Consequently D-F=Q(D-F)Q and

  ||D-F||_*=tr(D-F)<=tr(QDQ)<=r h^alpha,

which proves (10). The best rank-r truncation therefore also has
sum_(j>r)lambda_j(D)<=r h^alpha.

At every strict tolerance epsilon>0 such that r h^alpha<=epsilon,
(10) implies N_D(epsilon)<=r. The example A=0,B=P gives equality
whenever epsilon<1. Hence for h_L=exp(-tau L), the projection supremum
equals r for all L>0 satisfying

  r exp(-alpha tau L)<=exp(-L).                          (13)

In particular for tau>1/alpha this holds once
L>=log(r)/(alpha tau-1). For r=1 it also holds at tau=1/alpha.
For r>1, (13) does not hold at tau=1/alpha; no exact-r claim at that
boundary is made. The leading coefficient (9) is nevertheless zero
there, as follows independently from the rank-one joint law.

6. THE LOWER CONSTANTS ALREADY OCCUR ON CONNECTED WEIGHTED PATHS
================================================================
Every count coefficient (2), (8), and (9), including the fixed-spectrum
version in Section 4, is unchanged when the supremum is restricted to
connected weighted paths with arbitrary positive vertex masses held
fixed across the update, and exactly r existing edge conductances
increased. The r changed edges can have pairwise disjoint vertex
supports. All operators use symmetric mass normalization M^(-1/2)LM^(-1/2).
Masses, dimensions, and conductances may depend on the tolerance.

The matrix upper bounds apply immediately. For the lower, first give
each unit boundary block the stronger endpoint bound 1+h_L/2. Its
sharp coefficient is still C_alpha(tau): for every fixed eta>0 and all
sufficiently large L,

  exp(-(tau+eta)L)<=h_L/2<=exp(-tau L).

Monotonicity of the endpoint class sandwiches its limiting count
between C_alpha(tau+eta) and C_alpha(tau); let eta decrease to zero.
This includes tau=0. Section 6 of the bounded-spectrum dependency gives,
at every strict tolerance and every endpoint bound above one, equality
of the positive rank-one matrix and one-edge connected-path suprema.
Use that equality separately at each L and each unit boundary block.
It preserves the unit update norm exactly and supplies endpoint norms
at most 1+h_L/2.

For each subcritical prescribed eigenvalue e_j in (0,1), choose fixed
1<R_j<1/e_j. Apply the same pointwise path equality to a positive unit
rank-one sharp witness with endpoint bound R_j, then scale all its
conductances by e_j, keeping its masses fixed. The resulting connected
path block has update eigenvalue exactly e_j and endpoint norm at most
e_j R_j<1. Its count has coefficient c_0 after constant tolerance
rescaling. The norm-one and multiplicity-only lower constructions may
choose every subcritical e_j=1/2.

Take these r path blocks on disjoint vertex sets with their block-
diagonal mass matrix. Their direct-sum endpoint norm is at most
1+h_L/2, and their update eigenvalues are exactly those prescribed in
Sections 3 and 4. Join consecutive path endpoints by positive bridge
conductances, identical before and after the update. This gives one
connected path. The update operator is unchanged, so its exact rank,
norm, spectrum and multiplicity of one are unchanged. At each fixed L,
choose the bridges sufficiently weak that their mass-normalized
operator norm is below h_L/2 and that all finitely many eigenvalues
strictly above exp(-L) remain strictly above it. Operator-norm continuity
of fractional powers on finite positive semidefinite matrices justifies
the latter choice. Thus the endpoint bound 1+h_L and every chosen
strict count survive. This proves the graph lower assertions.

No uniform bound on masses, dimensions, or bridge sizes is needed.
This is a moving boundary with h_L>0 for every finite L. It makes no
claim of a connected positive graph realization at the exact endpoint
R=1, and makes no ordinary unit-vertex-mass assertion.

These results concern existence and sharp counts of matrix compression.
They make no claim about constructing rational poles, Gaussian Krylov
iteration counts, a matrix multiplication exponent, or runtime.
