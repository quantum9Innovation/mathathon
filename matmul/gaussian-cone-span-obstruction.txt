INDEPENDENT GAUSSIAN STARTS DO NOT REPAIR A ROBUST CONE-SPAN BRIDGE
================================================================

Working deduction, 2026-11-15 UTC. This note strengthens one quantifier
in the earlier quadratic-singular-model-search.txt obstruction: the
operator below is fixed before genuinely independent Gaussian starts.
It uses an explicit weighted Chebyshev calculation. Its restriction to
outputs in a short polynomial/Krylov span is essential. This is NOT a
raw-input arithmetic lower bound, a general adaptive-query lower bound,
or a first-discovery claim for Krylov iteration lower bounds.

1. PRECISE GAUSSIAN-START STATEMENT
==================================
Fix 0<c<=1 and put a=c/4. There are positive constants kappa_c,C_c,
gamma_c and a threshold m_c such that the following holds for m>=m_c.
Let theta=1/m, n=m-2, and define the n Chebyshev--Gauss points

  x_i=cos((i-1/2)pi/n),
  lambda_i=1-a/2+(a/2)x_i,                1<=i<=n.

The FIXED diagonal real SPD matrix is

  S=diag(1+4/m, 1, lambda_1,...,lambda_n).             (1)

Its top singular value is 1+4/m and its second singular value is exactly
one. All eigenvalues lie in [1-a,1+4/m], so its condition number is
bounded by an absolute constant. It is independent of all starts.

Let G be m-by-b with independent standard real Gaussian entries, where
1<=b<=m^(1/8), and put

  D=floor(kappa_c sqrt(m) log(m)),
  K_D(S,G)=span{G,SG,...,S^D G}.                        (2)

One may take kappa_c=sqrt(a)/100. Then

  Pr{there exists rank(C)<=1 with range(C) subset K_D(S,G)
                    and ||S-C||<=1+1/m}
       <= C_c exp(-gamma_c m^(1/3)).                   (3)

The output C need not be symmetric. Complex polynomial combinations and
complex rank-one C do not evade the proof. The real-output problem is a
special case. The statement for one Gaussian start also holds when that
start is normalized to a uniform sphere direction, since its Krylov
space does not change.

This is a statement about the whole random subspace, not one prescribed
algorithm or a predetermined polynomial. Coefficients, model fitting,
QR, column truncation and the final rank-one choice may depend arbitrarily
on all observed information, provided the output column lies in (2).

2. AN EXACT NECESSARY CONDITION FOR A RANK-ONE OUTPUT
==================================================
Let t=1+theta and lambda_h=1+4theta. Since every bulk eigenvalue is at
most one, S^2-t^2 I has precisely one positive eigenvalue
d_h=lambda_h^2-t^2. Suppose range(C) is contained in span(u), with u!=0,
and ||S-C||<=t. Projection gives

  ||(I-P_u)S||<=t,

so the quadratic form S^2-t^2 I is nonpositive on u-perp. If u_h=0,
the head vector itself contradicts this. Otherwise eliminate the head
coordinate from a vector y satisfying y*u=0 and use weighted Cauchy--
Schwarz on the remaining coordinates. The exact necessary condition is

  |u_h|^2 >= d_h sum_(bulk j) |u_j|^2/(t^2-lambda_j^2). (4)

Indeed the form on u-perp is
d_h |sum_(bulk)conj(u_j)y_j|^2/|u_h|^2
 minus sum_(bulk)(t^2-lambda_j^2)|y_j|^2. Its largest possible first
term relative to the second is exactly the right side of (4)/|u_h|^2.
This proves (4) for arbitrary, including nonsymmetric, rank-one C.

We may omit the common eigenvalue-one coordinate from the nonnegative
sum in (4). For the n remaining coordinates, write

  A_eta=1+2/(am)=cosh(eta),
  B_zeta=1+8/(am)=cosh(zeta).

For all sufficiently large m,

  sqrt(2/(am))<=eta<=2/sqrt(am),
  eta<=zeta<=3eta, zeta<=4/sqrt(am), eta,zeta<=1.       (5)

Also d_h>=6theta and t+lambda_i<=3. Since
t-lambda_i=(a/2)(A_eta-x_i), condition (4) implies

  |u_h|^2 >= (4theta/a)
                 sum_i |u_i|^2/(A_eta-x_i).           (6)

3. AN EXPLICIT WEIGHTED CHEBYSHEV BASIS
====================================
Let nu be the arcsine probability measure on [-1,1], or equivalently
dx/sqrt(1-x^2)/pi. Put z=e^(-eta). The Poisson kernel identity gives

  [1/(cosh eta-x)]dnu(x)
   =[1+2sum_(j>=1)z^j T_j(x)]dnu(x)/sinh eta.

For this weighted measure, the following polynomials are orthonormal:

  phi_0(x)=sqrt(sinh eta),
  phi_j(x)=[T_j(x)-z T_(j-1)(x)]/sqrt(z),       j>=1.  (7)

To verify this directly, use T_j(cos u)=cos(ju). The weighted integral
of T_j is z^j/sinh eta. For j>k>=1 the integral of T_j T_k is
(z^(j-k)+z^(j+k))/(2sinh eta). The difference in (7) is orthogonal to
every lower-degree polynomial. Its squared norm before division by z
is (1-z^2)/(2sinh eta)=z. The constant polynomial has weighted squared
norm 1/sinh eta. These formulas also verify the j=1 endpoint case.

Write phi(x) for the vector (phi_0,...,phi_D). Its Christoffel kernel
K_D(y,y)=||phi(y)||^2 has the following useful bound for y=cosh zeta,
where eta<=zeta<=3eta and 0<zeta<=1:

  K_D(cosh zeta,cosh zeta) <=32eta exp(2D zeta).       (8)

In fact, for j>=1,
  T_j(cosh zeta)-z T_(j-1)(cosh zeta)
    =[exp(j zeta)(1-exp(-eta-zeta))
      +exp(-j zeta)(1-exp(-eta+zeta))]/2
    <=(eta+zeta)exp(j zeta)/2<=2eta exp(j zeta).
The second exponential term is nonpositive. Sum the geometric squares,
use z>=e^-1, sinh eta<=2eta, and
1-exp(-2zeta)>=zeta/2. This gives (8), with slack in the constant.

Consequently, for every polynomial p of degree at most D,

  |p(B_zeta)|^2 <=K_D(B_zeta,B_zeta)
                   integral |p(x)|^2/(A_eta-x) dnu(x). (9)

The same inequality holds for complex coefficients, by ordinary
Cauchy--Schwarz in the orthonormal basis (7).

4. THE FINITE GRID PRESERVES THIS WEIGHTED INNER PRODUCT
=====================================================
The uniform Chebyshev--Gauss rule at x_1,...,x_n integrates polynomials
through degree 2n-1 exactly. Its rational-weight sum is explicit:

  (1/n)sum_i 1/(cosh eta-x_i)=tanh(n eta)/sinh eta.     (10)

For example, (10) follows from T_n'(cosh eta)/(n T_n(cosh eta)), the
logarithmic derivative at the roots of T_n. For every polynomial f of
degree at most 2D, D<=n, divide f(x)-f(A_eta) by A_eta-x. The quotient
has degree at most 2D-1 and is integrated exactly. Therefore

  G_n:=(1/n)sum_i phi(x_i)phi(x_i)^T/(A_eta-x_i)
       =I-delta_n phi(A_eta)phi(A_eta)^T,
  delta_n=(1-tanh(n eta))/sinh eta.                    (11)

This is an EXACT rank-one formula, not a heuristic quadrature limit.
By (8) at zeta=eta,

  delta_n K_D(A_eta,A_eta)<=64 exp(-2(n-D)eta).

For D in (2) and all sufficiently large m, D<=n/2 and this quantity
is at most 1/2. Hence

  (1/2)I <=G_n<=I.                                    (12)

5. A GAUSSIAN BLOCK GRAM LOWER BOUND
==================================
For every real x in [-1,1],

  ||phi(x)||^2/(A_eta-x) <=L_0:=3/eta+12D.             (13)

For the constant term the ratio is at most coth(eta/2)<=3/eta.
For j>=1, put x=cos u. The difference of consecutive Chebyshev values
has modulus at most 2sin(u/2), while 1-z<=eta. Using

  A_eta-x=[(1-z)^2+4z sin^2(u/2)]/(2z)

shows phi_j(x)^2/(A_eta-x)<=4/z<=4e<12. Summing proves (13).

Let g_i be row i of the bulk Gaussian block, independently N(0,I_b),
and define B_i=phi(x_i)phi(x_i)^T/(A_eta-x_i). Then B_i>=0,
||B_i||<=L_0, and sum_i B_i=n G_n. To get a bounded matrix sum from
the Gaussians, use

  V_i=B_i tensor [g_i g_i^T 1_(||g_i||^2<=16b)].

Rotational symmetry gives E[g_i g_i^T 1_(||g_i||^2<=16b)]=mu_b I_b.
For a chi-square variable Z with b degrees of freedom,

  E[Z 1_(Z>16b)]<=E[Z^2]/(16b)=(b+2)/16.

Thus 13/16<=mu_b<=1, uniformly in b>=1. The independent PSD summands
obey 0<=V_i<=16b L_0 I. Their expectation is at least (13n/32)I by
(12), and at most nI. The centered matrices E V_i-V_i have norm at
most 16b L_0 and total variance at most 16b L_0 n I. Indeed
E(V_i-EV_i)^2<=E V_i^2<=16b L_0 E V_i, and summing uses G_n<=I.

Apply the independent matrix Bernstein inequality with numerator
t^2 and denominator 3sigma^2+2Rt at t=n/8. With k=b(D+1),

  Pr{sum_i V_i not >=(n/4)I}
     <=k exp[-n/(3328 b L_0)].                         (14)

The constants follow from sigma^2<=16bL_0 n and R=16bL_0. Removing
the truncation only enlarges the PSD sum. Therefore outside the event
in (14), for ALL b-tuples of degree-at-most-D polynomials p_j,

  sum_i |sum_(j=1)^b (g_i)_j p_j(x_i)|^2/(A_eta-x_i)
       >=(n/4) sum_j integral |p_j(x)|^2/(A_eta-x)dnu. (15)

This is simultaneous over polynomials chosen after observing the
entire Gaussian block. The argument never treats those polynomials as
fixed independently of the seed. This point is essential.

Primary inequality used in (14): Mackey--Jordan--Chen--Farrell--Tropp,
Ann. Probab.42(2014), Corollary5.2, printed p922 (PDF p17):
  https://lmackey.github.io/papers/matstein-aop14.pdf
Its centered independent Hermitian assumptions apply to E V_i-V_i.

6. NO SUCCESSFUL RANK-ONE COLUMN LIES IN THE KRYLOV SPACE
======================================================
Any u in K_D(S,G) has entries sum_j(g_i)_j p_j(x_i), with degree-D
polynomials after the affine change from lambda to x. Let c be their
coefficient vector in (7), including the b channels. Let g_h be the
Gaussian row at the head. Equations (8)-(9) give

  |u_h|^2<=K_D(B_zeta,B_zeta)||g_h||^2||c||^2.         (16)

On the bulk event (15), a successful column must satisfy (6), and so

  |u_h|^2 >= (4/(am))(n/4)||c||^2
             >=(1/(2a))||c||^2,                       (17)

where n=m-2>=m/2. A zero coefficient vector gives u=0 and cannot be
successful; it is legitimate to cancel ||c||^2. Hence a necessary
condition for ANY successful column is

  ||g_h||^2 >=1/[2a K_D(B_zeta,B_zeta)].                (18)

Choose kappa_c=sqrt(a)/100 as in (2). By (5),
  2D zeta<= (2/25)log m.
Equations (5),(8),(18) now imply

  ||g_h||^2 >=m^(21/50)/(128sqrt(a)).                  (19)

This chi-square variable has b<=m^(1/8) degrees of freedom. Its moment
generating function at 1/4 is 2^(b/2), so (19) has probability at most
exp(-gamma_c m^(21/50)) for all sufficiently large m.

On the other hand, eta^-1=O_c(sqrt m), D=O_c(sqrt m log m), and
b<=m^(1/8). The bad-bulk probability (14) is at most

  O_c(m^(5/8)log m)exp[-gamma_c m^(3/8)/log m]
       <=C_c exp(-gamma'_c m^(1/3))                   (20)

after increasing m_c and decreasing positive constants. Combining
(19),(20) proves (3). No matrix, eigenbasis, or polynomial was chosen
after seeing the Gaussian starts.

7. A POLYNOMIAL-PRECISION CONE ORACLE CAN RETURN ONLY THIS SPAN
===========================================================
For any nonreal z with |Im z|>=c|z|, define the normalized raw feature

  F_z(S)=(1-z)(I-zS)^-1.

As in Section2 of quadratic-singular-model-search.txt, put
a_z=z/(1-z), so |a_z|<=1/c. For the matrix (1) and m large enough,
||S-I||<=c/4. The actual linear polynomial operator

  P_(z,L)(S)=sum_(k=0)^L a_z^k(S-I)^k

satisfies the UNIFORM operator error

  ||P_(z,L)(S)-F_z(S)||<=(4/3)4^(-L-1).                (21)

For source accuracy epsilon, let L be large enough that the right side
is at most epsilon. Returning P_(z,L)(S)G instead of F_z(S)G is a
simultaneously admissible operator-action approximation at every cone
pole, including arbitrary adaptive choices and arbitrarily many repeated
queries. For epsilon=m^-K with fixed K, L=O_K(log m), well below D in
(2). Every returned feature lies in K_D(S,G).

Consequently a method whose output column is a linear combination of
these fixed-start features cannot provide the target rank-one guarantee
with success probability bounded away from zero, even though G consists
of genuine independent Gaussians drawn AFTER a fixed well-conditioned
SPD input. Additional scalar fitting, feature-Gram whitening, adaptive
pole selection and nonlinear coefficient choice do not alter this span.
Complex conjugate poles and coefficients are covered as well.

More generally, allowing error at least the right side of (21) with
L=D permits this same bad span. Thus a uniformly robust guarantee for
this particular span-output model requires a precision logarithm of
order sqrt(m)log m. This is a necessary precision for the stated robust
bridge, not a lower bound on the arithmetic of any particular solver.

Important scope qualifications:
  * All queries act on the same supplied block G (or linear combinations
    of its columns). New arbitrary right-hand sides, and composition
    on previously returned vectors, are not covered by this oracle model.
  * The polynomial responses are coherent linear operator approximations,
    but need not be the resolvent family of one exact PSD matrix. No
    exact scalar/Gram extras or exact Herglotz-consistency promise is added.
    The earlier fixed-start note supplies a different, positivity-consistent
    example whose hard start is instead chosen with the operator.
  * Access to the differing raw matrices/factors may reveal information
    outside the feature span. No lower bound for that richer input model
    is proved. A common-backward-operator theorem or special error
    structure of a specific raw solver remains a possible way around this.

8. RELATION TO KNOWN LOWER BOUNDS AND THE NEXT RESEARCH ACTION
===========================================================
The inverse-square-root accuracy dependence and the use of Chebyshev
spectra in Krylov lower bounds are established work, not new claims here.
Musco--Musco2015 give the gap-free block Krylov spectral upper bound:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05477
Bakshi--Narayanan2023 prove stronger general matrix-vector lower bounds
for rank-one spectral LRA, including arbitrary adaptive queries. The
primary PDF, Theorem1.1, Remark1.3 and Section2.1, was inspected:
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03191
Their formulation also explicitly distinguishes matrix-vector complexity
from RAM/arithmetic complexity. Simchowitz--El Alaoui--Recht2018 gives
related PCA query lower bounds, which concern a different target:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.01221

The deduction here is a concrete fixed-SPD, independent-Gaussian-block
weighted-Chebyshev witness at the original theta=1/m scale, followed by
its precise robust far-cone filtering interpretation. It closes the
unjustified suggestion that merely drawing fresh Gaussian starts repairs
the earlier absolute-error span bridge. Its model remains narrower than
the original arbitrary-factor input problem. Historical priority for
these precise specializations has not been determined.

For progress on the original gate, the next action must therefore use
information or structure NOT certified merely by unrestricted absolute
accuracy of a batch of far-cone actions on a small Gaussian start block.
Near-real shifted solvers, a common backward operator, or genuinely
additional raw-factor information remain the concrete alternatives.
