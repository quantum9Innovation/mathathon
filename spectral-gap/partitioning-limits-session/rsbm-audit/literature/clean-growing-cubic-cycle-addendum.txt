PRIMARY ADDENDUM: A CLEAN GROWING CYCLE IN A UNIFORM SIMPLE CUBIC GRAPH
15 November 2026
====================================================================

Answer: YES. Both the growing cycle count and the needed cleanliness
are supported directly by primary theorems in the UNIFORM SIMPLE model.
No assertion of residual uniformity after conditioning or adaptive choices
is needed.

PRIMARY 1: McKay--Wormald--Wysocka2004
------------------------------------
Brendan D. McKay, Nicholas C. Wormald, Beata Wysocka,
Short cycles in random regular graphs,
Electronic Journal of Combinatorics11(2004), Research Paper66,12pages.
Author PDF actually inspected:
  https://users.monash.edu/~nwormald/papers/girth.pdf
Journal mirror:
  https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/EMIS/journals/EJC/Volume_11/PDF/v11i1r66.pdf

Read model convention, assumption(1.1), Theorems1--2, Section2's class R,
the overlapping-cycle calculation(2.7), Equation(2.9), and Lemma1.
The model is explicitly uniform over labelled simple d-regular graphs.
For d=d(N)>=3 and g=g(N)>=3 with (d-1)^(2g-1)=o(N), Theorem2 gives,
for any nonempty set C of cycle lengths in {3,...,g},

  P(no cycle of any length c in C)
    = exp(-sum_{c in C}(d-1)^c/(2c)+o(1)).

More directly relevant to cleanliness, Equation(2.9), PDFp5, gives

  P(some cycle of length in C shares an EDGE with a DIFFERENT
    cycle of length at most g)
    = O((d-1)^(2g-1)/N)=o(1).

This is exactly the overlap event needed below. A generic fixed-radius
local-tree statement would be weaker and would need growing-radius care.

PRIMARY 2: Johnson2015, explicit total-variation improvement
----------------------------------------------------------
Tobias Johnson, Exchangeable pairs, switchings, and random regular graphs,
Electronic Journal of Combinatorics22(1)(2015),P1.33.
  https://doi.org/10.37236/4659
  https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v22i1p33/pdf
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.0704v5

Actual v5 PDF dated17February2015 and publisher PDF inspected: model
definitions, Corollary8/Remark9, Theorem11 and its proof. For uniform
simple d-regular G on N vertices and admissible N,d, with C_j counting
j-cycles, and independent Z_j~Pois((d-1)^j/(2j)), Theorem11 states

  d_TV((C_3,...,C_r),(Z_3,...,Z_r))
     <= c6 sqrt(r)(d-1)^(3r/2-1)/N,

for all N>=1 and r,d>=3, with an absolute constant c6. When its auxiliary
small-r/small-d ranges fail, the theorem's bound is simply trivial.
Corollary8/Remark9 also gives the coarser bound
c5(d-1)^(2r-1)/N. Thus the proposed O(4^r/N) TV rate can safely be
credited to Johnson's explicit statement; McKay--Wormald--Wysocka's
Theorem2 supplies a sharper asymptotic specifically at zero.

PRECISE DERIVED CLEAN-CYCLE LEMMA
--------------------------------
Let N tend to infinity through even integers, G_N be uniform simple
cubic, and ell=floor(log log N), with natural logarithms for definiteness.
For large N, ell>=3. Put mu_ell=2^ell/(2ell). Then mu_ell->infinity
and 2^(2ell-1)=o(N). Consequently

  P(G_N has a clean ell-cycle)->1,

where clean means an induced ell-cycle whose ell vertices have pairwise
distinct third-neighbors, all outside the cycle. In fact every ell-cycle
is clean on the high-probability no-overlap event below.

Proof. Take d=3,g=ell,C={ell} in McKay--Wormald--Wysocka. Theorem2 gives
P(C_ell=0)=exp(-mu_ell+o(1))->0. Equation(2.9) gives failure probability
O(4^ell/N) for the event that an ell-cycle shares an edge with another
cycle of length at mostell.

On the complementary event, an ell-cycle cannot have a chord: that
chord and a shorter cycle arc form a different cycle of length<=ell,
sharing cycle edges. Hence the cubic third-neighbor of each cycle vertex
is outside. Two cycle vertices cannot have the same third-neighbor w:
the two edges to w together with a shortest arc of the ell-cycle form
a different cycle of length<=floor(ell/2)+2<=ell (also true for ell=3),
again sharing at least one cycle edge. This proves cleanliness.

Using Johnson's TV bound instead for the no-cycle probability yields the
fully explicit-shape estimate

  P(no clean ell-cycle)
   <= exp(-mu_ell)
       +O(sqrt(ell)2^(3ell/2)/N)+O(4^ell/N)
    = exp(-mu_ell)+O(4^ell/N).

Only the absolute constants in the primary results are suppressed.

NO CONDITIONAL-RANDOMNESS ASSUMPTION
------------------------------------
This lemma is an unconditional graph property. After drawing G_N, select
the lexicographically first clean ell-cycle if one exists. That is a
deterministic measurable function of the graph. For two sampled cubic
graphs, the union bound suffices to ensure both have such cycles with
probability1-o(1); independence is unnecessary for that conclusion if
their marginals are uniform. An adaptive cross graph may subsequently
be built using those cycles. Nothing here asserts the unexposed graph
edges remain uniformly random after this adaptive selection. The later
ambiguity construction must be verified deterministically on the two
clean-cycle witnesses.
