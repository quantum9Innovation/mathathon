Squared-polynomial signing averages: exact bipartite classification
Independent research note, 2026-11-15
Status: proved obstruction to a specified mechanism; no signing breakthrough.

Main conclusion
===============
For every finite simple bipartite graph G, and every probability distribution
with positive probability on every edge signing, the polynomial

    Q_G(x) = E_sigma det(x I - A_sigma^2)

is real-rooted if and only if G is a forest. In particular, increasing girth
does not restore this expected-polynomial real-rootedness: it fails for every
finite d-regular bipartite graph with d >= 2, whatever its girth.

This is a classification inside the bipartite class, not a classification of
all graphs. It does not contradict MSS: on bipartite graphs their ordinary
adjacency characteristic polynomials already control both spectral ends.

The ingredients extend further. A positive average of squares of monic
real-rooted polynomials is real-rooted exactly when all the polynomials are
identical. Thus, restricting to any signing family with different compressed
squared spectra still fails, even with a nonuniform distribution. Also, every
fixed nonconstant even polynomial transform p(A_sigma) fails the same test
on even cycles of arbitrarily large girth.

1. Positive-square averaging lemma
=================================
Let p_1,...,p_N be monic real-rooted real polynomials of the same degree k,
and let w_i > 0 with sum_i w_i = 1. Then

    S(x) = sum_i w_i p_i(x)^2

is real-rooted if and only if p_1 = ... = p_N.

Proof. Let h be the monic greatest common divisor of all p_i and write
p_i = h r_i. The polynomial h is real-rooted. The r_i have no common
complex zero, hence in particular no common real zero. Therefore

    S(x)/h(x)^2 = sum_i w_i r_i(x)^2 > 0  for every real x.

Its degree is 2(k-deg h), since every r_i is monic and all weights are
positive. If deg h < k this is a nonconstant polynomial with no real roots,
so S cannot be real-rooted. If deg h = k, monicity implies all p_i=h,
and S=h^2 is real-rooted. This also proves the claim for any finite support
distribution, with its support substituted for {1,...,N}. No independence
assumption on the random signs is needed.                                      QED

More exactly, if h is that greatest common divisor, then h^2 is the entire
real-root factor of S, with multiplicities. At a real zero a of h, the
multiplicity of S is exactly 2 min_i ord_a(p_i), because the leading
nonzero terms in the positive square sum cannot cancel. Thus S has exactly
2(k-deg h) nonreal roots, counted with multiplicity.

2. Bipartite squared-spectrum factorization
==========================================
Write a bipartite signed adjacency in block form

    A_sigma = [ 0       B_sigma ]
              [ B_sigma^T   0  ],

where B_sigma is a by b and a >= b. Define the monic degree-b polynomial

    p_sigma(x) = det(x I_b - B_sigma^T B_sigma).

All its zeros are real and nonnegative. The singular-value identity gives

    det(x I_(a+b) - A_sigma^2) = x^(a-b) p_sigma(x)^2.          (1)

Consequently Q_G is real-rooted exactly when all p_sigma in the support
are identical, by the positive-square lemma. Multiplication by x^(a-b)
does not remove nonreal roots.

The ordinary adjacency characteristic polynomial satisfies

    det(t I_(a+b) - A_sigma) = t^(a-b) p_sigma(t^2).           (2)

Thus equality of all p_sigma is equivalent to signing-independence of
the ordinary signed characteristic polynomial.

3. Signing-independence is equivalent to being a forest
======================================================
On a forest, every edge signing is diagonal-switching equivalent to the
all-positive signing. One chooses a root in each component and fixes the
vertex-switching signs successively along its tree edges. Hence all signed
adjacency matrices are orthogonally conjugate and all p_sigma identical.

Conversely, let C be a simple cycle of length ell. In the determinant
expansion of det(tI-A_sigma), regard signs as independent characters on
{+1,-1}^E. The Fourier coefficient of the character

    chi_C(sigma) = product_(e in E(C)) sigma_e

in the coefficient of t^(|V|-ell) is -2. Indeed, to use this character at
that degree, a contributing permutation must move exactly the ell cycle
vertices, use every cycle edge to odd multiplicity, and fix all other
vertices. Every edge in this character is then used exactly once. The only
possibilities are the two orientations of C. Each contributes -chi_C,
since its permutation sign is (-1)^(ell-1) and its ell off-diagonal
matrix entries contribute (-1)^ell. No matching or other permutation
can supply the same Fourier character at that degree.

This nonzero Fourier coefficient proves that the characteristic polynomial
varies with the signing. For bipartite G, ell is even and (2) implies that
the compressed polynomials p_sigma vary as well. Equation (1) and the lemma
finish the classification.                                                  QED

The shortest-cycle assumption is unnecessary. Even in a graph with chords,
the displayed Fourier character singles out the chosen cycle edges.

4. Arbitrarily large-girth exact examples
========================================
For the even cycle C_n, n >= 4, a signing's switching class is determined
by its cycle sign s in {+1,-1}. The adjacency characteristic polynomial is

    c_s(t) = 2[T_n(t/2)-s],

where T_n is the first-kind Chebyshev polynomial. Since n is even, put
H_n(x)=2 T_n(sqrt(x)/2); this is a real monic polynomial of degree n/2.
The two squared-adjacency characteristic polynomials are (H_n(x)-2s)^2,
so under the uniform signing distribution

    Q_(C_n)(x) = H_n(x)^2 + 4.                             (3)

It has no real roots. The C4 specialization is

    (x^2-4x+2)^2+4.

That specialization is already in Suvagiya v2, Remark 6, and is not claimed
as new here. Formula (3) and the bipartite classification show precisely
why the failure cannot be repaired by a large-girth assumption.

5. Every fixed even polynomial transform also fails at arbitrary girth
=====================================================================
Let p(t) be any fixed nonconstant even real polynomial of degree r, with
leading coefficient c != 0. For every n divisible by r, n >= 4, the even
cycle C_n has two switching classes. Their p(A_sigma) characteristic
polynomials are squares of monic real-rooted degree-n/2 polynomials, because
the eigenvalues of A_sigma occur in pairs +lambda,-lambda and p is even.

These two compressed polynomials differ. To see this, write n=rm and use
the two spectral grids

    lambda_(+,j) = 2 cos(2 pi j/n),
    lambda_(-,j) = 2 cos((2 pi j+pi)/n),  0 <= j < n.

The Laurent polynomial p(z+z^(-1))^m has maximum and minimum exponents
n and -n, each with coefficient c^m. Summing it over the two grids keeps
only exponents divisible by n. The constant coefficients cancel in the
difference, while the two extreme coefficients give exactly

    tr p(A_+)^m - tr p(A_-)^m = 4 n c^m != 0.             (4)

Thus the p(A_+) and p(A_-) spectra differ, as do their compressed monic
characteristic polynomials. By the square-averaging lemma, every mixture
giving both switching classes positive weight has a non-real-rooted average
characteristic polynomial of p(A_sigma).

Because n can be arbitrarily large, no fixed even polynomial p can restore
ordinary expected-characteristic-polynomial interlacing merely by requiring
large girth. This includes fixed Chebyshev filters, shifts of A_sigma^2,
and higher even powers. The conclusion concerns the full characteristic
polynomial of p(A_sigma). It does not rule out specialized compressed
polynomials, nonpositive combinations, rational matrix measures, or a
different constrained root-selection theorem.

6. Dilation interpretation and exact remaining gap
==================================================
The symmetric bipartite dilation of A_sigma is

    D(A_sigma) = [ 0        A_sigma ]
                 [ A_sigma    0    ].

Its norm equals ||A_sigma|| and

    det(tI-D(A_sigma)) = det(t^2 I-A_sigma^2).

For original bipartite G containing a cycle, the expected polynomial on the
right is Q_G(t^2), which has a nonreal zero: if z is a nonreal zero of Q_G,
every square root of z is nonreal. Thus imposing the equality constraints
between the two lifted copies of every signed edge does not produce an
MSS interlacing family for the ordinary dilation characteristic polynomials.
The obstruction already occurs for every cyclic bipartite original graph.

An unconstrained MSS signing of the bipartite double cover assigns its two
copies of each original edge independently. Its off-diagonal matrix need not
be symmetric and cannot be identified with a signing of the original graph.
The missing ingredient is a root-selection theorem respecting that equality
constraint, with a different polynomial object or a controlled relaxation.
The calculations here supply no such theorem and no 2+o_g(1) signing bound.

7. Taking one derivative: a repair that works on cycles, then fails
=================================================================
The obstruction above must not be read as ruling out derivative-based
polynomial mechanisms. In the balanced bipartite case every leaf polynomial
p_sigma^2 has repeated largest root. Its first derivative 2p_sigma p_sigma'
has the same largest root. Thus it is reasonable to test whether Q_G' can
be used in place of Q_G. On a single even cycle it passes the first gate:
(3) implies Q_G'=2H_n H_n', which is real-rooted.

It fails already on connected K3,3. Gauge its 3-by-3 sign matrix as

    B = [1 1 1; 1 a b; 1 c d],  a,b,c,d independently in {+1,-1}.

Among these sixteen equally likely switching classes, p_sigma has the
following exact distribution:

    multiplicity 9: x(x^2-9x+16),
    multiplicity 6: (x-4)^2(x-1),
    multiplicity 1: x^2(x-9).

Squaring and averaging yields

    Q(x) = x^6-18x^5+117x^4-336x^3+468x^2-288x+96,
    Q'(x)/6 = f(x)
       = x^5-15x^4+78x^3-168x^2+156x-48.

For an exact certificate that f has only one real root, its Sturm chain is

    f,
    5x^4-60x^3+234x^2-336x+156,
    (6/5)(4x^3-33x^2+64x-38),
    (11x^2-184x+354)/16,
    -384(190x-427)/121,
    142901/577600.

At negative infinity the signs are (-,+,-,+,+,+), giving three variations;
at positive infinity they are (+,+,+,+,-,+), giving two. Sturm's theorem
therefore gives exactly one real root, although deg f=5. This disproves
unrestricted first-derivative real-rootedness for connected regular
bipartite graphs.

For arbitrary girth there is also a simple disconnected regular example:
take the disjoint union of two copies of C_n, n even. Independence of the
two components gives Q=(H_n^2+4)^2, hence

    Q' = 4(H_n^2+4) H_n H_n'.

The factor H_n^2+4 has no real roots. Thus the derivative repair fails at
every even girth on this disconnected degree-two family. This observation
does not resolve the first-derivative mechanism for connected d>=3 graphs
of sufficiently large girth; no such theorem or counterexample is claimed.

8. Primary-source comparison and limits of novelty checking
=========================================================
MSS, arXiv:1304.4132v2, Theorem 5.1 proves real-rootedness of expected
ordinary signed adjacency characteristic polynomials under arbitrary
independent edge-sign probabilities; Theorem 5.2 supplies interlacing;
Theorem 5.3 selects the upper spectral edge. Section 2.2 explains why
bipartite symmetry controls the lower edge too. Lemma 4.5 gives the usual
equivalence between common interlacing and real-rooted convex combinations.
Read directly at https://arxiv.org/html/1304.4132 .

Suvagiya, arXiv:2607.17343v2, Section 7, Remark 6 gives the C4 formula
above and numerical observations for K3,3, Q3, Petersen, and K4. It explicitly
calls those observations finite computations rather than a classification,
and says a broader characterization remains open. The bipartite theorem
here is a proved subclass characterization; no general classification or
historical-priority claim is made. Version 2's introduction and Section 2
also explain that its bicycle-free transfer is not a new concentration
theorem. Read directly at https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17343v2 .

Xu, arXiv:2609.15591v4, Theorem 1.1 states a finite connected simple cubic
counterexample to exact general Bilu-Linial. That result is compatible with
the still-separate high-girth asymptotic target. Read directly at
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15591v4 .

Targeted searches for the bipartite forest characterization and for squared
signing averages did not locate the theorem as stated. This is not evidence
of historical novelty. The proof is elementary enough that prior implicit
appearances are quite possible.

Verification
============
verify_squared_average.py checks the factorization, the classification on
selected forests and cyclic bipartite examples, all-even-cycle identity (3)
for a finite range, trace identity (4) for several even polynomials and
cycle sizes, and the K3,3 derivative and Sturm certificate using exact
rational arithmetic. These are implementation checks;
the universal proofs are Sections 1--5 above.
