RSBM FINITE-FIELD PRIORITY AND APPLICABILITY AUDIT
November 15, 2026. signing_frontier; only this file is written by this task.

Outcome
-------
No inspected theorem directly resolves the full target in the exact
uniform-component model for every d1>d2>0 and every admissible block size.
However, the strong pointwise mixing theorem of Meszaros gives a short
and apparently useful PARTIAL COROLLARY: if d1>=3 and the block size N
is even, the internal matching-sum model has

  E |ker_Fp(A-(d1-d2)I)| = 2p+o_{p,d1,d2}(1)               (1)

for every fixed odd prime p not dividing d2, uniformly over every fixed
simple d2-regular bipartite cross matrix B. This proves planted-eigenvalue
simplicity with high probability in that matching-sum model. Conditioning
and classical contiguity transfer the HIGH-PROBABILITY CONCLUSION to two
independent uniform simple internal d1-regular graphs, still for even N.
Contiguity alone does not transfer the unbounded expectation (1).

This is a derived corollary of an existing theorem, not a claimed new
multitype mixing theorem. Its proof is written below for independent
review. The internal degree-two case (d1,d2)=(2,1), and odd block sizes
when d1 is even, remain outside this argument. Full-conjecture recovery
is not claimed. Historical priority of this partial corollary is unverified.

1. Exact model and the sufficient finite-field criterion
---------------------------------------------------------
Each block has N vertices. Independently choose uniform simple internal
d1-regular graphs with adjacency matrices C1,C2 and a uniform simple
d2-regular bipartite cross graph with biadjacency B. Thus

  A = [ C1   B  ],       lambda=d1-d2,
      [ B^T  C2 ]        sigma=(1_N,-1_N).

The identity A sigma=lambda sigma is exact. The all-one vector has
eigenvalue d1+d2, so it gives no additional forced vector modulo an odd
prime p not dividing d2. Primes dividing lambda, d1, or N need not be
excluded for the matching-sum argument below. Primes dividing 2d2 must
be excluded: then both quotient eigenvalues coincide modulo p.

For any integer realization let k_p=dim_Fp ker(A-lambda I). Since k_p>=1,

  P(k_p>=2) <= [E p^{k_p}-p]/(p^2-p).                      (2)

Real nullity at least two implies modular nullity at least two for EVERY
prime, because all minors of size 2N-1 then vanish as integers. Thus (1)
and (2) imply

  limsup_N P(real nullity>=2) <= 1/(p-1).

Let p tend to infinity only AFTER the limit superior in N. This gives
real nullity one with high probability. No estimate uniform in p is
needed for the error o_{p,d1,d2}(1); the coefficient 2 of p is independent
of p. More generally the requested bound limsup_N E p^{k_p}<=C p with
C independent of p suffices by the same argument.

2. Precise classical mixing input
---------------------------------
Meszaros, The distribution of sandpile groups of random regular graphs,
arXiv:1806.03736v4, Theorem 1.6, studies C=M1+...+Mr, r>=3, on EVEN N,
where Mj are independent uniform perfect-matching matrices. For a fixed
finite abelian group V it gives a summed point-probability error tending
to zero for Cq versus uniform measure on the exact attainable constraint
set R^S(q,r). For V=Fp and p odd, a NONCONSTANT q has minimal affine
coset Fp and the tensor restriction is vacuous, because 2 is invertible.
The constraint set is exactly

  {s in Fp^N : sum_i s_i = r sum_i q_i},

of cardinality p^{N-1}. Consequently, for the random C above, define

  e_N(q)=max_{s: sum s=r sum q}
          |P(Cq=s)-p^{-(N-1)}|   (q nonconstant).

The existing theorem implies

  delta_N := sum_{q nonconstant} e_N(q) ->0.              (3)

All parameters V=Fp and r are fixed in (3). The theorem is stronger than
the bare nonsingularity theorem at zero: its maximum over target s is
what allows the shifted, B-dependent targets used next. No coprimality
condition on p and r is needed for this mixing statement.

Primary source and exact scope inspected:
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.03736
  https://doi.org/10.1090/tran/8127
Read the Introduction through Theorems 1.5-1.7, definitions of R^S,
and the discussion of fixed-V uniformity; inspected Section 6's scope
and Section 7's matching-model setup and conclusion. This was not a
fresh full reading of all 67 pages. The older numbering Theorem 4 is
also reproduced explicitly in Nguyen--Wood, Section 2:
  https://arxiv.org/html/1806.10068

3. Full proof of the partial matching-model corollary
------------------------------------------------------
Let r=d1>=3, s=d2>=1 be fixed, N even, and C1,C2 independent copies
of C in Section 2. Fix ANY deterministic simple s-regular bipartite
biadjacency matrix B, and put lambda=r-s. Let p be odd with p not
dividing s. All quantities in this section are over Fp.

We count pairs (x,y) satisfying

  C1 x = lambda x - B y,
  C2 y = lambda y - B^T x.                                (4)

Sum the first equation's coordinates. Since all row and column sums of
Ci are r and of B are s, its necessary sum constraint is

  r sum x = (r-s) sum x-s sum y,
  equivalently sum x+sum y=0.                             (5)

The second equation yields the identical condition. Conversely (5)
ensures BOTH targets lie in their mixing constraint sets when x,y are
nonconstant. This remains true when p divides r, lambda, or N.

Case I: x and y both nonconstant.
Put u=p^{-(N-1)}. For a pair obeying (5), independence gives its
probability as (u+a_{x,y})(u+b_{x,y}), with

  |a_{x,y}|<=e_N(x),       |b_{x,y}|<=e_N(y).

Every pair violating (5) has probability zero. The number M_N of
nonconstant pairs obeying (5) is

  M_N=p^{2N-1}-2p^N+c_N,
  c_N=p if p does not divide N, and c_N=p^2 if p divides N.

Indeed a nonzero coordinate-sum linear functional has p^{N-1} points
in every fiber, including when p divides N; only the intersection of
the two constant-vector exclusions changes. Hence M_N u^2=p+o(1).
Summing the probability errors over all admissible pairs gives

  |sum_{Case I} P((4))-M_N u^2|
      <= 2u p^{N-1} delta_N + delta_N^2
       = 2 delta_N+delta_N^2 ->0.                         (6)

This estimate is independent of B and uses the SUMMED errors (3),
rather than a pointwise o(1) multiplied by exponentially many vectors.
Thus Case I contributes p+o(1).

Case II: x=a 1_N is constant and y is nonconstant.
The first equation of (4) is equivalent to

  B(y+a 1_N)=0.

For each a there are at most p^{N-rank B} possibilities for y. The
second equation, conditional on y, has probability at most u+e_N(y)
whenever its sum constraint holds, and zero otherwise. Consequently
the entire case contributes at most

  p^2 p^{-rank B} + p delta_N.                           (7)

There is an elementary rank bound rank_Fp B>=N/s^2. Choose rows
greedily with pairwise disjoint supports. Each row has a nonempty
support of size at most s, since B is simple and s>=1. Each selected
row intersects the supports of at most s^2 rows, since each column has
at most s nonzero entries. Thus at least N/s^2 pairwise disjoint,
nonzero rows can be chosen, and they are linearly independent over
any field. Equation (7) therefore tends to zero, uniformly in B.
The case y constant and x nonconstant is identical.

Case III: both x=a1_N and y=b1_N are constant.
The equations reduce to s(a+b)=0, so b=-a. There are exactly p
solutions, the deterministic planted line, each occurring surely.

Adding the cases proves E|ker(A-lambda I)|=2p+o(1), uniformly over B.
Together with (2) and the arbitrarily-large-prime argument this proves
real planted-eigenvalue simplicity with high probability in this
matching model, for r>=3 and even N. The randomness of B is unnecessary
for this conclusion.

4. Transfer to the exact simple internal model, with honest limits
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The unconditioned sum of r independent matching matrices may have
parallel edges. For fixed r its probability of being simple stays
bounded away from zero. Conditioning both independent internal graphs
on simplicity therefore preserves the probability-o(1) conclusion.
The conditioned matching-sum law is NOT identically uniform on simple
r-regular graphs: graphs can have different numbers of factorizations.
The required transfer uses classical CONTIGUITY for r>=3, not equality
of these laws and not a raw-moment transfer.

A directly inspected primary contiguity statement is Greenhill--Janson--
Kim--Wormald, Permutation pseudographs and contiguity, Theorem 1.3 and
Corollary 1.1. Take r degree-one summands; the corollary states that
their graph-restricted sum is contiguous with the uniform simple
r-regular model when the summed degree is at least three.
  https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~csg/papers/gjkw-revised.pdf

Contiguity is preserved under a fixed finite product of independent
measures. One elementary justification uses the equivalent uniform
absolute-continuity characterization: for each eta>0 there is delta>0
such that a sufficiently small reference probability (<delta) forces
the other probability to be <eta, for all sufficiently large N.
Apply this to sections of a product event and then split according to
whether the section probability exceeds delta. Thus the pair of
conditioned internal laws transfers to the pair of uniform internal
laws. The matching-model estimate was uniform over B. Alternatively,
if the transferred result were not uniform over B, choose a violating
deterministic sequence B_N and apply product contiguity to that sequence.

Therefore for r=d1>=3, even N, and independent uniform simple internal
graphs, the planted eigenvalue is simple with high probability uniformly
over all deterministic simple s=d2 regular cross graphs B. In particular
it holds for the requested independent uniform simple random B.

The expectation (1) itself is asserted ONLY before the contiguity step.
An unbounded random variable need not preserve its moments under
contiguity. The transferred claim is the real simplicity event.

Neither the matching input nor this transfer proves anything for odd N
when r is even: there are no perfect matchings on an odd vertex set.
This is an unresolved subsequence for this particular proof, not an
asserted obstruction to the conjecture.

5. Why the inspected Huang, Cook, and cokernel statements are not
   blanket solutions; what remains at (2,1)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Huang, Invertibility of adjacency matrices for random d-regular graphs,
arXiv:1807.06465v2, Theorem 1.1, treats one-type directed and undirected
configuration models with d>=3, p coprime to d, and gives the expected
number of NONZERO solutions Av=0 as 1+o(1) for odd p (with a separate
characteristic-two correction). Theorems 1.2-1.3 turn that into modular
and real nonsingularity. Its statement does not contain a prescribed
nonzero shift, a forced planted line, or independent regular blocks.
Section 2's walk enumeration is tied to sum of neighbor labels zero.
Changing it to lambda times the vertex label is an additional argument.
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.06465
  https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2021-0006
Read the full main-results section and initial enumeration setup in
the actual v2 PDF, not only its abstract. No full-paper audit claimed.

Nguyen--Wood, Cokernels of adjacency matrices of random r-regular graphs,
arXiv:1806.10068v2, Theorems 1.3 and 1.5, concern sums of permutations
or matchings, with the stated prime-to-r restrictions. Section 2 exposes
the stronger Meszaros input used above. Their nonsingularity conclusions
at zero do not alone imply shifted planted simplicity. Their Section 4
also explains the matching-to-uniform contiguity transfer; it is useful
for scope, not a substitute for the block calculation (4)-(7).
  https://arxiv.org/html/1806.10068
Inspected Sections 1-2 and the relevant nonsingularity/contiguity parts
of Section 4. No full-source claim.

Cook, On the singularity of adjacency matrices for random regular
digraphs, arXiv:1411.0243v3, studies a uniform NONSYMMETRIC matrix with
fixed row and column sums, with min(d,n-d)>=C log^2 n in the principal
theorem. Its signed Hadamard-product extension uses independent signs.
Those hypotheses do not cover fixed sparse symmetric RSBM blocks.
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.0243
Actual abstract, model definition, and main theorem scope inspected;
no proof audit or finite-field block extension is claimed.

At (d1,d2)=(2,1) the internal components are uniform simple 2-factors;
the cross matching can be gauged to B=I by relabeling the second block.
Writing x,y for a candidate lambda=1 eigenvector gives

  y=(I-C1)x,
  [C2 C1-C2-C1]x=0.                                     (8)

The planted vector corresponds to x=1_N. Equation (8) is a useful exact
reduction, not a nonsingularity theorem for that random product.

Meszaros's mixing theorem assumes internal degree at least three. It
cannot be invoked with degree two merely because the TOTAL graph degree
is three: the global matrix is sampled from a different, block-constrained
ensemble. Replacing each internal uniform 2-factor by a sum of two
matchings is invalid: the latter has only even cycles. Replacing it by
P+P^T for a uniform permutation also changes cycle weights. The inspected
Greenhill--Janson--Kim--Wormald source explicitly warns that distinct
degree-two weighted permutation models are not contiguous.

The only deterministic modular obstruction established here is the
collision when p divides 2d2; this is already excluded in (1)-(2).
I have not proved a further obstruction, or a matching Cp estimate,
for the exact (2,1) model. In particular (8) must not be presented as
showing it is covered by ordinary random cubic nonsingularity.

6. Additional nearby literature and bounded-search conclusion
-------------------------------------------------------------
Targeted searches included cokernels and random lifts, multitype or
block-regular configurations, shifted regular matrices, and prescribed
eigenvalues. No directly applicable all-parameter block theorem was
identified in the sources actually inspected.

Gorokhovsky, Time-inhomogeneous random walks on finite groups and
cokernels of random integer block matrices, arXiv:2405.11435 / CPC2026,
DOI10.1017/S0963548325100357, is a nearby dependent-entry universality
source. Inspected the actual v4 Introduction, Theorem 1.2, and Definitions
3.2 and 3.8. The theorem requires independent matrix blocks, each balanced
against every coset of every proper additive subgroup, with balance
epsilon_N=Omega(N^{-beta}) and 2alpha_1+alpha_2<1-2beta. In particular
beta<1/2. It does not directly apply here: a deterministic diagonal
coordinate forces balance zero (project to that coordinate modulo a
prime), while even a random off-diagonal coordinate has zero with
probability 1-O(1/N), bounding any block balance by O(1/N). A proposed
projection or modification to remove these obstructions would need a
new verification of its hypotheses, not just its title's word "block."
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11435
  https://arxiv.org/html/2405.11435
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548325100357

The separate actual-source audit
  spectral-gap/partitioning-rsbm-obstruction-session/source-audit.md
explains the Zhu--Zhu Lemma 9.1 citation gap and why random-lift local
laws do not exclude a single exact old/new eigenvalue collision. That
overbroad lemma is not used anywhere in the proof above.

Assessment: the fixed-prime-then-large-prime method and the required
strong mixing theorem are classical. A full multitype finite-field
theorem is unnecessary for the even-N, d1>=3 partial result: independent
internal mixing and the elementary sum constraint suffice. Extending
this argument to the missing odd-N subsequence or the exact (2,1)
ensemble is real remaining work. No complete Barucca-conjecture solution
or certified novelty claim follows from this note.
