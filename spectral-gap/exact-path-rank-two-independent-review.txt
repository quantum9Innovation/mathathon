1. Rank is exactly the edited vector's cyclic dimension

The rank-one fractional resolvent formula is a Gram integral with
strictly positive scalar weight on all t>0. A vector is in its kernel
exactly when it is orthogonal to every (A+tI)^(-1)u, hence to every
A^k u. This uses continuity/analyticity and the large-t resolvent
expansion; it does not assert that an integral of positive matrices
retains the rank of one summand. Congruence by A^(-alpha/2) preserves
the cyclic space and its orthogonal complement. Restriction to 1-perp
handles the common graph kernel. The resulting exact equality
rank C=dim span{b,Lb,L^2b,...} is correct for every 0<alpha<1.

2. Complete path classification

For an edge between positions j and j+1, L^k b has a nonzero
coefficient, the product of the intervening positive conductances,
at each available vertex at distance k along either arm. Earlier powers
vanish at that coordinate. Thus a length-r arm forces cyclic dimension
at least r+1. Cyclic dimension two forces j<=2 and N-j<=2: N<=4.

N=2 has rank one. N=3 always has rank two because Lb has a nonzero
coordinate outside the support of b. N=4 requires the middle edge.
Writing its weights (a,m,c),
  Lb=(-a,a+2m,-c-2m,c).
The first and last coordinates of L^2b=s Lb+p b give
  s=2(a+m)=2(c+m),
so a=c. Conversely reflection antisymmetry gives exactly a two
dimensional invariant cyclic space when a=c>0. This exhausts every
finite path, including arbitrary added vertices with the intended
extra correction eigenvalues exactly zero.

3. Independent general two-by-two trace identity

Let A>0 be two dimensional, B=A+uu^T, tau=u^T A^(-1)u,
K=sqrt(1+tau), t=tr A, d=sqrt(det A), and e=||u||^2.
For Q=A^(-1/4) B^(1/2) A^(-1/4), direct two-by-two functional calculus
gives

  det Q=K,
  tr Q=[(K+1)t+(K+1)^2*d+e]
             /sqrt[(t+2d)(t+e+2K*d)].                 (R1)

Derivation: sqrt(B)=(B+K*d I)/sqrt(t+e+2K*d).
The inverse-root formula and leverage identity give
  u^T A^(-1/2)u=(e+tau*d)/sqrt(t+2d).
Substitution in tr(A^(-1/2)sqrt(B)) proves (R1).

For the three-vertex path let r=edited/other conductance, c0=2/sqrt(3).
Then t/d=c0(sqrt(r)+1/sqrt(r)) and e/d=c0*tau*sqrt(r).
For the symmetric four-vertex path use r=middle/outer and c0=sqrt(2);
the same equations hold on the antisymmetric active subspace.
Putting z=sqrt(r) in (R1) yields

 (tr Q)^2=(K+1)^2 [c0*K*z^2+(K+1)z+c0]^2 /
   [(c0*z^2+2z+c0)(c0*K^2*z^2+2K*z+c0)].             (R2)

Let w=z+1/(K*z)>=2/sqrt(K), N=c0*K*z^2+(K+1)z+c0, and let D denote
the denominator product in (R2). Then
  D/(K*z^2)-N^2/(K*z^2)=(c0^2-1)(K-1)^2/K.
This constant is positive, and N^2/(K*z^2) is increasing in w.
Thus the global minimum is attained exactly at r=1/K and equals

 (K+1)/sqrt[1+kappa*(K-1)^2/
                   (K+1+2sqrt((1+kappa)K))^2],         (R3)

where kappa=c0^2-1 is 1/3 or 1. This matches the source's formula,
including its different canonical x coordinate. Differentiation shows
kappa/(K+1+2sqrt((1+kappa)K))^2 is increasing in kappa, so the
four-vertex family gives the global path minimum.

At alpha=1/2,tau=1 this minimum is approximately
  tr Q >= 2.4080334082788246,
with equality at middle/outer=1/sqrt(2). The corresponding eigenvalues
are approximately 1.3922789188125593 and 1.0157544894662653.
These decimals are diagnostics; the exact inequality (R3) is the result.

4. Explicit excluded target and exact rational gap

Target q=(5/4,4sqrt(2)/5) has distinct entries >1 and product sqrt(2).
The source's exact comparison proves every rank-two path has tr Q>12/5,
while this target has trace <12/5. I checked the rational reductions:
  424sqrt(2)>591 follows from 359552>349281;
  sqrt(2)<23/16 follows from 512<529.
This supplies a strict excluded neighborhood, not just nonattainment
of the equal-eigenvalue boundary point. The target therefore disproves
exact path realization even for a simple positive correction spectrum.

5. Independent exact SPD matrix witness

The target's matrix admissibility can be checked without relying on
the new general inverse-spectrum theorem. Set

  A_delta=diag(delta,1),
  u_delta=(sqrt(delta/2),1/sqrt(2)).

Its leverage is exactly one. With x=(1+delta)/sqrt(delta)>=2,
formula (R1) gives a continuous trace. At delta=1 it is 1+sqrt(2).
As delta decreases to zero the limiting trace squared is
  17/6+2sqrt(2).
The desired trace squared is
  1137/400+2sqrt(2),
which is larger by exactly 11/1200 and smaller than the delta=1
trace squared. The intermediate value theorem therefore gives an
exact positive-definite witness.

An explicit radical witness follows. Let K=sqrt(2) and let x be the
positive root of
  11x^2-(378+252K)x-(800+504K)=0.
This root exceeds two. Put
  y=2/(x+sqrt(x^2-4)),   delta=y^2.
Then A_delta,u_delta have relative eigenvalues exactly
(5/4,4sqrt(2)/5), because their trace and determinant equal the
specified sum and product. Numerically delta is approximately
0.00021158602083345455. I also checked the source's alternative
continuous-kappa construction and its explicit w formula; they agree.
