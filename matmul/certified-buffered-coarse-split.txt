CERTIFIED BUFFERED COARSE SPLITS WITHOUT AN EXACT EIGENBASIS ORACLE
=================================================================

Working deduction, 2026-11-15 UTC. This removes the supplied-exact-split
condition from near-real-deflated-raw-solves.txt. It uses an existing
fast approximate Hermitian diagonalization theorem on a bounded explicit
coarse transform, followed by a weighted leakage correction. It does NOT
bound the dimension of a general coarse spectral band, prove the original
arbitrary-factor LRA target, or assert finite-word stability of the raw
core inversions. Historical priority is not asserted.

1. THE PREPROCESSING THEOREM
----------------------------
As in the raw-factor notes, let

  S=Z K Z*, T=Z*Z=I+D, ||D||<=eta<=1/8,              (1)

where K is an explicit m-by-m Hermitian coarse core and Z,Z* have thin
raw-factor access. K is invertible after the already described orthogonal
support reduction; zero coarse eigenvalues contribute zero to S. Fix t>0.
The original real input can acquire a complex orthonormal coordinate
basis below; complex arithmetic has constant real-arithmetic cost.

There is a randomized, certifying preprocessing procedure using expected
m^(omega+o(1)) exact-real arithmetic, comparisons, positive square roots
and discrete randomness which produces an explicit orthonormal basis
[V_P,V_Q]. In these coordinates put H=t K^-1 and block it into P,Q.
The following conclusions hold for every ACCEPTED output:

  k:=dim(P) <= #{lambda in spec(K): t/3<=lambda<=3t},
  ||H_QP||<=10^(-5), ||H_PP||<4,
  spec(H_QQ) intersect [1/2,2] is empty.               (2)

The expected number of preprocessing attempts is bounded by a constant.
Alternatively cap the attempts at O(log(2/delta)); the failure probability
is then at most delta and the work gains that logarithmic factor.
All matrices used to certify the approximate diagonalization are explicit
m-by-m COARSE matrices. No true dense T is computed or certified.

The arithmetic bound has no log(||K||/t), log(||K^-1||t), spectral-gap
or raw-factor conditioning parameter. This uses the existing exact-field
model for forming and inverting explicit cores. End-to-end finite-word
precision for those potentially ill-conditioned inversions is excluded.

2. A BOUNDED TRANSFORM AND A CHECKABLE APPROXIMATE EVD
----------------------------------------------------
Form, using explicit small-core matrix arithmetic,

  R=2tK(K^2+t^2 I)^-1=2H(I+H^2)^-1.                 (3)

It is Hermitian, commutes with H, and ||R||<=1. Also

  ||H R||=||2t^2(K^2+t^2 I)^-1||<=2.                (4)

These identities are exact. The bounded transform prevents an accuracy
request proportional to t/||K|| in the approximate diagonalization.

Put xi=1/[10^12(m+1)^2]. Obtain candidate U,Lambda, Lambda real diagonal,
and ACCEPT it only if the exact, computable Frobenius tests give

  ||R-U Lambda U*||_F<=xi,
  ||U*U-I||_F<=xi.                                   (5)

Both tests cost O(m^omega) arithmetic, including their matrix products;
the sums of squared entries and their square roots are explicit.

The primary algorithm supplying candidates is Rikhav Shah,
Fast Hermitian Diagonalization with Nearly Optimal Precision (2024):
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.09880
Theorem 4.2, printed p22, gives backward error <=2epsilon||A|| and
singular values of U in [1-epsilon/3,1+epsilon/3], with the stated
near-matrix-multiplication operation count and logarithmic precision.
Algorithm 3, printed p17, explicitly accepts initial/window upper bounds
R0,R>=||A||; its residual guarantee is epsilon(R0+R). Thus an unknown
exact norm is not needed here: round R symmetrically to Frobenius error
<=xi/100, use R0=R_window=2, constant failure parameter, and request
epsilon_EVD<=xi/(100m). The two certificates (5) then pass on the
published success event. The requested accuracy is inverse polynomial
in m, so Theorem 4.2 uses O(log(m+1)) precision bits and
m^(omega+o(1)) floating point operations. Implementation of its scalar
rounding and random primitives in the present model is charged in
Section 6; they are not free oracles.

For ANALYSIS only, let W be the unitary polar factor of U. Certificate
(5) implies ||U-W||<=xi and ||Lambda||<=2. Hence

  R0_hat=W Lambda W*, ||R-R0_hat||<=6xi=:e.           (6)

No polar decomposition or inverse matrix square root is used by the
algorithm. W is only a proof device for interpreting (5).

3. A COMPUTED GREY-BAND GAP AND THE SELECTED SUBSPACE
----------------------------------------------------
Sort the entries of Lambda in [7/10,3/4], append the two endpoints,
and choose the midpoint c of the largest consecutive gap. The gap has
length at least 1/[20(m+1)], so

  7/10<=c<=3/4,
  dist(c,spec(Lambda))>=g:=1/[40(m+1)].                (7)

The endpoints are included in selecting the gap even when the list is
empty. Sorting costs O(m log(m+1)); midpoint selection uses arithmetic
and comparisons only. Since e<g/4, R has no eigenvalue at c, and the
number k of diagonal entries greater than c equals rank(P_*), where
P_* is the exact spectral projection 1_(c,infinity)(R), used only in
the analysis. Let A0 be an exact orthonormal basis of the selected
columns of U, computed by a thin Gram/Cholesky QR. Then

  ||(I-P_*)A0||<=theta:=16xi/g<10^(-6).              (8)

Here is an elementary separation argument, so no exact spectral
projector routine is being invoked. The selected columns of W have
diagonal values >=c+g, whereas R on range(I-P_*) has eigenvalues
<=c-g+e, by (6) and eigenvalue perturbation. Their cross projection
solves a Sylvester equation with right-hand side norm <=e. The integral
formula for that equation, with its ordered Hermitian spectra, bounds
the cross projection by e/(2g-e). The selected columns of U differ
from those of W by <=xi and have singular values >=sqrt(1-xi).
Exact QR changes their span by zero. These facts give (8), with slack.
Equal-rank orthogonal projections have norm difference equal to the
largest such cross-projection norm.

Finally, P_* selects positive lambda of K for which
2(lambda/t)/(1+(lambda/t)^2)>c>=7/10. This interval is contained in
[1/3,3], because the bounded transform equals 3/5 at 1/3 and 3.
Consequently k has the genuine buffered coarse-band bound in (2).
No gap in the original K or in the unknown true operator is assumed.

4. ONE BOUNDED FILTER CONTROLS WEIGHTED LEAKAGE
----------------------------------------------
Merely using A0 as the final basis is insufficient: very small coarse
eigenvalues can amplify its tiny leakage under K^-1. Instead form

  Y=R A0, and take an EXACT orthonormal basis V_P of range(Y).          (9)

On P_* the transform R is positive and >=c>=7/10. Equation (8)
therefore gives sigma_min(Y)>=.69. Writing V_P=Y C^-1 for its thin
QR factor, ||C^-1||<=2. Equations (3),(4),(8) imply

  ||(I-P_*)V_P||<=2theta,
  ||(I-P_*)H V_P||<=4theta.                           (10)

The second bound is the important one: H R is bounded even if H itself
has arbitrarily large norm. On P_*, H is positive and has norm <3,
since its eigenvalues solve 2h/(1+h^2)>7/10. Complete V_P to an exact
unitary basis and write P=V_P V_P*, Q=I-P. Equal ranks and (10) give
||P-P_*||<=2theta. It follows that

  ||Q H V_P||<=||Q P_* H P_* V_P||+||(I-P_*)H V_P||
              <=6theta+4theta=10theta,
  ||V_P*H V_P||<=3+4theta<4.                          (11)

All operations in (9), the unitary completion and the explicit H-block
formation use only small-core arithmetic, QR/Cholesky, comparisons and
square roots in O(m^omega) arithmetic up to the existing fixed exponent
slack. Exact generic eigenvectors are never computed.

The safe-complement spectral assertion in (2) needs care because the
split is not invariant. Suppose a unit vector v in Q satisfied
QHQ v=lambda v with lambda in [1/2,2]. Equation (11) gives
||(H-lambda I)v||<=10theta. On I-P_*, the spectrum of H is outside

  [(4-sqrt(7))/3, (4+sqrt(7))/3],

because c<=3/4. Its distance from [1/2,2] is therefore >1/25.
Projecting the residual yields ||(I-P_*)v||<=250theta. Meanwhile
||P_*v||<=||P_*-P||<=2theta, contradicting 1<=252theta.
Thus no such lambda exists. This proves the safe-complement claim
without treating approximate invariance as exact invariance.

5. THE RESULTING AFFINE SCHUR PENCIL AND NEAR-REAL SOLVES
-------------------------------------------------------
After the computed unitary coordinate change, continue to denote the
implicit factor by Z and its true Gram by T=I+D. For w=t(1+u),

  M(u)=(1+u)H-T,
  w(S-wI)^-1 h=-h-Z M(u)^-1 Z*h.                     (12)

Let

  F(u)=(1+u)H_QQ-T_QQ,
  A(u)=T_PQ-(1+u)H_PQ,
  B(u)=T_QP-(1+u)H_QP.

On the complex disk |u|<=1/3, (2) implies

  ||[(1+u)H_QQ-I]^-1||<=3,
  ||[(1+u)H_QQ-I]^-1 D_QQ||<=3/8,
  ||F(u)^-1||<=24/5,
  ||A(u)||,||B(u)||<=eta+(4/3)10^(-5)<1/4.            (13)

The scalar denominator proof of the first bound separates h<=0,
0<h<1/2, and h>2. These bounds are uniform on the whole disk, including
its real points. The only small-block inverse that may be badly
conditioned is the Schur complement

  C(u)=(1+u)H_PP-T_PP-A(u)F(u)^-1 B(u).               (14)

One true thin T-action on P reveals the unknown T_PP,T_QP; it costs k
raw columns and is charged. H_PP,H_PQ,H_QQ are all explicit coarse data.
The offblocks A,B are affine in u. In a complex basis B(u) is NOT
A(u)* at complex u; rather B(u)=A(conj(u))*. Analytic series must keep
the two affine coefficient families separate.

For actual vertical poles u=i y/t within this disk, |y|<=t/3,
the stronger safe inverse bound is
2 and Richardson contraction <=1/4. The full inverse norm remains
<=2mu/(1-eta)<=3mu, where mu=|w|/|Im w|, as in the earlier raw note.
Using ||A||,||B||<=1/4 in its Schur perturbation argument gives supported
solution error <9mu^2 delta||Z*h|| and normalized physical error
<12mu^2 delta||h||. Hence delta<=epsilon/(32mu^2) suffices, with
O(log(mu/epsilon)) refinement iterations. The offblock residual is
tolerated explicitly; it is not required to shrink proportionally to y.

Thus the former q(k+1)-width deflated algorithm becomes an actual
coarse-input algorithm at the same stated band-conditioned cost, with
the former C_split replaced by the charged preprocessing above.
The disk bounds and affine couplings (13) also permit a shared Taylor
construction of F(u)^-1 B(u); such pole sharing is a separate algorithmic
step and is not inferred merely from the split proof.

Poles with |Im w|>=t/4 already satisfy the fixed-cone bound
|Im w|/|w|>=1/sqrt(17)>eta and can use the original raw resolver.
Therefore the disk |u|<=1/4 contains every genuinely new near-real case;
the larger disk in (13) provides an analytic margin for shared series.

6. SCALAR PRECISION, RANDOMNESS AND CERTIFICATION ARE CHARGED
-----------------------------------------------------------
Forming (3) uses exact small-core inverses and products; these operations
can be ill conditioned numerically but have the stated exact-arithmetic
cost. Every entry of the resulting Hermitian R is bounded by one. It
can be rounded symmetrically to the required inverse-polynomial absolute
accuracy using O(log(m+1)) dyadic comparisons per entry. No scan through
the magnitude range of K is used in this rounding.

Shah's floating point algorithm is simulated at b=O(log(m+1)) bits.
Its bounded-input analysis has only polynomially bounded intermediate
values/exponents at the requested inverse-polynomial accuracy. A rounded
field operation can therefore be implemented with polynomially many
in b exact scalar operations/comparisons on dyadic grids. This adds
polylogarithmic arithmetic overhead, rather than a free rounding oracle.

The published theorem assumes approximate uniform and Gaussian sample
primitives. Uniform samples to b bits use O(b) fair random bits. Gaussian
samples can be supplied with poly(b) scalar work as follows: truncate
at sqrt(Cb), approximate the Gaussian CDF on that interval by integrating
a degree-O(b) Taylor polynomial for exp(-x^2/2), and invert the CDF on a
dyadic grid by bisection. Increase fixed constants so the CDF and inverse
errors are sufficiently smaller than the requested rounding scale.
Couple to exact quantile samples. Gaussian tails and magnitudes below
an inverse polynomial can each be excluded at inverse-polynomial failure
cost; on their complement the inverse-CDF error is small enough for the
relative coupling in Shah's Normal definition. There are polynomially
many calls per attempt, so choose the fixed precision constants to make
their total exceptional probability <1/100. Pi and all scalar Taylor
coefficients are computed to O(b) bits by convergent rational series.
No continuous random-real, Gaussian, logarithm or exponential oracle is
being assumed to have unit exact-field cost.

With constant Shah failure parameter and these charged primitive errors,
one attempt passes (5) with a fixed probability greater than one half.
Any accepted candidate is valid by its exact certificates, irrespective
of the random trajectory. Repeating independent attempts therefore has
constant expected count; capped repetition gives the stated confidence.
Undefined arithmetic or a violated guarded operation bound rejects an
attempt. These guards do not reject the published success trajectories.

7. WHAT HAS AND HAS NOT CHANGED
-------------------------------
The exact-split oracle and its uncharged algebraic eigenvectors have been
removed. The known coarse core now supplies a buffered, explicitly
computed split with sufficiently small weighted residual, valid uniformly
as the pole approaches the real axis. All true-T access remains thin.

The dimension k is still a real condition on the coarse spectrum. A
well-conditioned core with all positive eigenvalues near t has k=m.
Neither the supplied-core structure nor the approximate diagonalization
proves a universal sublinear band bound. Near-real inversion with a small
buffered band is now constructive; the arbitrary-factor LRA gate remains
open when the band is large, and a sharp final filter and extraction are
additional obligations.
