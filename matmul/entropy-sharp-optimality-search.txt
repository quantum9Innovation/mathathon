The main finding is a matching lower for the entropy case of the parent
positive approximation theorem. Its scalar lower mechanism is a short
consequence of classical Pekarskii1995 plus a degree-preserving parity
operation. It must not be promoted as a new rational-approximation
principle, and it gives NO lower for arbitrary semidefinite lifts.

1. STATEMENT AND MODEL
======================

Let f(x)=x log(x)-x+1, f2(x)=(x-1)^2/2, x>0. Write R_(p,q) for
real rational functions of numerator degree<=p, denominator degree<=q.
Define

 A_m=inf_(r in R_(m+1,m)) sup_(x>0) |f(x)-r(x)|/f2(x),

 P_m=inf sup_(x>0) |f(x)-fhat(x)|/f2(x),
 fhat(x)=sum_(i=1)^m u_i (x-1)^2/[1+T_i(x-1)],
 u_i>=0, 0<=T_i<=1.

The ratio at1 uses continuous extension when finite. Infinite-error
rationals do not affect the infimum. Zero weights allow padding.

THEOREM. There are fixed constants c,C>0 such that for every m>=1,

  c exp(-pi sqrt(2m)) <= A_m <= P_m <= C exp(-pi sqrt(2m)). (1.1)

The last upper is the entropy case a=1,b=2 of the independently
reviewed parent positive-rational-approximation.tex. The lower below
does not assume positive poles, positive weights or a quadrature form.

Consequently, for either best two-sided rational error class above,
the minimum pole parameter needed for error<=eta satisfies

  m_min(eta)=log^2(1/eta)/(2pi^2)+O(log(1/eta)).          (1.2)

The same leading resource count holds for positive one-sided lower
rules fhat<=f<=fhat+eta*f2: necessity follows from (1.1), sufficiency
from the parent's positive rescaling at fixed-factor smaller error.
The polynomial-bit preparation companion makes that sufficient pole
count constructive at its stated leading coefficient. No exact
multiplicative constant for A_m or P_m is claimed.

2. PRIMARY CLASSICAL INPUT ACTUALLY CHECKED
==========================================

A. A. Pekarskii, Best uniform rational approximations of Markov
functions, Algebra i Analiz7(2)(1995),121--132; English translation
St. Petersburg Math.J.7(2)(1996),277--285.
  https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/aa551
  https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=aa&option_lang=eng&paperid=551&what=fullt

Read the primary Russian original's Introduction and Theorems1--2;
visually checked printedp122, equation(1.4). Its Theorem2 says:
if a positive measure on [1,a], a>1, has density comparable, above
and below by fixed positive constants, to (t-1)^alpha, alpha>0,
then its Markov transform

  M(w)=integral_1^a dmu(t)/(t-w),  -1<=w<=1,

satisfies

  c_mu exp(-2pi sqrt(alpha*n))
       <= E_(n,n)(M,[-1,1])
       <= C_mu exp(-2pi sqrt(alpha*n)).                  (2.1)

The condition is alpha>0; it DOES include the integer alpha=1.
The noninteger restriction appears later when specializing to bare
power functions, not in this Markov-density theorem. The paper
explicitly credits the lower to Andersson's earlier work. Thus even
the lower half of (2.1) is established classical approximation theory.

We also use the elementary fact that a function analytic on a complex
neighborhood of a fixed real interval has polynomial approximants
with error<=C*rho^(-ell), rho>1. A Bernstein-ellipse/Chebyshev proof
suffices. We will explicitly charge their degree in every transfer.

3. INVERSION SYMMETRY AND A PARITY OPERATION WITHOUT DEGREE LOSS
==============================================================

Put

  H(x)=f(x)/(x-1)^2,  H(1)=1/2.

Elementary algebra gives the EXACT identity

  H(x)+H(1/x)=1.                                      (3.1)

H extends continuously to x=0 with value1 and x=infinity with value0.
Use u=(x-1)/(x+1), so x=(1+u)/(1-u), and let

  G(u)=H((1+u)/(1-u))-1/2,  -1<=u<=1.

Then G is continuous and odd. Any finite-error r in R_(m+1,m)
must have a double zero at1, after removing common numerator/
denominator factors. Hence r(x)/(x-1)^2 belongs to R_(m-1,m).
Its Mobius transform is a real rational function of type[m/m] in u,
bounded at both endpoints when the error is finite. Therefore

  A_m >= 2 E_(m,m)(G,[-1,1]).                           (3.2)

Here shifting by1/2 does not change diagonal rational degrees.

For completeness, the needed parity reduction does NOT require
invoking uniqueness or existence of a minimax rational approximant.
Let r(u)=P(u)/Q(u) have real coefficients, degrees<=m, and no actual
pole on[-1,1]. Cancel removable factors and change sign so Q>0 there.
Define

  r_odd(u)=[P(u)-P(-u)]/[Q(u)+Q(-u)].                   (3.3)

The denominator is strictly positive and even; the numerator is odd.
Both degrees remain<=m. Also r_odd(u) is the convex combination

  [Q(u)r(u)+Q(-u)*(-r(-u))]/[Q(u)+Q(-u)].

Because G is odd, each summand approximates G(u) with the same error
bound. Thus ||G-r_odd||_infinity<=||G-r||_infinity. This is a direct
pointwise argument; no unproved linear convexity of rational classes
is used.

Writing k=floor(m/2), every such r_odd has the form

  r_odd(u)=u R(u^2),       R in R_(k,k).                (3.4)

For m=2k the numerator of R actually has degree<=k-1, a stronger
restriction that may be dropped for a lower. An odd denominator
cannot arise in(3.3), and its value at0 is positive.

4. AN EXACT ONE-ENDPOINT MARKOV REPRESENTATION
=============================================

For y=u^2 let A(y)=atanh(sqrt(y))/sqrt(y), A(0)=1. Directly from
log(x)=2atanh(u),

  G(u)=u F(u^2),
  F(y)=[(1-y)A(y)-1]/(2y),  F(0)=-1/3, F(1)=-1/2.

The removable value at0 follows by the ordinary power series. Since
A(y)=integral_0^1 dt/(1-y*t^2), we obtain the particularly useful
positive Markov representation

  J(y):=-F(y)= (1/4) integral_0^1
                         (1-v)/[sqrt(v)*(1-y*v)] dv.    (4.1)

Near y=1 this has a nonzero (1-y)log(1-y) singularity. No limiting
argument through noninteger power exponents is needed.

We claim a fixed-prefactor endpoint lower

  E_(k,k)(J,[1/2,1]) >= c_0 exp(-2pi sqrt(k))            (4.2)

for all sufficiently large k. To apply(2.1), split(4.1) into v in
[1/2,1] and [0,1/2]. Put w=4y-3. The first piece becomes EXACTLY

  J_loc((w+3)/4)
    =integral_1^5 [(t-1)/(2(t+3)^(3/2))]/(t-w) dt.      (4.3)

The density on[1,5] is comparable to t-1 by positive fixed constants.
Thus(2.1), with alpha=1, gives

  E_(n,n)(w -> J_loc((w+3)/4),[-1,1])
                                 >= a exp(-2pi sqrt(n)). (4.4)

The other piece J_an((w+3)/4) is analytic on a fixed neighborhood
of[-1,1], since its possible real poles are w>=5. Let p_ell be a
polynomial with ||J_an-p_ell||<=B*rho^(-ell), rho>1 fixed. If a
type[k/k] rational approximates J with error delta, subtracting
p_ell gives a rational of type[k+ell/k], hence of diagonal degree
k+ell, approximating J_loc with error at mostdelta+B*rho^(-ell).
Consequently

  E_(k,k)(J,[1/2,1])
    >= a exp(-2pi sqrt(k+ell))-B*rho^(-ell).             (4.5)

Choose ell=ceil(K sqrt(k+1)), with K log(rho)>2pi. Then
sqrt(k+ell)-sqrt(k) is bounded above by a fixed constant, whereas
rho^(-ell)/exp(-2pi sqrt(k)) tends tozero. This proves(4.2) with a
fixed positive c_0, not just a logarithmic-exponent lower. Analytic
subtraction consumes O(sqrt(k)) degree and changes only a fixed
multiplicative factor in the root-exponential scale.

5. FINISH THE MATCHING LOWER AND THE POLE BUDGET
===============================================

By(3.4),(4.1), any odd approximant has error

  sup_(0<=y<=1) sqrt(y)*|F(y)-R(y)|
     >= (1/sqrt2) E_(k,k)(J,[1/2,1])
     >= (c_0/sqrt2) exp(-2pi sqrt(k)).                  (5.1)

Combining(3.2),(3.3),(5.1), with k=floor(m/2), gives

  A_m >= sqrt2*c_0 exp(-2pi sqrt(floor(m/2)))
       >= sqrt2*c_0 exp(-pi sqrt(2m))                   (5.2)

for all sufficiently large m. A_m<=P_m holds because every positive
m-kernel function has type[m+1/m]: with m finite denominators the
common numerator has degree<=m+1, while a node T=0 removes one
finite denominator and adds a quadratic polynomial, still within
that same degree bound. Infinite-error endpoint cases can be omitted.
The parent positive upper supplies the final inequality of(1.1).

To cover finitely many small m, use monotonicity A_m>=A_M for m<=M
and a sufficiently large M where(5.2) already gives a positive lower.
Enlarge the upper constant in the same finite range. Taking logarithms
of(1.1) proves(1.2). No matrix or dimension limit enters this proof.

6. WHAT IS ALREADY A CLASSICAL CONSEQUENCE, AND A POSITIVITY PITFALL
==================================================================

The ALL-REAL entropy rational rate can also be obtained from the same
classical ingredients without the parent positive construction. The
upper half of Pekarskii's theorem, the same analytic subtraction with
O(sqrt(k)) polynomial degree, and a split at v=1/2 give rational
R_k approximating F on[0,1] at C exp(-2pi sqrt(k)). Adding the small
constant F(1)-R_k(1) enforces R_k(1)=-1/2, at most doubling error.
Then

  Hhat(u)=1/2+u R_k(u^2)

has Hhat(1)=0 and Hhat(-1)=1. Its type in u is[2k+1/2k]. Under the
Mobius change to x, the zero at u=1 makes its numerator degree at
most2k and denominator degree at most2k+1. Multiplication by(x-1)^2
therefore gives a type[2k+2/2k+1] approximation to f, i.e. parameter
m=2k+1 in(1.1). Choosing the preceding odd parameter also covers even
m with only a fixed-factor root-exponential cost. Thus the all-real
upper and lower rate are classical consequences of(2.1) and symmetry;
this note does not invent a new asymptotic approximation mechanism.

BUT positivity at the entropy weight endpoint does NOT automatically
follow from Faust--Fawzi2023 Theorem11. That theorem assumes in its
condition(25) that b(x)/x^2 tends tozero at infinity. Here b=f2 and
b(x)/x^2 tends to1/2. Their Corollary2 explicitly excludes beta=2.
Consequently it would be an INVALID shortcut to assert that the
preceding all-real construction already supplies the positive entropy
upper by that theorem alone. An endpoint extension of their positivity
theorem would need proof. The independently reviewed parent positive
construction supplies exactly the needed positive upper for(1.1).

The sharp matching conclusion is therefore useful for interpreting
that construction: its leading pole budget cannot be improved by
dropping positive-residue constraints. The extra positive structure
is obtained without a leading-order degree penalty. This precise
class-level optimality is not a claimed major conceptual breakthrough.

7. THE LOWER ALREADY APPLIES TO BINARY CLASSICAL DIVERGENCES
==========================================================

The scalar test is not an artifact of allowing unnormalized positive
matrices. Let fhat be a positive rational-kernel generator and h=f-fhat.
Then h(1)=h'(1)=0 and h is twice continuously differentiable near1.
For any fixed x>0 and sufficiently small p>0 take binary probabilities

  sigma=(p,1-p),     rho=(p*x,1-p*x),
  y=(1-p*x)/(1-p)=1-p(x-1)/(1-p).

Their divergence difference is

  D_f(rho||sigma)-D_fhat(rho||sigma)
       =p*h(x)+(1-p)*h(y)=p*h(x)+O(p^2),

whereas

  chi^2(rho||sigma)=p(x-1)^2+(1-p)(y-1)^2
                  =p(x-1)^2+O(p^2).

Thus a uniform quantum/classical error bound

  |D_f-D_fhat|<=eta*chi_P^2/2

on all trace-one positive definite pairs already implies, by dividing
by p and letting p decrease tozero,

  |f(x)-fhat(x)|<=eta*f2(x) for EVERY x>0.               (7.1)

The converse follows from the nonnegative scalar/Petz spectral formula.
The one-sided variants transfer identically. Hence(1.2) is necessary
even if the error requirement is tested only on commuting two-state
trace-one inputs, provided the approximant is produced by ONE scalar
positive m-kernel generator as above. The rare-event functional
domination argument is elementary and not claimed as a new principle.

8. WHY THIS IS NOT AN ARBITRARY SDP EXTENSION LOWER
=================================================

The proof lower-bounds rational degree, hence the number of terms in
the specified positive rational generator. It does NOT lower-bound
the total size of an arbitrary spectrahedral lift. A small lifted
SDP can represent algebraic functions of very large rational degree,
or no finite rational degree at all. Nested square-root/mean lifts
are a concrete mechanism for escaping the rational function class.

Fawzi--Saunderson--Parrilo's logarithm approximations explicitly use
such inverse scaling/squaring constructions:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.00812
  https://arxiv.org/html/1705.00812
Any proposed SDP-size lower would have to exclude these algebraic
compositions and all other lifts, rather than merely count poles.
No such lower is claimed here. In particular the parent's 2m/2m+1
small-block Petz lifts are optimal in their rational-generator POLE
resource, not proved optimal over all possible semidefinite lifts.
