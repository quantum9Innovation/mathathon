1. EXACT RANK-ONE REDUCTION
---------------------------
Let

  R_(s,alpha) = sup tr[((H+vv*)^alpha-H^alpha)^s],
  H>=0, ||v||=1, all finite dimensions.

Clearly H^+>=R. Conversely, write E=B-A=E_+-E_- and set
C=A+E_+=B+E_-. The two increasing PSD paths A->C and B->C split into
positive spectral rank-one increments. An increment t uu*, t>0,
has output power trace at most R*t^(alpha*s) by scaling both inputs
by 1/t. The Schatten s-power triangle inequality, applied to all
increments on both paths, gives

  tr|B^alpha-A^alpha|^s <= R tr|B-A|^(alpha*s), 0<s<=1.

Thus H^+=R without a factor depending on rank or number of paths.
For each normalized positive rank-one correction Delta,

  0<=Delta<=(H+I)^alpha-H^alpha<=I.

The first inequalities use operator monotonicity; the last uses scalar
functional calculus for H. This support bound is uniform and is needed
for the layer-cake representation starting at L=0.

2. GAUSSIAN UPPER: ALL ERRORS AND THE LEADING COEFFICIENT
-------------------------------------------------------
Write g(x)=a*(-x)_++b*x_+, kappa=max(a,b), and suppose
||F(x+iy)||<=M_d exp[-g(x)] on |y|<=d. Fix d first; it is never
allowed to depend on L without a separate estimate for M_d.

For ell=L^(3/4), the entire windows

  phi_j(z)=pi^(-1/2) int_[j ell,(j+1)ell] exp[-(z-u)^2] du

sum to one, have positive real values, and satisfy
|phi_j(x+iy)|<=exp(d^2)phi_j(x). Their boundary norms obey

  int ||F(x+iy)phi_j(x+iy)|| dx
      <=2 M_d exp[d^2+kappa^2/4] ell exp(-m_j),
  m_j=min_(I_j) g.

The convolution estimate follows from
g(x)>=g(u)-kappa|x-u| and
pi^(-1/2)int exp[-r^2+kappa|r|]dr<=2 exp(kappa^2/4).

Retain intervals meeting [-(L+ell)/a,(L+ell)/b]. Their number J is
O(L/ell), and the omitted integral is O(M_d exp[-L-ell]). This is
a convolution-tail estimate, so no discontinuous cutoff is introduced
into an analytic integrand.

For each retained interval use

  Q_j=max(ell,L-m_j+3ell), h_j=2*pi*d/Q_j.

The strip trapezoidal theorem gives error
O(M_d ell exp[-m_j-Q_j]); m_j+Q_j>=L+3ell. Summing costs only
O(M_d L exp[-L-3ell]). All horizontal-strip integrability and decay
hypotheses hold for these windows, and scalar duality preserves the
dimension-independent norm estimate for matrices.

Keep nodes within distance 2 sqrt(L) of I_j. Since h_j=O(ell^-1),
the discarded lattice Gaussian mass is bounded uniformly by
O(M_d ell exp[-2L]) per window. This deliberately conservative exponent
is enough; the summed tail is O(M_d L exp[-2L]). The three errors
sum to less than exp(-L) for all sufficiently large L. The threshold
depends on M_d; in the application M_d is uniform over all examples.

The node count is at most

  sum_j [ (ell+4 sqrt(L)) Q_j/(2*pi*d) +2 ].

Using Q_j<=(L-m_j)_++3ell and the kappa-Lipschitz property of
(L-g)_+, the main sum is

  sum_j ell (L-m_j)_+
     = (L^2/2)(1/a+1/b)+O(L ell).

The 3ell overhead is O(L ell); enlarging the windows contributes
O(L^2 sqrt(L)/ell); integer rounding contributes O(J). Each is
O(L^(7/4)) or smaller. Therefore

  #nodes <= L^2(1/a+1/b)/(4*pi*d)+O(L^(7/4)).

There is no missing factor two. Every retained weight h_j phi_j(nh_j)
is positive. Repeated nodes may be merged or counted repeatedly; either
choice preserves the rank upper bound.

For the fractional correction use

  F(z)=(sin(pi alpha)/pi) exp[(alpha+1)z]
          [(H+exp(z))^-1-(H+vv*+exp(z))^-1].

It is analytic on |Im z|<pi, and the sector resolvent inequality
|h+t|>=cos(d/2)(h+|t|), h>=0, gives the two estimates
O(|t|^alpha) and O(|t|^(alpha-1)). Thus a=alpha, b=beta,
and real samples are PSD of rank one. Their M_d depends only on
alpha,d. Consequently, uniformly over finite dimensions and inputs,

  N_Delta(exp(-L))<=c_d L^2+C_d L^(7/4),
  c_d=1/(4*pi*d*alpha*beta), L>=L_0(alpha,d).

The finite initial L-range is controlled by the count at L_0.
Layer cake now gives

  R_(s,alpha)<=2c_d/s^2+O_d(s^(-7/4))+O_d(1).

Take s->0 first and d->pi second. This proves the claimed limsup.
In particular, taking a supremum after integrating is harmless because
the count bound, error constants, and initial-range bound are uniform.

3. LOWER: WOODBURY FACTORIZATION AND BOTH TAILS
---------------------------------------------
Fix epsilon in (0,1), delta>0, and normalize

  rho(x)=c0 exp(epsilon x), x<=0;
  rho(x)=c0 exp(-delta x), x>=0;
  c0=(1/epsilon+1/delta)^-1.

Define m(t)=int rho(x)/(exp(x)+t) dx. On I_R=[-R,R], use the bounded
positive multiplication operator H_R=exp(x) and v_R=sqrt(rho(x)),
without renormalization. Its input norm t_R=||v_R||^2 is at most one.
The actual Woodbury denominator is 1+m_R(t), with m_R<=m. Therefore
replacing it by 1+m, and then restricting t=exp(y) to y in I_R,
decreases the positive correction in Loewner order:

  Delta_R >= T_R T_R*.

Direct kernel algebra gives

  T_R=M_a C_R M_b,
  C(x-y)=1/[2 cosh((x-y)/2)], C_R=P_R C P_R,
  a(x)=sqrt(sin(pi alpha)/pi)*sqrt(rho(x))*exp(-x/2),
  b(x)=exp(alpha x/2)/sqrt(1+m(exp(x))),
  q(x)=(a(x)b(x))^2
       =(sin(pi alpha)/pi)rho(x)exp(-beta x)/(1+m(exp(x))).

The convolution symbol for the Fourier convention int e^(-i xi x) is

  hat C(xi)=pi sech(pi xi).

At zero, the negative half-line of m gives

  m(t)~c0 t^(epsilon-1)int_0^infinity u^(epsilon-1)/(1+u) du
      =c0*pi/sin(pi epsilon)*t^(epsilon-1).

The positive half-line contributes only O(1). At infinity m(t)<=1/t.
Therefore

  q(x)~sin(pi alpha)sin(pi epsilon)/pi^2 * exp(alpha x), x->-infinity,
  q(x)~(sin(pi alpha)/pi)c0 exp[-(beta+delta)x], x->+infinity.

In particular the left exponent is alpha, not alpha+epsilon or beta.
The normalization c0 causes no loss: epsilon,delta are fixed before
s->0, and all fixed positive prefactors tend to one after taking
the s-th power.

4. LOWER: SIMILARITY, ALT, JENSEN, AND FINITE-MATRIX TRANSFER
-----------------------------------------------------------
On a fixed finite interval a,b are bounded above and below by positive
constants. Let D=M_sqrt(ab), L=M_sqrt(a/b). Then

  T_R=L (D C_R D) L^-1.

The positive compact operator S_R=D C_R D has the same positive
eigenvalues as T_R. Weyl's multiplicative eigenvalue/singular-value
inequality, followed by increasing convex exp(p x), gives

  tr|T_R|^p >= tr S_R^p, p>0.

No condition number of the similarity is introduced. For 0<p<=1,
the reversed ALT inequality is exactly

  tr(D C_R D)^p >= tr(D^p C_R^p D^p)
                 = tr(M_(ab)^p C_R^p).

The direction is correct. It follows directly from the standard
ALT statement with r=p and outer exponent one. The usual positive-
operator form applies on this Hilbert space; all operators here are
bounded, C_R is compact, and extended positive traces suffice. One
may also use the trace-ideal approximation version of the finite
inequality. The analytic C_R kernel on a bounded interval has
geometrically decaying approximation numbers, so it belongs to S_p
for every p>0; no trace-ideal divergence is hidden at this step.

Operator concavity of t^p gives the compression inequality

  C_R^p >= P_R C^p P_R, 0<p<=1.

Multiplication by the positive weight and taking a trace preserve this
direction. The resulting trace equals

  [int_(I_R) (ab)^p dx] [int_R (pi sech(pi xi))^p dxi]/(2*pi).

For an explicit justification of the diagonal formula, its right side
is the Hilbert--Schmidt norm squared of
C^(p/2) P_R M_(ab)^(p/2). Plancherel gives precisely the factor 1/(2pi).

Combining the valid direct inequality Delta_R>=T_R T_R*, min-max,
similarity, ALT, and Jensen with p=2s proves, for s<=1/2,

  tr Delta_R^s >= [int_(I_R)q(x)^s dx] J_s/(2*pi),
  J_s=int_R (pi sech(pi xi))^(2s) dxi.

For the finite-matrix step, approximate exp(x) uniformly on I_R by
positive functions H_(R,n) constant on finitely many cells, holding
v_R fixed. Bounded continuous functional calculus gives
Delta_(R,n)->Delta_R in operator norm. Each correction is supported
on the finite-dimensional invariant span of v_R times cell indicators:
on its orthogonal complement E_R vanishes and H_(R,n) acts without
coupling, so the two powers agree there. These are exact finite PSD
examples with input rank-one norm t_R<=1.

Hence R_(s,alpha)>=tr Delta_(R,n)^s/t_R^(alpha*s)
>=tr Delta_(R,n)^s. Each fixed partial sum of singular-value powers
converges under operator-norm convergence. Taking n->infinity and
then the supremum over partial-sum lengths proves
R_(s,alpha)>=tr Delta_R^s, even without assuming trace convergence.
The norm limit is compact because the approximants are finite rank.
Finally let R->infinity in the scalar integral by monotone convergence.

The two elementary factors satisfy

  s int_R q(x)^s dx ->1/alpha+1/(beta+delta),
  J_s=pi^(2s-1)*sqrt(pi)*Gamma(s)/Gamma(s+1/2),
  s J_s->1/pi.

Thus

  liminf s^2 R_(s,alpha)
       >=[1/alpha+1/(beta+delta)]/(2*pi^2).

Let delta->0 after s->0. This proves the matching lower constant
using finite matrices only. No uniformity in delta is required.

5. CORRECT THE FORMAL GLOBAL-COMPRESSION WORDING
----------------------------------------------
The informal global presentation says TT* dominates the square of the
double compression. This is false in general: with

  T=[[1,1],[1,1]], P=diag(1,0),

the matrix TT*-(PTP)(PTP)*=[[1,2],[2,2]] has negative determinant.
The correct alternatives are

  s_j(PTP)<=s_j(T),

or PTT*P>=PTP T*P on the compressed output space, followed by
compression eigenvalue monotonicity. Neither alternative incurs a
constant. More simply, the preferred proof in items 3--4 starts with
bounded H_R and directly obtains Delta_R>=T_R T_R*, so this false
global operator ordering never enters the rigorous argument.

6. THE MOMENT-TO-COUNT STEP: A PRECISE DEFICIT LEMMA
--------------------------------------------------
Put c=1/(4*pi^2*alpha*beta). Choose a finite example Delta_s with
tr Delta_s^s>=R_(s,alpha)-1, and let N_s(L)=N_(Delta_s)(exp(-L)).
Define n_s(x)=s^2 N_s(x/s), x>=0. The exact moment limit says

  int_0^infinity exp(-x)n_s(x)dx ->2c.

For every fixed d<pi the uniform count upper, extended over its finite
initial range, gives FOR ALL x>=0

  n_s(x)<=c_d x^2+C_d s^(1/4)x^(7/4)+K_d s^2,
  c_d=1/(4*pi*d*alpha*beta).

Consequently limsup n_s(x0)<=c x0^2 at every fixed x0>0. To prove
the reverse, suppose along a sequence s->0 that
n_s(x0)<=c x0^2-eta. Choose 0<epsilon<x0 such that
c(x0^2-x^2)<=eta/2 on J=[x0-epsilon,x0]. Since n_s is nondecreasing,

  n_s(x)-c x^2<=-eta/2, x in J.

Writing D=(eta/2)int_J exp(-x)dx>0, the uniform upper outside J gives

  limsup int_0^infinity exp(-x)[n_s(x)-c x^2]dx
        <=-D+2(c_d-c).

The polynomial errors integrate to zero for fixed d. Now choose d<pi
so near pi that 2(c_d-c)<D/2. The right side is negative, contradicting
the exact moment limit. Hence n_s(x0)->c x0^2 for every x0>0.

Taking x0=1 and s=1/L gives a finite example at every sufficiently
large target L with

  N_*(L)>=N_s(L)=(c-o(1))L^2.

Together with the uniform upper this proves the limit. Monotonicity
is used in the correct direction: a deficit at x0 persists immediately
to its LEFT. Moments of unrelated examples alone would not establish
the conclusion without this monotonicity and the sharp uniform upper.

7. COMPRESSION INTERPRETATION AND SCOPE
-------------------------------------
For operator-norm error at most exp(-L), the minimum approximation
rank of a positive Delta equals the number of its eigenvalues strictly
greater than exp(-L). This is the best-rank spectral approximation
theorem and remains true when arbitrary, nonpositive approximants are
allowed. Thus the count limit is exactly the universal worst-case
rank coefficient, not merely a coefficient for one quadrature method.

For any fixed positive integer r, take r identical direct sums for the
lower. The perturbation has rank r and norm one. For the upper, the
same resolvent integrand has sample rank at most r and the same norm
envelope, giving coefficient r*c. Even indefinite differences between
PSD endpoints have this count upper: each sample has rank at most r,
and the sector estimates only need ||B-A||=1. Positivity of samples
is only needed if a positive quadrature approximant is itself desired.

The exact theorem is for fixed alpha; no simultaneous alpha->0 or
alpha->1 asymptotic has been audited. The examples may depend on s or
L and their dimension may grow arbitrarily. This does not claim an
eigenvalue counting asymptotic for every single fixed update.

10. CENTRAL RESOLVENT IDENTITY AND THE ODD CHANNELS
-------------------------------------------------
Choose the probability density

  rho(x)=c0 exp(x), x<=0; rho(x)=c0 exp(-delta x), x>=0;
  c0=(1+1/delta)^-1, delta>0.

In the original sign basis put H=diag(D,-D), D=exp(x),
v=(w,w)/sqrt(2), w=sqrt(rho), E=vv*, and A=H-E/2, B=H+E/2.
In the swap-eigenbasis these become

  H=[[0,D],[D,0]], v=(w,0), E=diag(ww*,0).

The central Woodbury calculation is

  R_B(z)-R_A(z)
      =-R_H(z) E R_H(z)/(1-m(z)^2/4), m(z)=v*R_H(z)v.

It follows by subtracting the two formulas with denominators
1+m/2 and 1-m/2; there is no missing factor two. For z=it,

  R_H(it)=[[it R,D R],[D R,it R]], R=(D^2+t^2)^-1,
  m(it)=iM(t), M(t)=t int rho(x)/(exp(2x)+t^2) dx,
  omega(t)=1/(1+M(t)^2/4).

The product R_H(it) E R_H(it) has diagonal blocks
-t^2 Rww*R and D Rww*R D, and cross blocks proportional to it.
Combining the two conjugate poles in the odd scalar integral therefore
gives EXACTLY

  f_o(B)-f_o(A)=diag(S_+,-S_-),
  S_+=c_o int_0^infinity t^(alpha+2) omega Rww*R dt,
  S_-=c_o int_0^infinity t^alpha omega D Rww*R D dt,
  c_o=2 cos(pi alpha/2)/pi.

Both channels are positive. With C_1(u)=1/(2 cosh u), one has
hat C_1(xi)=(pi/2)sech(pi xi/2). The substitution t=exp(y) gives

  S_+=T_+ T_+*,
  T_+=M_(sqrt(c_o rho) exp(-x)) C_1
                 M_(exp((alpha+1)x/2) sqrt(omega(exp(x)))),

  S_-=T_- T_-*,
  T_-=M_sqrt(c_o rho) C_1
                 M_(exp((alpha-1)x/2) sqrt(omega(exp(x)))).

The kernel identity used here is
C_1(x-y)=exp(x+y)/(exp(2x)+exp(2y)). In each factorization
the square of the product of its multipliers is exactly

  q_o(x)=c_o rho(x) exp(-beta x) omega(exp(x)).

Thus the previous similarity/ALT/Jensen lower applies separately to
each channel with this same scalar weight. The signs in the output
cause no cancellation in tr|Delta|^s: that trace is the SUM of both
positive channel traces.

11. EVEN MAP: TWO SINGULAR CONTRIBUTIONS AND TWO SIMILARITIES
----------------------------------------------------------
The even scalar integral instead uses the difference of the two poles.
The above cross blocks then give EXACTLY

  f_e(B)-f_e(A)=[[0,O],[O*,0]],
  O=F D,
  F=c_e int_0^infinity t^(alpha+1) omega Rww*R dt >=0,
  c_e=2 sin(pi alpha/2)/pi.

In particular tr|f_e(B)-f_e(A)|^s=2 tr|O|^s. On a fixed bounded

  S=D^(1/2) F D^(1/2),
  O=D^(-1/2) S D^(1/2).

No estimate by the similarity condition number is needed: Weyl's

tr|O|^s>=tr S^s. Logarithmic substitution gives S=U U*, where

  U=sqrt(c_e) M_(sqrt(rho) exp(-x/2)) C_1
                          M_(exp(alpha x/2) sqrt(omega(exp(x)))).

This formula includes the full dt=exp(y)dy factor. Its product weight is

  q_e(x)=c_e rho(x) exp(-beta x) omega(exp(x)).

Apply the similarity/ALT/Jensen lower to U at exponent p=2s. This is
the SECOND similarity; the first turned O into S. Consequently tr|O|^s
has the same leading coefficient as EACH odd channel, and the factor
two from the full off-diagonal operator makes the total coefficients
equal for the even and odd maps.

12. TAILS AND FINITE-MATRIX PASSAGE FOR THE FULL-LINE LOWER
--------------------------------------------------------
At zero, changing variables a=exp(x) gives

  M(t)=c0 arctan(1/t)
       +c0 t int_1^infinity a^(-delta-1)/(a^2+t^2) da.

The second term is O(t), so M(t)->c0*pi/2. At infinity
M(t)<=1/t->0. Therefore omega has positive finite limits at both
ends. Both q_o and q_e have left exponential slope alpha and right
slope beta+delta, exactly as required.

For C_1, the Fourier integral is

  J_(1,s)=(pi/2)^(2s)*(2/pi)*sqrt(pi)*Gamma(s)/Gamma(s+1/2),
  s J_(1,s)->2/pi.

Together with s int q^s ->1/alpha+1/(beta+delta), the factor 1/(2pi)
from Plancherel yields the single-channel coefficient

  [1/alpha+1/(beta+delta)]/pi^2.

Doubling, then sending delta->0 AFTER s->0, yields
2/(pi^2*alpha*beta), matching the full-line upper.

All these formulas can and should be implemented first on I_R=[-R,R].
Keep rho unnormalized there. Its M_R(t)<=M(t), hence
omega_R(t)>=omega(t). For the odd map, each actual positive channel
therefore dominates its global-omega factor restricted also in y to I_R.

For the even map, first form the actual bounded similarity
S_R=D_R^(1/2) F_R D_R^(1/2)>=0. Replacing omega_R by smaller omega
and restricting y lowers S_R in Loewner order. This gives the positive
U_R U_R* needed for the second similarity/ALT/Jensen step. Replacing
weights directly inside an arbitrary nonselfadjoint O would not itself
justify a singular-value inequality, but that invalid move is absent.

Approximate D_R by finite step functions, keeping w_R fixed. Both full
Hermitian endpoints converge in operator norm, and both continuous
functional calculi converge uniformly on their common bounded real
spectral interval. Corrections are supported in the direct sum of the
two finite cell-vector spans. Their input norm is
t_R=int_(I_R)rho<=1, so dividing by t_R^(alpha*s) can only increase
the ratio. Fixed singular-value partial sums pass to the limit and
give the desired lower. Finally monotone convergence applies to
the scalar q-integral. Thus this lower uses genuine finite matrices;
no unbounded-operator functional calculus is required.

13. RECTANGULAR POLAR AND ABSOLUTE MAPS
-------------------------------------
The rectangular polar map P_alpha(X)=U|X|^alpha has EXACTLY the same
optimal power-ratio constant as the full-Hermitian odd map. Indeed
the Hermitian dilation J(X)=[[0,X],[X*,0]] satisfies

  f_o(J(X))=[[0,P_alpha(X)],[P_alpha(X)*,0]].

Both numerator and denominator power traces in its ratio acquire the
same factor two. This proves the rectangular upper from the Hermitian
one; the Hermitian square subclass proves the reverse inequality.

There is also a valid extension to the rectangular absolute map
Q_alpha(X)=|X|^alpha, although Section 6 initially refrained from
asserting it. Its exact leading coefficient is ALSO

  lim_(s down to 0) s^2 H_(s,alpha)(Q_alpha)
       =2/(pi^2*alpha*beta).

The Hermitian square subclass supplies the even-map lower. For the
matching upper it is essential to use the right diagonal block of the
even dilation integrand, rather than lose a factor two from the complete
dilation inequality. Here is a direct rank count.

If Y-X=E=u v* has rank one, then

  Y*Y-X*X=(X*u)v*+v(u*X)+(u*u)vv*

has range contained in span{X*u,v}, so its rank is at most two. Let

  G_X(t)=X*X (X*X+t^2 I)^-1.

For real t, and in fact for every admissible complex t, the resolvent
identity gives rank(G_Y(t)-G_X(t))<=2. The difference is the right
diagonal block of F_t(J(Y))-F_t(J(X)), where
F_t(Z)=Z^2(Z^2+t^2 I)^-1. Since ||J(E)||=||E||, the already verified
dilation resolvent estimate implies

  ||G_Y(t)-G_X(t)||<=C_d min(1,||E||/|t|), |arg t|<=d<pi/2.

After t=||E|| exp(y), the logarithmic integrand for the absolute
fractional-power difference has strip pi/2, sample rank TWO, and the
same two-sided exponential envelope. The Gaussian quadrature gives
exactly the whole-line rank coefficient and hence exactly its moment
limsup for each rank-one rectangular increment.

Finally telescope an arbitrary rectangular difference along its singular
value decomposition E=sum_j t_j u_j v_j*. The Schatten s-power triangle
and positive degree-alpha homogeneity give denominator
