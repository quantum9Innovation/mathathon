Algorithm proof checks
----------------------
1. For distinct ordinary graph edges, the Frobenius identity is exact:
     ||E||_F^2 = sum_v(sum_{e incident v} d_e)^2+2 sum_e d_e^2.
   Hence a<=sqrt(k)||E|| and b/a<=sqrt(2k), including signed changes.
   Duplicate edits must first be merged. This prevents a hidden arbitrary
   representation-cancellation parameter in the unit-mass graph theorem.

2. R-S=RES=(RU)J(SU)^T is an exact rank-at-most-k identity. No common
   nullspace, connectivity, small inverse, or nonzero spectral gap is used.
   Both shifted endpoint systems are SPD SDD even for disconnected graphs.

3. Relative energy error eta gives Euclidean error <=eta||u||/t for a
   column with RHS u, because A>=tI and u^TA^-1u<=||u||^2/t. Frobenius
   summation over columns gives block error <=eta sqrt(b)/t. Expanding
   Vhat J What^T gives the claimed (2eta+eta^2)b/t^2 bound. The argument
   does not require a linear, symmetric, or shared approximate solver.

4. The fractional resolvent integral has the correct sign and weight.
   The complex-sector inequality and R-S bounds yield a strip envelope
   a^alpha min(exp(alpha x),exp(-(1-alpha)x)), independently of graph
   eigenvalues. Contour shifting and the trapezoid Fourier sum give the
   claimed exponentially small infinite-rule error; both tails and node
   counts are dimension independent after intrinsic normalization by a.

5. The sum of solve errors is bounded by a geometric series dominated by
   the smallest retained node. With eta=c chi^-1 rho^(1/alpha), its power
   of rho is exactly one: 1/alpha-(1-alpha)/alpha=1. The conservative
   rho=eps/[C(k+1)] absorbs a^alpha/||E||^alpha<=k^(alpha/2). Approximate
   positive weights can be budgeted using the norm-integrable envelope.

6. Returned factors require only diagonal sign/scalar rescaling. They
   avoid an uncharged n-by-k by k-by-k multiply. Symmetrization has the
   stated 2k per-node signed factor and cannot increase spectral error.
   Construction has 2kq column solves and O(nkq) additional factor work.
   Explicit dense expansion is separately and correctly charged.

7. The runtime must remain EXPECTED, as in KMP Theorem 4.6. Arbitrary
   confidence is supported by KMP's preconditioning-chain statement;
   alternatively a robust amplification gives it without any ambiguity:
   independently run a constant-failure energy solver at eta/3 accuracy
   s=O(log(kq/delta)) times, choose an output minimizing the median of
   pairwise A-energy distances. If a majority are good, a good candidate
   has median distance <=2eta||x||_A/3; the selected candidate shares a
   good neighbor and has error <=eta||x||_A. Sparse evaluations of all
   pairwise squared distances cost O((m+n)s^2), within the suppressed
   logarithmic factors. A Chernoff bound and union bound finish the event.

8. Arbitrary vertex masses need the explicitly stated log(b/a) parameter
   for signed edits. The ordinary-graph b/a bound cannot silently be used
   there. For positive updates and arbitrary masses b=tr(E)<=sqrt(k)a,
   so the additional parameter is controlled in that special case.

9. For arbitrary alpha, the theorem explicitly counts scalar-power
   evaluations. The alpha=1/2 specialization has an ordinary arithmetic
   and square-root realization using dyadic logarithmic nodes and scalar
   approximations of fixed constants. No bit-complexity or floating-point
   stability theorem has been established.

Path realization proof checks
-----------------------------
1. Restricting to the cyclic subspace loses no nonzero correction singular
   values. A+tau I handles singular A while retaining all strict counts
   above a fixed threshold for sufficiently small tau. Complex rank-one
   data reduce to a real diagonal model by removing phases of u.

2. Lanczos gives an irreducible SPD Jacobi block with negative off-diagonal
   entries. Its inverse is strictly positive, hence d=T^-1e1>0. DTD has
   path off-diagonals and row sums d1 e1, so a single grounded end edge of
   conductance d1 realizes it. Masses d_i^2 give precisely T, rather than
   merely a congruent operator with different fractional calculus.

3. The finite-ground-mass formula is exact: off-diagonal ground coupling
   is -e1/R, and the ground diagonal is d1/R^2. Its null vector is (R,d).
   Increasing conductance by d1^2 produces v=(-d1/R,e1), so its update
   norm is 1+d1^2/R^2. Scaling both conductance sets by its reciprocal
   enforces exact unit update norm without changing masses or edge count.

4. In the R-to-infinity limit the base and updated operators converge to
   0 direct-sum T and 0 direct-sum (T+e1e1^T). Continuity of fractional
   powers transfers every finite STRICT singular count, including when
   other singular values happen to equal the threshold. This proves
   equality of supremal count functions at every positive threshold.

5. For fixed r, r disjoint norm-one witnesses have orthogonal update
   vectors and total update norm one. Arbitrarily weak new edges between
   consecutive path endpoints turn their disjoint union into one connected
   path; they are unchanged by the r edits. Continuity preserves the
   summed strict counts. The generic PSD upper gives the matching sharp
   leading coefficient. No finite-threshold rank-r universality is needed.

6. The sharp class uses positive masses. The manuscript correctly avoids
   claiming the same sharp coefficient for ordinary unit-mass paths or
   for loop-free degree-normalized edge updates. A reversible-chain
   interpretation uses compensating holding loops and fixed masses.

  Theorem 4.6 explicitly states expected nearly-linear energy-error solve

  the randomized preconditioning-chain event.
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.4842

and symmetric Jacobi matrices, ELA 24 (2013), 237--254, primary PDF read

  https://doi.org/10.1016/S0024-3795(96)00316-3
